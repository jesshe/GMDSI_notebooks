## 1. Gaussian-field representations from spatial statistics and UQ (SPDE/Matérn, KL and PCA truncations, FFT sampling, hierarchical hyperparameters) versus pilot points and dense covariance matrices

### Takeaway
The spatial-statistics and PDE-constrained-inversion communities represent a Gaussian prior by a sparse precision operator (the inverse of an elliptic differential operator: SPDE / Whittle-Matérn / "BiLaplacian") rather than by a dense covariance matrix, which makes sampling, anisotropy, non-stationarity and unknown hyperparameters cheap at 10^5-10^6 cells; this has been used in adjoint-based groundwater and poroelastic inversion (hIPPYlib) but I found no published use inside a PEST/PEST++-style non-intrusive ensemble workflow. The second transferable idea is hierarchical (hyperparameter-uncertain) priors: several studies show that fixing the variogram is a strong and often unjustified assumption, but also that a plain iterative ensemble smoother handles uncertain anisotropy/range badly unless the hyperparameter-to-field derivative is supplied analytically.

### Cited Findings
- SPDE approach: a Gaussian field with Matérn covariance is the solution of a fractional elliptic SPDE driven by white noise, and a finite-element discretisation gives a Gaussian Markov random field whose precision matrix is sparse; the construction extends to non-stationary, anisotropic and manifold-valued fields by letting the SPDE coefficients vary in space — Lindgren, Rue & Lindström (2011), "An explicit link between Gaussian fields and Gaussian Markov random fields: the stochastic partial differential equation approach", JRSS-B 73(4):423-498 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2011.00777.x)
- Ten-year review: the computational appeal is that one works with the precision rather than the covariance, which yields sparse structure; the review places the SPDE method next to covariance-function, spectral, RKHS and graph characterisations, covers a non-Gaussian generalisation (Bolin), and lists software: R-INLA, inlabru and rSPDE for Gaussian SPDE models and ngme for non-Gaussian ones — Lindgren, Bolin & Rue (2022), "The SPDE approach for Gaussian and non-Gaussian fields: 10 years and still running", Spatial Statistics 50:100599, doi:10.1016/j.spasta.2022.100599 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01084)
- hIPPYlib (PDE-constrained Bayesian inversion library, FEniCS + PETSc, GPL) defines the prior covariance as the inverse of the ν-th power of a Laplacian-like operator, C_prior = (−γΔ + δI)^(−ν) with ν > d/2; γ and δ set the correlation length (∝ sqrt(γ/δ)) and pointwise variance, they "can be constant (in which case the prior is stationary) or spatially varying", and an anisotropic operator A = −γ∇·(Θ∇) + δI with s.p.d. tensor Θ models stronger correlation in one direction; the paper refers to Lindgren et al. (2011) for exact expressions and uses Robin boundary conditions to reduce boundary artefacts; the class is called `BiLaplacianPrior` — Villa, Petra & Ghattas (2021), "hIPPYlib: an extensible software framework for large-scale inverse problems governed by PDEs; Part I", ACM TOMS (journal volume/DOI ‡: 47(2), art. 16, doi:10.1145/3428447) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948)
- Same paper, quantitative: in the elliptic coefficient-field example the parameter has 4,225 degrees of freedom, there are 50 observations, and retaining about r = 30 generalized eigenvectors of the prior-preconditioned data-misfit Hessian is sufficient for the low-rank Laplace approximation of the posterior; MAP and linearised-posterior cost "measured in linearized forward PDE solves" is "independent of the parameter dimension"; prior samples are drawn by exploiting the covariance being the inverse of a differential operator (i.e. by PDE solves on white noise, not by factorising a dense matrix) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948)
- Groundwater application of the Whittle-Matérn/low-rank-Hessian machinery: Bayesian inference of laterally heterogeneous permeability in a transient, fully coupled 3-D poroelastic aquifer model of a Nevada aquifer test from InSAR surface deformation; Part I gives the MAP estimate (WRR 2020, doi:10.1029/2020WR027391), Part II quantifies uncertainty with a generalized preconditioned Crank-Nicolson MCMC whose proposal is a low-rank Laplace approximation, on the grounds that "the data are informative in only a few directions of parameter space" — Alghamdi, Hesse, Chen, Villa & Ghattas (2021), WRR, doi:10.1029/2021WR029775 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04577)
- hIPPYlib-MUQ couples hIPPYlib to the MUQ sampling library; its abstract reports roughly 50x speed-ups over conventional black-box and gradient-based MCMC when Hessian information of the log-posterior is exploited — Kim, Villa, Parno, Marzouk, Ghattas & Petra, "hIPPYlib-MUQ: a Bayesian inference software framework for integration of data with complex predictive models under uncertainty" (arXiv 2021-2022; journal version in ACM TOMS ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00713)
- Function-space justification: priors built from inverse elliptic operators are trace-class, guaranteeing bounded pointwise variance and a well-posed infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problem (hIPPYlib paper citing Stuart 2010 and Bui-Thanh et al. 2013) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948); Stuart (2010), "Inverse problems: a Bayesian perspective", Acta Numerica 19:451-559 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492910000061)
- Hierarchical Matérn priors from imaging: non-stationary Matérn priors are constructed via SPDEs with a hyperprior (Gaussian or Cauchy random field) on a spatially varying correlation length, so that Bayesian inversion can balance the competing demands of smoothness and edge preservation; demonstrated on interpolation and numerical differentiation only — Roininen, Girolami, Lasanen & Markkanen, "Hyperpriors for Matérn fields with applications in Bayesian inversion" (arXiv 2016; journal version Inverse Problems and Imaging 2019 ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02989)
- Ensemble Kalman inversion and parameterization: because basic EKI solutions lie in the linear span of the initial ensemble, the parameterization largely decides success; hierarchical parameterizations that learn length-scale (and, in the full paper, amplitude and regularity) of Whittle-Matérn fields and geometric/level-set parameterizations are demonstrated on electrical impedance tomography, groundwater flow and source inversion — Chada, Iglesias, Roininen & Stuart (2018), "Parameterizations for ensemble Kalman inversion", Inverse Problems 34:055009, doi:10.1088/1361-6420/aab6d9 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01781)
- Hierarchical priors with an iterative ensemble smoother (reservoir/groundwater-type test): with uncertain anisotropy orientation, correlation range and range ratio written in non-centred form m = m_pr + L(θ)z, a standard IES (200 members, Gaspari-Cohn localization) on a 30×15-cell two-phase flow problem (450 latent variables + 3 hyperparameters, water-cut data at 6 producers) ended with mean squared data mismatch ≈13,000 against an expected ≈240; a "hybrid IES" that computes dm/d(θ,z) analytically and only the data-to-field sensitivity from the ensemble reached ≈1,151 with 100 members (reference: ≈365 with the correct covariance, ≈2,142 with orientation wrong by π/2). The author concludes that a straightforward iterative ensemble smoother fails for the non-centred hierarchical model with uncertain anisotropy (spurious correlations and strong nonlinearity; the ensemble represents the local sensitivity matrix poorly), while stating that the hierarchical approach is "much more robust against model misspecification"; only stationary geometric anisotropy on a small 2-D case was tested — Oliver (2022), "Hybrid iterative ensemble smoother for history matching of hierarchical models", Mathematical Geosciences, doi:10.1007/s11004-022-10014-0 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01116)
- MCMC with uncertain geostatistical hyperparameters in hydrogeology: circulant-embedding generation of the Gaussian field plus interpolation-based dimension reduction lets DREAM-type MCMC infer a 10,000-cell 2-D conductivity field jointly with mean, sill, integral scales, anisotropy and the Matérn smoothness parameter from synthetic tracer data; dimension reduced by a factor of 40 without preventing a good data fit; reported to beat a Karhunen-Loève parameterization, sequential Gibbs sampling and the method of anchored distributions for equal budget; the posterior realizations covered a wide range of geostatistical models, which the authors take as questioning the common practice of assuming a fixed variogram — Laloy, Linde, Jacques & Vrugt (2015), WRR 51(6):4224-4243, doi:10.1002/2014WR016395 [Source](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3011n0x1)
- pCN-MCMC with parallel tempering extended to uncertain hyperparameters of multi-Gaussian log-conductivity fields — Xiao, Xu, Reuschen, Nowak & Hendricks Franssen (2021), WRR, doi:10.1029/2021WR030051 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/2021wr030051)
- Low-rank (principal-component) geostatistical inversion that is non-intrusive: the Principal Component Geostatistical Approach uses the leading principal components of the prior covariance to avoid Jacobian assembly; it is "matrix-free", can be linked to any simulator, is reported in search-returned PCGA literature (specific paper not opened) to have been applied to hydraulic tomography with 3 million unknowns, and in an MRI-tracer sandbox case reconstructed 99,072 log-K values with κ = 500 forward runs per iteration instead of the 51,584 a full geostatistical solution would need — Kitanidis & Lee (2014), WRR, doi:10.1002/2013WR014630 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/2013wr014630); Lee, Yoon, Kitanidis, Werth & Valocchi (2016), WRR, doi:10.1002/2015WR018483 [Source](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1257809-scalable-subsurface-inverse-modeling-huge-data-sets-application-tracer-concentration-breakthrough-data-from-magnetic-resonance-imaging)
- FFT-based sampling and hierarchical matrices (background, not re-verified): circulant embedding gives exact stationary Gaussian samples on regular grids at FFT cost (Dietrich & Newsam 1997, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 18:1088-1107 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827592240555); FFT moving-average generator separates white noise from covariance structure (Le Ravalec, Noetinger & Hu 2000, Math. Geology 32:701-723 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1007542406333); hierarchical-matrix compression of dense covariance for large-scale geostatistical inversion (Saibaba & Kitanidis 2012, WRR 48:W05522 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR011778)
- What pyEMU offers today (inspected in the copy of pyEMU vendored in this repository): `pyemu.utils.geostats` provides `ExpVario`, `GauVario` and `SphVario` variogram classes (no Matérn class), `OrdinaryKrige`, and `SpecSim2d`, documented as "2-D unconditional spectral simulation for regular grids"; `pyemu.helpers.geostatistical_draws` constructs "a parameter ensemble from a prior covariance matrix implied by geostatistical structure(s) and parameter bounds"; `kl_setup`/`kl_apply` give a Karhunen-Loève alternative to pilot points; `zero_order_tikhonov` and `first_order_pearson_tikhonov` build the regularisation equations [Source](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/utils/geostats.py) [Source](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/utils/helpers.py) [Source](https://pyemu.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pyemu.utils.html)
- Current pilot-point practice as described by its developers: Tikhonov regularization and truncated-subspace methods "optimally and unconditionally stabilize the inverse problem", removing the need to pre-limit pilot-point numbers; pilot points are now used in multi-scale schemes (McKenna et al. 2020; White et al. 2020) and with iterative ensemble smoothers; extension from two-point to multiple-point statistics is named as the main future direction — White & Lavenue (2023), "Advances in the pilot point inverse method: Où en sommes-nous maintenant?", Comptes Rendus Géoscience 355(S1):9-17, doi:10.5802/crgeos.161 [Source](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.161/)

### Inferences
- A dense prior covariance for N cells needs N² doubles: about 80 GB at N = 10^5 and 8 TB at N = 10^6, which is why PEST practice confines the covariance to pilot points (10^2-10^4 points) and kriges to the grid. Pilot points are therefore a low-rank (inducing-point) approximation of the Gaussian prior; SPDE precision matrices, FFT generators and PCGA are three other approximations with different trade-offs (sparse and unstructured-mesh-friendly; exact but regular-grid only; low-rank but smooth).
- The SPDE generator is naturally "non-centred": field = solve(A(θ), white noise z). In a PstFrom-style workflow, θ (range, anisotropy tensor, variance, possibly spatially varying through a few hyper-pilot-points) and z (one standard-normal number per mesh node) can both be exposed as parameters, with the sparse solve done in the pre-processor. That would give grid-scale stochastic parameters with a geologically meaningful, non-stationary covariance without ever forming a covariance matrix, and independent z values make the IES prior ensemble trivial to draw. Oliver (2022) is the warning: the z-and-θ coupling is strongly nonlinear, so plain PESTPP-IES would likely need either the hybrid derivative trick, staged estimation (hyperparameters first with few super-parameters), or multimodal/local updates.
- PCGA is structurally the nearest outside method to PEST's SVD-assist: both spend a fixed number of model runs per iteration on a subspace. PCGA chooses the subspace from the prior covariance alone; SVD-assist chooses it from the Jacobian.
- For unstructured MODFLOW 6 (DISV/DISU) grids, FFT-based generators (including pyEMU's `SpecSim2d`) do not apply directly, while SPDE/FEM precision matrices do; this is where the transfer has most practical value.

- Two targeted searches found no paper that uses an SPDE/GMRF sparse-precision prior inside an ensemble smoother or EnKF for groundwater or reservoir history matching. Absence in two searches is not proof of absence.
- I could not re-verify in this session the non-stationary pilot-point utilities that I recall exist in PLPROC and pypestutils (spatially varying bearing, anisotropy and range treated as adjustable hyperparameters); one search returned nothing on pypestutils. The report writer should check https://github.com/pypest/pypestutils and the PLPROC manual before stating what PEST-suite tooling already covers.

- GSTools and gstlearn (which I recall implements SPDE-based simulation and kriging) were not opened; their capabilities are not documented here.
- Parameter dimension and data count for the Alghamdi et al. Nevada study were not on the abstract page.

## 2. Non-Gaussian and categorical geology: MPS/training images, truncated and pluri-Gaussian, object/process/rule-based models, level sets, implicit structural models, and hierarchical structure-plus-property approaches

### Takeaway
Categorical priors can now be conditioned to head data by several routes (MPS resampling, PoPEx, ESMDA on multi-resolution MPS variables, level sets with EnKF/EKI, travelling pilot points, latent-variable generators), but every head-to-head test shows the result is dominated by the choice of training image or conceptual model, and ensemble smoothers in particular produce confident-but-wrong connected features. The most defensible direction for decision-support groundwater modelling is hierarchical: sample hydrostratigraphic structure from a geology-driven generator, sample within-unit properties conditionally, and treat structure as an uncertain (or scenario) variable rather than a fixed zonation; data-space inversion offers a way to do this without ever adjusting the categorical field.

### Cited Findings
- Review framing: modern geostatistical simulation can generate realizations consistent with a conceptual geological model and rock physics maps them to observables, but the most general inversion frameworks are "often computationally intractable" at scale, so one must simplify the geological prior, the forward model or the inversion; data should also be used to falsify or corroborate geological scenarios rather than only estimate parameters — Linde, Renard, Mukerji & Caers (2015), Advances in Water Resources 86:86-101, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.09.019 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01602)
- MPS foundations (background, not re-verified): snesim (Strebelle 2002, Math. Geology 34:1-21 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1014009426274); Direct Sampling (Mariethoz, Renard & Straubhaar 2010, WRR 46:W11536 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008WR007621); textbook Mariethoz & Caers (2014), "Multiple-point geostatistics: stochastic modeling with training images", Wiley ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118662953)
- Benchmark of three discrete inversion methods on a groundwater problem (synthetic pumping test in a fluvial channelized aquifer; 128×128 cells of 5 m; 720 transient head data from 10 wells): PoPEx with MPS (16,384 unknowns, 50,000 forward runs), ESMDA with MPS using continuous variables on coarse "pyramid" levels (about 204 unknowns; ensemble 128 × 16 assimilations = 2,048 runs) and DREAM-ZS on a Wasserstein-GAN latent space (50 unknowns; 160,000 runs). All performed well with the training image that generated the truth and degraded with two alternative training images. PoPEx was "the most robust" with fewest geological artefacts but slow; ESMDA converged very fast then plateaued, produced confident but incorrect channel patterns and sometimes failed to place the pumping well in the permeable facies; DREAM-ZS/WGAN often gave the best data match and protection-zone prediction but was overconfident in placing incorrect features, and its facies Brier score worsened while CRPS on data improved — Juda, Straubhaar & Renard (2023), Comptes Rendus Géoscience 355(S1):19-44, doi:10.5802/crgeos.160 [Source](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.160/)
- MPS-based samplers behind that benchmark (background, not re-verified): iterative spatial resampling (Mariethoz, Renard & Caers 2010, WRR 46:W11530 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010WR009274); sequential Gibbs sampling / extended Metropolis (Hansen, Cordua & Mosegaard 2012, Comput. Geosci. 16:593-611 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-011-9271-1); posterior population expansion (Jäggli, Straubhaar & Renard 2017, WRR 53:2902-2916 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR019550)
- PEST-compatible categorical parameterization: the Traveling Pilot Point (TRIPS) method estimates pilot-point locations as well as values, with Tikhonov regularization to keep fields realistic, and was combined with null-space Monte Carlo and a polynomial-chaos method (NSMC gave more ensemble variability) — Khambhammettu, Renard & Doherty (2020), Advances in Water Resources 138:103556, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2020.103556 [Source](https://www.peeref.com/works/20033073); a follow-up applies TRIPS with an iterative ensemble smoother to a hypothetical contaminated aquifer with categorical facies in MODFLOW 6 (Groundwater, 2023; details not retrievable) [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37945376/)
- Level sets with ensemble filters (reservoir): facies boundaries are represented by level-set functions defined on a set of "representing nodes" with Gaussian values, interpolated to the grid, and the nodal values are the EnKF state — Chang, Zhang & Lu (2010), "History matching of facies distribution with the EnKF and level set parameterization", J. Comput. Phys. 229(20):8011-8030, doi:10.1016/j.jcp.2010.07.005 [Source](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:2638301)
- Bayesian level sets: treating the level-set function as the unknown with a Gaussian-field prior gives a well-posed posterior (Lipschitz in the data) despite the discontinuous level-set-to-data map, with MCMC that updates the interface implicitly — Iglesias, Lu & Stuart (2016), Interfaces and Free Boundaries 18(2):181-217, doi:10.4171/IFB/362 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00313); results are sensitive to the prior's length and amplitude scales, and a single hierarchical scale parameter sampled by Metropolis-within-Gibbs fixes this and outperforms non-hierarchical level sets — Dunlop, Iglesias & Stuart, "Hierarchical Bayesian level set inversion" (arXiv 2016; Statistics and Computing 2017 ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.03605); level-set parameterization combined with hierarchical length-scale learning inside ensemble Kalman inversion gives piecewise-constant reconstructions with interfaces of unknown topology, including a groundwater-flow example — Chada et al. (2018) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01781)
- Level-set and truncated-Gaussian background (not re-verified): Dorn & Lesselier (2006), "Level set methods for inverse scattering", Inverse Problems 22:R67 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/22/4/R01); Cardiff & Kitanidis (2009), "Bayesian inversion for facies detection: an extensible level set framework", WRR 45:W10416 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008WR007675); truncated pluri-Gaussian facies updated by EnKF (Liu & Oliver 2005, J. - Hierarchical stratigraphy → facies → property modelling for aquifers: ArchPy is an open-source Python module that generates stochastic, reproducible hierarchical models from boreholes and a "stratigraphic pile" that encodes expert knowledge and the simulation algorithm (including MPS) for each unit; demonstrated on the Quaternary upper Aare valley aquifer, Switzerland — Schorpp, Straubhaar & Renard (2022), Frontiers in Earth Science 10:884075, doi:10.3389/feart.2022.884075 [Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.884075/text)
- Interpretation uncertainty of a manually built hydrostratigraphic layer model propagated through a groundwater model: 50 structural realizations for each of three uncertainty levels (150 total) for a 127 km² Danish area were generated by perturbing interpretation points with depth- and geophysics-resolution-dependent standard deviations and sequential Gaussian simulation of residuals; a steady-state MODFLOW-NWT model with 8 units was run with 200 behavioural parameter sets retained from 10,000 GLUE samples of 5 parameters (the same sets reused for all structures, no recalibration). Parameter uncertainty produced a larger spread in capture-zone area and median travel time than interpretation uncertainty, except at a synthetic well field where the two were comparable; the authors stress they did not treat conceptual-model uncertainty, which they say has a larger effect, and that GLUE with few parameters may inflate parameter uncertainty — Enemark et al. (2024), "Incorporating interpretation uncertainties from deterministic 3D hydrostratigraphic models in groundwater models", HESS 28:505-523, doi:10.5194/hess-28-505-2024 [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/505/2024/)
- Combined categorical + continuous hierarchical Bayesian inversion: multi-Gaussian within-facies heterogeneity and MPS facies are combined in one hierarchical model sampled with a parallel-tempering sequential-Gibbs MCMC derived from detailed balance; tested on synthetic channelized flow fields; weak data produced multimodal posteriors — Reuschen, Xu & Nowak (2020), Advances in Water Resources 141:103614, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2020.103614 [Source](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1262893840221708288)
- Structural errors survive dense geophysics: geostatistical simulation of 3-D hydrostratigraphic zones with boreholes as hard data and AEM-derived structural probabilities as soft data improved predictive capability of a zone-calibrated groundwater model, but the authors stress that structural models will always contain errors because even dense AEM cannot perfectly resolve structure, and that those errors can bias parameter estimates and predictions — Christensen, Minsley & Christensen (2017), WRR 53(2):1019-1038 [Source](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70193686)
- Non-intrusive alternative for categorical priors: data space inversion with 100 HydroGeoSphere runs of ALLUVSIM channel-belt realizations (≈204,000 cell parameters; 760 head observations) conditioned predictions without adjusting the fields; when PESTPP-IES was instead applied directly to the fields, 30 of 100 realizations were lost to convergence failure, objective functions ranged 1,075-16,098 after 3 iterations and channel connectivity degraded so that some posterior fast travel times exceeded prior values, whereas the DSI surrogate (87 latent parameters) reached objective functions of 814-830 in 6 iterations — Delottier, Doherty & Brunner (2023), Geosci. Model Dev. 16:4213-4231, doi:10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023 [Source](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/4213/2023/)

### Inferences
- With sparse heads, data cannot discriminate between training images; Juda et al. show that the posterior simply inherits the prior's geometry, and that a good data fit is not evidence of a good facies model. For decision-support work this argues for carrying several conceptual/structural scenarios (or continuous scenario "metaparameters" such as proportion, orientation and width) rather than investing in a single sophisticated categorical prior.
- The three failure signatures in Juda et al. map onto three algorithm families a PEST user might consider: resampling methods preserve geology but need 10^4-10^5 runs; ensemble smoothers are cheap but smear or mis-place connected features; low-dimensional latent generators fit well but are overconfident. None is clearly adequate for travel-time or connectivity-sensitive predictions.
- Level-set fields defined on pilot-point-like "representing nodes" (Chang, Zhang & Lu) are directly expressible with PstFrom plus a thresholding pre-processor, and are probably the lowest-effort categorical parameterization for PESTPP-IES; the hierarchical length-scale result of Dunlop et al. suggests the threshold field's variogram range should itself be adjustable.
- Enemark et al.'s finding (structure matters less than parameters) is conditional on thick, laterally extensive aquifers, only five parameters and no recalibration per structure; it should not be generalised to settings where aquitard continuity or channel connectivity controls the prediction.

### Gaps
- No published study was found that inverts a GemPy (or other implicit) structural model against hydraulic heads within a groundwater model; the search specifically for this returned only gravity/magnetics/topology examples.
- Object-based, process-based and rule-based facies models (e.g. ALLUVSIM, FLUMY, surface-based models) were not researched individually; the only verified use here is ALLUVSIM as a prior generator inside DSI.
- Pluri-Gaussian + iterative ensemble smoother results for groundwater (as opposed to reservoir) models were not verified.
- The 2023 TRIPS + IES Groundwater paper's quantitative results could not be retrieved (PubMed page blocked).
- Software pages for SGeMS, MPSlib, G2S and DeeSse were not opened; the only verified performance figure is DeeSse taking 417-1,180 s per 2-D realization versus about 0.1 s for a trained spatial GAN in Laloy et al. (2018) (see section 3).

## 3. Deep generative priors and latent-space inversion (VAE, GAN, normalizing flows, diffusion/score models; plug-and-play and diffusion posterior sampling)

### Takeaway
Generative-network priors have progressed from 2-D binary channel demonstrations with MCMC (2017-2018) to 3-D, half-million-cell, multi-scenario latent diffusion models conditioned to production data with ESMDA, MCMC and SMC (2025-2026), with first groundwater-specific diffusion papers (head and concentration conditioning) appearing as preprints in late 2025. The consistent caveats are: the latent-to-field map is strongly nonlinear, so gradient and ensemble-Kalman updates are unreliable (ESMDA in latent space under-fits and retains too much uncertainty relative to MCMC/SMC in the newest study); results are only as good as the training image; and almost all demonstrations are synthetic with no model error.

### Cited Findings
- First VAE/GAN geologic priors in hydrogeology: a spatial GAN trained on a training image gives a very low-dimensional latent representation (25 latent variables for a 125×125 2-D binary case; 27 for a 3-D case) usable with DREAM(ZS) MCMC. Case 1: 49 steady-state heads, noise SD 0.01 m, convergence after 48,400 iterations per chain × 8 chains (387,200 forward runs). Case 2: 3-D transient hydraulic tomography with 1,568 drawdowns, 16 chains × 50,000 iterations (≈800,000 runs, 6.5 days on 16 cores) and "the posterior distribution was not appropriately sampled". Training took 3-12 h on a GPU, was unstable (network chosen by inspection among 50 epochs), and direct conditioning to point data was not available; generation was >10,000× faster than DeeSse MPS — Laloy, Hérault, Jacques & Linde (2018), WRR 54:381-406, doi:10.1002/2017WR022148 [Source](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1708.04975); the preceding VAE paper is Laloy, Hérault, Lee, Jacques & Linde (2017), Advances in Water Resources 110:387-405 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.09.029)
- Gradient-based inversion in a GAN latent space can fail even for a linear forward model: with a channelized training image and 576 low-noise crosshole GPR data, only 13% of Adam gradient-descent trials reached the target misfit in 10,000 iterations and Gauss-Newton with a finite-difference Jacobian failed, whereas global probabilistic optimisation always succeeded; attributed to the highly nonlinear latent-to-image map — Laloy, Linde, Ruffino, Hérault, Gasso & Jacques (2019), Computers & Geosciences 133:104333, doi:10.1016/j.cageo.2019.104333 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09140)
- Why: the nonlinearity is caused mainly by changes in topology and curvature induced by the generator; there is a trade-off between generative accuracy and feasibility of low-cost inversion; a VAE with controlled latent structure and stochastic gradient descent performs better than earlier GAN-based gradient inversion — Lopez-Alvis, Laloy, Nguyen & Hermans (2021), Computers & Geosciences 152:104762 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12056)
- Normalizing flows for variational inference over a GAN or VAE latent space (crosshole GPR, channelized models): about seven times cheaper than MCMC in the authors' examples; the VAE-based variant needs fewer samples per iteration and gives a more accurate posterior approximation than the GAN-based one — Levy, Laloy & Linde (2023), Computers & Geosciences 171:105263 [Source](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_BB2F0E925B0B)
- Ensemble smoother + deep generative networks (reservoir): seven formulations (VAE, GAN, WGAN, VAE-GAN, PCA + Cycle-GAN, PCA + style transfer, VAE + style loss) all generated realistic channel facies and all gave "successful assimilation of facies and production data" with ES-MDA on a 60×60 (3,600-cell) test (Ne = 200, Na = 10); the GAN generator was "relatively insensitive to perturbations in z, which may be an indication of mode collapse", and WGAN + ES-MDA converged more slowly; training took 23 min to 4.5 h per network on one GPU. On a larger 2-D case (reported as 200×40 gridblocks; 12 producers, 5 injectors; VAE latent dimension 1,024 trained on 50,000 realizations; Ne = 200, Na = 16) VAE + ES-MDA without localization "resulted in a near collapse of the ensemble variance"; two localization strategies (local analysis; PCA latent variables living on the model grid) resolved it. The authors note real models with millions of cells "cannot be addressed with our current implementations" — Canchumuni, Castro, Potratz, Emerick & Pacheco (2021), Computational Geosciences 25:433-466, doi:10.1007/s10596-020-10015-0 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10638); earlier VAE + ES-MDA paper (Canchumuni, Emerick & Pacheco 2019) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06900)
- VAE versus GAN with ES-MDA for groundwater flow and transport data (synthetic categorical and continuous K fields): GANs produced higher-quality realizations, VAEs localized the true channels better — Bao, Li & Davis (2022), Mathematical Geosciences 54:1017-1042, doi:10.1007/s11004-022-10003-3 [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11004-022-10003-3)
- Adversarial autoencoder + deep surrogate + iterative local-updating ensemble smoother (ILUES) for non-Gaussian conductivity from solute-transport data in 2-D and 3-D: the autoencoder is reported robust across heterogeneity patterns and the surrogate cuts run time substantially — Mo, Zabaras, Shi & Wu (2020), WRR, doi:10.1029/2019WR026082 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11828)
- CNN-PCA: PCA realizations post-processed by a CNN trained with a style-type loss built from pre-trained CNN filter responses (a multipoint-statistics metric); demonstrated on binary channelized and bimodal deltaic-fan systems with derivative-free optimisation and subspace randomized maximum likelihood — Liu, Sun & Durlofsky (arXiv 2018; Mathematical Geosciences 2019 ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02716)
- Latent diffusion model (LDM) as geomodel parameterization, 2-D: VAE (downsampling 8) + U-net denoiser trained on 4,000 Petrel realizations of a 64×64 three-facies channel-levee-mud system (≈2 h on one A100 GPU); 64 latent variables; hard-data honoured at 98% accuracy; ESMDA with Ne = 200, Na = 10, 70 rate observations; posterior P10-P90 generally bracket the true data; in the uncertain-facies-property case the mud log-permeability posterior mode (≈27 md) missed the truth (≈40 md) because of low flow sensitivity; stated limits: 2-D, three facies, one geological scenario at a time — Di Federico & Durlofsky, "Latent diffusion models for parameterization and data assimilation of facies-based geomodels", Computers & Geosciences (DOI registered 2024; issue year not confirmed), doi:10.1016/j.cageo.2024.105755 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14815)
- 3-D LDM with uncertain geological scenario: mud fraction, channel orientation and channel width are treated as uncertain metaparameters, with a perceptual loss in training; ensemble history matching in latent space reduced uncertainty in forecasts and in the scenario parameters for three synthetic truths — Di Federico & Durlofsky (2025), Mathematical Geosciences, doi:10.1007/s11004-025-10245-x [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16621v2)
- Latent-space ESMDA versus MCMC and SMC (preprint, not peer reviewed): 128×128×32 = 524,288 cells compressed to 1,024 latent variables (3,000 Petrel training realizations; mud fraction 0.72-0.87, orientation 30-60°, width 4-6 cells); 75 rate observations with 5% noise; simulator ≈10 min per run on 16 cores. ESMDA with Ne = 1,000, Na = 10 (≈80 h): model-space ESMDA reached RMSE 33 STB/d and normalised P90-P10 spread 0.11-0.12 but gave geologically unrealistic posteriors (smeared channels, wrong facies ordering); latent-space ESMDA preserved realism but reached RMSE 68-90 STB/d and spread 0.28-0.31. pCN-MCMC (5 chains, up to 150,000 iterations) and SMC (1,000 particles, adaptive tempering) in the latent space, made feasible by a CNN-LSTM surrogate (≈0.001 s per evaluation; mean relative errors 3.7-6.3%; surrogate error added to the data covariance, C_total = C_d + C_surr), agreed with each other and achieved lower mismatch and more uncertainty reduction than latent ESMDA. The authors conclude Kalman-type ensemble methods "may provide overestimated posterior uncertainty" with highly nonlinear parameterizations; only synthetic truths, no model error — Di Federico, Teng & Durlofsky (2026), arXiv:2606.11140 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140)
- Diffusion posterior sampling adapted to geoscience: a corrected DPS likelihood approximation that accounts for the noise contamination inherent in the diffusion process, applied to multivariate (facies + acoustic impedance) models conditioned on well-log hard data and nonlinear full-stack seismic data; reported to be statistically more robust and cheaper than original DPS and faster than outer-loop MCMC around a generator — Miele & Linde (2026), Computers & Geosciences 207:106076, doi:10.1016/j.cageo.2025.106076 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15809v4)
- Groundwater-specific diffusion work (both preprints, not peer reviewed): "Diffusion-Inversion-Net" trains a DDPM offline and conditions it directly on sparse heads, solute concentrations and hard K data, generating posterior ensembles without iterative forward simulation (Zhang, Yang, Zhang & Jiang, Nov 2025; abstract reports no quantitative metrics) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16926v1); "LD-DIM" couples a pretrained latent diffusion prior to a differentiable finite-volume solver with adjoint/automatic differentiation to reconstruct Gaussian and bi-material conductivity fields from sparse heads, claiming better stability and accuracy than PINN and physics-embedded VAE baselines (Lin & He, Dec 2025) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22421)
- Function-space diffusion for CCS (workshop paper): Fun-DDPS combines a diffusion prior over geological parameters with a neural-operator surrogate for guidance; the authors describe it as the first validation of a diffusion inverse solver against asymptotically exact rejection-sampling posteriors (Jensen-Shannon divergence < 0.06) and report 4× better sample efficiency than rejection sampling — Ju, Yao, Anandkumar, Benson & Wen (2026), ICLR AI&PDE workshop [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12274)
- Imaging origins: Diffusion Posterior Sampling handles noisy linear and nonlinear inverse problems by combining reverse diffusion with a likelihood-gradient step (Chung, Kim, McCann, Klasky & Ye, ICLR 2023) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687); DDRM is an unsupervised sampler for linear inverse problems using a pretrained diffusion model (Kawar, Elad, Ermon & Song 2022; NeurIPS 2022 per secondary citations) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11793); survey of pretrained diffusion models as training-free priors, including the difficulties of latent diffusion (Daras, Chung, Lai, Mitsufuji, Ye, Milanfar, Dimakis & Delbracio 2024, arXiv preprint) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00083); plug-and-play priors (Venkatakrishnan, Bouman & Wohlberg 2013, IEEE GlobalSIP ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1109/GlobalSIP.2013.6737048); score-based SDE framework (Song et al. 2021, ICLR ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456)
- Stability benchmark (preprint): generative/diffusion regularizers are tested for convergent regularization, robustness to out-of-distribution data and robustness to errors in the forward operator or noise model against modern variational methods; the abstract says the results show where generative priors give state-of-the-art reconstructions and "where they fall short or may be problematic" — Denker, Hertrich & Neumayer (2026), arXiv:2605.10076 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10076)
- Training-image dependence shown directly for a WGAN prior: see Juda et al. (2023) in section 2 [Source](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.160/)

### Inferences
- What has been shown for heads: (i) steady-state and transient heads condition GAN/VAE latent variables through MCMC at a cost of 10^5-10^6 runs (Laloy 2018; Juda 2023); (ii) ES-MDA with VAE/GAN latents works on small 2-D flow-and-transport problems (Bao 2022; Mo 2020); (iii) diffusion priors conditioned on heads exist only as preprints with differentiable solvers or amortised conditional generation. For production data the evidence is much stronger and 3-D. Nothing found demonstrates a generative prior on a real regional groundwater model with head/flow data.
- The 2026 Di Federico-Teng-Durlofsky result reverses the usual worry: in a nonlinear latent space, ESMDA did not collapse but under-updated (higher mismatch, wider posterior than MCMC/SMC). Combined with Canchumuni's variance collapse without localization, the message for PESTPP-IES users is that neither over- nor under-dispersion can be assumed, and that a cheap surrogate + SMC check is the emerging way to audit a latent-space smoother.
- Amortised conditional generators (DIN-style) bypass the simulator at inference time and therefore cannot honour site-specific boundary conditions, stresses or model structure unless these were varied in training; they are better viewed as fast prior/proposal generators than as replacements for history matching.
- Diffusion posterior sampling needs the gradient of the data misfit with respect to the field at every denoising step. For MODFLOW-type black-box models that means an adjoint, a differentiable surrogate or an ensemble-estimated gradient; this is the main barrier to non-intrusive use.
- Latent variables without spatial meaning defeat distance-based localization; Canchumuni's "PCA on the grid then network post-processing" and local-analysis strategies are the two known work-arounds, and both are compatible with a PstFrom-style pre-processor.

### Gaps
- Mosser, Dubrule & Blunt (GAN prior for seismic inversion; "DeepFlow"), Chan & Elsheikh, and the original Laloy et al. (2017) VAE paper were not re-opened; they are cited from memory only where marked.
- Quantitative results of the two groundwater diffusion preprints (grid size, number of heads, error metrics) were not on the abstract pages; the DIN HTML version returned 404.
- No study was found that trains a generative prior on outputs of process-based or geology-driven simulators for a real aquifer system and then conditions on field head data.
- I did not find peer-reviewed evidence on mode collapse in diffusion-based geologic priors specifically; the mode-collapse evidence here is for GANs (Canchumuni et al.).
- Grid sizes for Mo et al. (2020) and Liu, Sun & Durlofsky (2019) were not on the pages read.

## 4. Sparsity- and structure-promoting regularization (TV and generalisations, L1/dictionaries/wavelets, compressed sensing, focusing regularizers, cross-gradients, learned regularizers, Bayesian sparsity priors) versus PEST's smoothing Tikhonov regularization

### Takeaway
Edge-preserving and sparsity-promoting regularizers are mature in imaging and geophysics and have each been tried once or twice in hydrogeology (TV prior for facies, learned sparse dictionaries for channels, cross-gradient hydrogeophysics), but none is available in PEST/PEST++, whose regularisation is quadratic (preferred value / preferred difference, optionally covariance-weighted). They are straightforward to bolt onto a Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt scheme via iteratively reweighted least squares, but have no natural counterpart in ensemble smoothers, and the Bayesian literature warns that TV-type priors are not discretization-invariant; Cauchy, horseshoe and Besov priors are the principled replacements.

### Cited Findings
- TV as a Bayesian prior for hydrogeologic facies: a Laplace prior on the parameter gradient (total variation), with hyperparameters estimated by expectation-maximisation, identifies discrete geologic structures with abrupt boundaries better than the classical (Gaussian) geostatistical approach in synthetic tests — Lee & Kitanidis (2013), "Bayesian inversion with total variation prior for discrete geologic structure identification", WRR 49:7658-7669, doi:10.1002/2012WR013431 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/2012wr013431)
- Follow-up use in hydrogeology: "Linear functional minimization for inverse modeling" (Barajas-Solano et al. 2015, WRR; full author list ‡) builds on Lee & Kitanidis's TV prior, and a TV-regularised hydraulic inverse-modelling presentation (Lin et al., AGU 2016) is hosted by the MADS project; neither was opened beyond its title and the search summary [Source](https://mads.gitlab.io/papers/Barajas%20et%20al%202015%20Linear%20functional%20minimization%20for%20inverse%20modeling.pdf)
- Limits of TV as a prior: total-variation priors are not discretization-invariant, so the edge-preserving property is lost as the mesh is refined; Cauchy difference priors built from a discretised Cauchy random walk retain edge preservation and were demonstrated on 1-D deconvolution and 2-D X-ray tomography — Markkanen, Roininen, Huttunen & Lasanen (2019), "Cauchy difference priors for edge-preserving Bayesian inversion", J. Inverse and Ill-posed Problems, doi:10.1515/jiip-2017-0048 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06135); the original analysis is Lassas & Siltanen (2004), Inverse Problems 20:1537 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/20/5/013); Besov-space (wavelet) priors are discretization-invariant (Lassas, Saksman & Siltanen 2009, Inverse Problems and Imaging 3:87-122 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.3934/ipi.2009.3.87)
- Review and extension of Cauchy Markov random field priors with comparisons of variants — Chada, Roininen & Suuronen, "Cauchy Markov random field priors for Bayesian inversion" (arXiv 2021; author order as returned by the search) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12488)
- Horseshoe (hierarchical shrinkage) priors for edge preservation: the unknown is conditionally Gaussian given half-Cauchy global and local scale hyperparameters; the authors note that shrinkage priors had not previously been fully applied to large-scale linear inverse problems requiring edge-preserving solutions — Uribe, Dong & Hansen, "Horseshoe priors for edge-preserving linear Bayesian inversion" (arXiv 2022; SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 2023 ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09147)
- Sparse learned dictionaries for subsurface flow calibration: K-SVD dictionaries learned from a library of prior realizations, with l1-norm-regularised inversion in the dictionary coefficients — Khaninezhad, Jafarpour & Li (2012), Advances in Water Resources 39:106-121, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.09.002 [Source](https://www.peeref.com/works/6417320); K-SVD's high learning cost motivated dictionaries learned from low-rank spectral representations (Liu & Jafarpour 2013, WRR) [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/wrcr.20545); adaptive sparse representation to identify the prior (continuity model) during calibration (Computational Geosciences 2014) [Source](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10596-013-9378-7); learned facies connectivity and discreteness imposed as regularization functions (Khaninezhad, Golmohammadi & Jafarpour 2018, WRR) [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/2017wr022284)
- Mixed and spatially variable Lp norms in geophysics: different norms (on the model and on each gradient component) can be applied in different regions; solved by iteratively reweighted least squares with a Lawson approximation and a two-stage algorithm; demonstrated on 2-D travel-time tomography and used to generate an ensemble of solutions spanning model character — Fournier & Oldenburg (2019), "Inversion using spatially variable mixed ℓp norms", Geophys. J. Int. 218(1):268-282, doi:10.1093/gji/ggz156 [Source](https://storage.googleapis.com/simpeg/papers/2019/ggz156.pdf); this is the sparse-regularisation implementation in SimPEG [Source](https://mirageoscience-simpeg-drivers.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/latest/fundamentals/regularization/sparse.html)
- Focusing / minimum-gradient-support and cross-gradient background (not re-verified): Portniaguine & Zhdanov (1999), "Focusing geophysical inversion images", Geophysics 64:874-887 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1444596); Gallardo & Meju (2003), cross-gradients joint inversion, Geophys. Res. Lett. 30(13):1658 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003GL017370); Rudin, Osher & Fatemi (1992), TV denoising, Physica D 60:259-268 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(92)90242-F); total generalized variation (Bredies, Kunisch & Pock 2010, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 3:492-526 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1137/090769521)
- Structure-coupled joint inversion with hydrological data: cross-gradient constraints between radar slowness and log hydraulic diffusivity (or permeability) when jointly inverting crosshole GPR with hydraulic tomography or tracer data penalise models whose gradients are not parallel; joint models had higher resolution and less scattered cross-property plots than individual inversions — Lochbühler, Doetsch, Brauchler & Linde (2013), Geophysics [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01360); a review notes that most published joint inversions rely on the Gallardo-Meju cross-gradient function, that in its standard form the constraint is invalid when lithology and state variables change in uncorrelated ways, and (per the search summary of that review, not checked against the full text) that the added value of cross-gradient constraints still had to be demonstrated — Linde & Doetsch (2016) [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01626)
- Learned regularizers and data-driven inversion surveys (not re-verified): Arridge, Maass, Öktem & Schönlieb (2019), "Solving inverse problems using data-driven models", Acta Numerica 28:1-174 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492919000059); Ongie et al. (2020), "Deep learning techniques for inverse problems in imaging", IEEE JSAIT 1:39-56 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSAIT.2020.2991563); adversarial regularizers (Lunz, Öktem & Schönlieb 2018, NeurIPS ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11572)
- PEST-side baseline: pyEMU's helper functions construct zero-order (preferred-value) Tikhonov equations and first-order "Pearson" (preferred-difference, covariance-derived) Tikhonov equations — both quadratic penalties [Source](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/utils/helpers.py); the GMDSI regularisation tutorial frames the target objective function (PHIMLIM) as the control on over-fitting and notes that in practice model error, not measurement noise, dominates misfit so a higher PHIMLIM is appropriate [Source](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks/tree/main/tutorials/part1_09_regularization)

### Inferences
- Comparison with PEST Tikhonov: preferred-value/preferred-difference regularisation is the MAP estimator for a Gaussian prior; it returns the smoothest field compatible with the data, which is the right answer to "what is the minimum-error-variance estimate" but a poor picture of facies-controlled systems. TV / Lp / MGS regularizers replace the quadratic penalty with a sparsity-promoting one on differences, giving blocky fields; they are implemented by IRLS, i.e. by repeatedly re-weighting ordinary preferred-difference equations. A PEST user could emulate this today by updating regularisation weights between GLM iterations (weights ∝ 1/sqrt(Δ² + ε²)), without changing PEST.
- None of these has a clean ensemble-smoother equivalent, because the IES prior is defined by the sample covariance of the ensemble (second moments only). The practical ensemble route to "blocky" posteriors is therefore a non-Gaussian generator (level set, truncated Gaussian, hierarchical Matérn with heavy-tailed length-scale field) rather than a non-quadratic penalty.
- With sparse heads the data cannot locate edges; edge-preserving priors will place sharp boundaries where the prior or auxiliary data (AEM, cross-gradient coupling to a geophysical image) dictate. Their value in groundwater is therefore mainly in joint/structure-guided settings, not in head-only calibration.
- Sparse-dictionary methods are conceptually the linear ancestor of deep generative priors (a learned basis from a realization library) and remain attractive when only a few hundred prior realizations exist: far too few to train a diffusion model but enough for K-SVD or PCA.

### Gaps
- I found no documentation that PEST, PEST++ or pyEMU implement L1, TV or minimum-gradient-support regularisation; this is an absence-of-evidence statement based on the pyEMU source inspected and my recollection of the manuals, which were not re-read in this session.
- No groundwater study using horseshoe, Cauchy or Besov priors was found.
- Compressed-sensing work by Jafarpour and co-workers with DCT bases and EnKF (2008-2010) was not re-opened and is not cited.
- The Denker et al. (2026) benchmark's specific findings on learned regularizer stability were not available from the abstract.

## 5. Trans-dimensional and adaptive parameterization (reversible-jump MCMC, Voronoi and tree partitions, refinement indicators, zonation optimisation, adaptive pilot-point placement, multiresolution)

### Takeaway
Letting the data choose the number and location of parameters is well developed in geophysics (Voronoi and wavelet-tree reversible-jump samplers) and has two direct groundwater transfers: reversible-jump "smart pilot points" and gradient-based refinement indicators for zonation. Both remain confined to small or fast models (thousands to hundreds of thousands of forward runs, or adjoint gradients), so for regional models the realistic transfer is the weaker idea of multi-scale and adaptively densified pilot points rather than trans-dimensional sampling.

### Cited Findings
- Reversible-jump MCMC with Voronoi cells for 2-D seismic tomography: number, position and value of cells are unknown; applied to Rayleigh-wave velocities across Australia — Bodin & Sambridge (2009), "Seismic tomography with the reversible jump algorithm", Geophys. J. Int. 178:1411-1436 [Source](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/seismic-tomography-with-the-reversible-jump-algorithm/); the first geophysical application was to layered resistivity sounding, showing the natural parsimony of Bayesian trans-dimensional sampling — Malinverno (2002), Geophys. J. Int. 151:675-688 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-246X.2002.01847.x); overview: Sambridge, Bodin, Gallagher & Tkalčić (2013), "Transdimensional inference in the geosciences", Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 371:20110547 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2011.0547)
- Trans-dimensional trees: a sampler over tree-structured (e.g. wavelet) parameterizations that is more flexible and efficient than Voronoi samplers and extends to 3-D; demonstrated on 2-D seismic and 3-D teleseismic tomography with uncertainty — Hawkins & Sambridge (2015), "Geophysical imaging using trans-dimensional trees", Geophys. J. Int. 203:972-1000 [Source](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/geophysical-imaging-using-trans-dimensional-trees/)
- Smart pilot points: reversible-jump MCMC estimates the number, locations and values of pilot points jointly, searching for a minimum number (parsimony); calibrated to tracer breakthrough curves on a synthetic case and a field tracer-tomography data set; the authors state that "the RJ-MCMC algorithm is computationally demanding and this challenges the application for model calibration" (thousands of rapid model evaluations) — Jiménez, Mariethoz, Brauchler & Bayer (2016), WRR 52(5):3966-3983, doi:10.1002/2015WR017922 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/2015wr017922)
- The same reversible-jump machinery was applied to discrete fracture network geometry inversion — Somogyvári, Jalali et al. (2017), "Synthetic fracture network characterization with transdimensional inversion", WRR (full author list ‡) [Source](https://applied.geo.uni-halle.de/files/2025/11/Somogyvari-Jalali-et-al-2017-Synthetic-fracture-network-characterization.pdf)
- Refinement and coarsening indicators: indicators computed cheaply from the gradient of the least-squares misfit build a transmissivity zonation step by step and avoid over-parameterization — Ben Ameur, Chavent & Jaffré (2002), Inverse Problems 18:775-794, doi:10.1088/0266-5611/18/3/317 [Source](https://dx.doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/18/3/317); extended to multidimensional parameters [Source](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/JIIP.2008.008/html) and to joint estimation of storage coefficient and transmissivity (Riahi, Ben Ameur, Jaffré & Bouhlila 2018) [Source](https://picof.sciencesconf.org/conference/picof/pages/abstract_riahi.pdf); adaptive multiscale permeability estimation in reservoirs: Grimstad, Mannseth, Nævdal & Urkedal (2003), Computational Geosciences 7:1-25 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022417923824)
- Pilot-point placement and density remain an open practical question: a 2023 comparison of pilot-point strategies when measurements are unevenly distributed describes placement as "not fully addressed" — Aissat et al. (2023), Hydrogeology Journal 31:2381-2400 [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10040-023-02737-z); developers' view is that regularisation removes the need to limit pilot-point numbers and that multi-scale pilot-point schemes represent different heterogeneity scales (White & Lavenue 2023) [Source](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.161/)
- Multi-resolution parameterization with an ensemble smoother for categorical fields: ESMDA updating continuous variables on the coarse levels of an MPS Gaussian pyramid (≈204 parameters for a 16,384-cell field) — Juda et al. (2023) [Source](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.160/)

### Inferences
- PstFrom's layered multipliers (constant × zone × pilot point × grid) are already a fixed multi-resolution parameterization; what the trans-dimensional and refinement-indicator literature adds is a data-driven rule for where additional resolution is warranted. A non-intrusive analogue is available from quantities PEST users already compute: parameter identifiability or the diagonal of the resolution matrix from a Jacobian (GLM) or from ensemble cross-covariances (IES) can serve as a refinement indicator to densify pilot points between stages.
- Trans-dimensional posteriors average over model complexity and therefore give smoother, more honest uncertainty than a single fixed zonation, but the cost (10^5-10^6 forward runs in geophysical examples) puts them out of reach for regional transient models unless a surrogate is used.
- The parsimony argument (few smart pilot points) runs against the GMDSI position that more parameters reduce structural-error-induced bias (section 7). The two are reconciled when predictions depend on unresolved detail: parsimony is appropriate for estimating the field, not for quantifying predictive uncertainty.

### Gaps
- No work was found (in the limited searches possible) on trans-dimensional or adaptive parameterization coupled to iterative ensemble smoothers.
- Zonation optimisation with Voronoi tessellations in groundwater (e.g. Tsai, Sun & Yeh 2003) and trans-dimensional Gaussian-process methods (Ray & Myer 2019) were not searched or verified.
- Published guidance on adaptive pilot-point placement using sensitivity or D-optimality criteria (e.g. LaVenue & Pickens 1992) was not re-verified.

## 6. Data-integration priors: hydrogeophysics and joint inversion, airborne EM for hydrostratigraphy, petrophysical uncertainty, tracers, temperature, InSAR, GRACE

### Takeaway
Geophysical and unconventional data are best used as structural or soft-prior information with explicit petrophysical uncertainty; fully coupled or joint hydrogeophysical inversion has repeatedly been shown to help only when both the hydrological model and the petrophysical link are sound, and a field-scale PEST-era comparison preferred sequential inversion. The same lesson applies to environmental tracers: assimilating information-rich data into a structurally deficient model can bias decision-relevant forecasts.

### Cited Findings
- Taxonomy and state of joint inversion: structural joint inversion (cross-gradients) is often preferred over petrophysical coupling; coupled hydrogeophysical inversion treats time-lapse geophysical data as indirect hydrological observables through a flow/transport simulator; open challenges are validity of coupling strategies across scales, spatial statistics of petrophysical relationships, influence of model conceptualisation, fully probabilistic joint inversion and complex priors — Linde & Doetsch (2016), "Joint inversion in hydrogeophysics and near-surface geophysics", in Integrated Imaging of the Earth (AGU/Wiley), doi:10.1002/9781118929063.ch7 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01626)
- Coupled inversion helps only if the hydrologic model is structurally sound: in a synthetic 1-D infiltration experiment monitored with surface electrical surveys, coupling substantially reduced uncertainty in hydrologic properties, but "if the hydrologic model exhibits structural errors, the coupled inversion may not improve the hydrologic interpretation" — Hinnell, Ferré, Vrugt, Huisman, Moysey, Rings & Kowalsky (2010), WRR 46(4), doi:10.1029/2008WR007060 [Source](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/providers/122535/records/65de48cf0f3e94b9e5ccf792)
- Field-scale comparison with a groundwater model: sequential (calibrate the groundwater model to inverted geophysical models) versus joint (simultaneous, linked by a petrophysical relationship) inversion with TDEM (synthetic) and ERT (real) data; with a high-quality petrophysical relationship the joint approach improved groundwater parameters only insignificantly relative to sequential and slightly degraded geophysical estimates; with a poor relationship sequential did relatively better; the authors favour sequential inversion given error, fit, complexity and computational burden — Herckenrath, Fiandaca, Auken & Bauer-Gottwein (2013), HESS 17:4043-4060, doi:10.5194/hess-17-4043-2013 [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/17/4043/2013/)
- Petrophysical uncertainty: assuming an exact petrophysical link in Bayesian hydrogeophysical inversion (synthetic crosshole GPR) biases the inferred variance of hydrogeological properties and makes uncertainty estimates and Bayes-factor model selection overconfident; accounting for scatter around the relationship gives more realistic uncertainty — Brunetti & Linde (2018), Advances in Water Resources 111:346-359, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.11.028 [Source](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1567104)
- AEM to hydrostratigraphic zones, automated: resistivity is translated to clay fraction using borehole lithology, the resistivity and clay-fraction voxel models are k-means clustered into zones, and zone conductivities are calibrated to heads and stream discharge; of 11 cluster models (1-11 zones), performance improved up to 5 clusters and not beyond, and the cluster model was competitive with comparable hydrological models (Danish case) — Marker, Foged, He, Christiansen, Refsgaard, Auken & Bauer-Gottwein (2015), HESS 19:3875-3890, doi:10.5194/hess-19-3875-2015 [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/19/3875/2015/)
- AEM as soft data for stochastic structure: see Christensen, Minsley & Christensen (2017) in section 2 — dense AEM can noticeably improve estimates of sediment distribution compared with borehole data alone and improve predictions of a zone-calibrated model, but residual structural error remains and can bias parameters [Source](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70193686); geophysical resolution enters the interpretation-uncertainty model of Enemark et al. (2024) [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/505/2024/)
- Unconventional observations, review: heads and discharge alone under-constrain flow models; across reviewed studies tracer concentrations and exchange fluxes were beneficial for a majority of calibrated models, whereas temperature observations improved heat-transport simulation but often worsened other outcomes unless measured within about 2 m of the surface-water/groundwater interface; most models are still calibrated manually — Schilling, Cook & Brunner (2019), Reviews of Geophysics 57:146-182, doi:10.1029/2018RG000619 [Source](https://libra.unine.ch/handle/123456789/29238)
- Counter-example with imperfect models: (i) FOSM data worth in a Heretaunga Plains model (302×501 cells, 822 pilot-point parameters, 6,167 heads, 92 fluxes, 52 tritium-derived mean residence times) found tritium of moderate worth for low-flow forecasts; (ii) paired complex/simple Hauraki Plains models (7, 2 and 1 layers; 141,268, 50,180 and 29,050 parameters; PESTPP-IES with 100 realizations; 20 tritium concentrations) showed that assimilating tritium into the 1- and 2-layer models biased the 10-year nitrate-load forecast (for the 1-layer model, excluding tritium changed the mean forecast by about 40%), while the 7-layer model was largely unaffected. Recommendations: assimilate information-rich data strategically and cautiously rather than indiscriminately, use paired simple-complex analysis to detect bias, and process concentrations into spatially or temporally integrated quantities — Knowling, White, Moore, Rakowski & Hayley (2020), HESS 24:1677-1689, doi:10.5194/hess-24-1677-2020 [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/1677/2020/)
- InSAR as aquifer-characterisation data: a single InSAR deformation map informs enough lateral permeability modes to detect major aquifer structures in a Nevada aquifer test and to reduce uncertainty in pressure and displacement predictions, with lateral displacement trends consistent with independent GPS (Alghamdi et al. 2020, 2021, WRR) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04577); a later study by the same group reports that one InSAR map provided significantly more information than multiple GPS time series in that case [Source](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/advancing-aquifer-characterization-through-the-integration-of-sat/)
- Broader context (not re-verified): Binley et al. (2015), "The emergence of hydrogeophysics for improved understanding of subsurface processes over multiple scales", WRR 51:3837-3866 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR017016)

### Inferences
- For a PEST/PEST++ user the defensible recipe that emerges is sequential and probabilistic: (1) invert geophysics separately; (2) convert to a probabilistic structural prior (zone probabilities, clay-fraction field, cluster membership) with stated resolution-dependent uncertainty; (3) carry petrophysical-relationship parameters and scatter as uncertain rather than exact; (4) sample structure in the prior ensemble. This avoids importing geophysical inversion artefacts as hard constraints and avoids the fragility of coupled inversion documented by Hinnell and Herckenrath.
- Hinnell (coupled geophysics), Knowling (tritium) and Schilling (temperature) are the same phenomenon: a new data type exposes a model defect in a process the model was not built to simulate well, and parameters compensate. Whether to assimilate a data type should be decided by a prior-data-conflict or paired-model test, not by its nominal information content.
- InSAR is unusual among "extra" data in being spatially exhaustive; Alghamdi et al. show it resolves a handful of lateral permeability modes, which is the same order as what a sparse head network resolves, but in different directions of parameter space. That complementarity is the argument for including it, provided storage/compaction physics is in the model.

- GRACE as conditioning information for regional groundwater models: no source was obtained (search budget exhausted before this query ran).
- Recent large AEM-to-groundwater-model workflows (California Central Valley, Mississippi Alluvial Plain) and Smith & Knight's InSAR + AEM subsidence modelling were not retrieved.
- InSAR assimilation with PEST++/ensemble smoothers in regional subsidence models was not retrieved.
- Whether Herckenrath et al. used PEST could not be confirmed from the page read (a secondary source says a related Herckenrath study estimated six parameters with PEST).

## 7. Model structural error and discrepancy: statistical discrepancy models, NWP practice, ensemble-method treatments in reservoir and groundwater modelling, multi-model approaches, embedded and learned error models; practical recipes

### Takeaway
Every community that has looked closely reaches the same two conclusions: ignoring model error gives biased parameters and overconfident predictions, and an additive discrepancy term is not identifiable from the calibration data alone, so it works only with informative priors, scale separation, orthogonality constraints or physical constraints. The recipes that are practical for non-intrusive ensemble workflows are (i) estimate a correlated total-error covariance from residuals and iterate (Oliver & Alfonzo; Lu & Chen), (ii) estimate a low-dimensional error model jointly with parameters (Rammay; Xu & Valocchi), (iii) screen for prior-data conflict and use paired simple/complex models to detect bias (Doherty, White, Knowling), (iv) down-weight or transform data that the model cannot be expected to reproduce (Doherty & Welter), and (v) move to data-space or multi-model predictive methods when parameters are not themselves of interest.

### Cited Findings
- Statistical foundation: calibration with an additive Gaussian-process discrepancy between simulator and reality — Kennedy & O'Hagan (2001), "Bayesian calibration of computer models", JRSS-B 63(3):425-464 ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294); an analysis that ignores discrepancy "may lead to biased and over-confident parameter estimates and predictions", but discrepancy is confounded with calibration parameters and this "will only be resolved with meaningful priors"; only with realistic prior information on the discrepancy are true parameter values recovered — Brynjarsdóttir & O'Hagan (2014), "Learning about physical parameters: the importance of model discrepancy", Inverse Problems 30:114007 [Source](https://tonyohagan.co.uk/academic/abs/simmach.html)
- Groundwater, structural noise: misfit not attributable to measurement error is structural noise that is highly correlated in space and time with a possibly near-singular covariance matrix; proposed mitigations include filtering it out before fitting, or weighting towards observations that most resemble the predictions required — Doherty & Welter (2010), "A short exploration of structural noise", WRR 46, doi:10.1029/2009WR008377 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/2009wr008377)
- Groundwater, linear subspace analysis of simplification: a scalable linear method quantifies how predictions from a simplified model are affected by calibration; bias induced by model error is prediction-specific and can go unnoticed by the modeller, structural defects may be invisible during calibration, and how calibration is done affects how model error manifests — White, Doherty & Hughes (2014), "Quantifying the predictive consequences of model error with linear subspace analysis", WRR 50(2):1152-1173, doi:10.1002/2013WR014767 [Source](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/quantifying-predictive-consequences-model-error-linear-subspace-analysis); calibration of a simplified model can reduce error for some predictions while increasing it for others — Watson, Doherty & Christensen (2013), WRR, doi:10.1002/wrcr.20145 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/wrcr.20145)
- GMDSI teaching position (tutorial notebooks in this repository): over-simplified parameterization introduces structural error and can make history matching harmful for a prediction, so "using more parameters is better" provided regularisation is used; observation weights should represent "both measurement error and model error"; the ideal objective function is rarely reached "because of model error"; blotchy calibrated fields are symptomatic of "parameter values compensating for model structural error"; and prior-data conflict / model criticism should be done on the prior ensemble before running the smoother [Source](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks)
- Reservoir, total-error workflow: a workflow of model construction, calibration, criticism and improvement for assimilating biased observations into inadequate models; the estimated total observation-error covariance is larger than the measurement-error covariance, so data need not be matched to instrument accuracy — Oliver & Alfonzo (2018), "Calibration of imperfect models to biased observations", Computational Geosciences 22:145-161 (volume/pages ‡), doi:10.1007/s10596-017-9678-4 [Source](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10596-017-9678-4); applied to seismic data assimilation with an imperfect model (Alfonzo & Oliver, Computational Geosciences) [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10596-019-09849-0)
- Residual-based model-error quantification: after a standard LM-EnRML history match, a correlated "total error" (model + observation) is estimated from the data residuals by residual analysis and fed back into a second assimilation; after model improvement, updates to physical parameters are smaller and forecasts improve; a stated limitation is the zero-mean assumption for the total error — Lu & Chen (2020), SPE Journal, doi:10.2118/199358-PA [Source](https://oa.mg/work/10.2118/199358-pa) [Source](https://jpt.spe.org/model-error-estimation-improves-forecasting)
- Joint estimation of an error model with ES-MDA: parameters of an imperfect (coarsened/upscaled, or geologically simplified) model and of a predefined-form error model are estimated together; calibrating without the error term gives "extreme values of the calibrated model parameters and a biased posterior distribution", while including it gives less biased parameters, better forecast accuracy and reliability on held-out data, and more consistent results across ES-MDA runs — Rammay, Elsheikh & Chen (2019), J. Hydrology 576:764-783, doi:10.1016/j.jhydrol.2019.02.056 [Source](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/quantification-of-prediction-uncertainty-using-imperfect-subsurfa/); follow-ups: "Robust algorithms for history matching of imperfect subsurface models" (SPE Journal 2020, doi:10.2118/193838-PA) [Source](https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.2118/193838-pa) and a "flexible iterative ensemble smoother" for perfect and imperfect models (Computational Geosciences) [Source](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10596-020-10008-z.pdf)
- Stochastic model errors inside iterative smoothers: formulations for consistently including additive stochastic model error in ESMDA and IES — Evensen (2019), "Accounting for model errors in iterative ensemble smoothers", Computational Geosciences 23:761-775, doi:10.1007/s10596-019-9819-z [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00237v1)
- Proxy-solver error, local basis: model error from an approximate forward solver is isolated by projecting the residual onto a local orthonormal basis built from the K nearest entries in a dictionary of (parameters, detailed-minus-proxy response) pairs, and removed before the likelihood is evaluated; removes posterior bias in MCMC — Köpke, Irving & Elsheikh (2018), Advances in Water Resources 116:195-207, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.11.013 [Source](https://serval.unil.ch/en/notice/serval:BIB_C920392FB030); ES-MDA version on crosshole GPR (800 unknowns, 1,600 data, 8 iterations): ignoring proxy error produces strong artefacts for ensembles above 160; the correction largely removes bias as long as the ensemble size stays below about 8× the number of detailed-solver runs per iteration, and relies on an orthogonality assumption the authors flag as needing more investigation — Köpke, Elsheikh & Irving (2019), Frontiers in Environmental Science 7:34, doi:10.3389/fenvs.2019.00034 [Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2019.00034/full)
- Groundwater, Gaussian-process error model: a fully Bayesian scheme infers groundwater-model parameters and a GP (data-driven) error model jointly with DREAM; compared with calibration that ignores structural error it reduced predictive bias and gave prediction intervals that better matched validation data — Xu & Valocchi (2015), "A Bayesian approach to improved calibration and prediction of groundwater models with structural error" (journal reported inconsistently by the sources I saw; I recall WRR 51:9290-9311 ‡) [Source](https://www.peeref.com/works/1473174); applied to a real regional groundwater-flow model using machine-learning surrogates and HPC, giving noticeably different parameter posteriors, more accurate predictions with reasonable credible intervals, and a variance decomposition into parameter, structural and measurement contributions — Xu, Valocchi, Ye & Liang (2017), WRR 53(5), doi:10.1002/2016WR019831 [Source](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1532997)
- 2026 groundwater example with physical constraints: a GP structural-error model for reactive transport is fitted with a combined likelihood containing sub-likelihood terms for several physical constraints (e.g. element mass balance) with dynamically adapted weights; in a synthetic 3-D PCE case, carbon and chlorine mass-balance error rates fell from about 48.6% and 30.7% (unconstrained) to 23.6% and 16.5%; in a cadmium-phosphate column experiment validation NSE rose from 0.49 (no constraint) to 0.62 (one) to 0.94 (several); constraints limit over-fitting of the error model's hyperparameters — Tian, Zeng, Pan, Wang & Wu (2026), HESS 30:6459-6479, doi:10.5194/hess-30-6459-2026 [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/6459/2026/)
- Embedded model error: rather than adding a discrepancy to outputs, model-error terms are embedded in selected model parameters (represented with polynomial chaos) and inferred jointly, which keeps predictions physically consistent and helps disambiguate model error from data error; the authors state model error "is often the largest contributor to the overall predictive uncertainty" — Sargsyan, Huan & Najm (2019), "Embedded model error representation for Bayesian model calibration", Int. J. Uncertainty Quantification 9(4) [Source](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1529284); 2026 extension (preprint): embedded GP error terms with orthogonality constraints give "nearly uncorrelated posteriors" between model and error parameters and meaningful stand-alone model predictions, with the added dimension handled by a likelihood-informed subspace; extrapolation beyond the data recovers the prior predictive — Kuppa, Sargsyan, Panesi & Najm (2026), arXiv:2602.17923 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17923)
- NWP practice: weak-constraint 4D-Var "can accurately estimate the actual model errors and the initial state only when background and model errors have different spatial scales and when the observations are unbiased and spatially homogeneous" — Laloyaux et al. (2020), "Exploring the potential and limitations of weak-constraint 4D-Var", QJRMS, doi:10.1002/qj.3891 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/qj.3891); operationally it is applied in the stratosphere, where systematic model error is large [Source](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2020/19514-improving-handling-model-bias-data-assimilation.pdf); neural networks can reproduce the weak-constraint model-error estimates — Bonavita & Laloyaux (2020), JAMES, doi:10.1029/2020MS002232 [Source](https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/2020ms002232); stochastic parameterization schemes are used routinely at operational centres to represent model inadequacy — Berner et al. (2017), BAMS 98:565-587 [Source](https://hal.sorbonne-universite.fr/UPMC/insu-04833707v1); additive inflation (adding draws from an assumed model-error distribution to the forecast ensemble) and multiplicative inflation are the standard EnKF devices; on Lorenz-96 with model error, hybrid additive + multiplicative schemes improve analysis RMSE relative to multiplicative inflation alone (Scheffler et al. 2022, "Dynamical effects of inflation in ensemble-based data assimilation under the presence of model error", QJRMS) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06769)
- Multi-model approaches: Bayesian model selection and averaging choose among models and converge on a single model as data accumulate, whereas only Bayesian stacking is aimed at improving predictions by combining models — Höge, Guthke & Nowak (2019), "The hydrologist's guide to Bayesian model selection, averaging and combination", J. Hydrology 572:96-107, doi:10.1016/j.jhydrol.2019.01.072 [Source](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/106842); Höge, Guthke & Nowak (2020), "Bayesian model weighting: the many faces of model averaging", Water 12(2):309 [Source](https://elib.uni-stuttgart.de/items/bb7f5d0f-a78c-42d5-b0ee-1be78b007ad9/full)
- Data-space methods as a response to parameter compensation: DSI conditions predictions through the prior covariance between simulated observations and predictions and never adjusts parameter fields; it is available in the PEST/PEST++ suite (Delottier et al. 2023) [Source](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/4213/2023/), is implemented in pyEMU through the `EnDS` class and `prep_for_dsi()` with optional normal-score transformation and conditioning by pestpp-ies, and is cheap enough to permit rejection sampling on the emulator [Source](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks/tree/main/tutorials/part2_10_eva_and_dsi); a modified DSI for geothermal reservoir prediction is compared with other UQ methods on two problems (de Beer et al., arXiv preprint 2024-2025) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15401)
- Surrogate error as model error in ensemble/MCMC workflows: surrogate error covariance is added to the data-error covariance (C_total = C_d + C_surr) in Di Federico, Teng & Durlofsky (2026, preprint) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140)

### Inferences
- Practical recipe list for a PESTPP-IES user, ordered by cost: (a) run the prior ensemble and check that it covers each observation group (prior-data conflict); drop, transform or re-weight groups it cannot reach rather than letting parameters chase them; (b) set weights/noise to total error, not instrument error, and include temporal/spatial correlation in the noise ensemble; (c) after a first IES pass, estimate the correlated residual covariance by observation group and repeat (Lu & Chen two-pass scheme), stopping when further fit only moves parameters; (d) prefer differenced or integrated observations (head differences, drawdowns, cumulative fluxes, time-integrated concentrations) that filter structural noise; (e) add nuisance "error-model" parameters where the defect is understood (bias per observation group, boundary-condition multipliers, coarse-grid correction terms) and estimate them jointly (Rammay); (f) for key predictions, run a paired simple/complex analysis or a DSI analysis to see whether the parameter-space result is biased; (g) carry structural alternatives as scenarios and combine with stacking rather than BMA if prediction is the goal.
- The identifiability warning is consistent across fields: Brynjarsdóttir & O'Hagan (priors needed), Laloyaux et al. (scale separation and unbiased observations needed), Kuppa et al. (orthogonality constraints needed), Tian et al. (physical constraints needed). A discrepancy model without one of these simply moves the non-uniqueness from parameters to the error term; for groundwater models, where the null space is already large, an unconstrained GP discrepancy will absorb signal that should inform parameters.
- Inflation-type treatments (larger noise, additive inflation) protect against overconfidence but not against bias; only structural changes (more parameters, error-model terms, better physics) address bias. That is the same distinction White, Doherty & Hughes draw between predictions that are sensitive to solution-space versus null-space parameter components.
- Embedded model error is philosophically close to GMDSI's highly parameterized approach (put stochastic terms where the physics is uncertain, not on the outputs); the transferable addition is the explicit separation of "model-error" parameters whose posterior variance is not allowed to collapse with more data.

## 8. Dimension reduction of data and parameters: likelihood-informed and active subspaces, goal-oriented reduction, and the connection to PEST's solution/null-space decomposition and super-parameters

### Takeaway
The likelihood-informed subspace (LIS) of the UQ literature is the rigorous, nonlinear and prior-weighted generalisation of PEST's solution space: it is spanned by the dominant generalized eigenvectors of the data-misfit Hessian relative to the prior precision, the posterior is approximated as "posterior on the subspace × prior on the complement" (which is what null-space Monte Carlo does heuristically), and certified error bounds now exist. Reported informed dimensions are small (tens out of thousands), consistent with PEST experience; the genuinely new ideas for groundwater practice are globalised (posterior-averaged) subspaces, goal-oriented subspaces, and data-space reduction.

### Cited Findings
- LIS definition and use: a low-dimensional subspace where the likelihood dominates the prior is identified; the posterior is approximated as the product of a lower-dimensional posterior on the LIS and the prior marginal on the complement, so MCMC runs in few dimensions, with Rao-Blackwellised variance reduction; demonstrated on groundwater permeability inference governed by an elliptic PDE and on atmospheric remote sensing — Cui, Martin, Marzouk, Solonen & Spantini (2014), "Likelihood-informed dimension reduction for nonlinear inverse problems", Inverse Problems 30:114015, doi:10.1088/0266-5611/30/11/114015 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4680)
- Certified version: for nonlinear forward models and non-Gaussian priors/noise, the informed subspace is the dominant eigenspace of a matrix built from gradients of the log-likelihood, and the approximation error has a computable upper bound derived from logarithmic Sobolev inequalities — Zahm, Cui, Law, Spantini & Marzouk, "Certified dimension reduction in nonlinear Bayesian inverse problems" (arXiv 2018; Mathematics of Computation 2022 ‡) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03712)
- Concrete eigenproblem and typical rank: hIPPYlib computes the generalized eigenpairs H_misfit v = λ R v (R = prior precision) with randomized eigensolvers and discards eigenvalues small relative to 1; in the 4,225-parameter elliptic example about 30 eigenvectors suffice, fewer than the 50 observations, "reflecting redundancy in the data" — Villa, Petra & Ghattas (2021) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948)
- Same structure in a real aquifer problem: InSAR data inform only a few directions of the permeability field, proven in 1-D and shown numerically for a 3-D poroelastic model; the low-rank Laplace approximation is used as an MCMC proposal — Alghamdi et al. (2021) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04577)
- Active subspaces in hydrology: for a 21-parameter lumped karst model (LuKARS, Kerschbaum spring, Austria) the active subspace identifies the parameter directions that dominate the prior-to-posterior update and MCMC is run in the low-dimensional subspace — Teixeira Parente, Bittner, Mattis, Chiogna & Wohlmuth (2019), WRR 55(8):7086-7107, doi:10.1029/2019WR024739 [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03283); for a HydroGeoSphere catchment model (≈1 million elements, 32 parameters) two active variables were sufficient, a third-order polynomial in them served as a meta-model to pre-screen parameter sets, and of 10,000 adaptively sampled runs 4,533 were behavioural versus 588 of 10,000 for plain Monte Carlo — Erdal & Cirpka (2019), HESS 23:3787-3805, doi:10.5194/hess-23-3787-2019 [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/23/3787/2019/)
- Data-space reduction: DSI writes simulated observations and predictions as d = d̄ + C_d^(1/2) x, where C_d is estimated from a prior ensemble of model outputs and x are standard-normal latent "super-parameter-like" variables that become the PEST adjustable parameters; in the Delottier et al. test 100 prior runs yielded 87 latent parameters at a 0.999 singular-value energy threshold — [Source](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks/tree/main/tutorials/part0_intro_to_eva_and_dsi) [Source](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/4213/2023/); method lineage in the GMDSI notebook: Sun & Durlofsky (2017) ‡ [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-016-9672-8) and Lima et al. (2020) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-020-09933-w)
- LIS used to tame the extra dimensions of an embedded GP model-error representation — Kuppa et al. (2026, preprint) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17923)
- PEST-side counterparts (not re-verified): SVD-assist defines super-parameters from the singular vectors of the weighted Jacobian and estimates only those (Tonkin & Doherty 2005, WRR 41:W10412 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR003995); null-space Monte Carlo projects prior samples onto the calibration null space around a calibrated field (Tonkin & Doherty 2009, WRR 45:W00B10 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007WR006678); goal-oriented inference theory, in which only the parameter directions that both data inform and predictions depend on need be inferred (Lieberman & Willcox 2013, SIAM Review 55:493-519 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1137/130913110); active-subspace theory (Constantine, Dow & Wang 2014, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 36:A1500 ‡) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1137/130916138)

### Inferences
- Mapping to PEST vocabulary: with Karhunen-Loève (prior-whitened) parameters and inverse-noise weights, the eigenvectors of the prior-preconditioned Gauss-Newton Hessian are the right singular vectors of the weighted Jacobian; "eigenvalue ≫ 1" is the criterion "singular value above the noise level", i.e. the solution-space truncation point. LIS adds (i) averaging the Hessian over prior/posterior samples so the subspace is valid globally rather than at one linearisation point, (ii) an explicit posterior approximation (prior on the complement) with an error bound, and (iii) prior weighting by construction, which PEST only has when parameters are KL-scaled.
- Ensemble estimate of the LIS is available at no extra model-run cost in IES (cross-covariance of parameters and simulated observations). This suggests a non-intrusive use: update only LIS coordinates with the smoother and leave complement coordinates at their prior draws, a dimension-reduction alternative to distance-based localization that also works for parameters without spatial location (hyperparameters, latent variables of generative priors). This is my proposal; I found no paper testing it for groundwater.
- Active-subspace examples in hydrology are all low-dimensional (21-32 parameters) and use polynomial response surfaces; they are a sensitivity/screening tool rather than an alternative to highly parameterized inversion.
- DSI is goal-oriented reduction in data space: the retained dimension (87 in the example) is bounded by the prior ensemble size, so predictive variance outside the span of 100 runs is invisible; this is the data-space analogue of the IES subspace limitation noted by Chada et al. (2018).

### Gaps
- No application of LIS or certified dimension reduction to a regional MODFLOW-type model, or inside an ensemble smoother, was found.
- Spantini et al. (2015) on optimal low-rank posterior approximations and Baptista-Marzouk-Zahm on joint data/parameter reduction were not retrieved.
- The exact reduced dimensions and speed-ups in Cui et al. (2014) were not on the abstract page.
- Tonkin & Doherty (2005, 2009), Moore & Doherty (2005), Constantine et al. (2014) and Lieberman & Willcox (2013) are cited from memory.

## 9. Ranked shortlist of the most promising transfer opportunities for groundwater model parameterization, regularization and model-error treatment

### Takeaway
Ranked by expected benefit under the stated constraints (sparse heads and flows, 10^3-10^6 parameters, need for defensible priors, non-intrusive ensemble workflows), the best near-term transfers are model-error workflows and hierarchical/hyperparameter-uncertain Gaussian priors; structural-plus-property hierarchical priors and data-space methods come next; deep generative priors are promising but not yet demonstrated for head-conditioned regional models; edge-preserving regularizers and trans-dimensional samplers are niche.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence that model-error treatment changes answers materially in ensemble workflows: biased posteriors and extreme parameter values without an error model (Rammay et al. 2019) [Source](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/quantification-of-prediction-uncertainty-using-imperfect-subsurfa/); about 40% change in a nitrate-load forecast from assimilating tritium into a one-layer model (Knowling et al. 2020) [Source](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/1677/2020/)
- Evidence that fixed variogram hyperparameters are a strong assumption and that naive IES fails when they are freed: Laloy et al. (2015) [Source](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3011n0x1); Oliver (2022) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01116)
- Evidence that the categorical prior dominates head-conditioned posteriors and that smoothers mis-place connected features: Juda et al. (2023) [Source](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.160/); Delottier et al. (2023) [Source](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/4213/2023/)
- Evidence on scale and limits of latent diffusion priors: 524,288 cells, 1,024 latents, ESMDA under-performing MCMC/SMC, synthetic only (Di Federico, Teng & Durlofsky 2026, preprint) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140)
- Evidence that informed dimensions are small and computable: Villa et al. (2021) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948); Cui et al. (2014) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4680)

### Inferences
Ranking, with reasoning:

1. Residual-based total-error estimation plus model criticism as a standard two-pass IES workflow (Oliver & Alfonzo; Lu & Chen; Doherty & Welter; Knowling). Why first: model error is, by the admission of every community surveyed, the dominant cause of bias and overconfidence; the method is entirely non-intrusive (it changes weights, noise ensembles and which observations are assimilated), costs one extra smoother pass, and has peer-reviewed demonstrations in reservoir ensembles. Risks: zero-mean assumption; inflation prevents overconfidence but not bias.

2. Hierarchical Gaussian priors with uncertain range, anisotropy, variance and, where warranted, smoothness, in non-centred form, generated by an SPDE (sparse precision) or FFT operator in the pre-processor. Why second: it attacks the least defensible element of current practice (a hand-picked stationary variogram) while staying inside the multi-Gaussian framework that IES handles best; SPDE fields scale linearly, work on unstructured grids, and give non-stationarity and anisotropy tensors for free; mature open software exists (fmesher/INLA, hIPPYlib, FEniCS). Risks: Oliver (2022) shows plain IES fails on the hyperparameters; needs analytic chain-rule derivatives through the generator (cheap, the generator is linear in z) or a staged scheme. No groundwater ensemble demonstration yet: a clear research gap.

3. Jointly estimated low-dimensional error models and embedded model-error parameters (Rammay; Xu & Valocchi; Sargsyan-Huan-Najm; Tian et al. 2026), with identifiability enforced by physical or orthogonality constraints. Why third: addresses bias rather than just dispersion, and fits PEST's "more parameters, properly regularised" philosophy; requires case-specific design of the error model and informative priors.

4. Hierarchical structure-plus-property priors sampled from geology-driven generators (ArchPy, interpretation-uncertainty simulation, AEM-derived cluster or probability models), with structure handled as prior-ensemble variability and conditioning done either on within-unit properties only or in data space (DSI). Why fourth: gives geological defensibility and honest structural uncertainty with existing tools and no training; DSI sidesteps the smoother's damage to categorical fields. Risks: DSI's subspace is limited by prior ensemble size and hides parameter fields; structure is not updated by data unless a categorical parameterization (level set on pilot points, TRIPS) is added.

5. Likelihood-informed-subspace updating and goal-oriented reduction as successors to SVD-assist and as a localization substitute in IES. Why fifth: strong theory, small informed dimensions in every example, and directly reuses quantities IES already computes; but untested in this setting, so it is a research item rather than a recipe.

6. Latent diffusion (and VAE) priors for facies-controlled systems. Why sixth: best available representation of complex geology at 3-D scale and now able to carry uncertain scenario parameters; but needs thousands of training realizations from a trusted geomodeller, is training-image dependent, interacts poorly with Kalman-type updates, and has no peer-reviewed head-conditioned field demonstration. Most promising short-term use: as a fast generator of prior ensembles for DSI or for surrogate-accelerated SMC, not as IES parameters.

7. Sequential, probabilistic use of geophysics and unconventional data (AEM-derived structure with petrophysical uncertainty; InSAR; tracers after bias screening). Why seventh: high value where data exist, but it is a data-availability question more than a methods transfer, and the literature's main message is cautionary (coupled inversion and information-rich data amplify structural error).

8. Edge-preserving regularizers and sparsity priors (TV/Lp by IRLS; Cauchy/horseshoe priors; learned dictionaries). Why eighth: cheap to emulate in GLM workflows and useful for hypothesis-testing "blocky" alternatives, but heads cannot locate edges, there is no ensemble-smoother equivalent, and TV is not discretization-invariant.

9. Trans-dimensional and refinement-indicator parameterization. Why last: elegant and honest about model complexity, but cost (or adjoint requirement) is prohibitive for regional models; the transferable residue is identifiability-driven pilot-point densification within multi-scale PstFrom setups.
