Baseline used for every comparison (what a PEST/PEST++ user has today):

- **PESTPP-IES** is an iterative ensemble smoother whose default update is the ensemble Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt form of Chen and Oliver (2013), with the "standard Kalman update" (optionally iterated as the multiple-data-assimilation scheme of Emerick and Reynolds 2013, `ies_use_mda`) as the alternative. The manual states that it "calculates samples of the posterior parameter probability distribution", that the cost is "often far lower" than MCMC at moderate to high parameter numbers, and that the number of realisations should exceed the dimensionality of the solution space. Options include a user localisation matrix (`ies_localizer`), automatic adaptive localisation (`ies_autoadaloc`, `ies_autoadaloc_sigma_dist`, default 1.0), prior-data conflict detection (`ies_drop_conflicts`, `ies_pdc_sigma_distance`), rejection of lagging realisations (`ies_bad_phi`, `ies_bad_phi_sigma`), a multimodal solution process using local neighbourhoods of realisations (`ies_multimodal_alpha`, values 0.1 to 0.25 suggested) and covariance reinflation (`ies_n_iter_reinflate`) — [PEST++ user manual, section 9](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V]; [White 2018, "A model-independent iterative ensemble smoother for efficient history-matching and uncertainty quantification in very high dimensions", Environmental Modelling & Software 109:191-201](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2018.06.009); [White, Hunt, Fienen, Doherty 2020, "Approaches to highly parameterized inversion: PEST++ Version 5", USGS TM 7-C26](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm7C26).
- **Null-space Monte Carlo (NSMC)** starts from a calibrated model and its Jacobian, projects prior parameter samples onto the calibration null space and re-calibrates each with a small number of extra iterations — [Tonkin & Doherty 2009, "Calibration-constrained Monte Carlo analysis of highly parameterized models using subspace techniques", Water Resources Research 45:W00B10](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007WR006678).
- **FOSM / linear analysis** (PREDUNC, GENLINPRED, pyEMU `Schur`) is the Schur-complement conditioning of a Gaussian prior under a linearised model; its cost is that of one Jacobian — [Doherty, Hunt & Tonkin 2010, USGS SIR 2010-5211](https://doi.org/10.3133/sir20105211).
- **DSI in PEST++/pyEMU** builds a linear PCA emulator d = d_mean + C_d^(1/2) x from a prior ensemble of simulated observations and predictions, optionally after normal-score transformation, and conditions the latent vector x with PESTPP-IES; no model parameters are adjusted — [GMDSI notebooks, tutorials part0_intro_to_eva_and_dsi and part2_10_eva_and_dsi](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks) [V, local copy]; [Delottier, Doherty & Brunner 2023, Geoscientific Model Development 16:4213-4231](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023) [V].

## Key question 1. Ensemble data assimilation: what refinements to ensemble Kalman filters and smoothers have the NWP, ocean and reservoir communities developed, and what is known about bias and variance underestimation?

### Takeaway
PESTPP-IES already contains the main reservoir-engineering ideas (LM-EnRML, ES-MDA, correlation-based adaptive localisation, bad-realisation rejection); what it lacks relative to NWP, ocean and applied-mathematics practice is principled variance maintenance (sampling-error-corrected gains, relaxation-type inflation, hybrid static/ensemble covariances), multilevel/multifidelity ensembles, adaptive tempering schedules with stopping rules, and a "calibrate-emulate-sample" route to a properly Bayesian posterior for low-dimensional parameter blocks. Theory and benchmarks agree that all Kalman-type ensemble methods are exact only for linear-Gaussian problems, are biased for nonlinear ones even with infinite ensembles, and systematically underestimate variance when the ensemble is small relative to the number of informative data directions.

### Cited Findings

#### Reviews that define the field
- Standard references: Evensen's EnKF monograph (2009) and the open-access textbook that unifies EnKF, ES, ES-MDA, EnRML/IES and particle methods under one Bayesian derivation (2022) — [Evensen 2009, "Data Assimilation: The Ensemble Kalman Filter", Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-03711-5); [Evensen, Vossepoel & van Leeuwen 2022, "Data Assimilation Fundamentals", Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-96709-3).
- Geoscience-wide overview of variational, ensemble and hybrid methods and their open problems (non-Gaussianity, coupled systems, model error) — [Carrassi, Bocquet, Bertino & Evensen 2018, "Data assimilation in the geosciences: An overview of methods, issues, and perspectives", WIREs Climate Change 9:e535](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcc.535).
- Operational NWP practice: the EnKF review for atmospheric DA (ensembles of order 10^2 members against state dimensions many orders larger, made workable by localisation and inflation) and the review of operational variational and ensemble-variational schemes — [Houtekamer & Zhang 2016, Monthly Weather Review 144:4489-4532](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-15-0440.1); [Bannister 2017, QJRMS 143:607-633](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2982).
- Reservoir history matching review, including RML, EnKF and the first iterative smoothers — [Oliver & Chen 2011, "Recent progress on reservoir history matching: a review", Computational Geosciences 15:185-221](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-010-9194-2).
- Hydrogeology-side reviews that already note the transfer of EnKF/ES and MCMC ideas and the need for multi-fidelity and non-Gaussian methods — [Zhou, Gómez-Hernández & Li 2014, "Inverse methods in hydrogeology: Evolution and recent trends", Advances in Water Resources 63:22-37](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2013.10.014); [Linde, Ginsbourger, Irving, Nobile & Doucet 2017, "On uncertainty quantification in hydrogeology and hydrogeophysics", Advances in Water Resources 110:166-181](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.10.014).
- Most recent theoretical synthesis (2025): a 170-page Acta Numerica article derives ensemble Kalman methods for both state estimation and inverse problems as particle approximations of mean-field models, and states that analysis of their accuracy, "especially for uncertainty quantification", has lagged behind their empirical success — [Calvello, Reich & Stuart 2025, "Ensemble Kalman methods: a mean-field perspective", Acta Numerica 34:123-291](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492924000060) [V].

#### Covariance localisation (distance-based, adaptive, correlation-based)
- Distance-based tapering of ensemble covariances with a compactly supported fifth-order correlation function, and its justification as a filter of sampling noise — [Gaspari & Cohn 1999, QJRMS 125:723-757](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.49712555417); [Hamill, Whitaker & Snyder 2001, Monthly Weather Review 129:2776-2790](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2001)129<2776:DDFOBE>2.0.CO;2); [Houtekamer & Mitchell 2001, Monthly Weather Review 129:123-137](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2001)129<0123:ASEKFF>2.0.CO;2).
- Domain ("local analysis") localisation: the LETKF solves an independent small problem in ensemble space for each grid point, which is the same structure as the per-parameter local solves in PESTPP-IES — [Hunt, Kostelich & Szunyogh 2007, Physica D 230:112-126](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2006.11.008); [GMDSI localisation tutorial, noting one truncated-SVD solve per adjustable parameter and multithreading via `ies_num_threads`](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks) [V, local copy].
- Statistical (non-distance) localisation for cases with no meaningful distance: hierarchical/group filters and sampling-error-correction look-up tables that shrink each regression coefficient as a function of sample correlation and ensemble size; empirical localisation functions learned from OSSEs; linear-filtering theory for optimal localisation of sample covariances — [Anderson 2012, "Localization and sampling error correction in ensemble Kalman filter data assimilation", Monthly Weather Review 140:2359-2371](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-11-00013.1); [Ménétrier, Montmerle, Michel & Berre 2015, Monthly Weather Review 143:1622-1643](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-14-00157.1).
- Flow-adaptive localisation built from powers of ensemble correlations — [Bishop & Hodyss 2009, "Ensemble covariances adaptively localized with ECO-RAP", Tellus A 61:84-96](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0870.2008.00371.x).
- Reservoir-specific developments (the direct ancestors of `ies_autoadaloc`): localisation regions from sensitivities plus prior correlation lengths; analysis of cross-covariance localisation for multiphase flow; localisation inside iterative smoothers; bootstrap screening of the Kalman gain; correlation-based adaptive localisation that thresholds sample correlations against a noise level estimated by shuffling realisations, which works for non-local parameters and non-local data such as 4D seismic — [Emerick & Reynolds 2011, Computational Geosciences 15:251-269](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-010-9198-y); [Chen & Oliver 2010, Computational Geosciences 14:579-601](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-009-9174-6); [Chen & Oliver 2017, "Localization and regularization for iterative ensemble smoothers", Computational Geosciences 21:13-30](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-016-9599-7); [Zhang & Oliver 2010, "Improving the ensemble estimate of the Kalman gain by bootstrap sampling", Mathematical Geosciences 42:327-345](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-010-9267-8); [Luo, Bhakta & Nævdal 2018, SPE Journal 23(2):396-427](https://doi.org/10.2118/185936-PA); [Luo & Bhakta 2020, "Automatic and adaptive localization for ensemble-based history matching", Journal of Petroleum Science and Engineering 184:106559](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.petrol.2019.106559).
- Groundwater status: PESTPP-IES implements both an explicit localisation matrix and automatic adaptive localisation in which any correlation below the background-error mean plus `ies_autoadaloc_sigma_dist` standard deviations is treated as non-significant; the GMDSI tutorial warns that the automatic form "doesn't resolve the level of localization" of an explicit matrix, and that for more than roughly 30,000 parameters adding realisations can be faster in wall time than fully localised solves — [GMDSI notebooks, part2_06_ies localisation tutorial](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks) [V, local copy]; [PEST++ user manual, section 9.1.11](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].
- Recent theory (2023 to 2024): a quantitative explanation of why even crude localisation helps, and non-asymptotic bounds showing that the ensemble size needed scales with an effective dimension of the covariance, which localisation reduces; a localised ensemble Kalman inversion that breaks the subspace property — [Morzfeld & Hodyss 2023, "A theory for why even simple covariance localization is so useful in ensemble data assimilation", Monthly Weather Review 151:717-736](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-22-0255.1); [Al-Ghattas & Sanz-Alonso 2024, "Non-asymptotic analysis of ensemble Kalman updates: effective dimension and localization", Information and Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03246); [Tong & Morzfeld 2023, "Localized ensemble Kalman inversion", Inverse Problems 39:064002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10821); [Lee 2021, "Sampling error correction in ensemble Kalman inversion"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11341).

#### Inflation and variance maintenance
- Why small ensembles under-disperse: because the gain is a nonlinear function of a noisy sample covariance, the analysis variance is underestimated on average even for a perfect linear model; the effect grows as ensemble size falls — [Sacher & Bartello 2008, "Sampling errors in ensemble Kalman filtering. Part I: Theory", Monthly Weather Review 136:3035-3049](https://doi.org/10.1175/2007MWR2323.1); [Furrer & Bengtsson 2007, Journal of Multivariate Analysis 98:227-255](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmva.2006.08.003).
- Remedies used operationally: multiplicative inflation with Bayesian, spatially and temporally adaptive estimation of the inflation factor; observation-space adaptive inflation; relaxation of posterior perturbations (RTPP) or posterior spread (RTPS) back toward the prior — [Anderson 2007, Tellus A 59:210-224](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0870.2006.00216.x); [Anderson 2009, Tellus A 61:72-83](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0870.2008.00361.x); [Miyoshi 2011, Monthly Weather Review 139:1519-1535](https://doi.org/10.1175/2010MWR3570.1); [Zhang, Snyder & Sun 2004, Monthly Weather Review 132:1238-1253](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2004)132<1238:IOIEAO>2.0.CO;2); [Whitaker & Hamill 2012, "Evaluating methods to account for system errors in ensemble data assimilation", Monthly Weather Review 140:3078-3089](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-11-00276.1).
- Reservoir evidence that localisation alone does not protect non-local (global, scalar) parameters from variance loss, with a comparison of mitigation methods — [Lacerda, Emerick & Pires 2019, "Methods to mitigate loss of variance due to sampling errors in ensemble data assimilation with non-local model parameters", Journal of Petroleum Science and Engineering 172:690-706](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.petrol.2018.08.056).
- Groundwater evidence: "filter inbreeding" (variance underestimation) was documented for joint state-parameter EnKF in groundwater flow in 2008, with damping and larger ensembles as remedies — [Hendricks Franssen & Kinzelbach 2008, Water Resources Research 44:W09408](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007WR006505).
- PEST++ status: the manual acknowledges that across iterations "the variance of the parameter ensemble reduces, sometime dramatically" and offers covariance reinflation (resetting variance to the prior ensemble state after a set number of iterations) plus regularisation against ensemble-diversity collapse — [PEST++ user manual, sections 9.1.15 and 9.2.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].

#### Hybrid ensemble-variational methods
- Hybrid covariances blend a static (climatological) covariance with a localised ensemble covariance inside a variational cost function; 4DEnVar replaces the tangent-linear/adjoint model with 4-D ensemble covariances. These are the operational standard at major centres — [Hamill & Snyder 2000, Monthly Weather Review 128:2905-2919](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2000)128<2905:AHEKFV>2.0.CO;2); [Lorenc 2003, QJRMS 129:3183-3203](https://doi.org/10.1256/qj.02.132); [Clayton, Lorenc & Barker 2013, QJRMS 139:1445-1461](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2054); [Buehner et al. 2015, Monthly Weather Review 143:2532-2559](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-14-00354.1); [Bannister 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2982).
- ECMWF's Ensemble of Data Assimilations runs an ensemble of independent 4D-Var minimisations with perturbed observations and model, and feeds the resulting variances back into the deterministic analysis — [Bonavita, Isaksen & Hólm 2012, QJRMS 138:1540-1559](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.1899).
- Nonlinear ensemble-variational smoothers that iterate a Gauss-Newton solve in ensemble space over a time window (IEnKF, IEnKS) — [Sakov, Oliver & Bertino 2012, Monthly Weather Review 140:1988-2004](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-11-00176.1); [Bocquet & Sakov 2014, QJRMS 140:1521-1535](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2236).

#### Iterative ensemble smoothers (ES-MDA, EnRML, LM-EnRML, subspace EnRML)
- Ensemble smoother: a single global update of all parameters with all data — [van Leeuwen & Evensen 1996, Monthly Weather Review 124:2898-2913](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1996)124<2898:DAAIMI>2.0.CO;2).
- ES-MDA: the same data are assimilated N_a times with the measurement-error covariance inflated by factors alpha_i satisfying sum(1/alpha_i) = 1, which reproduces the single ES update in the linear-Gaussian case and acts as a tempering of the likelihood in the nonlinear case; cost is N_a times the ensemble size — [Emerick & Reynolds 2013, "Ensemble smoother with multiple data assimilation", Computers & Geosciences 55:3-15](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2012.03.011).
- Choosing the inflation schedule adaptively by a discrepancy principle rather than fixing N_a in advance — [Le, Emerick & Reynolds 2016, SPE Journal 21(6):2195-2207](https://doi.org/10.2118/173214-PA); [Rafiee & Reynolds 2017, Inverse Problems 33:115003](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/aa8cb2); [Iglesias & Yang 2021, "Adaptive regularisation for ensemble Kalman inversion", Inverse Problems 37:025008](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/abd29b).
- EnRML and its Levenberg-Marquardt form: an ensemble approximation of randomised maximum likelihood in which each realisation minimises its own perturbed objective using an ensemble-average sensitivity; the "approximate" LM-EnRML drops the explicit prior-covariance term from the Hessian — [Gu & Oliver 2007, SPE Journal 12(4):438-446](https://doi.org/10.2118/108438-PA); [Chen & Oliver 2012, Mathematical Geosciences 44:1-26](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-011-9376-z); [Chen & Oliver 2013, "Levenberg-Marquardt forms of the iterative ensemble smoother for efficient history matching and uncertainty quantification", Computational Geosciences 17:689-703](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-013-9351-5).
- Regularised variants derived from a minimum-average-cost problem (RLM-MAC) and from Hanke's regularising Levenberg-Marquardt scheme; in synthetic reservoir tests benchmarked against MCMC, the regularised smoothers gave "more robust and stable approximation of the posterior" mean and variance than unregularised ones — [Luo, Stordal, Lorentzen & Nævdal 2015, SPE Journal 20(5):962-982](https://doi.org/10.2118/176023-PA); [Iglesias 2014/2015, "Iterative regularization for ensemble data assimilation in reservoir models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.5375) [V].
- Evensen's analysis of ES, ES-MDA and IES as approximate solvers of the same ensemble of perturbed cost functions (2018), followed by the subspace EnRML that searches in the ensemble subspace and scales to very large data sets (2019) and the revised stochastic IES that removes ad hoc approximations of the Chen-Oliver form (2019) — [Evensen 2018, "Analysis of iterative ensemble smoothers for solving inverse problems", Computational Geosciences 22:885-908](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-018-9731-y); [Evensen, Raanes, Stordal & Hove 2019, Frontiers in Applied Mathematics and Statistics 5:47](https://doi.org/10.3389/fams.2019.00047); [Raanes, Stordal & Evensen 2019, "Revising the stochastic iterative ensemble smoother", Nonlinear Processes in Geophysics 26:325-338](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-26-325-2019).
- Consistent treatment of model error and correlated measurement errors in iterative smoothers — [Evensen 2019, "Accounting for model errors in iterative ensemble smoothers", Computational Geosciences 23:761-775](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-019-9819-z); [Evensen 2021, "Formulating the history matching problem with consistent error statistics", Computational Geosciences 25:945-970](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-021-10032-7).
- Groundwater status: IES is mainstream through PESTPP-IES (2018 onward); a 2025 USGS note on choosing iterations and realisations reports that ensembles of 100 to 250 realisations and two iterations were generally sufficient in the cases examined — [White 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2018.06.009); [USGS publication page, "PEST++IES: how many iterations and realizations? Finding the point of diminishing returns" (2025)](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/pesties-how-many-iterations-realizations-finding-point-diminishing-returns) (search-result summary only; the page timed out when opened).

#### Square-root and deterministic variants
- Deterministic filters avoid perturbing observations and so remove one source of sampling noise: EAKF, EnSRF, ETKF, the unifying square-root framework, and the DEnKF linear approximation — [Anderson 2001, Monthly Weather Review 129:2884-2903](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2001)129<2884:AEAKFF>2.0.CO;2); [Whitaker & Hamill 2002, Monthly Weather Review 130:1913-1924](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2002)130<1913:EDAWPO>2.0.CO;2); [Bishop, Etherton & Majumdar 2001, Monthly Weather Review 129:420-436](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2001)129<0420:ASWTET>2.0.CO;2); [Tippett et al. 2003, Monthly Weather Review 131:1485-1490](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2003)131<1485:ESRF>2.0.CO;2); [Sakov & Oke 2008, Tellus A 60:361-371](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0870.2007.00299.x).
- PEST++ status: observation noise realisations can be switched off with `ies_no_noise`, which makes the update deterministic in the data but is not a square-root scheme — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V]; [PESTPP-IES help page](https://help.pesthomepage.org/pestpp-ies.html) [V].

#### Multilevel and multifidelity ensemble Kalman methods
- Multilevel EnKF: telescoping sum of ensemble corrections across a hierarchy of discretisations, with proven asymptotic cost reduction over single-level EnKF — [Hoel, Law & Tempone 2016, "Multilevel ensemble Kalman filtering", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 54(3):1813-1839](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M100955X); [Chernov, Hoel, Law, Nobile & Tempone 2021, Numerische Mathematik 147:71-125](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-020-01159-3).
- Reservoir assessment (2020): the multilevel EnKF "does not perform well" on the reservoir history-matching problems tested because its theoretical assumptions are not met by reservoir-model hierarchies; a multilevel hybrid EnKF that pools levels into one hybrid covariance was proposed instead — [Fossum, Mannseth & Stordal 2020, "Assessment of multilevel ensemble-based data assimilation for reservoir history matching", Computational Geosciences 24:217-239](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10596-019-09911-x) [V, search summary].
- Follow-up multilevel hybrid ensemble smoother (MLHES) and its iterative version for inverted seismic data, with cheap corrections for multilevel modelling error; experiments indicate the multilevel smoother outperforms its single-level counterparts at equal cost — [Nezhadali, Bhakta, Fossum & Mannseth 2021, Frontiers in Applied Mathematics and Statistics 7:673077](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fams.2021.673077/pdf) [V, search summary]; [Nezhadali et al. 2022, "Iterative multilevel assimilation of inverted seismic data", Computational Geosciences 26](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10596-021-10125-3) [V, search summary].
- Multifidelity EnKF: a reduced-order model serves as a control variate for the full-model ensemble statistics — [Popov, Mou, Sandu & Iliescu 2021, "A multifidelity ensemble Kalman filter with reduced order control variates", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 43(2):A1134-A1162](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1349965).

#### Ensemble Kalman inversion (EKI), its analysis, regularised and constrained forms, and the ensemble Kalman sampler
- EKI recasts the iterated EnKF as a derivative-free optimiser for inverse problems; the ensemble stays in the linear span of the initial ensemble (subspace property). The original demonstrations included a groundwater-flow (Darcy) inverse problem — [Iglesias, Law & Stuart 2013, "Ensemble Kalman methods for inverse problems", Inverse Problems 29:045001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/29/4/045001); background framework in [Stuart 2010, "Inverse problems: a Bayesian perspective", Acta Numerica 19:451-559](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492910000061).
- Continuous-time (gradient-flow) analysis: in the linear case the EKI ensemble collapses toward the minimiser of the data misfit within the initial subspace, so its final spread is not a posterior spread — [Schillings & Stuart 2017, "Analysis of the ensemble Kalman filter for inverse problems", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 55(3):1264-1290](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M105959X).
- Regularising iterative ensemble Kalman method with discrepancy-principle stopping, Tikhonov-regularised EKI (TEKI), constrained EKI (box and general convex constraints), and EKI as a general derivative-free trainer — [Iglesias 2016, Inverse Problems 32:025002](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/32/2/025002); [Chada, Stuart & Tong 2020, "Tikhonov regularization within ensemble Kalman inversion", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 58(2):1263-1294](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1242331); [Albers, Blancquart, Levine, Seylabi & Stuart 2019, "Ensemble Kalman methods with constraints", Inverse Problems 35:095007](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/ab1c09); [Kovachki & Stuart 2019, Inverse Problems 35:095005](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/ab1c3a).
- A unified view of EKI, ES-MDA/tempering and iterative EnKF variants, with new variants — [Chada, Chen & Sanz-Alonso 2021, "Iterative ensemble Kalman methods: A unified perspective with some new variants", Foundations of Data Science 3(3):331-369](https://doi.org/10.3934/fods.2021011).
- Ensemble Kalman sampler (EKS): an interacting Langevin system preconditioned by the ensemble covariance whose mean-field Fokker-Planck equation has the posterior as invariant measure; the authors describe the practical algorithm as an "approximate sampler" — [Garbuno-Inigo, Hoffmann, Li & Stuart 2020, "Interacting Langevin diffusions: Gradient structure and ensemble Kalman sampler", SIAM Journal on Applied Dynamical Systems 19(1):412-441](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08866) [V]; affine-invariant finite-ensemble correction (ALDI) in [Garbuno-Inigo, Nüsken & Reich 2020, SIAM Journal on Applied Dynamical Systems 19(3):1633-1658](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1304891).
- Unscented Kalman inversion and related Gaussian-approximation Kalman iterations, designed to give a usable Gaussian posterior approximation with 2d+1 runs per iteration for d parameters — [Huang, Schneider & Stuart 2022, "Iterated Kalman methodology for inverse problems", Journal of Computational Physics 463:111262](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2022.111262); [Huang, Huang, Reich & Stuart 2022, "Efficient derivative-free Bayesian inference for large-scale inverse problems", Inverse Problems 38:125006](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/ac99fa).

#### Calibrate-Emulate-Sample (CES, CliMA group)
- Three stages: calibrate with an ensemble Kalman method (which concentrates model runs where posterior mass is), emulate the parameter-to-data map with a Gaussian process trained on those runs, and sample the emulated posterior with MCMC. The method targets maps that are expensive, derivative-free and possibly noisy and "requires only a small number of evaluations" — [Cleary, Garbuno-Inigo, Lan, Schneider & Stuart 2021, "Calibrate, emulate, sample", Journal of Computational Physics 424:109716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03689) [V].
- Climate demonstration: calibration and UQ of convection parameters in an idealised GCM "requires only O(10^2) model runs", against O(10^5) typically needed by MCMC — [Dunbar, Garbuno-Inigo, Schneider & Stuart 2021, Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems 13:e2020MS002454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13262) [V].
- Software: EnsembleKalmanProcesses.jl (EKI, EKS, UKI, sparsity and constraint handling, failure-tolerant updates) and CalibrateEmulateSample.jl — [Dunbar et al. 2022, Journal of Open Source Software 7(80):4869](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.04869); [Dunbar et al. 2024, Journal of Open Source Software 9(97):6372](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.06372).

#### What is known about bias and variance underestimation
- Large-ensemble limit: for a nonlinear forward map the EnKF analysis ensemble does not converge to the Bayesian posterior; it converges to the distribution of a linear (best-linear-estimator) update of the prior — [Ernst, Sprungk & Starkloff 2015, "Analysis of the ensemble and polynomial chaos Kalman filters in Bayesian inverse problems", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 3(1):823-851](https://doi.org/10.1137/140981319).
- Reservoir benchmark against a long MCMC chain (2013): nine ensemble-based methods plus an adjoint-based RML implementation were compared on data match, uncertainty quantification and cost for a small but highly nonlinear reservoir model whose reference posterior came from "a very long" Markov chain — [Emerick & Reynolds 2013, "Investigation of the sampling performance of ensemble-based methods with a simple reservoir model", Computational Geosciences 17:325-350](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-012-9333-z) [V, abstract]. The paper is routinely cited for the conclusion that ES-MDA gave better data matches and uncertainty quantification than EnKF and single-step ES at comparable cost, with RML closest to MCMC (conclusion not re-verified from the full text in this session).
- Reservoir benchmark against a dimension-robust MCMC gold standard (2013): linearisation about the MAP, RML and EnKF-type methods were tested for how well they reproduce the posterior mean and variance; the authors report "substantial discrepancies" in the approximation properties of the standard ad hoc Gaussian approximations — [Iglesias, Law & Stuart 2013, "Evaluation of Gaussian approximations for data assimilation in reservoir models", Computational Geosciences 17:851-885](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1779) [V].
- Subsurface-hydrology benchmark (2024): five scenarios with 10,000 log-conductivity parameters (100 x 100 grid), 25 head data (steady) or 250 (transient), and reference posteriors from pCN parallel-tempering MCMC with 800,000 steps per run, repeated four times. A standard EnKF with 1,000 members scored 0.3 to 0.4 on the normalised error metrics; with 100 members the metrics worsened and the paper reports filter collapse with posterior standard deviation shrinking too far — [Xu, Xiao, Reuschen, Wildt, Hendricks Franssen & Nowak 2024, "Towards a community-wide effort for benchmarking in subsurface hydrological inversion", Hydrology and Earth System Sciences 28:5375-5400](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-5375-2024) [V].
- Variability between repeated synthetic experiments is large enough that many repetitions are needed before EnKF variants can be ranked — [Keller, Hendricks Franssen & Marquart 2018, "Comparing seven variants of the ensemble Kalman filter: How many synthetic experiments are needed?", Water Resources Research 54:6299-6318](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018WR023374).
- Direction of the error is not always under-dispersion: with a strongly nonlinear latent-diffusion parameterisation of 3-D channelised models, latent-space ES-MDA reduced uncertainty less than surrogate-based MCMC and SMC, and the authors conclude that "ensemble Kalman methods may provide overestimated posterior uncertainty with highly nonlinear parameterizations" — [Di Federico, Teng & Durlofsky 2026, arXiv:2606.11140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140) [V].
- IES applied with only 100 realisations to a 204,000-parameter integrated surface-subsurface model retained 70 realisations after three iterations with objective functions from 1,075 to 16,098 against roughly 760 expected, illustrating incomplete convergence when run failures and nonlinearity combine — [Delottier, Doherty & Brunner 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023) [V].

#### Software outside groundwater
- DART (NCAR; EAKF, adaptive inflation, sampling-error correction, QCEFF) — [Anderson et al. 2009, Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society 90:1283-1296](https://doi.org/10.1175/2009BAMS2618.1). PDAF (AWI; LETKF/ESTKF, localisation, also coupled to subsurface models as TSMP-PDAF) — [Nerger & Hiller 2013, Computers & Geosciences 55:110-118](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2012.03.026); [Kurtz et al. 2016, Geoscientific Model Development 9:1341-1360](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-9-1341-2016). Equinor's ERT and its standalone `iterative_ensemble_smoother` Python package (ES-MDA and subspace IES with adaptive localisation) — [github.com/equinor/iterative_ensemble_smoother](https://github.com/equinor/iterative_ensemble_smoother). DAPPER for method benchmarking — [github.com/nansencenter/DAPPER](https://github.com/nansencenter/DAPPER).

### Inferences
- PESTPP-IES sits in the EnRML/RML family, so in a linear-Gaussian problem with a large ensemble it samples the posterior, and elsewhere it inherits (a) the RML approximation error for nonlinear models and (b) small-ensemble sampling error. NSMC shares (a) but replaces (b) with a linearisation of the null space around a single calibrated point; FOSM assumes both linearity and Gaussianity but has no sampling error. None of the three is asymptotically exact for a nonlinear model.
- The largest low-effort gain available to PESTPP-IES from NWP is variance bookkeeping rather than a new update equation: sampling-error-corrected gains (Anderson 2012), RTPS-type relaxation as a principled alternative to resetting variance by reinflation, and adaptive tempering schedules with a discrepancy-principle stopping rule (Le et al. 2016; Iglesias & Yang 2021) in place of a fixed NOPTMAX. All are internal algorithm changes that keep the workflow model-independent.
- A hybrid covariance (prior geostatistical covariance blended with ensemble covariance) is the NWP answer to a rank-deficient ensemble; in an IES it would let the update reach directions outside the span of 100 to 300 realisations. No PEST++ implementation or groundwater test of this was found.
- ECMWF's EDA is structurally an RML ensemble with an adjoint-based minimiser; it is the closest operational analogue of "NSMC done properly" and suggests that where adjoints exist (MODFLOW 6 adjoint work is covered by another researcher) an ensemble of regularised inversions is a credible alternative to IES.
- Multilevel smoothers transfer more easily to groundwater than to NWP because groundwater models have natural hierarchies (coarsened grids, steady-state versus transient, shortened simulation periods, DSI emulators). The reservoir experience says to use the hybrid-covariance multilevel form, not the telescoping MLEnKF.
- CES is attractive for the low-dimensional part of a groundwater problem (global multipliers, boundary conductances, recharge factors, geostatistical hyperparameters): IES runs already provide the training set, so the extra cost is emulator fitting and emulator MCMC.

### Gaps
- No published head-to-head of PESTPP-IES itself (GLM form, with and without localisation, multimodal option or reinflation) against an MCMC reference posterior was found; the 2024 HESS benchmark supplies reference solutions but its published comparison covers only a standard EnKF.
- Per-method numerical results of Emerick & Reynolds (2013) and Iglesias, Law & Stuart (2013) (which method came closest for mean and for variance, and by how much) could not be extracted because the full texts were not accessible.
- The exact quantitative recommendation of Keller et al. (2018) on the number of synthetic repetitions, and the full content of the 2025 USGS IES note, were not verified.
- No groundwater application of EKS, TEKI, UKI or CES was found; this is absence of evidence from a truncated search, not proof of absence.
- Operational NWP changes during 2025 to 2026 (for example machine-learned DA components) were not surveyed.

## Key question 2. Non-Gaussian ensemble and particle methods

### Takeaway
Pure particle filters remain infeasible at high dimension, but three families have matured enough to matter for groundwater: marginal-transform and quantile-conserving ensemble filters (cheap, non-intrusive, already partly present as normal-score transforms), localised or tempered particle/hybrid filters, and transport-based nonlinear updates (transport-map filters and smoothers, particle flow, SVGD). Gaussian-mixture or clustered updates, which PESTPP-IES offers as its multimodal option, are the pragmatic middle ground; all of these still lack exactness guarantees with ensembles of a few hundred.

### Cited Findings

#### Particle filters and why they collapse
- The ensemble size needed to avoid weight collapse grows exponentially with the variance of the log-likelihood (an effective dimension); the classic illustration is that about 10^11 particles would be needed for a 200-dimensional Gaussian example — [Snyder, Bengtsson, Bickel & Anderson 2008, "Obstacles to high-dimensional particle filtering", Monthly Weather Review 136:4629-4640](https://doi.org/10.1175/2008MWR2529.1); background in [van Leeuwen 2009, "Particle filtering in geophysical systems", Monthly Weather Review 137:4089-4114](https://doi.org/10.1175/2009MWR2835.1).
- The 2019 review groups the remedies into proposal-density (equal-weight) filters, transportation filters, localisation, and hybrids with the EnKF, and reports the first tests in operational-scale systems — [van Leeuwen, Künsch, Nerger, Potthast & Reich 2019, "Particle filters for high-dimensional geoscience applications: A review", QJRMS 145:2335-2365](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3551).
- Localised particle filters: a local PF with spatially varying weights and a merging step; a local PF in LETKF-style domains; the Localized Adaptive Particle Filter run inside the German Weather Service's operational global framework — [Poterjoy 2016, Monthly Weather Review 144:59-76](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-15-0163.1); [Penny & Miyoshi 2016, Nonlinear Processes in Geophysics 23:391-405](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-23-391-2016); [Potthast, Walter & Rhodin 2019, Monthly Weather Review 147:345-362](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-18-0028.1).
- EnKF-PF bridges: a tempering split of the likelihood between an EnKF step and a PF step; the ensemble transform particle filter that replaces resampling by an optimal-transport linear map — [Frei & Künsch 2013, "Bridging the ensemble Kalman and particle filters", Biometrika 100(4):781-800](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/ast020); [Reich 2013, "A nonparametric ensemble transform method for Bayesian inference", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 35(4):A2013-A2024](https://doi.org/10.1137/130907367).
- Tempering link to smoothers: ES-MDA and iterative smoothers can be written as annealed importance sampling with Gaussian approximations at each temperature, which exposes where importance weights or MCMC move steps could be added — [Stordal & Elsheikh 2015, "Iterative ensemble smoothers in the annealed importance sampling framework", Advances in Water Resources 86:231-239](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.09.030).
- Groundwater application: nested particle filters for joint state estimation and online parameter optimisation in a groundwater model — [Ramgraber, Albert & Schirmer 2019, Water Resources Research 55:9724-9747](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018WR024408).

#### Particle flow and mapping filters
- Particles are moved by a deterministic flow in pseudo-time from prior to posterior using kernel-embedded gradients of the log-posterior; a matrix-kernel particle flow filter was reported stable on a 1,000-dimensional Lorenz-96 system with 20 particles — [Pulido & van Leeuwen 2019, "Sequential Monte Carlo with kernel embedded mappings: The mapping particle filter", Journal of Computational Physics 396:400-415](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2019.06.060); [Hu & van Leeuwen 2021, "A particle flow filter for high-dimensional system applications", QJRMS 147:2352-2374](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.4028).
- Failure mode: these flows need the gradient of the log-likelihood with respect to the state or parameters, that is an adjoint or an ensemble approximation of it — [Hu & van Leeuwen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.4028).

#### Gaussian-mixture, cluster and rank-histogram filters
- Gaussian-mixture and kernel filters update each mixture component with a Kalman step and reweight components — [Anderson & Anderson 1999, Monthly Weather Review 127:2741-2758](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1999)127<2741:AMCIOT>2.0.CO;2); [Hoteit, Pham, Triantafyllou & Korres 2008, Monthly Weather Review 136:317-334](https://doi.org/10.1175/2007MWR1927.1); adaptive Gaussian mixture filter for reservoirs in [Stordal, Karlsen, Nævdal, Skaug & Vallès 2011, Computational Geosciences 15:293-305](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-010-9207-1).
- Rank histogram filter: a non-Gaussian scalar update in observation space followed by linear regression onto state variables; generalised in 2022 to 2023 into the quantile-conserving ensemble filter framework (QCEFF), where observation increments are computed with any chosen continuous prior family and regressed in probit/probability-integral-transformed space, which respects bounds and skewness — [Anderson 2010, "A non-Gaussian ensemble filter update for data assimilation", Monthly Weather Review 138:4186-4198](https://doi.org/10.1175/2010MWR3253.1); [Anderson 2022, "A quantile-conserving ensemble filter framework. Part I", Monthly Weather Review 150:1061-1074](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-21-0229.1).
- Groundwater analogues: the iterative local updating ensemble smoother (ILUES) updates each realisation using its neighbours in a combined parameter-data distance and recovers multimodal posteriors in tests against MCMC — [Zhang, Lin, Li, Wu & Zeng 2018, Water Resources Research 54:1716-1733](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR020906). PESTPP-IES's multimodal solution process uses the same local-neighbourhood idea — [PEST++ user manual, section 9.1.14](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].

#### Normal-score / Gaussian anamorphosis
- Marginal transformation of states, parameters or data to Gaussian before the Kalman update originated in ocean-ecosystem DA — [Bertino, Evensen & Wackernagel 2003, International Statistical Review 71:223-241](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1751-5823.2003.tb00194.x); [Simon & Bertino 2009, Ocean Science 5:495-510](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-5-495-2009).
- Groundwater applications: the normal-score EnKF for bimodal conductivity fields, and anamorphosis of data for hydraulic tomography — [Zhou, Gómez-Hernández, Hendricks Franssen & Li 2011, Advances in Water Resources 34:844-864](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.04.014); [Schöniger, Nowak & Hendricks Franssen 2012, Water Resources Research 48:W04502](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010462).
- PEST++/pyEMU status: the DSI workflow has an optional normal-score transform of observations and predictions (`apply_normal_score_transform`, with optional tail extrapolation) — [pyEMU `EnDS.prep_for_dsi`, GMDSI DSI tutorial](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks) [V, local copy].
- Known limit: marginal transforms make marginals Gaussian but leave the multivariate dependence non-Gaussian, so the linear update remains an approximation — [Zhou et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.04.014).

#### Nonlinear ensemble transport and transport-map filters
- The EnKF is the special case of a linear prior-to-posterior transport; replacing it with a sparse, monotone triangular (Knothe-Rosenblatt) map estimated from the joint forecast ensemble of states and synthetic observations gives a consistent nonlinear filter whose bias can be reduced at fixed ensemble size, with localisation expressed as sparsity of the map — [Spantini, Baptista & Marzouk 2022, "Coupling techniques for nonlinear ensemble filtering", SIAM Review 64(4):921-953](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1312204).
- Smoothing versions (ensemble transport smoothers), written by a hydrogeologist-led team, unify EnKS/ES/RTS-type smoothers as linear special cases and add nonlinear updates — [Ramgraber, Baptista, McLaughlin & Marzouk 2023, "Ensemble transport smoothing. Part I: Unified framework", Journal of Computational Physics: X 17:100134](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcpx.2023.100134).
- Map parameterisation and software — [Baptista, Marzouk & Zahm 2023, "On the representation and learning of monotone triangular transport maps", Foundations of Computational Mathematics](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-023-09630-x); [MParT: Monotone Parameterization Toolkit, Journal of Open Source Software 7(80):4843](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.04843).

#### Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD) and projected/ensemble variants
- SVGD transports a set of particles along the kernelised gradient of the KL divergence; it is deterministic and needs gradients of the log-posterior — [Liu & Wang 2016, "Stein variational gradient descent", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04471).
- Newton and projected variants use Hessian information and likelihood-informed subspaces to work in high nominal dimension — [Detommaso, Cui, Marzouk, Spantini & Scheichl 2018, "A Stein variational Newton method", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.03085); [Chen & Ghattas 2020, "Projected Stein variational gradient descent", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03469).
- Failure mode: marginal variances collapse as dimension grows relative to particle number — [Zhuo, Liu, Shi, Zhu, Chen & Zhang 2018, "Message passing Stein variational gradient descent", ICML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04425).
- Geoscience and groundwater applications: SVGD for seismic tomography; SVGD for non-Gaussian parameter inference in hydrogeological models; a derivative-free ensemble SVGD with an adjoint-free (ensemble) gradient and p-kernels for history matching — [Zhang & Curtis 2020, Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth 125:e2019JB018589](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JB018589); [Ramgraber, Weatherl, Blumensaat & Schirmer 2021, Water Resources Research 57:e2020WR029339](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020WR029339); [Stordal, Moraes, Raanes & Evensen 2021, "p-Kernel Stein variational gradient descent for data assimilation and history matching", Mathematical Geosciences 53:375-393](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-021-09937-x).

#### Randomised maximum likelihood and randomise-then-optimise
- RML draws a prior sample and a perturbed observation and minimises the resulting objective; it samples the posterior exactly for linear-Gaussian problems and approximately otherwise — [Kitanidis 1995, "Quasi-linear geostatistical theory for inversing", Water Resources Research 31:2411-2419](https://doi.org/10.1029/95WR01945); [Oliver & Chen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-010-9194-2).
- Randomise-then-optimise (RTO) gives the proposal density of such optimisation-based samples in closed form so that a Metropolis or importance-weight correction can make them exact; scalable randomised-MAP samplers for PDE problems and a weighted RML with improved sampling followed — [Bardsley, Solonen, Haario & Laine 2014, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 36(4):A1895-A1910](https://doi.org/10.1137/140964023); [Wang, Bui-Thanh & Ghattas 2018, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 40(1):A142-A171](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1060625); [Ba, de Wiljes, Oliver & Reich 2022, "Randomized maximum likelihood based posterior sampling", Computational Geosciences 26:217-239](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-021-10100-y).

### Inferences
- The cheapest transfer is on the marginal side: QCEFF-style updates (choose a bounded or skewed prior family per observed quantity, update in probit space) are a superset of normal-score transformation and need only ensemble statistics, so they fit a non-intrusive tool. No groundwater test was found.
- Transport-map smoothers are the theoretically cleanest generalisation of an ensemble smoother, but map estimation needs sparsity (conditional independence) structure; with 10^3 to 10^6 parameters and 100 to 300 realisations the nonlinear terms would have to be confined to a low-dimensional data/latent space, which is exactly the DSI setting.
- RTO-type weights offer a route to correcting IES/NSMC ensembles toward the true posterior, but exact weights need Jacobian determinants, which are unavailable in a Jacobian-free IES; ensemble approximations of those weights are untested at scale.
- SVGD, particle flow and Stein-Newton methods depend on log-posterior gradients; without an adjoint they fall back on ensemble gradients and then offer little beyond IES while adding a variance-collapse risk.

### Gaps
- No published groundwater or reservoir application of QCEFF or of transport-map smoothers at field scale was found.
- Quantitative skill of localised particle filters relative to LETKF in operational tests (Potthast et al. 2019 and later) was not verified in this session.
- Dimension and run counts in Ramgraber et al. (2021, SVGD) and Zhang et al. (2018, ILUES) were not re-checked.

## Key question 3. MCMC for expensive models and high dimensions: what dimensions and run budgets are realistic?

### Takeaway
Derivative-free MCMC (DREAM family, adaptive Metropolis, affine-invariant ensembles) is realistic up to roughly 10^1 to low 10^2 parameters at 10^4 to 10^6 forward runs; function-space samplers (pCN, DILI) and gradient/Hessian samplers (HMC, stochastic Newton) remove the dimension dependence in principle but need either 10^5 to 10^6 runs or adjoints. For models that take minutes to hours, the only MCMC routes that fit a few thousand fine-model runs are delayed-acceptance and multilevel schemes with a cheap coarse model or emulator, applied in a reduced parameter space; their sequential fine-level chain is the main obstacle to using a few hundred parallel workers.

### Cited Findings

#### Derivative-free samplers used in hydrology
- DREAM runs multiple chains with differential-evolution proposals and subspace sampling; DREAM(ZS) samples from an archive so three chains suffice; MT-DREAM(ZS) adds multiple-try proposals for parallel machines — [Vrugt 2016, "Markov chain Monte Carlo simulation using the DREAM software package", Environmental Modelling & Software 75:273-316](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2015.08.013); [Laloy & Vrugt 2012, "High-dimensional posterior exploration of hydrologic models using multiple-try DREAM(ZS) and high-performance computing", Water Resources Research 48:W01526](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010608).
- Adaptive Metropolis and delayed-rejection adaptive Metropolis learn a proposal covariance from the chain history — [Haario, Saksman & Tamminen 2001, Bernoulli 7(2):223-242](https://doi.org/10.2307/3318737); [Haario, Laine, Mira & Saksman 2006, "DRAM: Efficient adaptive MCMC", Statistics and Computing 16:339-354](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-006-9438-0).
- Scaling: random-walk Metropolis needs a number of steps proportional to dimension d, MALA to d^(1/3), HMC to d^(1/4) — [Roberts & Rosenthal 2001, "Optimal scaling for various Metropolis-Hastings algorithms", Statistical Science 16(4):351-367](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1015346320); [Beskos, Pillai, Roberts, Sanz-Serna & Stuart 2013, "Optimal tuning of the hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm", Bernoulli 19(5A):1501-1534](https://doi.org/10.3150/12-BEJ414).
- Groundwater comparison with PEST tooling (2010): DREAM and null-space Monte Carlo were compared on a strongly nonlinear, highly parameterised groundwater model — [Keating, Doherty, Vrugt & Kang 2010, "Optimization and uncertainty assessment of strongly nonlinear groundwater models with high parameter dimensionality", Water Resources Research 46:W10517](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008584).
- Groundwater surrogate-accelerated DREAM: two-stage MCMC with a polynomial chaos surrogate for a 3-D groundwater model; a Kalman-inspired proposal inside DREAM (DREAM(KZS)) to speed convergence — [Laloy, Rogiers, Vrugt, Mallants & Jacques 2013, Water Resources Research 49:2664-2682](https://doi.org/10.1002/wrcr.20226); [Zhang, Vrugt, Shi, Lin, Wu & Zeng 2020, Water Resources Research 56:e2019WR025474](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019WR025474).
- Affine-invariant ensemble samplers (emcee) dominate astrophysics for low-dimensional problems but lose efficiency and can fail to converge as dimension rises to the order of 50 to 100 — [Goodman & Weare 2010, Communications in Applied Mathematics and Computational Science 5(1):65-80](https://doi.org/10.2140/camcos.2010.5.65); [Foreman-Mackey, Hogg, Lang & Goodman 2013, "emcee: The MCMC Hammer", PASP 125:306-312](https://doi.org/10.1086/670067); [Huijser, Goodman & Brewer 2022, "Properties of the affine invariant ensemble sampler's 'stretch move' in high dimensions"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02230).

#### Dimension-robust samplers on function space
- pCN proposals preserve a Gaussian prior, so acceptance rates do not degenerate under mesh refinement — [Cotter, Roberts, Stuart & White 2013, "MCMC methods for functions: modifying old algorithms to make them faster", Statistical Science 28(3):424-446](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-STS421).
- DILI adapts the proposal in a low-dimensional likelihood-informed subspace found from posterior-averaged Gauss-Newton Hessians and uses pCN in the complement; it needs Jacobian or adjoint actions — [Cui, Law & Marzouk 2016, "Dimension-independent likelihood-informed MCMC", Journal of Computational Physics 304:109-137](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2015.10.008); geometric variants in [Beskos, Girolami, Lan, Farrell & Stuart 2017, Journal of Computational Physics 335:327-351](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2016.12.041); multilevel DILI in [Cui, Detommaso & Scheichl 2024, Inverse Problems 40:035005](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12431).
- Groundwater applications: pCN with parallel tempering for multi-Gaussian log-conductivity fields; a sequential pCN hybrid; these produced the 2024 benchmark references, where each scenario of 10,000 parameters required 800,000 MCMC steps per run and four repeated runs — [Xu, Reuschen, Nowak & Hendricks Franssen 2020, Water Resources Research 56:e2020WR027110](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020WR027110); [Reuschen, Jobst & Nowak 2021, Water Resources Research 57:e2021WR030051](https://doi.org/10.1029/2021WR030051); [Xu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-5375-2024) [V].

#### Gradient and Hessian-based samplers
- HMC/NUTS and Riemannian-manifold variants — [Hoffman & Gelman 2014, "The No-U-Turn Sampler", Journal of Machine Learning Research 15:1593-1623](https://jmlr.org/papers/v15/hoffman14a.html); [Girolami & Calderhead 2011, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society B 73(2):123-214](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2010.00765.x); software in [Carpenter et al. 2017, "Stan", Journal of Statistical Software 76(1)](https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v076.i01).
- Geophysical HMC with adjoint gradients: linearised tomography and elastic full-waveform inversion — [Fichtner, Zunino & Gebraad 2019, Geophysical Journal International 216:1344-1363](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggy496); [Gebraad, Boehm & Fichtner 2020, Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth 125:e2019JB018428](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JB018428).
- Stochastic Newton MCMC uses a local Gaussian proposal built from the adjoint gradient and a low-rank Hessian; demonstrated on seismic and ice-sheet inverse problems — [Martin, Wilcox, Burstedde & Ghattas 2012, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 34(3):A1460-A1487](https://doi.org/10.1137/110845598); [Petra, Martin, Stadler & Ghattas 2014, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 36(4):A1525-A1555](https://doi.org/10.1137/130934805).

#### Delayed acceptance, multilevel MCMC and multilevel delayed acceptance (MLDA)
- Two-stage / delayed-acceptance MCMC screens proposals with a cheap approximation and corrects with the fine model, preserving the exact posterior — [Christen & Fox 2005, "Markov chain Monte Carlo using an approximation", Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics 14(4):795-810](https://doi.org/10.1198/106186005X76983); reservoir origin in [Efendiev, Hou & Luo 2006, "Preconditioning Markov chain Monte Carlo simulations using coarse-scale models", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 28(2):776-803](https://doi.org/10.1137/050628568).
- Adaptive delayed acceptance with an adaptive error model, applied to a large-scale 3-D geothermal reservoir model — [Cui, Fox & O'Sullivan 2011, Water Resources Research 47:W10521](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010WR010352).
- Multilevel MCMC for subsurface flow: a hierarchy of grids and KL truncations with a telescoping estimator — [Dodwell, Ketelsen, Scheichl & Teckentrup 2015, "A hierarchical multilevel Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm with applications to uncertainty quantification in subsurface flow", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 3(1):1075-1108](https://doi.org/10.1137/130915005).
- MLDA extends delayed acceptance to any number of levels with subchains of arbitrary length, proves detailed balance, adds multilevel variance reduction and an adaptive Gaussian error model for coarse-level bias. In the subsurface-flow example (64 KL modes of a log-Gaussian conductivity field, 25 head data, three meshes with 5, 17 and 65 points per direction, two chains of 20,000 samples each, subchain lengths 5), the average effective sample size was 326 of 40,000 without the adaptive error model and 1,012 of 40,000 with it; coarse subchains added about 50% to the cost of each fine-level sample. The authors note that the algorithm "must be sequential, which complicates parallelisation considerably" — [Lykkegaard, Dodwell, Fox, Mingas & Scheichl 2023, "Multilevel delayed acceptance MCMC", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 11(1):1-30](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03876) [V, full preprint read].
- Groundwater-flow delayed acceptance with a deep-neural-network first stage — [Lykkegaard, Dodwell & Moxey 2021, "Accelerating uncertainty quantification of groundwater flow modelling using a deep neural network proxy", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 383:113895](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2021.113895).
- Recent extensions (2024 to 2025): a machine-learned coarsest level inside a four-level multigrid MCMC on a groundwater-flow benchmark gave about a twofold speed-up over standard multilevel MCMC at similar accuracy; multi-fidelity delayed acceptance uses neural networks trained offline to fuse heterogeneous coarse solvers and was tested on steady groundwater flow and a reaction-diffusion problem — [Reddy & Fairbanks 2024, arXiv:2405.11179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11179) [V]; [Zacchei, Conti, Frangi & Manzoni 2025, arXiv:2512.16430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16430) [V].
- Local-approximation MCMC builds and refines a local surrogate during sampling while keeping asymptotic exactness; the parallel version cut posterior characterisation from days to hours on a subsurface-hydrology problem and from months to days on a glaciology problem — [Conrad, Davis, Marzouk, Pillai & Smith 2018, "Parallel local approximation MCMC for expensive models", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 6:339-373](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02788) [V].
- Software that makes these non-intrusive: MLDA shipped in PyMC3 from version 3.10 (stated in the paper) and now in tinyDA; UM-Bridge provides a language-agnostic HTTP interface between UQ codes and containerised simulators; MUQ implements multilevel/multi-index MCMC — [tinyDA](https://github.com/mikkelbue/tinyDA); [Seelinger et al. 2023, "UM-Bridge", Journal of Open Source Software 8(83):4748](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.04748); [Seelinger et al. 2025, "Democratizing uncertainty quantification", Journal of Computational Physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13768); [Parno, Davis & Seelinger 2021, "MUQ", Journal of Open Source Software 6(68):3076](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.03076).

#### Sequential Monte Carlo, tempering and nested sampling
- SMC samplers move a weighted population through a sequence of tempered posteriors with resample-move steps; all likelihood evaluations within a stage are independent and hence parallel — [Del Moral, Doucet & Jasra 2006, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society B 68(3):411-436](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2006.00553.x); engineering form (TMCMC) in [Ching & Chen 2007, Journal of Engineering Mechanics 133(7):816-832](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9399(2007)133:7(816)).
- Nested sampling targets the evidence and yields posterior samples as a by-product; MultiNest and PolyChord are the cosmology workhorses, with practical reach of tens to low hundreds of dimensions — [Skilling 2006, Bayesian Analysis 1(4):833-859](https://doi.org/10.1214/06-BA127); [Feroz, Hobson & Bridges 2009, MNRAS 398:1601-1614](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2009.14548.x); [Handley, Hobson & Lasenby 2015, MNRAS 453:4384-4398](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stv1911); reviews in [Ashton et al. 2022, Nature Reviews Methods Primers 2:39](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43586-022-00121-x) and [Buchner 2023, Statistics Surveys 17:169-215](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-SS144).
- Groundwater applications of evidence computation: nested sampling for subsurface flow model selection; a comparison of nine ways to compute Bayesian model evidence against brute-force Monte Carlo — [Elsheikh, Wheeler & Hoteit 2013, Water Resources Research 49:8383-8399](https://doi.org/10.1002/2012WR013406); [Schöniger, Wöhling, Samaniego & Nowak 2014, Water Resources Research 50:9484-9513](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014WR016062).
- Surrogate-enabled MCMC and SMC in a latent space agreed with each other and gave tighter, better-matching posteriors than latent-space ES-MDA for 3-D channelised models (2026) — [Di Federico, Teng & Durlofsky 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140) [V].

### Inferences
- Realistic budgets for a groundwater model with a run time of minutes to hours and a few hundred workers: IES uses roughly 10^3 runs (for example 200 realisations times 3 to 5 iterations); DREAM-type samplers need 10^4 to 10^6 runs and are usable only for 10^1 to 10^2 parameters; pCN-type reference solutions for 10^4 parameters need about 10^6 or more runs even for a 2-D steady model; MLDA can bring fine-model evaluations down to 10^3 to 10^4 if a coarse model with 10 to 100 times lower cost and a reduced parameterisation of fewer than about 100 dimensions are available.
- For PEST users the natural coarse levels already exist: the DSI/PCA emulator, a GPR emulator, a coarsened or steady-state model. A delayed-acceptance chain with the IES posterior ensemble as proposal source (differential-evolution or Kalman-inspired proposals) is non-intrusive and would give an asymptotically exact check on IES for a reduced parameter set.
- The sequential fine-level chain conflicts with "a few hundred parallel runs". Two mitigations appear in the literature: many independent short chains started from IES realisations, and SMC/tempering, where each stage is an embarrassingly parallel batch that maps directly onto a PEST++ run manager.
- HMC, stochastic Newton, DILI and Riemannian methods are not transferable without adjoints; with a MODFLOW 6 adjoint they become candidates, but that sits in the other researcher's scope.
- Nested sampling is the standard tool for Bayes factors between conceptual models in cosmology; for groundwater it is restricted to low-dimensional conceptual-model comparisons with an emulator in the loop.

### Gaps
- Numbers from Keating et al. (2010) (parameter count, runs for DREAM versus NSMC, degree of agreement) could not be retrieved (publisher returned HTTP 403).
- Speed-up factors and dimensions in Cui, Fox & O'Sullivan (2011), Dodwell et al. (2015), Laloy & Vrugt (2012) and Lykkegaard et al. (2021) were not re-verified.
- No field-scale MODFLOW or FEFLOW application of MLDA through UM-Bridge was found; published MLDA groundwater cases are synthetic Darcy problems.

## Key question 4. Multilevel and multifidelity uncertainty propagation and rare-event methods

### Takeaway
For the forward-propagation half of the problem (turning a prior or posterior ensemble into prediction statistics), multilevel and multifidelity Monte Carlo estimators are mature, unbiased with respect to the high-fidelity model, fully non-intrusive, and well matched to parallel batches; they have been demonstrated on Darcy flow since 2011 but are almost unused in applied groundwater practice. Stochastic collocation and polynomial chaos do not reach the 10^3 to 10^6 parameter range, and rare-event samplers are needed whenever the decision depends on exceedance probabilities below about 1%, which IES ensembles of a few hundred cannot resolve.

### Cited Findings
- MLMC: a telescoping sum over a grid hierarchy places most samples on coarse grids; the cost to reach a given root-mean-square error can fall from that of standard Monte Carlo on the finest grid to nearly that of a single fine solve times the variance — [Giles 2008, "Multilevel Monte Carlo path simulation", Operations Research 56(3):607-617](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1070.0496); [Giles 2015, "Multilevel Monte Carlo methods", Acta Numerica 24:259-328](https://doi.org/10.1017/S096249291500001X).
- Groundwater-relevant demonstration (2011): MLMC for elliptic PDEs with lognormal random coefficients (Darcy flow), with large savings for rough, short-correlation-length fields — [Cliffe, Giles, Scheichl & Teckentrup 2011, Computing and Visualization in Science 14:3-15](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00791-011-0160-x).
- Multi-index Monte Carlo generalises the hierarchy to several discretisation parameters — [Haji-Ali, Nobile & Tempone 2016, Numerische Mathematik 132:767-806](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-015-0734-5).
- Multifidelity Monte Carlo uses any set of correlated low-fidelity models (coarse grids, reduced-order models, data-fit surrogates, simplified physics) as control variates with an optimal allocation of samples; the estimator stays unbiased for the high-fidelity mean regardless of low-fidelity quality — [Peherstorfer, Willcox & Gunzburger 2016, "Optimal model management for multifidelity Monte Carlo estimation", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 38(5):A3163-A3194](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1046472); survey of multifidelity propagation, inference and optimisation in [Peherstorfer, Willcox & Gunzburger 2018, SIAM Review 60(3):550-591](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082469).
- Approximate control variates unify MLMC and MFMC and show both are sub-optimal relative to what the model correlations allow; multilevel best linear unbiased estimators give the optimal linear combination for any model set — [Gorodetsky, Geraci, Eldred & Jakeman 2020, "A generalized approximate control variate framework for multifidelity uncertainty quantification", Journal of Computational Physics 408:109257](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2020.109257); [Schaden & Ullmann 2020, "On multilevel best linear unbiased estimators", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 8(2):601-635](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1263534).
- Quasi-Monte Carlo for random-coefficient elliptic PDEs: randomised lattice rules achieve convergence close to O(1/N) with dimension-independent constants under coefficient-decay conditions, and were demonstrated on lognormal Darcy problems with very high stochastic dimension — [Graham, Kuo, Nuyens, Scheichl & Sloan 2011, Journal of Computational Physics 230:3668-3694](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2011.01.023); [Kuo & Nuyens 2016, Foundations of Computational Mathematics 16:1631-1696](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-016-9329-5); multilevel QMC in [Kuo, Scheichl, Schwab, Sloan & Ullmann 2017, Mathematics of Computation 86:2827-2860](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3207).
- Stochastic collocation, sparse grids and polynomial chaos: spectral convergence for smooth, low-dimensional parameter dependence; cost grows rapidly with dimension even with sparsity, and the most recent benchmark of sparse PCE solvers goes to about 100 input dimensions — [Xiu & Karniadakis 2002, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 24(2):619-644](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827501387826); [Babuška, Nobile & Tempone 2007, SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 45(3):1005-1034](https://doi.org/10.1137/050645142); [Nobile, Tempone & Webster 2008, SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 46(5):2309-2345](https://doi.org/10.1137/060663660); [Blatman & Sudret 2011, Journal of Computational Physics 230:2345-2367](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2010.12.021); [Lüthen, Marelli & Sudret 2021, "Sparse polynomial chaos expansions: Literature survey and benchmark", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 9(2):593-649](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1315774).
- Rare events: subset simulation writes a small failure probability as a product of larger conditional probabilities sampled by MCMC; multilevel subset simulation and multifidelity importance sampling combine it with model hierarchies (the multilevel version was demonstrated on a Darcy-type diffusion problem); the cross-entropy method adapts a parametric biasing density, optionally preconditioned with low-fidelity models — [Au & Beck 2001, Probabilistic Engineering Mechanics 16:263-277](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0266-8920(01)00019-4); [Ullmann & Papaioannou 2015, "Multilevel estimation of rare events", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 3(1):922-953](https://doi.org/10.1137/140992953); [Peherstorfer, Cui, Marzouk & Willcox 2016, "Multifidelity importance sampling", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 300:490-509](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2015.12.002); [Peherstorfer, Kramer & Willcox 2018, SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 6(2):737-761](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1122992); [de Boer, Kroese, Mannor & Rubinstein 2005, "A tutorial on the cross-entropy method", Annals of Operations Research 134:19-67](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-005-5724-z).
- Software: Dakota (MLMC, MFMC, ACV, PCE, collocation, reliability methods), UQLab (PCE, Kriging, subset simulation, Bayesian inversion), MUQ, NASA's MXMCPy for ACV allocation — [Dakota](https://dakota.sandia.gov); [UQLab](https://www.uqlab.com); [MUQ](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.03076); [MXMCPy](https://github.com/nasa/MXMCPy).
- Groundwater precedent for pairing a complex and a simple model to correct predictive bias and quantify uncertainty exists in the PEST literature — [Doherty & Christensen 2011, "Use of paired simple and complex models to reduce predictive bias and quantify uncertainty", Water Resources Research 47:W12534](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010763).

### Inferences
- A posterior parameter ensemble from IES can be propagated to expensive predictions (long predictive scenarios, transport, particle tracking) with an MFMC or MLBLUE estimator: run the cheap model for all realisations and the expensive predictive model for a subset chosen by the optimal allocation. This needs only run management and post-processing, so it can sit around PEST++ unchanged.
- Because IES realisations are exchangeable but few, control variates raise the effective sample size for moments and quantiles of predictions without more fine-model runs; this is the most direct way to stabilise 5th/95th percentile estimates from 100 to 300 realisations.
- QMC or Latin-hypercube designs for the prior ensemble are a zero-cost variance reduction for prior predictive statistics and for DSI training sets, but the benefit shrinks when the effective dimension is large and the response is rough.
- PCE, sparse grids and collocation are suitable only after aggressive dimension reduction (tens of parameters) and for smooth outputs; they do not compete with ensemble methods at pilot-point or grid-scale dimensions.
- Decisions framed as "probability that drawdown or concentration exceeds a threshold" at the 1% level or below are outside what IES, NSMC or FOSM deliver reliably: IES lacks samples, FOSM assumes Gaussian tails. Subset simulation driven by a DSI or GPR emulator with a multifidelity importance-sampling correction on the full model is the obvious transfer.

### Gaps
- No published use of MFMC, ACV or MLBLUE for a field-scale groundwater predictive-uncertainty study was found.
- No verified groundwater application of subset simulation or cross-entropy rare-event estimation with a real-world model was found.
- Quantitative speed-ups in Cliffe et al. (2011) and Peherstorfer et al. (2016) were not re-checked in this session.

## Key question 5. Variational and transport-based inference

### Takeaway
The most scalable results in this family (posteriors over 10^5 to 10^6 parameters at a cost independent of dimension) come from Laplace approximations with low-rank Hessians, which are the adjoint-based relatives of FOSM with SVD-assist; likelihood-informed and active subspaces give a rigorous way to choose the reduced space in which more exact samplers can then work. Normalising-flow variational inference, transport maps, tensor trains and diffusion-based posterior samplers are advancing quickly but either need gradients, large simulation budgets, or a learned generative prior, and their calibration for subsurface problems is still being established in 2025 to 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Low-rank Laplace approximation: the posterior covariance is the prior covariance minus a low-rank update built from the dominant eigenpairs of the prior-preconditioned data-misfit Hessian, computed matrix-free with adjoint and incremental-adjoint solves by randomised methods. The number of PDE solves depends on the number of data-informed directions, not on parameter dimension; demonstrations reach about 10^6 parameters in global seismic inversion and Antarctic ice-sheet inversion with propagation to predictions — [Flath, Wilcox, Akçelik, Hill, van Bloemen Waanders & Ghattas 2011, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 33(1):407-432](https://doi.org/10.1137/090780717); [Bui-Thanh, Ghattas, Martin & Stadler 2013, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 35(6):A2494-A2523](https://doi.org/10.1137/12089586X); [Isaac, Petra, Stadler & Ghattas 2015, Journal of Computational Physics 296:348-368](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2015.04.047); software in [Villa, Petra & Ghattas 2021, "hIPPYlib", ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 47(2):16](https://doi.org/10.1145/3428447).
- Optimality: the low-rank update along the leading generalised eigenvectors of the Hessian-prior pencil is the optimal approximation of the Gaussian posterior covariance in natural matrix distances — [Spantini, Solonen, Cui, Martin, Tenorio & Marzouk 2015, "Optimal low-rank approximations of Bayesian linear inverse problems", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 37(6):A2451-A2487](https://doi.org/10.1137/140977308).
- Laplace as a proposal: the Laplace approximation converges to the posterior as noise decreases, and Laplace-based importance sampling and quasi-Monte Carlo remain robust in that limit where prior-based sampling degenerates — [Schillings, Sprungk & Wacker 2020, Numerische Mathematik 145:915-971](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-020-01131-1).
- Likelihood-informed subspaces (LIS): for nonlinear problems the data-informed directions are the dominant eigenvectors of the posterior-expected prior-preconditioned Gauss-Newton Hessian; certified versions bound the KL error of restricting inference to that subspace — [Cui, Martin, Marzouk, Solonen & Spantini 2014, Inverse Problems 30:114015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/30/11/114015); [Zahm, Cui, Law, Spantini & Marzouk 2022, "Certified dimension reduction in nonlinear Bayesian inverse problems", Mathematics of Computation 91:1789-1835](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3737).
- Active subspaces: eigenvectors of the average outer product of output gradients; used to accelerate MCMC, and applied to subsurface-flow models for global sensitivity analysis and adaptive sampling of behavioural parameter sets — [Constantine, Dow & Wang 2014, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 36(4):A1500-A1524](https://doi.org/10.1137/130916138); [Constantine, Kent & Bui-Thanh 2016, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 38(5):A2779-A2805](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1042127); [Erdal & Cirpka 2019, Hydrology and Earth System Sciences 23:3787-3805](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-23-3787-2019).
- Variational inference with normalising flows: an invertible neural map is optimised to minimise KL divergence to the posterior; needs gradients of the log-posterior and, like mean-field VI, tends to under-represent posterior variance and tails when the family is too restrictive — [Rezende & Mohamed 2015, ICML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05770); [Papamakarios, Nalisnick, Rezende, Mohamed & Lakshminarayanan 2021, Journal of Machine Learning Research 22(57)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02762); [Blei, Kucukelbir & McAuliffe 2017, "Variational inference: A review for statisticians", JASA 112:859-877](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2017.1285773).
- Geophysical applications of flow-based VI and amortised VI with correction: seismic tomography with normalising flows; amortised posterior networks corrected by a physics-based latent distribution — [Zhao, Curtis & Zhang 2022, Geophysical Journal International 228:213-239](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggab298); [Siahkoohi, Rizzuti, Orozco & Herrmann 2023, Geophysics 88(3):R297-R322](https://doi.org/10.1190/geo2022-0472.1).
- Measure transport: a deterministic triangular map pushes a reference (or the prior) to the posterior; it can be computed variationally from the unnormalised density, used to precondition MCMC, or built greedily in low-dimensional "lazy" layers — [El Moselhy & Marzouk 2012, "Bayesian inference with optimal maps", Journal of Computational Physics 231:7815-7850](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2012.07.022); [Marzouk, Moselhy, Parno & Spantini 2016, "Sampling via measure transport: An introduction"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05023); [Parno & Marzouk 2018, "Transport map accelerated Markov chain Monte Carlo", SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 6(2):645-682](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1134640); [Brennan, Bigoni, Zahm, Spantini & Marzouk 2020, "Greedy inference with structure-exploiting lazy maps", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00031).
- Tensor-train posterior approximations: the unnormalised density is approximated in tensor-train format by cross approximation and sampled through an inverse Rosenblatt transport; deep compositions of such maps (DIRT) handle concentrated posteriors — [Dolgov, Anaya-Izquierdo, Fox & Scheichl 2020, Statistics and Computing 30:603-625](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-019-09910-z); [Cui & Dolgov 2022, Foundations of Computational Mathematics 22:1863-1922](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-021-09537-5).
- Score-based and diffusion posterior sampling: a diffusion model trained on prior samples supplies the prior score, and the likelihood score is approximated during the reverse process (diffusion posterior sampling); score-based data assimilation applies this to trajectories; a training-free ensemble score filter tracked Lorenz-96 systems of up to 10^6 dimensions with strongly nonlinear observations and was reported to outperform LETKF — [Song et al. 2021, ICLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456); [Chung, Kim, McCann, Klasky & Ye 2023, "Diffusion posterior sampling for general noisy inverse problems", ICLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687); [Rozet & Louppe 2023, "Score-based data assimilation", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10574); [Bao, Zhang & Zhang 2024, "An ensemble score filter for tracking high-dimensional nonlinear dynamical systems"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00983) [V]; survey in [Daras et al. 2024, "A survey on diffusion models for inverse problems"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00083).
- Subsurface status 2025 to 2026: a corrected diffusion-posterior-sampling likelihood approximation for multivariate subsurface inversion; function-space decoupled diffusion posterior sampling for CO2-storage flow with sparse observations; and the finding that latent-diffusion parameterisations combined with ES-MDA can misstate posterior uncertainty relative to MCMC/SMC — ["Diffusion models for multivariate subsurface generation and efficient probabilistic inversion", arXiv:2507.15809 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15809v1) [V, search summary]; [Fun-DDPS, arXiv:2602.12274 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12274) [V, search summary]; [Di Federico, Teng & Durlofsky 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140) [V].

### Inferences
- FOSM with an SVD-assisted Jacobian is the finite-difference, explicit-matrix version of the low-rank Laplace approximation. The transferable ideas are (a) randomised, matrix-free computation of the dominant data-informed subspace, which with an adjoint makes the cost independent of parameter count, and (b) using the Laplace/FOSM posterior as an importance or MCMC proposal so that the linear-Gaussian result can be corrected instead of merely reported.
- The LIS is the rigorous counterpart of PEST's solution space. Estimating it from an ensemble (ensemble-averaged Jacobian approximations or the cross-covariances IES already forms) would tell a user how many realisations are needed (the PEST++ rule of thumb says more than the solution-space dimension) and would define the reduced space for MLDA, SMC or CES.
- Flow-based VI, transport maps and tensor trains need many density or gradient evaluations and are plausible only on an emulator or in a reduced space of roughly 10 to 100 dimensions.
- Diffusion posterior sampling shifts the burden to a trained prior and still needs likelihood gradients or ensemble surrogates of them; it belongs with the generative-prior topic handled by another researcher. The 2026 evidence that ensemble Kalman updates in learned latent spaces can be miscalibrated is directly relevant to any PEST++ workflow that wraps IES around a learned parameterisation.

### Gaps
- No groundwater study was found that estimates a likelihood-informed subspace and uses it to size an IES ensemble or to run reduced-space MCMC.
- Parameter counts and solve counts in Bui-Thanh et al. (2013) and Isaac et al. (2015) are quoted as orders of magnitude; exact figures were not re-verified.
- Authors and full results of arXiv:2507.15809 and arXiv:2602.12274 were seen only through search summaries.

## Key question 6. Simulation-based (likelihood-free) inference and its relation to data space inversion, Bayesian evidential learning and direct forecasting

### Takeaway
DSI and BEL are linear-Gaussian (after transformation) members of the same family as neural posterior estimation: all learn the relationship between simulated data and a target directly from a prior ensemble and never invert for parameters. Neural SBI removes the linear-Gaussian assumption and brings formal calibration diagnostics, but its demonstrated reach is tens of parameters or prediction summaries with 10^3 to 10^6 simulations, and it is known to be overconfident under small budgets and under model misspecification; for groundwater budgets of 10^2 to 10^3 runs the realistic transfers are the diagnostics, ensembling, and nonlinear low-dimensional prediction-space estimators.

### Cited Findings

#### Approximate Bayesian computation
- ABC accepts prior draws whose simulated summary statistics fall within a tolerance of the observed ones; regression adjustment and sequential (SMC/PMC) versions improve efficiency — [Beaumont, Zhang & Balding 2002, Genetics 162:2025-2035](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/162.4.2025); [Sisson, Fan & Tanaka 2007, PNAS 104:1760-1765](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0607208104); [Toni, Welch, Strelkowa, Ipsen & Stumpf 2009, Journal of the Royal Society Interface 6:187-202](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2008.0172).
- Hydrology: ABC as diagnostic model evaluation with signature-based summaries, and DREAM(ABC) — [Vrugt & Sadegh 2013, Water Resources Research 49:4335-4345](https://doi.org/10.1002/wrcr.20354); [Sadegh & Vrugt 2014, Water Resources Research 50:6767-6787](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014WR015386).
- Groundwater: emulator-enabled ABC for computationally expensive regional groundwater models (CSIRO) — [Cui, Peeters, Pagendam, Pickett, Jin, Crosbie, Raiber, Rassam & Gilfedder 2018, Journal of Hydrology 564:191-207](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2018.07.005).
- PEST/pyEMU already demonstrates rejection sampling on the DSI emulator, which is ABC with the emulator as simulator — [GMDSI notebooks, intro_to_eva_and_dsi, "Rejection sampling with DSI"](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks) [V, local copy].

#### Neural posterior, likelihood and ratio estimation
- Overview of the field and its three neural targets (posterior, likelihood, likelihood ratio), with amortised and sequential variants — [Cranmer, Brehmer & Louppe 2020, "The frontier of simulation-based inference", PNAS 117(48):30055-30062](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912789117).
- Key algorithms: SNPE-A, SNPE-C/APT, sequential neural likelihood, neural ratio estimation with amortised MCMC, truncated sequential proposals (TSNPE, TMNRE), flow-matching posterior estimation, and transformer-based "all-in-one" estimators that condition on arbitrary subsets of parameters and data — [Papamakarios & Murray 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06376); [Greenberg, Nonnenmacher & Macke 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07488); [Papamakarios, Sterratt & Murray 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07226); [Hermans, Begy & Louppe 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04057); [Deistler, Gonçalves & Macke 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04815); [Miller, Cole, Forré, Louppe & Weniger 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01214); [Wildberger et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17161); [Gloeckler et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09636).
- Handling failed simulations: a classifier can be trained to predict which parameter sets produce invalid runs so that they are excluded from proposals rather than discarded silently — [Lueckmann et al. 2017, "Flexible statistical inference for mechanistic models of neural dynamics", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01861).
- Software and guidance: the `sbi` toolkit (2020; updated workflow paper 2025), BayesFlow for amortised workflows, and a 2025 practical guide covering simulator and prior set-up, network choice, training, inference and validation with diagnostics at each stage — [Tejero-Cantero et al. 2020, Journal of Open Source Software 5(52):2505](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.02505); [Boelts et al. 2025, "sbi reloaded"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17337); [Radev et al. 2023, "BayesFlow: Amortized Bayesian workflows with neural networks", Journal of Open Source Software 8(89):5702](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.05702); [Deistler et al. 2025, "Simulation-based inference: A practical guide"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12939) [V]; statistics-side review in [Zammit-Mangion, Sainsbury-Dale & Huser 2025, "Neural methods for amortized inference", Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12484).
- Benchmark evidence (2021): neural methods generally beat classical ABC, sequential estimation improves sample efficiency, no algorithm is uniformly best, the choice of metric is critical, and "even state-of-the-art algorithms have substantial room for improvement" — [Lueckmann, Boelts, Greenberg, Gonçalves & Macke 2021, "Benchmarking simulation-based inference", AISTATS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04653) [V].
- Reliability evidence (2022): across NPE, NRE, SNL and ABC variants, all benchmarked algorithms can produce overconfident posteriors; ensembles of posterior estimators are more reliable — [Hermans, Delaunoy, Rozet, Wehenkel, Begy & Louppe 2022, "A trust crisis in simulation-based inference? Your posterior approximations can be unfaithful", TMLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581) [V]; conservative training in [Delaunoy et al. 2022, "Towards reliable simulation-based inference with balanced neural ratio estimation", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13624).
- Misspecification: neural SBI can fail badly when the observed data lie outside the simulator's range; detection and robustification methods exist — [Cannon, Ward & Schmon 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.01845); [Ward, Cannon, Beaumont, Fasiolo & Schmon 2022, "Robust neural posterior estimation and statistical model criticism", NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06564); [Schmitt, Bürkner, Köthe & Radev 2023, "Detecting model misspecification in amortized Bayesian inference with neural networks"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08866).
- Scale in physics: gravitational-wave parameter estimation with amortised NPE (15 source parameters, trained on millions of simulated waveforms) followed by importance sampling against the true likelihood to restore exactness and provide an efficiency diagnostic; cosmology with learned compression plus density estimation and active learning — [Dax et al. 2021, Physical Review Letters 127:241103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12594); [Dax et al. 2023, "Neural importance sampling for rapid and reliable gravitational-wave inference", Physical Review Letters 130:171403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05686); [Alsing, Charnock, Feeney & Wandelt 2019, MNRAS 488:4440-4458](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00007).

#### Hydrology and groundwater applications of neural SBI
- Watershed simulator (ParFlow-CLM, Taylor River catchment, 1 km resolution): two scalar parameters (Manning's coefficient and hydraulic conductivity) were inferred by neural density estimation using an LSTM surrogate trained on 183 ParFlow runs and 5,000 surrogate simulations. When the surrogate generated the observations, inference was accurate; when ParFlow did, posteriors were overconfident and biased (Mahalanobis distance about 7 versus 0.3), and an ensemble of 10 surrogates or performance weighting reduced this to about 1 at the cost of precision — [Hull, Leonarduzzi, De La Fuente, Tran, Bennett, Melchior, Maxwell & Condon 2024, "Simulation-based inference for parameter estimation of complex watershed simulators", Hydrology and Earth System Sciences 28:4685-4713](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-4685-2024) [V].
- Darcy-flow conductivity estimation with a variational autoencoder for feature extraction and a normalising-flow neural likelihood sampled by differential-evolution MCMC (2026 preprint); the authors report less overconfident estimates under model-reality mismatch — [Bottacini, Torzoni & Manzoni 2026, "Neural Markov chain Monte Carlo", arXiv:2602.19597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19597) [V].
- Amortised conditional normalising flows as the analysis step of a sequential data-assimilation system for CO2 storage monitoring — [Gahlot, Orozco, Yin, Bruer & Herrmann 2024/2025, "An uncertainty-aware digital shadow for underground multimodal CO2 storage monitoring"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01218).

#### Data space inversion, Bayesian evidential learning and direct forecasting
- Prediction-focused analysis and direct forecasting: canonical correlation between dimension-reduced data and prediction variables from a prior ensemble, then linear-Gaussian regression in canonical space — [Scheidt, Renard & Caers 2015, Mathematical Geosciences 47:173-191](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-014-9521-6); [Satija & Caers 2015, Advances in Water Resources 77:69-81](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.01.002).
- BEL as a protocol (prior definition, prior falsification, sensitivity analysis, dimension reduction, statistical learning of the data-prediction relationship, posterior falsification) with groundwater and geothermal applications including experimental design for wellhead protection — [Scheidt, Li & Caers 2018, "Quantifying Uncertainty in Subsurface Systems", AGU/Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119325888); [Hermans, Nguyen, Klepikova, Dassargues & Caers 2018, Water Resources Research 54:2931-2948](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR022135); [Thibaut, Laloy & Hermans 2021, Journal of Hydrology 603:126903](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2021.126903).
- DSI: posterior predictions are sampled by conditioning a PCA-parameterised data vector (history plus forecast) on observed history, originally with RML and later with ES-MDA — [Sun & Durlofsky 2017, "A new data-space inversion procedure for efficient uncertainty quantification in subsurface flow problems", Mathematical Geosciences 49:679-715](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-016-9672-8); [Lima, Emerick & Ortiz 2020, "Data-space inversion with ensemble smoother", Computational Geosciences 24:1179-1200](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-020-09933-w).
- Groundwater DSI with PEST/PEST++: for a HydroGeoSphere stream-aquifer model (about 10 minutes per run, 204,000 parameters, 760 head observations), DSI used only the 100 prior runs, retained 87 singular values at 0.999 energy, and reached objective functions of 814 to 830 after six emulator-IES iterations; its posterior for infiltration was "very similar" to the IES posterior and its travel-time uncertainty was lower. Stated limits: sampling error from 100 realisations, reliance on Gaussianity, and no access to the parameter fields behind predictive extremes — [Delottier, Doherty & Brunner 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023) [V].
- pyEMU status: the DSI machinery was moved onto a pure-Python worker (1.3.8), an emulators module was added (1.4.0), and release 1.7.0 lists a DSI autoencoder ("DSI-AE"), DSI pattern support and a partial-least-squares emulator; earlier releases added Gaussian-process-regression emulation helpers — [pyEMU releases](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/releases) [V].

#### Diagnostics developed for SBI
- Simulation-based calibration: for parameters drawn from the prior and data simulated from them, the rank of the true value among posterior draws must be uniform; deviations diagnose over- and under-dispersion and bias — [Talts, Betancourt, Simpson, Vehtari & Gelman 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788); earlier form in [Cook, Gelman & Rubin 2006, Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics 15:675-692](https://doi.org/10.1198/106186006X136976).
- Expected coverage tests and the sample-only TARP test, which checks joint (not just marginal) coverage without density evaluation — [Hermans et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581) [V]; [Lemos, Coogan, Hezaveh & Perreault-Levasseur 2023, "Sampling-based accuracy testing of posterior estimators for general inference", ICML](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03026).

### Inferences
- Mapping between vocabularies: DSI is amortisable posterior estimation of predictions with a linear-Gaussian conditional density in PCA space; BEL is the same with CCA and optional kernel density estimation; NPE replaces the linear-Gaussian map with a conditional normalising flow. DSI conditioned by IES on the emulator is closer to "sequential neural likelihood plus sampler" than to amortised NPE, because the emulator is a (linear) likelihood surrogate.
- Scaling: SBI cost is governed by the dimension of the target and of the data summary, not of the nuisance parameters. A 10^6-parameter groundwater model is therefore not an obstacle if the target is a handful of predictions, exactly the DSI argument. The obstacle is the training-set size: published neural SBI uses 10^3 to 10^6 simulations, against 10^2 to 10^3 affordable groundwater runs. With a few hundred runs, linear or mildly nonlinear estimators (DSI, PLS, DSI-AE, GPR on PCA scores) are the appropriate capacity.
- The transferable SBI assets at groundwater budgets are procedural: (a) SBC and coverage tests can be run on DSI at negligible cost by holding out prior realisations as synthetic truths; (b) ensembling several emulators or estimators reduces overconfidence (shown both in the SBI literature and in the ParFlow study); (c) misspecification checks ask whether the observed data lie inside the simulated data cloud, which is the same question as BEL prior falsification and PEST++ prior-data conflict detection; (d) importance sampling against the real model (as in gravitational-wave work) is a way to correct and score an amortised estimate when some extra runs are affordable.
- Amortisation matters for data-worth and monitoring-design studies: once trained, an estimator returns the posterior for any hypothetical data set, which is what DSI-based data-worth analysis in Delottier et al. exploits in linear form.

### Gaps
- No verified study was found that compares NPE/NLE against DSI, IES or MCMC on the same groundwater model with a fixed simulation budget.
- The original paper behind the "amortized likelihood-free inference with noisy and incomplete data" log-conductivity study surfaced only through a secondary summary site and was not verified.
- Training-set sizes typical of BEL and DSI studies (Sun & Durlofsky 2017; Hermans et al. 2018) were not re-checked.
- The release year of pyEMU 1.7.0 was not shown on the page read.

## Key question 7. Emulator-based calibration from statistics

### Takeaway
The statistics community offers two things groundwater practice largely lacks: an explicit model-discrepancy term in calibration (Kennedy-O'Hagan), and Bayes linear history matching, which does not seek a posterior at all but iteratively rules out implausible regions of input space using cheap emulators and an implausibility measure that adds observation, emulator and structural variances. History matching in this sense is proven on expensive simulators with 20 to 30 inputs in cosmology, climate, epidemiology and reservoirs, is non-intrusive and tolerant of failed runs, and suits the low-dimensional global and structural parameters of a groundwater model as a precursor to IES.

### Cited Findings
- Gaussian-process emulation of deterministic simulators and the Bayesian calibration framework that models reality as simulator output plus a GP discrepancy plus observation error — [Sacks, Welch, Mitchell & Wynn 1989, "Design and analysis of computer experiments", Statistical Science 4(4):409-423](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1177012413); [Kennedy & O'Hagan 2001, "Bayesian calibration of computer models", Journal of the Royal Statistical Society B 63(3):425-464](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294).
- High-dimensional outputs are handled by emulating principal-component coefficients — [Higdon, Gattiker, Williams & Rightley 2008, "Computer model calibration using high-dimensional output", JASA 103:570-583](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214507000000888).
- Ignoring discrepancy gives over-confident, biased parameters; including it gives better predictions but the physical parameters are identifiable only with informative priors on the discrepancy — [Brynjarsdóttir & O'Hagan 2014, "Learning about physical parameters: the importance of model discrepancy", Inverse Problems 30:114007](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/30/11/114007).
- Design rule of thumb: about 10 runs per input dimension for an initial GP design — [Loeppky, Sacks & Welch 2009, "Choosing the sample size of a computer experiment: A practical guide", Technometrics 51(4):366-376](https://doi.org/10.1198/TECH.2009.08040).
- Bayes linear history matching originated in reservoir pressure matching (1997) and was developed into the multi-wave implausibility method for the Galform galaxy-formation model — [Craig, Goldstein, Seheult & Smith 1997, "Pressure matching for hydrocarbon reservoirs: A case study in the use of Bayes linear strategies for large computer experiments", Case Studies in Bayesian Statistics III](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-2290-3_2); [Vernon, Goldstein & Bower 2010, "Galaxy formation: a Bayesian uncertainty analysis", Bayesian Analysis 5(4):619-669](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-BA524); summary in [Vernon, Goldstein & Bower 2014, Statistical Science 29(1):81-90](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4976) [V].
- The implausibility measure divides the squared difference between observation and emulator expectation by the sum of emulator, observation and model-discrepancy variances; waves of new runs in the current not-ruled-out-yet region refit the emulators and shrink that region; a cutoff of 3 follows Pukelsheim's three-sigma rule — [Iskauskas et al. 2024, "Emulation and history matching using the hmer package", Journal of Statistical Software](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05265) [V, search summary]; [Andrianakis et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003968) [V].
- Epidemiology: a stochastic individual-based HIV model with 22 inputs was matched to 18 outputs over 9 waves, starting from a 220-point Latin hypercube and using 500 runs per wave from wave 4; runs in the final region had a 65% probability of matching all 18 outputs — [Andrianakis, Vernon, McCreesh, McKinley, Oakley, Nsubuga, Goldstein & White 2015, "Bayesian history matching of complex infectious disease models using emulation: a tutorial and a case study on HIV in Uganda", PLoS Computational Biology 11(1):e1003968](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003968) [V].
- Climate: history matching of a perturbed-physics ensemble to rule out parameter space; calibration-optimal bases for spatial output; the "High-Tune" tool for parameterisation development — [Williamson, Goldstein, Allison, Blaker, Challenor, Jackson & Yamazaki 2013, Climate Dynamics 41:1703-1729](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00382-013-1896-4); [Salter, Williamson, Scinocca & Kharin 2019, JASA 114:1800-1814](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2018.1514306); [Couvreux et al. 2021, Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems 13:e2020MS002217](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020MS002217).
- Systems biology and reservoirs: history matching for global parameter searches, and Bayesian emulation with explicit model discrepancy for reservoir models with many inputs and outputs — [Vernon et al. 2018, BMC Systems Biology 12:1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12918-017-0484-3); [Nandi Formentin et al. 2020, "Accounting for model discrepancy in uncertainty analysis by combining numerical simulation and Bayesian emulation techniques", ECMOR XVII](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1134224) [V, search summary].
- Automation: a semi-automatic SMC algorithm for history matching finds that the non-implausible region "can be multi-modal, highly irregular, and hard to sample uniformly" and samples it more reliably at extra cost — [Drovandi, Nott & Pagendam 2021, SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03133) [V].
- Groundwater applications of K-O'H-style discrepancy modelling and of emulator-based preselection: data-driven structural-error models added to groundwater calibration, including a regional model with surrogates; GP emulation with active subspaces to sample behavioural parameter sets of a subsurface-flow model — [Xu & Valocchi 2015, Water Resources Research 51:9290-9311](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR017912); [Xu, Valocchi, Ye & Liang 2017, Water Resources Research 53:4084-4105](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR019831); [Erdal & Cirpka 2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-23-3787-2019). Surrogate reviews for water resources: [Razavi, Tolson & Burn 2012, Water Resources Research 48:W07401](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR011527); [Asher, Croke, Jakeman & Peeters 2015, Water Resources Research 51:5957-5973](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR016967).
- Bayesian-optimisation-style adaptive design: expected-improvement sequential design; its usual domain is expensive functions of fewer than about 20 inputs; variants target the posterior or the likelihood rather than an optimum (BOLFI, parallel GP-surrogate inference, variational Bayesian Monte Carlo); trust-region methods extend the dimension reach — [Jones, Schonlau & Welch 1998, Journal of Global Optimization 13:455-492](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008306431147); [Frazier 2018, "A tutorial on Bayesian optimization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02811); [Gutmann & Corander 2016, Journal of Machine Learning Research 17(125)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-017.html); [Järvenpää, Gutmann, Vehtari & Marttinen 2021, Bayesian Analysis 16(1):147-178](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-BA1200); [Acerbi 2018, NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05558); [Eriksson, Pearce, Gardner, Turner & Poloczek 2019, NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01739).
- Deep GP and neural emulators with uncertainty: deep GPs for non-stationary responses with active learning; stochastic-imputation deep GP emulation (dgpsi); deep ensembles as a simple predictive-uncertainty baseline — [Damianou & Lawrence 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.0358); [Sauer, Gramacy & Higdon 2023, Technometrics 65(1):4-18](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.2021.2008505); [Ming, Williamson & Guillas 2023, Technometrics 65(2):150-161](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.2022.2124311); [Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell 2017, NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474).
- PEST++/pyEMU status: GPR emulation helpers (`prep_for_gpr`) and a pure-Python worker exist in pyEMU, aimed at replacing the model inside PEST++ tools — [pyEMU releases](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/releases) [V].

### Inferences
- History matching and IES answer different questions. IES returns an approximate posterior sample conditional on the prior and noise model being right; history matching returns the set of inputs not yet contradicted by data given all acknowledged uncertainties, and can return the empty set, which is an explicit signal of structural inadequacy that IES expresses only indirectly through poor fits or prior-data conflict.
- The implausibility denominator is where a groundwater modeller's "structural noise" would enter as a number. Compared with weighting for visibility, it forces a statement of how wrong the model is allowed to be for each output.
- Reach: GP emulators need on the order of 10 runs per active input per wave, so history matching is suited to 10 to 50 global, zonal or hyperparameter inputs, not to pilot-point fields. A hybrid is natural: history-match the low-dimensional inputs with spatial heterogeneity treated as stochastic simulator noise (as in the stochastic HIV model), then run IES inside the non-implausible region.
- Failed runs are handled gracefully: failures are simply absent training points, and a classifier or emulator of failure can be used to avoid regions where the model crashes.
- CES (key question 1) and K-O'H calibration share the emulator-plus-MCMC structure; CES differs by placing training points with an ensemble Kalman method, which is what a PEST++ user gets for free from an IES run.

### Gaps
- No publication applying Bayes linear history matching with implausibility waves to a groundwater flow or transport model was found in the searches that were possible; this may reflect the truncated search.
- The application model, dimensions and run counts in Drovandi, Nott & Pagendam (2021) are not stated in the abstract and were not checked in the full text.
- Number of Galform inputs, waves and volume-reduction factors in Vernon et al. (2010), and the volume-reduction factor in Andrianakis et al. (2015), were not confirmed in machine-readable form.

## Key question 8. Diagnostics and validation of uncertainty estimates

### Takeaway
NWP and SBI have standard, cheap tests of whether an ensemble is statistically reliable (rank histograms, spread-skill consistency, CRPS, simulation-based calibration, coverage tests), and statistics has formal prior-data conflict and posterior predictive checks; groundwater practice uses almost none of them routinely, and published comparisons of ensemble smoothers against reference posteriors are few, small and do not include PESTPP-IES. The 2024 HESS benchmark now provides open reference posteriors against which this can be done.

### Cited Findings
- Proper scoring rules: a score is proper if the forecaster's expected score is optimised by reporting the true belief; the CRPS is the standard proper score for scalar ensemble forecasts and decomposes into reliability and resolution parts — [Gneiting & Raftery 2007, "Strictly proper scoring rules, prediction, and estimation", JASA 102:359-378](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437); [Hersbach 2000, Weather and Forecasting 15:559-570](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0434(2000)015<0559:DOTCRP>2.0.CO;2).
- Rank histograms: if truth and ensemble members are exchangeable the rank of the truth is uniform; U-shapes indicate under-dispersion, domes over-dispersion, slopes bias, and flat histograms can still hide conditional biases — [Hamill 2001, "Interpretation of rank histograms for verifying ensemble forecasts", Monthly Weather Review 129:550-560](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(2001)129<0550:IORHFV>2.0.CO;2).
- Spread-skill consistency: ensemble spread should match the RMSE of the ensemble mean after a finite-ensemble correction — [Fortin, Abaza, Anctil & Turcotte 2014, Journal of Hydrometeorology 15:1708-1713](https://doi.org/10.1175/JHM-D-14-0008.1).
- Observation-space consistency diagnostics estimate whether assumed observation and background error covariances are consistent with innovation statistics — [Desroziers, Berre, Chapnik & Poli 2005, QJRMS 131:3385-3396](https://doi.org/10.1256/qj.05.108).
- Simulation-based calibration and coverage tests for any posterior approximation (see key question 6) — [Talts et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788); [Lemos et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03026); [Hermans et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581) [V].
- Bayesian workflow: prior predictive checks, posterior predictive checks, convergence diagnostics (rank-normalised split R-hat and effective sample size) — [Gelman et al. 2020, "Bayesian workflow"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01808); [Vehtari, Gelman, Simpson, Carpenter & Bürkner 2021, Bayesian Analysis 16(2):667-718](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-BA1221).
- Prior-data conflict: a formal check compares the observed value of a minimal sufficient statistic with its prior predictive distribution — [Evans & Moshonov 2006, "Checking for prior-data conflict", Bayesian Analysis 1(4):893-914](https://doi.org/10.1214/06-BA129). PESTPP-IES flags observations whose prior simulated distribution does not overlap the observed value plus noise within `ies_pdc_sigma_distance` standard deviations and can drop them — [PEST++ user manual, section 9.1.13](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V]. BEL's prior falsification asks the multivariate version of the same question in reduced data space — [Scheidt, Li & Caers 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119325888); automated in [Yin, Strebelle & Caers 2020, "AutoBEL v1.0", Geoscientific Model Development 13:651-672](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-13-651-2020).
- Model-error diagnosis in history matching: calibrating imperfect models to biased observations, with residual analysis used to build a total-error covariance iteratively — [Oliver & Alfonzo 2018, "Calibration of imperfect models to biased observations", Computational Geosciences 22:145-161](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9678-4); groundwater antecedent on structural noise in [Doherty & Welter 2010, "A short exploration of structural noise", Water Resources Research 46:W05525](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008377).
- Benchmark comparisons against reference posteriors: reservoir toy model with long-chain MCMC (2013) — [Emerick & Reynolds 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-012-9333-z) [V, abstract]; Gaussian approximations versus function-space MCMC (2013) — [Iglesias, Law & Stuart 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1779) [V]; regularised iterative smoothers versus MCMC (2014) — [Iglesias 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.5375) [V]; categorical fields, where ES-MDA, PoPEx and DREAM-ZS were compared and prior choice strongly affected results — [Juda, Renard & Straubhaar 2023, "Comparison of three recent discrete stochastic inversion methods and influence of the prior choice", Comptes Rendus Géoscience 355(S1)](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/item/10.5802/crgeos.160.pdf) [V, search summary]; ILUES versus MCMC for multimodal posteriors — [Zhang et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR020906).
- Open groundwater benchmark with accuracy metrics (normalised MAE and RMSE, averaged Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance, normalised energy distance), cost metrics (number of forward calls, overhead, parallel-efficiency loss) and descriptors of intrusiveness and reproducibility; reference data and codes are public — [Xu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-5375-2024) [V].
- The 2024 benchmark paper notes that earlier comparisons mostly tested against a synthetic truth rather than an accurate inverse solution, so estimated post-calibration variances could not be evaluated properly — [Xu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-5375-2024) [V, search summary].

### Inferences
- A single synthetic truth falling inside a posterior ensemble says almost nothing about calibration. The SBC protocol (many synthetic truths drawn from the prior, rank statistics of each truth in its own posterior) is the correct test. For full IES this costs one IES run per synthetic truth and is rarely affordable; for DSI, GPR or any emulator-based posterior it is nearly free because held-out prior realisations serve as truths.
- For real-data studies the usable checks are predictive: hold out observations (later time periods, other wells) and score the posterior ensemble with CRPS and rank histograms. This is standard in NWP and requires only post-processing of PEST++ ensemble output.
- The four HESS benchmark metrics could be adopted as a regression suite for PESTPP-IES options (localisation, reinflation, multimodal, MDA versus GLM), filling the main gap identified under key question 1.
- Prior-data conflict detection in PEST++ is marginal (one observation at a time); the multivariate Mahalanobis or one-class tests used in BEL and SBI misspecification work would catch conflicts in patterns (gradients, trends) that marginal tests miss.

### Gaps
- No published SBC or coverage study of PESTPP-IES, NSMC or PEST++ DSI was found.
- Table 5 of Xu et al. (2024) (per-metric EnKF scores) was not extracted; only the 0.3 to 0.4 range in the text was seen.
- Whether any group has yet run an iterative ensemble smoother on the HESS 2024 benchmark was not established.

## Synthesis question. Which transfers are most promising for groundwater history matching and uncertainty analysis, and which can be implemented non-intrusively around existing PEST++ tooling?

### Takeaway
The best value lies in methods that reuse runs a PEST++ workflow already makes: calibration diagnostics, multifidelity estimators for prediction statistics, variance-preserving refinements of the IES update, and emulator-based exact samplers or history matching for the low-dimensional part of the problem. Methods that need log-posterior gradients, or more than about 10^4 fine-model runs, or training sets of 10^4 or more simulations, are not transferable to models with run times of minutes to hours unless an adjoint or a trustworthy emulator is available.

### Cited Findings
- Constraints that drive the ranking: IES cost scales with ensemble size, not parameter count, and PESTPP-IES tolerates failed runs and drops poorly performing realisations — [White 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2018.06.009); [GMDSI notebooks, IES basics tutorial on `ies_bad_phi` and `ies_bad_phi_sigma`](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks) [V, local copy].
- Evidence that small ensembles misstate variance in groundwater-type problems: collapse at 100 members versus acceptable behaviour at 1,000 in the 10,000-parameter benchmark — [Xu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-5375-2024) [V].
- Evidence that emulator-plus-sampler routes reach a Bayesian posterior in O(10^2) model runs for low-dimensional parameters — [Dunbar et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13262) [V].
- Evidence that delayed acceptance with an adaptive error model makes very crude coarse models useful (effective sample size roughly tripled in the Darcy example) but remains sequential — [Lykkegaard et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03876) [V].
- Evidence that data-space methods work from about 100 prior runs for a 204,000-parameter model — [Delottier, Doherty & Brunner 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023) [V].
- Evidence that approximate posteriors from neural estimators and from ensemble Kalman updates in learned latent spaces can be miscalibrated in either direction — [Hermans et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581) [V]; [Hull et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-4685-2024) [V]; [Di Federico, Teng & Durlofsky 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140) [V].
- Evidence that multilevel ensembles need the hybrid-covariance form in subsurface problems — [Fossum, Mannseth & Stordal 2020](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10596-019-09911-x) [V, search summary].
- Language-agnostic coupling of UQ libraries to external simulators exists (UM-Bridge), which is the same design principle as PEST's template/instruction-file interface — [Seelinger et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.04748).

### Inferences

#### Ranked shortlist (1 = highest expected value per unit effort)

1. **Calibration and reliability diagnostics for ensembles (SBC, coverage, rank histograms, CRPS, multivariate prior-data conflict).** Reasoning: every other choice on this list is currently made without evidence about how well calibrated IES, NSMC or DSI posteriors are; the tools are standard elsewhere and cost only post-processing or emulator runs. Non-intrusive: yes, entirely (pyEMU post-processing; SBC on DSI/GPR emulators using held-out prior realisations; hold-out CRPS on real data). Adds to current practice: a quantitative answer to "is 200 realisations enough here?" and a regression test for IES options against the open HESS 2024 references.

2. **Multifidelity and control-variate estimators (MFMC, ACV, MLBLUE) for prediction statistics, and multilevel hybrid ensembles for the smoother itself.** Reasoning: unbiased with respect to the full model, embarrassingly parallel, and groundwater models have ready-made low-fidelity partners (coarse grid, steady state, DSI or GPR emulator). Propagation use is a pure wrapper around PEST++ run management and is non-intrusive. A multilevel hybrid smoother would enlarge the effective ensemble at fixed cost but requires changes inside the IES update (still model-independent). Compared with today: tighter percentile estimates from the same number of fine runs; no change to assumptions.

3. **Variance-preserving refinements of the IES update: sampling-error-corrected or bootstrap-screened gains, relaxation-type inflation (RTPS), adaptive tempering schedules with discrepancy-principle stopping, and the revised/subspace EnRML formulation.** Reasoning: directly attacks the documented failure mode (under-dispersion and collapse with ensembles of about 100) using methods with long operational records; complements the existing `ies_autoadaloc` and reinflation options. Requires C++ changes in PESTPP-IES but no change to the model interface, so the workflow remains non-intrusive for users. A hybrid static-plus-ensemble covariance is a further step in the same direction.

4. **Emulator-in-the-loop exact sampling for a reduced parameter set: Calibrate-Emulate-Sample, delayed-acceptance/MLDA, or tempered SMC with the IES ensemble as starting population.** Reasoning: gives an asymptotically exact posterior (CES: exact for the emulated model; DA/MLDA: exact for the fine model) for 10 to 100 parameters at O(10^2) to O(10^4) fine runs, and so supplies the reference against which IES can be judged on a real model. Non-intrusive: yes (tinyDA, PyMC, UM-Bridge or a pyEMU worker can drive the same PEST interface; training data come from IES runs). Limits: needs dimension reduction (PCA/KL, likelihood-informed subspace); MCMC fine-level chains are sequential, so SMC or many short chains fit parallel resources better.

5. **Bayes linear history matching with implausibility (waves of emulation) for global, structural and hyperparameters, before IES.** Reasoning: explicit model-discrepancy variance, can return "no acceptable model", robust to failed runs, proven on 20 to 30 input simulators in several fields, and open software exists (hmer). Non-intrusive: yes. Limits: tens of inputs; spatial heterogeneity must be treated as stochastic variability or reduced to hyperparameters. Compared with today: replaces ad hoc bound setting and weighting-for-visibility with a quantified tolerance; complements, not replaces, IES.

6. **Nonlinear, diagnosable data-space estimators (SBI ideas applied to DSI): ensembles of emulators, PLS/autoencoder/GPR latent maps, quantile-conserving or normal-score marginals, importance-sampling correction against the full model.** Reasoning: DSI already delivers posterior predictions from about 100 runs; SBI contributes the diagnostics, the ensembling remedy for overconfidence and, where budgets reach 10^3 runs, conditional flows for low-dimensional predictions. Non-intrusive: yes (pyEMU already ships DSI, DSI-AE and GPR helpers). Limit: full neural SBI needs more simulations than most groundwater projects can afford.

7. **Rare-event estimation for exceedance probabilities (subset simulation or cross-entropy on an emulator with multifidelity importance-sampling correction).** Reasoning: the only defensible way to quote probabilities below about 1%; today's FOSM tails are Gaussian by assumption and IES tails are empty. Non-intrusive: yes. Limit: needs a posterior sampler or emulator that is trustworthy in the tails, so depends on items 1, 4 and 6.

8. **Non-Gaussian ensemble updates (QCEFF marginal updates, transport-map smoothers, RTO-style weighting of RML/IES samples).** Reasoning: principled generalisations of normal-score transformation and of the multimodal option, but evidence at realistic dimension and ensemble size is thin. Mostly internal to the update (model-independent). Worth prototyping in data space first.

9. **Laplace/low-rank and likelihood-informed-subspace machinery.** Reasoning: this is FOSM done matrix-free; its distinctive benefits (dimension-independent cost, 10^6 parameters) need an adjoint, which another researcher covers. The non-intrusive part worth adopting now is ensemble estimation of the informed subspace to size ensembles and define reduced spaces for item 4, and Laplace-based importance sampling to correct FOSM predictions.

Not recommended for transfer under the stated constraints: bootstrap or SIR particle filters without localisation (weight collapse); HMC/NUTS, stochastic Newton, DILI, SVGD and particle-flow filters without an adjoint; affine-invariant ensemble MCMC and nested sampling beyond a few tens of parameters; polynomial chaos, sparse grids and Bayesian optimisation beyond a few tens of inputs; diffusion posterior sampling without a trained generative prior and likelihood gradients.

### Gaps
- The ranking is a judgement based on published demonstrations in other fields plus a small number of groundwater studies; none of items 1 to 9 has been evaluated end-to-end on a field-scale groundwater model with PEST++ in a published comparison that was found.
- The web-search budget shared across this research effort was exhausted part-way through; later verification relied on direct retrieval of known papers. Topics that would have benefited from further searching: 2025 to 2026 developments in operational NWP data assimilation, groundwater applications of history matching with implausibility, groundwater applications of MFMC/ACV and subset simulation, QCEFF outside the atmosphere, and any PEST_HP "ensemble space inversion" developments.
- Items marked without [V] should be spot-checked against the DOI before being quoted with numbers in a final report.
