## Cross-disciplinary survey: how other fields solve very high-dimensional PDE-governed inverse and optimization problems (overview of the objective)

### Takeaway
Every field that inverts for 10^5 to 10^9 unknowns has stopped forming a Jacobian: they compute the gradient of a scalar misfit with one forward and one adjoint solve, obtain curvature from Hessian-vector products (two more linearized solves each), and stop iterating after tens to a few thousand solves regardless of n. Groundwater is the outlier in still treating the finite-difference Jacobian as the default, and the reason is software (model-independence), not mathematics.

### Cited Findings

**Seismic full-waveform inversion (FWI) and adjoint tomography**
- What it is: minimise a waveform misfit over velocity fields; the gradient comes from correlating the forward wavefield with a back-propagated (adjoint) residual wavefield, at a cost independent of the number of parameters — [Plessix, 2006, "A review of the adjoint-state method for computing the gradient of a functional with geophysical applications", Geophys. J. Int. 167(2):495-503](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2006.02978.x) [R]; [Tromp, Tape & Liu, 2005, "Seismic tomography, adjoint methods, time reversal and banana-doughnut kernels", Geophys. J. Int. 160:195-216](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2004.02453.x) [R]; [Virieux & Operto, 2009, "An overview of full-waveform inversion in exploration geophysics", Geophysics 74(6):WCC1-WCC26](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.3238367) [R].
- Scale: FWI involves "millions of unknowns in 2D and billions in 3D", so the inverse Hessian is never formed explicitly — [Métivier, Brossier, Virieux & Operto, 2013, "Full waveform inversion and the truncated Newton method", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 35(2):B401-B437 (search summary of the open PDF)](https://doi.org/10.1137/120877854) [V].
- A reproducible small example: 2-D Marmousi-II, 4 km × 16 km on a 10 m grid, 300 sources, 15 iterations (20 function evaluations), about 10^4 PDE solves in total — [Louboutin, Lange, Luporini, Kukreja, Witte, Herrmann, Velesko & Gorman, 2019, "Devito (v3.1.0): an embedded domain-specific language for finite differences and geophysical exploration", Geosci. Model Dev. 12:1165-1187](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-12-1165-2019) [V].
- Regional adjoint tomography of southern California: 143 earthquakes, 16 iterations, 6,800 wavefield simulations, 168,000 processor-hours — [Tape, Liu, Maggi & Tromp, 2010, "Seismic tomography of the southern California crust based on spectral-element and adjoint methods", Geophys. J. Int. 180:433-462](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2009.04429.x) [R].
- Global adjoint tomography: GLAD-M15 used 253 earthquakes and 15 iterations; GLAD-M25 extended this to 1,480 earthquakes — [Bozdağ et al., 2016, "Global adjoint tomography: first-generation model", Geophys. J. Int. 207(3):1739-1766](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggw356) [R]; [Lei et al., 2020, "Global adjoint tomography - model GLAD-M25", Geophys. J. Int. 223(1):1-21](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggaa253) [R; DOI resolution checked].
- Bayesian extreme scale: 1.067 million parameters, 630 million wave-propagation spatial unknowns over 2,400 time steps, up to 262,144 cores — [Bui-Thanh, Burstedde, Ghattas, Martin, Stadler & Wilcox, 2012, "Extreme-scale UQ for Bayesian inverse problems governed by PDEs", SC12 conference paper](https://sc12.supercomputing.org/schedule/event_detail.php-evid=gb132.html) [V, via search summary].
- Software: SEISCOPE optimization toolbox — [Métivier & Brossier, 2016, "The SEISCOPE optimization toolbox: a large-scale nonlinear optimization library based on reverse communication", Geophysics 81(2):F1-F15](https://doi.org/10.1190/geo2015-0031.1) [R]; Devito and its Julia front end JUDI (link above) [V].

**Numerical weather prediction: 4D-Var and hybrid ensemble-variational**
- What it is: fit a model trajectory to all observations in a time window by adjusting the initial state, with the gradient supplied by the adjoint model — [Le Dimet & Talagrand, 1986, "Variational algorithms for analysis and assimilation of meteorological observations: theoretical aspects", Tellus A 38:97-110](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0870.1986.tb00459.x) [R]; [Talagrand & Courtier, 1987, "Variational assimilation of meteorological observations with the adjoint vorticity equation. I: Theory", Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 113:1311-1328](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.49711347812) [R].
- Incremental 4D-Var made it operational: outer loops run the full nonlinear model, inner loops minimise a quadratic with lower-resolution tangent-linear and adjoint models — [Courtier, Thépaut & Hollingsworth, 1994, "A strategy for operational implementation of 4D-Var, using an incremental approach", Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 120:1367-1387](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.49712051912) [R]; [Rabier, Järvinen, Klinker, Mahfouf & Simmons, 2000, "The ECMWF operational implementation of four-dimensional variational assimilation. I", Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 126:1143-1170](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.49712656415) [R].
- Scale (ECMWF, 2018): control vector O(10^9); three outer loops at about 9 km (TCo1279); three inner minimisations at about 80, 60 and 50 km; about 30 inner iterations per minimisation (hard limit 50 never reached); about 45 minutes wall-clock in total, each extra outer loop adding about 15 minutes; a single outer loop (a purely linear update) "causes large degradation" — [Bonavita, Lean & Holm, 2018, "Nonlinear effects in 4D-Var", Nonlin. - Observation count (dated, 2003): more than 1.6 million data per assimilation cycle, rising to about 3.5 million with AIRS — [ECMWF Technical Memorandum, "Diagnostics of linear and incremental approximations in 4D-Var", 2003 (figure taken from a search summary; the memo itself was not opened)](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2003/12814-diagnostics-linear-and-incremental-approximations-4d-var.pdf) [V, weak].
- Hybrid methods blend ensemble covariances into the variational cost function; 4DEnVar removes the tangent-linear and adjoint models altogether by using ensemble trajectories — [Bannister, 2017, "A review of operational methods of variational and ensemble-variational data assimilation", Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 143:607-633](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2982) [R]. ### Inferences
- Across the fields above, total cost of a deterministic inversion sits between about 10^2 and 10^5 forward-equivalent solves for n between 10^4 and 10^9. One PEST Jacobian at n = 10^5 already costs 10^5 runs.
- None of these communities achieved this non-intrusively. Isaac et al. call their framework "adamantly intrusive". The price of dimension-independent cost has been ownership of the simulator source.
- Two fields (reservoir engineering and NWP) partially retreated to ensemble methods for exactly the reason groundwater never adopted adjoints: adjoint code is expensive to build and keep in step with the simulator. The Met Office result that the adjoint-based hybrid still beat the adjoint-free one is the main documented evidence that the adjoint buys accuracy, not just speed.

## Key question 1: Adjoint-state fundamentals and implementations; what an adjoint costs relative to a forward run and to finite-difference Jacobians

### Takeaway
A discrete adjoint of an implicit groundwater-type simulator is a backward-in-time sequence of linear solves with the transposed system matrix, so it costs about as much as (or less than) the forward run even when the forward run is nonlinear: 2.2 to 4 forward-run times for MF6-ADJ, 2 to 5.6 for the mature MITgcm/TAF adjoint, and about one fifth of a forward solve for a nonlinear Stokes ice model. Against a finite-difference Jacobian the adjoint wins by a factor of roughly n/m, so it is decisive at 10^4-10^6 parameters with 10^2-10^3 observations and loses for an explicit Jacobian once m approaches n, at which point every large-scale field switches to matrix-free products.

### Cited Findings

**The method**
- A Lagrangian recipe gives the gradient of any scalar functional with one extra linear solve of the adjoint equations, independent of the number of parameters — [Plessix, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2006.02978.x) [R].
- In groundwater terms the adjoint state is "the sensitivity of a given performance measure to a unit injection of water in each active model cell for each solution time"; if the performance measure is surface-water/groundwater exchange, the adjoint state is the capture fraction — [White (INTERA), "The adjoint state method: highly scalable sensitivity analysis", CWEMF presentation 28.4, 2025](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V].
- The adjoint variable was originally read as "importance" in nuclear engineering; it acts as a weighting (impulse-response) function convolved with forward states, an interpretation that a review of over 60 groundwater applications found was often lost in transfer — [Turnadge, Neupauer, Batelaan, Crosbie & Simmons, 2023, "Increasing our understanding of the adjoint state method of model sensitivity calculation", MODSIM2023, 25th International Congress on Modelling and Simulation, Darwin, p. 970](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2023/files/turnadge.pdf) [V].

**Discrete versus continuous adjoints**
- Continuous ("differentiate then discretise") and discrete ("discretise then differentiate") adjoints differ at finite resolution; only the discrete adjoint gives the exact gradient of the discrete objective that an optimiser sees — [Giles & Pierce, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1011430410075) [R]; [Nadarajah & Jameson, 2000, "A comparison of the continuous and discrete adjoint approach to automatic aerodynamic optimization", AIAA 2000-667](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2000-667) [R]; [Sirkes & Tziperman, 1997, "Finite difference of adjoint or adjoint of finite difference?", Mon. Weather Rev. 125:3373-3378](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1997)125<3373:FDOAOA>2.0.CO;2) [R].
- Continuous adjoints remain valued for insight into state-parameter relationships — [Turnadge et al., 2023](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2023/files/turnadge.pdf) [V].
- MF6-ADJ is a discrete adjoint: it harvests the conductance matrix, right-hand side, head and saturation from MODFLOW 6 at each time step through the API, stores them in HDF5, then solves the adjoint backward in time and applies the chain rule to parameters — [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V]; [Hayek, White, Markovich, Hughes & Lavenue, 2025, "MF6-ADJ: a non-intrusive adjoint sensitivity capability for MODFLOW 6", Groundwater 63(6):874-888](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V, read via PMC12652305].

**Adjoints of implicit time stepping and of nonlinear (Newton) solves**
- "Forward solve (linear/nonlinear): only done once ... Adjoint solve (always linear!): one per performance measure" — [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V].
- Ice-sheet evidence that the adjoint of a nonlinear solve is cheaper than the solve: each nonlinear Stokes solution took about 5 Newton iterations (5 linearised solves), the gradient cost one linearised solve, and each Hessian-vector product two (one incremental forward, one incremental adjoint) — [Isaac et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221) [V].
- Iterative solvers are not differentiated through. MITgcm's Tapenade adjoint treats the pressure solver as a black box and simply calls the solver on the adjoint system — [Gaikwad et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11952) [V]. The theory for adjoints of fixed-point iterations is in [Christianson, 1994, "Reverse accumulation and attractive fixed points", Optim. Methods Softw. 3:311-326](https://doi.org/10.1080/10556789408805572) [R].
- General-purpose discrete adjoints for implicit time integrators exist in PETSc and have been proposed for subsurface flow inversion with TAO — [Zhang, Constantinescu & Smith, 2022, "PETSc TSAdjoint: a discrete adjoint ODE solver for first-order and second-order sensitivity analysis", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 44(1):C1-C24](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M140078X) [R]; [Wang, Karra & O'Malley, 2019, "Large-scale inversion of subsurface flow using discrete adjoint method", arXiv:1906.01132](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01132) [V; the abstract gives no problem sizes or timings].

**Checkpointing**
- The adjoint runs backward but needs forward states. Binomial checkpointing ("revolve") gives provably optimal trade-offs between stored snapshots and recomputation — [Griewank & Walther, 2000, "Algorithm 799: revolve: an implementation of checkpointing for the reverse or adjoint mode of computational differentiation", ACM Trans. Math. Softw. 26(1):19-45](https://doi.org/10.1145/347837.347846) [R]. Dynamic variants handle unknown step counts — [Wang, Moin & Iaccarino, 2009, "Minimal repetition dynamic checkpointing algorithm for unsteady adjoint calculation", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 31(4):2549-2567](https://doi.org/10.1137/080727890) [R].
- In Devito, revolve-based checkpointing costs about log(nt)+2 extra PDE solves; boundary-wavefield reconstruction is a lower-memory alternative costing one extra forward solve — [Louboutin et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-12-1165-2019) [V].
- In MITgcm, TAF uses recompute-from-restart driven by about 350 STORE directives in the dynamical core and about 600 in packages; Tapenade checkpoints at every procedure call by default, which the authors blame for most of its slowdown; selective checkpointing gave 2-3× speed-ups — [Gaikwad et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11952) [V].
- MF6-ADJ avoids checkpointing by writing all forward solution components to HDF5 once; MODFLOW 6 is not needed after that, and further performance measures can be added without rerunning the forward model — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].

- Theory behind the table: reverse-mode differentiation bounds the gradient cost at a small constant multiple of the function cost, independent of the number of inputs (the "cheap gradient principle") — [Griewank & Walther, 2008, "Evaluating Derivatives: Principles and Techniques of Algorithmic Differentiation", 2nd ed., SIAM](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9780898717761) [R].

**Adjoints through boundary packages and coupled processes**
- MF6-ADJ currently differentiates with respect to NPF k11 and k33, STO ss (the paper's figures also show specific yield), WEL rates, RCH, and GHB, DRN, RIV and SFR stage/elevation and conductance. Performance measures are heads, boundary fluxes (GHB, RIV, SFR, DRN) and the sum of squared weighted head residuals. It handles confined and unconfined flow, standard and Newton-Raphson formulations, and DIS, DISV and DISU grids — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V]; [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V].
- Not supported: MAW, MVR, CSUB, UZF, and any GWT transport model. CHD cells give zero adjoint states and can mask sensitivities; the authors advise converting CHD to GHB. LAK, dry-cell handling and rewetting are not discussed in the paper — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- Coupled flow-transport adjoints were derived for groundwater 35 years ago — [Sun & Yeh, 1990, "Coupled inverse problems in groundwater modeling: 1. Sensitivity analysis and parameter identification", Water Resour. Res. 26(10):2507-2525](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR026i010p02507) [V, via search].
- Other fields handle switch-laden physics by writing regularised, simplified linearised physics for the tangent-linear and adjoint models — [Janisková & Lopez, 2013, "Linearized physics for data assimilation at ECMWF", in Data Assimilation for Atmospheric, Oceanic and Hydrologic Applications (Vol. II), Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-35088-7_11) [R]. ECMWF's inner loops run "full-physics tangent-linear and adjoint models", and growing nonlinearity is dealt with by adding outer loops — [Bonavita et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-25-713-2018) [V].

**Against finite-difference Jacobians**
- Freyberg, 17,625 recharge parameters: perturbation needs 17,626 runs (≈2 days serial); MF6-ADJ needs one forward plus one adjoint, under 1 minute. San Pedro (115,531 active cells; sensitivities of stream depletion to pumping rate, Kh and Kv): perturbation would need 346,596 runs (≈40 days serial) against ≈30 s. The abstract reports speed-ups "ranging from hundreds to tens of thousands of times" — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V]. (The author's later slides say "~600K model runs" for San Pedro; the paper's 346,596 is the documented figure — [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V].)
- The scaling flips with the number of outputs: each performance measure needs its own adjoint solve, a full Jacobian costs m+1 solves, and the advantage "shrinks when hundreds or thousands" of performance measures are analysed; the gradient of a single objective needs one forward and one adjoint, which suits L-BFGS — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- Accuracy: in systematic MODFLOW-2005 tests using PEST's JACTEST, adjoint sensitivities were significantly smoother than perturbation sensitivities in every case and "routinely more accurate"; some perturbation noise came from file reading and writing that the adjoint avoids — [Fienen & Clemo, 2011, "Accuracy and efficiency of adjoint-state in MODFLOW-2005", NGWA Ground Water Summit abstract](https://ngwa.confex.com/ngwa/2011gws/webprogram/Paper7824.html) [V]. MF6-ADJ's Freyberg test likewise attributed irregular perturbation sensitivities to finite-difference error and increment choice — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- The PEST++ manual itself warns that finite-difference derivative integrity degrades with output round-off and poor solver convergence — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].

### Inferences
- Cost model per GLM/Gauss-Newton iteration (derived from the findings above; A ≈ 1-4 F for an implicit groundwater adjoint):

| Quantity | Finite difference (PEST) | Adjoint / matrix-free |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit Jacobian | (n+1) F forward differences; (2n+1) F central | F + m·A |
| Gradient of the objective Φ | (n+1) F | F + A |
| One Gauss-Newton step, matrix-free with k inner CG/LSQR iterations | not available | F + k·(TL + A), about 1 + 2k solves |

- Worked corners of the range in the brief, in solve-equivalents per iteration:
  - n = 10^4, m = 10^2: FD 10^4 runs; adjoint Jacobian ≈ 10^2 solves. About 100× fewer.
  - n = 10^6, m = 10^2: FD 10^6 runs; adjoint Jacobian ≈ 10^2 solves. About 10^4× fewer.
  - n = 10^4, m = 10^5: FD 10^4 runs; adjoint Jacobian 10^5 solves. FD (or a tangent-linear/sensitivity-equation method) is cheaper for an explicit Jacobian.
  - n = 10^6, m = 10^5: neither explicit route is practical, and the dense Jacobian alone is 10^11 entries (about 800 GB in double precision). Only matrix-free methods apply: at 50-250 inner iterations per Newton step (the range Isaac et al. report), a step costs 10^2-5×10^2 linearised solves irrespective of n and m.
- The break-even for an explicit Jacobian is m ≈ n·F/A. With transient data sets of 10^4-10^5 head observations and pilot-point counts of 10^3-10^4, many current PEST problems sit on the wrong side of it; the adjoint only pays off there if the algorithm changes too (scalar-objective gradients or matrix-free products), not merely the way the Jacobian is filled.
- Wall-clock comparisons are kinder to PEST than CPU counts suggest, because FD Jacobian runs are perfectly parallel across agents, while adjoint solves for different observations are also independent and share the stored forward matrices (multi-right-hand-side solves, or one factorisation in steady state).
- Groundwater models have 10^1-10^4 time steps and modest state sizes, so storing the whole forward trajectory (as MF6-ADJ does) is usually feasible; revolve-style checkpointing matters mainly for long transport runs.
- The adjoint's "always linear" backward solve means the adjoint of a slow, Newton-iterated unconfined or SFR-coupled MODFLOW 6 model should cost a smaller fraction of the forward run than the 2-4× seen on the small, fast test models. This is an expectation from the ice-sheet evidence, not a measured MF6 result.

### Gaps
- No published MF6-ADJ timings for large transient models (10^6 cells, hundreds of stress periods) or for the memory and disk footprint of the HDF5 forward store.
- No published test of adjoint accuracy near drain/river switching, cell drying and rewetting, or upstream-weighting transitions for MODFLOW 6.
- The quantitative operation-count bound in Griewank & Walther (commonly quoted as 3-5×) was not re-read.

## Key question 2: Automatic and algorithmic differentiation of simulators

### Takeaway
AD can produce an adjoint of a large legacy Fortran model, but the 25-year MITgcm record shows it is a standing engineering commitment: hundreds of hand-placed directives, hand-written wrappers for MPI, I/O and solvers, regression tests to stop the adjoint breaking, and run-time ratios from 2 to 36 depending on tool and tuning. High-level approaches (dolfin-adjoint, Firedrake, Devito, JAX/Julia) remove that burden only for codes written in those frameworks. For MODFLOW 6 the route actually taken is neither: a hand-derived discrete adjoint outside the Fortran code, fed through the API.

### Inferences
- Three workable routes exist for a groundwater code: (a) AD of the Fortran (MITgcm model) — high standing cost, justified only with a funded maintainer; (b) rewrite in a differentiable framework (DPFEHM, Julia/JAX) — cheap adjoints but loses the MODFLOW package ecosystem, regulatory acceptance and GUI support; (c) API-fed external discrete adjoint (MF6-ADJ) — low coupling to the Fortran, but every package's contribution to the Jacobian must be re-derived by hand in Python, which is why MAW, MVR, UZF, CSUB and GWT are missing.
- Route (c) is fragile in a different way: it depends on the MODFLOW API continuing to expose the assembled matrix, right-hand side and package internals. That is an interface-stability risk, not a differentiation risk.
- The MITgcm lesson most relevant to MODFLOW is to treat solvers as black boxes and differentiate only the residual assembly. MF6-ADJ already does this by taking the assembled conductance matrix.
- Operator-overloading tools (CoDiPack, ADOL-C) target C++; MODFLOW 6 is object-oriented Fortran, so only source-transformation (Tapenade, with limited support for modern Fortran features) or LLVM-level Enzyme (through an LLVM Fortran front end) could apply. No evidence was found that anyone has tried.

### Gaps
- No source found for AD (Tapenade, Enzyme, or other) applied to MODFLOW-2005, MODFLOW 6, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere.
- ISSM's later move to CoDiPack and its performance, and Enzyme results on MITgcm itself, were not found.
- JAX- or PyTorch-based groundwater flow simulators published in 2025-2026 could not be surveyed: the search budget ran out before that query executed.
- dolfin-adjoint's reported adjoint-to-forward run-time ratios (Farrell et al. 2013) were not re-read.

## Key question 3: What adjoint capability already exists for groundwater codes, what it has been used for, and why uptake has been limited

### Takeaway
The theory has been in the groundwater literature since 1985 and a production adjoint existed for MODFLOW-2005 from 2007, but it lapsed for lack of maintenance. As of late 2026 the one live, open capability for a mainstream code is INTERA's MF6-ADJ (published September 2025): flow only, one adjoint solve per performance measure, no transport and no advanced packages, and no documented coupling to PEST++. Kitanidis and Lee's PCGA reaches 10^6 unknowns in about 10^2-10^3 runs without any adjoint, which is the main home-grown competitor.

### Cited Findings

**MODFLOW family**
- MODFLOW-2000 shipped a sensitivity-equation (forward sensitivity) process whose cost scales with the number of parameters; it was hard to maintain and lacked many later packages — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- Clemo (2007) added an adjoint process (ADJ) to MODFLOW-2005. It was intrusive and became incompatible with later MODFLOW-2005 releases through lack of maintenance — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V]. In 2011 it was described as supporting "many of the common MODFLOW packages" and was benchmarked against PEST perturbation sensitivities — [Fienen & Clemo, 2011](https://ngwa.confex.com/ngwa/2011gws/webprogram/Paper7824.html) [V].
- MF6-ADJ: Python package `mf6adj`, CC0 licence, on PyPI and conda-forge, source at github.com/INTERA-Inc/mf6adj with 368 commits on main when read; authors all at INTERA; no funding statement and no GMDSI acknowledgement in the paper or README — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V]; [mf6adj GitHub repository](https://github.com/INTERA-Inc/mf6adj) [V]; [mf6adj on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mf6adj) [V, via search].
- MF6-ADJ validation: RMSE of 6×10^-4 d/m² against the analytical adjoint state of Lu & Vesselinov (2015) on a 100×80 grid; RMSE of 2.35×10^-5 against perturbation on a 121-cell nested DISV grid (122 runs versus 2) — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- MF6-ADJ documented examples: synthetic dewatering, San Pedro, structured Freyberg, a box model, and the Zaidel water-table problem (a Newton-solver benchmark) — [mf6adj documentation](https://mf6adj.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) [V].
- MF6-ADJ stated future directions (November 2025): quasi-Newton optimisation, randomized maximum likelihood, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, and emulator training — [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V].
- Neither the MF6-ADJ paper, README nor documentation index mentions PEST++, pyEMU or data worth; the paper mentions PEST only to note that a full Jacobian needs m+1 runs — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V]; [mf6adj GitHub repository](https://github.com/INTERA-Inc/mf6adj) [V].

**Hooks on the PEST side**
- PEST can read model-calculated derivatives through the JACFILE control variable and an external derivatives file; PEST_HP cannot — [PEST help, "Sensitivities"](https://help.pesthomepage.org/sensitivities.html) [V, via search summary]. The PEST++ control-file tables list JACFILE and EXTDERFLE — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].
- PESTPP-GLM accepts a precomputed Jacobian for its first iteration through `base_jacobian()`; with `hotstart_resfile()` it then needs no model runs before testing upgrades. PESTPP-OPT also accepts `base_jacobian()` and can solve a chance-constrained linear programme with no model runs at all — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].
- ModelMuse does not support PEST's derivatives command-line section "because the models supported by ModelMuse do not have the capability of writing any elements of the Jacobian matrix" — [USGS ModelMuse help, "Unsupported PEST capabilities"](https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/ModelMuse/Help/unsupported_pest_capabilities.html) [V, via search summary].

- PFLOTRAN: the published inversion capability is ERT, with an explicitly built Jacobian; coupled flow-transport inversion is a stated future goal — [Jaysaval et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-961-2023) [V]. Math. Softw. 47(2):16; arXiv:1909.03948](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].

**Principal Component Geostatistical Approach (PCGA)**
- PCGA is the quasi-linear geostatistical approach — [Kitanidis, 1995, "Quasi-linear geostatistical theory for inversing", Water Resour. Res. 31(10):2411-2419](https://doi.org/10.1029/95WR01945) [V, citation in pyPCGA bibliography; pages and DOI R] — made Jacobian-free: the prior covariance is truncated to its K leading eigenpairs, and every Jacobian product is a finite-difference directional derivative of the forward model — [Kitanidis & Lee, 2014, "Principal component geostatistical approach for large-dimensional inverse problems", Water Resour. Res. 50(7):5428-5443](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013WR014630) [V, via pyPCGA documentation]; [Lee & Kitanidis, 2014, "Large-scale hydraulic tomography and joint inversion of head and tracer data using the principal component geostatistical approach (PCGA)", Water Resour. Res. 50(7):5410-5427](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014WR015483) [V, via pyPCGA documentation].
- Cost: 2 + p + K forward runs per Gauss-Newton iteration (p drift terms), independent of n and of m. The classical geostatistical approach is practical only up to n ≈ 10^4. Guidance is to start with K ≈ 100 or to retain about 80% of prior variance — [pyPCGA documentation, "Theory"](https://pypcga.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html) [V].
- Linear algebra: direct solves up to about 100 observations, matrix-free Krylov solvers above that, and a fast exact preconditioner for very large data sets — [pyPCGA documentation](https://pypcga.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html) [V]; [Lee, Yoon, Kitanidis, Werth & Valocchi, 2016, "Scalable subsurface inverse modeling of huge data sets with an application to tracer concentration breakthrough data from magnetic resonance imaging", Water Resour. Res. 52(7):5213-5231](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR018483) [V citation; numbers not read].
- Reported scales: about 100 forward runs for domains of about 10^6 unknowns with TOUGH — [Kokkinaki et al., TOUGH Symposium 2018 slides](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~jonghyun/files/TOUGH2018_Kokkinaki.pdf) [V, via search summary]; a TOUGH2 heat/pressure inversion with 29,800 unknowns and 4,000 measurements in 1,353 forward runs — [Lee et al., InterPore 2018 slides](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~jonghyun/files/Lee_heat_inversion_InterPore2018.pdf) [V, via search summary]; a packaged toy case with 4,225 unknowns, 51 observations and 50 components — [pyPCGA on PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/pypcga) [V, via search summary].
- pyPCGA posterior covariance is the inverse Gauss-Newton Hessian at the MAP point; the documentation warns that BFGS-type approximations may not converge to the true Hessian and should not be used as posterior covariances — [pyPCGA documentation](https://pypcga.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html) [V].

**Why uptake has been limited (documented reasons)**
- Intrusiveness and maintenance: "traditionally, someone has to modify the forward model code; this leads to long-term maintenance and upkeep issues" — [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V]; implementing the adjoint "generally requires coding within the forward model" — [Fienen & Clemo, 2011](https://ngwa.confex.com/ngwa/2011gws/webprogram/Paper7824.html) [V].
- Package coverage lags the simulator (MAW, MVR, CSUB, UZF, GWT missing in 2025) — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- Cost scales with the number of outputs, which blunts the advantage for history matching against thousands of observations unless the algorithm is changed — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- Conceptual barrier: the physical meaning of the adjoint state did not transfer with the method — [Turnadge et al., 2023](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2023/files/turnadge.pdf) [V].
- Tooling barrier: mainstream GUIs assume the model cannot write derivatives — [ModelMuse help](https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/ModelMuse/Help/unsupported_pest_capabilities.html) [V].
- An ensemble alternative arrived first: PESTPP-IES estimates a "randomized Jacobian" from the ensemble, with cost tied to ensemble size, and the manual notes this even relaxes the solver-precision demands of finite differences — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].

### Inferences
- Obstacles that the sources do not state but that follow from them:
  - Model independence. A PEST "model" is usually a batch script chaining pre-processors (pilot points to arrays, multipliers, PLPROC), one or more simulators, and post-processors (differences, time-averaging, particle tracks, budget summaries). An adjoint of MODFLOW covers only the middle link. The chain rule through pilot-point kriging factors and multiplier arrays is linear and easy; through arbitrary post-processing it is not.
  - Non-differentiable features. Drains and rivers switch on and off, cells dry and rewet, SFR routing has thresholds, ET has an extinction depth. The Newton formulation smooths some of these. A discrete adjoint returns the exact derivative of the converged discrete solution, which is correct locally but says nothing about what happens across a switch. Finite differences have the same problem and add increment-dependence on top, so the adjoint is no worse, but neither removes the local-minimum risk.
  - Local minima. FWI's long experience with cycle skipping shows that exact gradients at 10^6 parameters make starting-model and continuation strategy more important, not less. GLM with pilot points and Tikhonov regularisation is implicitly a low-dimensional, heavily regularised search and is more forgiving.
  - Parallel run managers made the n-run Jacobian affordable in wall-clock terms up to about 10^3-10^4 parameters, which is where most practice sits.
- The claim in the assignment brief that the MODFLOW 6 adjoint was "developed with GMDSI/INTERA" could not be confirmed. Authors and repository are INTERA; the GMDSI_notebooks repository contains no occurrence of "adjoint" or "mf6adj" (checked by grep in this session).
- MF6-ADJ plus `base_jacobian()` already allows an adjoint-filled JCO file to drive PESTPP-GLM's first iteration, FOSM and PESTPP-OPT without changing PEST++. It covers only the first iteration; later iterations would revert to finite differences unless PESTPP-GLM is restarted each iteration or the external-derivatives route (JACFILE) is used with PEST.

### Gaps

- Recalled but unverified: use of Clemo's ADJ code in Boise hydraulic-tomography studies (Cardiff, Barrash & Kitanidis) and as a derivative source for bgaPEST (Fienen et al., USGS TM 7-C9). The writer should check before citing.

## Key question 4: Optimization algorithms on top of gradients

### Takeaway
The large-scale default is inexact (truncated) Gauss-Newton or Newton with conjugate-gradient inner iterations on Hessian-vector products, preconditioned by the prior or regularisation operator, with L-BFGS as the low-effort fallback; both show iteration counts that do not grow with n. Matrix-free Levenberg-Marquardt is the same idea in least-squares clothing and is the smallest step from what PEST does now. The FWI community's remedies for local minima (multiscale continuation, alternative misfits, extended search spaces) and for many-source cost (source encoding, stochastic gradients) have direct groundwater analogues that have barely been tried.

### Cited Findings

**Gauss-Newton and inexact Newton-Krylov**
- hIPPYlib's reference implementation: inexact Newton-CG with Eisenstat-Walker forcing (to avoid over-solving) and Steihaug termination (to handle negative curvature), globalised by Armijo line search; for a wide class of problems outer Newton and inner CG iteration counts are independent of mesh size and hence of parameter dimension, because the data-misfit Hessian is compact and the regularisation operator is used as preconditioner — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].
- Measured: 39-42 Newton iterations and 48-66 CG iterations per Newton step from 10,371 to 1,456,225 parameters — [Isaac et al., 2015, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221) [V]. On the continental problem, 213 Newton iterations at 239 CG each, with continuation on the regularisation parameter to warm-start — [Isaac et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221) [V].
- Each Hessian-vector product costs one incremental forward and one incremental adjoint solve — [Isaac et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221) [V]; [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].
- Underlying theory — [Eisenstat & Walker, 1996, "Choosing the forcing terms in an inexact Newton method", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 17(1):16-32](https://doi.org/10.1137/0917003) [R]; [Steihaug, 1983, "The conjugate gradient method and trust regions in large scale optimization", SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 20(3):626-637](https://doi.org/10.1137/0720042) [R]; [Nash, 2000, "A survey of truncated-Newton methods", J. Comput. Appl. Math. 124:45-59](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0377-0427(00)00426-X) [R].
- Incremental 4D-Var is a truncated Gauss-Newton method: three outer (nonlinear) iterations, each with about 30 inner iterations on tangent-linear and adjoint models — [Bonavita et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-25-713-2018) [V]; [Gratton, Lawless & Nichols, 2007, "Approximate Gauss-Newton methods for nonlinear least squares problems", SIAM J. Optim. 18(1):106-132](https://doi.org/10.1137/050624935) [R]. Inner loops are preconditioned with limited-memory approximations built from earlier Lanczos vectors — [Tshimanga, Gratton, Weaver & Sartenaer, 2008, "Limited-memory preconditioners, with application to incremental four-dimensional variational data assimilation", Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 134:751-769](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.228) [R].

**Truncated Newton with second-order adjoints**
- Full-Newton Hessian-vector products come from a second-order adjoint — [Wang, Navon, Le Dimet & Zou, 1992, "The second order adjoint analysis: theory and applications", Meteorol. Atmos. Phys. 50:3-20](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01025501) [R]. For FWI a matrix-free truncated Newton method was built this way and compared with nonlinear CG and l-BFGS on two realistic cases — [Métivier et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1137/120877854) [V, via search summary]; survey version [Métivier, Brossier, Operto & Virieux, 2017, "Full waveform inversion and the truncated Newton method", SIAM Review 59(1):153-195](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1093239) [R].

**L-BFGS and limited-memory quasi-Newton**
- Algorithms — [Liu & Nocedal, 1989, "On the limited memory BFGS method for large scale optimization", Math. Program. 45:503-528](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01589116) [R]; with bounds, [Byrd, Lu, Nocedal & Zhu, 1995, "A limited memory algorithm for bound constrained optimization", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 16(5):1190-1208](https://doi.org/10.1137/0916069) [R].
- In FWI with source encoding on a Gulf-of-Mexico-style synthetic, l-BFGS needed the lowest computational cost among first- and second-order methods — [Castellanos, Métivier, Operto, Brossier & Virieux, 2015, "Fast full waveform inversion with source encoding and second-order optimization methods", Geophys. J. Int. 200(2):720-744 (HAL record)](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01888507) [V, via search summary].
- Used in reservoir history matching — [Gao & Reynolds, 2006](https://doi.org/10.2118/90058-PA) [R]; in ECCO with a memory of 4 — [ECCO 2025 tutorial](https://ecco-summer-school.github.io/ecco-2025/tutorials/pcluster/ecco_adj.html) [V]; and named by the MF6-ADJ authors as the natural consumer of a one-forward-one-adjoint objective gradient — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- The SEISCOPE toolbox packages steepest descent, nonlinear CG, l-BFGS, preconditioned l-BFGS and truncated Newton behind a reverse-communication interface, so the optimiser never calls the simulator directly — [Métivier & Brossier, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1190/geo2015-0031.1) [R].

**Levenberg-Marquardt in matrix-free form**
- LSQR and LSMR solve damped least-squares problems using only products with J and its transpose — [Paige & Saunders, 1982, "LSQR: an algorithm for sparse linear equations and sparse least squares", ACM Trans. Math. Softw. 8(1):43-71](https://doi.org/10.1145/355984.355989) [R]; [Fong & Saunders, 2011, "LSMR: an iterative algorithm for sparse least-squares problems", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 33(5):2950-2971](https://doi.org/10.1137/10079687X) [R].
- In groundwater, a Krylov-subspace Levenberg-Marquardt that recycles the subspace across damping parameters was published for highly parameterised problems — [Lin, O'Malley & Vesselinov, 2016, "A computationally efficient parallel Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm for highly parameterized inverse model analyses", Water Resour. Res. 52(9):6948-6977](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR019028) [R].
- PESTPP-GLM's default solver is already randomized: `svd_pack(redsvd)` applies randomized SVD to the (modified) JᵀQJ matrix and computes only the singular components needed, but it operates on an explicit, finite-difference Jacobian — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].

**Full-space (SAND, all-at-once, one-shot) versus reduced-space (NAND)**
- Full-space methods treat states, parameters and adjoints as simultaneous unknowns of the KKT system and avoid converging the forward problem at every iterate — [Biegler et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-55508-4) [R]; [Haber & Ascher, 2001, "Preconditioned all-at-once methods for large, sparse parameter estimation problems", Inverse Problems 17:1847-1864](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/17/6/319) [R]; [Biros & Ghattas, 2005, "Parallel Lagrange-Newton-Krylov-Schur methods for PDE-constrained optimization. Part I", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 27(2):687-713](https://doi.org/10.1137/S106482750241565X) [R].
- In FWI the same idea (relax the wave-equation constraint into a penalty) is used to escape local minima — [van Leeuwen & Herrmann, 2013, "Mitigating local minima in full-waveform inversion by expanding the search space", Geophys. J. Int. 195(1):661-667](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggt258) [R].

**Constraints: interior-point and SQP**
- General large-scale interior point — [Wächter & Biegler, 2006, "On the implementation of an interior-point filter line-search algorithm for large-scale nonlinear programming", Math. Program. 106:25-57](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-004-0559-y) [R]. An interior-point quasi-Newton method with adjoint gradients solves the bilevel sensor-placement problem in [Alexanderian, Petra, Stadler & Ghattas, 2016 (arXiv:1410.5899)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5899) [V, via search summary].
- ROL (Trilinos) implements unconstrained, constrained and under-uncertainty algorithms with a PDE-constrained interface, but "the user has to interface with other software packages for the definition and implementation of the forward and adjoint problems" — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021, related-software review](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V]. PETSc TAO provides bound-constrained Newton and limited-memory methods — [PETSc/TAO manual](https://petsc.org/release/manual/tao/) [R].

**Multigrid optimisation (MG/OPT)**
- Coarse-grid optimisation problems supply search directions for the fine one — [Nash, 2000, "A multigrid approach to discretized optimization problems", Optim. Methods Softw. 14:99-116](https://doi.org/10.1080/10556780008805795) [R]; [Lewis & Nash, 2005, "Model problems for the multigrid optimization of systems governed by differential equations", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 26(6):1811-1837](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827502407792) [R]; review [Borzì & Schulz, 2009, "Multigrid methods for PDE optimization", SIAM Review 51(2):361-395](https://doi.org/10.1137/060671590) [R]. Incremental 4D-Var's low-resolution inner loops are a production instance — [Bonavita et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-25-713-2018) [V].

**Multiscale continuation and cycle-skipping remedies**
- Invert low frequencies first, then widen the band — [Bunks, Saleck, Zaleski & Chavent, 1995, "Multiscale seismic waveform inversion", Geophysics 60(5):1457-1473](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1443880) [R]; [Virieux & Operto, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.3238367) [R].
- Convexify the misfit — optimal transport, [Métivier, Brossier, Mérigot, Oudet & Virieux, 2016, "Measuring the misfit between seismograms using an optimal transport distance: application to full waveform inversion", Geophys. J. Int. 205(1):345-377](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggw014) [R]; adaptive waveform inversion, [Warner & Guasch, 2016, "Adaptive waveform inversion: theory", Geophysics 81(6):R429-R445](https://doi.org/10.1190/geo2015-0387.1) [R].

**Source encoding, stochastic gradients, data sketching**
- Encoding many sources into a few random super-shots cuts per-iteration cost from the number of sources to O(1) simulations — [Krebs, Anderson, Hinkley, Neelamani, Lee, Baumstein & Lacasse, 2009, "Fast full-wavefield seismic inversion using encoded sources", Geophysics 74(6):WCC177-WCC188](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.3230502) [R]; analysed as stochastic optimisation in [Haber, Chung & Herrmann, 2012, "An effective method for parameter estimation with PDE constraints with multiple right-hand sides", SIAM J. Optim. 22(3):739-757](https://doi.org/10.1137/11081126X) [R]; growing-batch hybrids in [Friedlander & Schmidt, 2012, "Hybrid deterministic-stochastic methods for data fitting", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 34(3):A1380-A1405](https://doi.org/10.1137/110830629) [R].
- Randomised compression of the data misfit — [Le, Myers, Bui-Thanh & Nguyen, 2017, "A data-scalable randomized misfit approach for solving large-scale PDE-constrained inverse problems", Inverse Problems 33:065003](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/aa6cbd) [R]; applied to groundwater in [Lin, Le, O'Malley, Vesselinov & Bui-Thanh, 2017, "Large-scale inverse model analyses employing fast randomized data reduction", Water Resour. Res. 53:6784-6801](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR020299) [R].

### Inferences
- Mapping to PEST practice:
  - PEST GLM = Gauss-Newton with Marquardt damping on an explicit J. Matrix-free GN-CG or LSQR-LM is the same algorithm with J replaced by two operators: J·v (one tangent-linear solve, or one finite-difference directional run as PCGA does) and Jᵀ·w (one adjoint solve).
  - Tikhonov regularisation with a covariance-based weight matrix plays the role of the prior preconditioner; mesh-independence results suggest the number of CG iterations would track the number of data-informed directions (PEST's solution-space dimension), not n.
  - Frequency continuation corresponds to coarse-to-fine parameterisation (zones → pilot points → grid-scale multipliers) and to regularisation continuation, which PEST's target-objective-function control already approximates.
  - Cycle skipping has groundwater cousins: transient head or concentration signals whose timing is wrong by more than a characteristic period (seasonal cycles, breakthrough arrival), and threshold features. Misfits on temporal moments, arrival times or differences (which PEST users already construct as derived observations) are the analogue of travel-time or optimal-transport misfits.
  - Source encoding applies wherever the model is linear in its sources and there are many of them: confined-flow hydraulic tomography with tens to hundreds of pumping tests is the clearest case.
- L-BFGS needs only the scalar-objective gradient, which MF6-ADJ's "sum of squared weighted head residuals" performance measure already supplies; it is the lowest-effort route to grid-scale parameterisation. It gives no usable posterior covariance, so it must be paired with a low-rank Hessian step (next question) for uncertainty.
- Full-space methods and MG/OPT require access to the simulator's residual and state vectors during optimisation; with MODFLOW held behind an API they are research topics, not near-term options.

- No verified head-to-head numbers (iterations, gradient evaluations) for truncated Newton versus l-BFGS in FWI; the HAL PDF was blocked.
- No groundwater study was found that applies frequency-style continuation, optimal-transport misfits or source encoding with adjoint gradients; absence was not established by a dedicated search.
- Quantitative speed-ups in Lin et al. (2016, 2017) and Krebs et al. (2009) were not re-read.

## Key question 5: Low-rank and randomized Hessian methods and their link to uncertainty, and to PEST's SVD-assist, super-parameters and FOSM

### Takeaway
The "Laplace approximation with a low-rank prior-preconditioned data-misfit Hessian" is PEST/pyEMU linear (FOSM) analysis and SVD-assist under another name, with one decisive difference: the dominant subspace is found by randomized eigensolvers from about 2(r+l) linearised solves, where r is the number of data-informed directions, instead of from a Jacobian costing n runs. Documented ranks are 30 of 4,225, about 40 of 78,558, about 700 of 67,770, and about 5,000 of 409,545 to 1,190,403.

### Cited Findings

**The construction**
- Posterior covariance at the MAP point is the inverse Hessian; the data-misfit Hessian is formally dense and each column would need an incremental forward and adjoint solve, but its eigenvalues decay rapidly "since the data contain limited information about the (infinite-dimensional) parameter field". A low-rank approximation of the prior-preconditioned misfit Hessian then gives the posterior covariance as the prior minus a low-rank update, and eigenvalues small compared with 1 can be neglected — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V]; [Bui-Thanh, Ghattas, Martin & Stadler, 2013, "A computational framework for infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems, Part I: the linearized case, with application to global seismic inversion", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 35(6):A2494-A2523](https://doi.org/10.1137/12089586X) [V, read from author PDF].
- Original low-rank paper — [Flath, Wilcox, Akçelik, Hill, van Bloemen Waanders & Ghattas, 2011, "Fast algorithms for Bayesian uncertainty quantification in large-scale linear inverse problems based on low-rank partial Hessian approximations", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 33(1):407-432](https://doi.org/10.1137/090780717) [R]. Flath's 2013 PhD thesis applied Hessian-based methods to groundwater flow — [Flath, 2013, "Hessian-based response surface approximations for uncertainty quantification in large-scale statistical inverse problems, with applications to groundwater flow", PhD dissertation, University of Texas at Austin (cited in Villa et al.)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V, citation only].
- Optimality: the low-rank update built from the leading generalized eigenpairs of the (Hessian, prior precision) pencil is the optimal approximation of the posterior covariance for linear-Gaussian problems — [Spantini, Solonen, Cui, Martin, Tenorio & Marzouk, 2015, "Optimal low-rank approximations of Bayesian linear inverse problems", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 37(6):A2451-A2487](https://doi.org/10.1137/140977308) [R].
- Randomized algorithms — [Halko, Martinsson & Tropp, 2011, "Finding structure with randomness: probabilistic algorithms for constructing approximate matrix decompositions", SIAM Review 53(2):217-288](https://doi.org/10.1137/090771806) [R]; [Martinsson & Tropp, 2020, "Randomized numerical linear algebra: foundations and algorithms", Acta Numerica 29:403-572](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492920000021) [R]. hIPPYlib implements single-pass and double-pass randomized generalized eigensolvers — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].

**Documented ranks and costs**
- Steady Darcy-type problem: about r = 30 eigenvectors retained out of 4,225 parameters with 50 observations (r below the observation count, "reflecting redundancy in the data"); cost 2(r+l) forward and adjoint PDE solves with oversampling l = 20, i.e. about 100 linearised solves — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].
- Transient advection-diffusion initial-condition problem: 10,847 parameters, 433,880 space-time state unknowns, 1,200 data, about 70 eigenvectors retained — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].
- Global seismic: about 40 eigenvalues for 78,558 parameters (one source), at least 700 for 67,770 parameters (three sources, 130 receivers), "a reduction of between two and three orders of magnitude"; the dominant spectrum is independent of discretisation; problems up to 431,000 parameters; 1,200-4,096 cores for 10-20 hours — [Bui-Thanh et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1137/12089586X) [V].
- Antarctic ice sheet: 5,000 Hessian-vector products (10,000 linearised Stokes solves) for 409,545 parameters, about a tenth of the cost of finding the MAP point. The spectra for the 409,545- and 1,190,403-parameter meshes "lie on top of each other", and "only about 5,000 (out of 1,190,403) modes" of the basal sliding field are informed by the data — [Isaac et al., 2015, Figure 5 and Section 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221) [V].
- In the ice-sheet work the same machinery carries uncertainty through to a scalar prediction (ice mass flux to the ocean), i.e. data → inference → prediction in a fixed number of solves — [Isaac et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221) [V].

**Beyond the Gaussian approximation (connections to sampling only)**
- The MAP-point Hessian serves as proposal covariance for MCMC — [Martin, Wilcox, Burstedde & Ghattas, 2012, "A stochastic Newton MCMC method for large-scale statistical inverse problems with application to seismic inversion", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 34(3):A1460-A1487](https://doi.org/10.1137/110845598) [R]; [Petra, Martin, Stadler & Ghattas, 2014, "A computational framework for infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems, Part II: stochastic Newton MCMC with application to ice sheet flow inverse problems", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 36(4):A1525-A1555](https://doi.org/10.1137/130934805) [V, via search summary of arXiv:1308.6221].
- Likelihood-informed subspaces generalise the low-rank idea to nonlinear problems by averaging the prior-preconditioned Hessian over posterior samples — [Cui, Martin, Marzouk, Solonen & Spantini, 2014, "Likelihood-informed dimension reduction for nonlinear inverse problems", Inverse Problems 30:114015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/30/11/114015) [V, citation confirmed].
- Randomize-then-optimize with adjoints: each posterior sample is a perturbed MAP solve — [Wang, Bui-Thanh & Ghattas, 2018, "A randomized maximum a posteriori method for posterior sampling of high dimensional nonlinear Bayesian inverse problems", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 40(1):A142-A171](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1060625) [R].
- Software coupling Hessian-based inversion to samplers — [Kim, Villa, Parno, Marzouk, Ghattas & Petra, 2023, "hIPPYlib-MUQ: a Bayesian inference software framework for integration of data with complex predictive models under uncertainty", ACM Trans. Math. Softw. 49(2):17](https://doi.org/10.1145/3580278) [R]; [Parno, Davis & Seelinger, 2021, "MUQ: the MIT Uncertainty Quantification library", J. Open Source Softw. 6(68):3076](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.03076) [R]. MUQ "expects forward models to come equipped with gradients/Hessians to permit large-scale solution" — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].

**Other geoscience uses**
- Hessian-based uncertainty for global ocean state estimation — [Kalmikov & Heimbach, 2014, "A Hessian-based method for uncertainty quantification in global ocean state estimation", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 36(5):S267-S295](https://doi.org/10.1137/130925311) [R].
- In hydrogeology, randomized low-rank methods for posterior variance in the geostatistical approach — [Saibaba & Kitanidis, 2015, "Fast computation of uncertainty quantification measures in the geostatistical approach to solve inverse problems", Adv. Water Resour. 82:124-138](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.04.012) [V, citation in pyPCGA bibliography].

**The PEST side**
- SVD-assist: compute the full Jacobian once, take its SVD, define super-parameters as the leading right singular vectors, and thereafter spend one run per super-parameter per iteration — [Tonkin & Doherty, 2005, "A hybrid regularized inversion methodology for highly parameterized environmental models", Water Resour. Res. 41:W10412](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR003995) [R].
- PESTPP-GLM: randomized SVD (`redsvd`) is the default solver; FOSM uses the last Jacobian calculated or read, plus a prior parameter covariance; FOSM-based posterior parameter realisations can be drawn and run, and the lowest-phi realisation accepted — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V].
- pyEMU implements the Schur-complement posterior covariance and forecast variance from a Jacobian — [White, Fienen & Doherty, 2016, "A python framework for environmental model uncertainty analysis", Environ. Model. Softw. 85:217-228](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2016.08.017) [R].
- PCGA is the derivative-free member of this family: low-rank prior (K components), Gauss-Newton Hessian, posterior covariance from the same linear system — [pyPCGA documentation](https://pypcga.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html) [V].

### Inferences
- Term-by-term correspondence:

| Applied-mathematics term | PEST / pyEMU term |
|---|---|
| Gauss-Newton data-misfit Hessian JᵀΓ_noise⁻¹J | JᵀQJ (normal matrix) |
| Prior-preconditioned misfit Hessian, eigenpairs (λ_i, v_i) | SVD of Q^½ J C_prior^½; squared singular values and right singular vectors |
| Retain λ_i ≳ 1 | Solution space / number of super-parameters; identifiability cut-off where data information exceeds prior information |
| Laplace approximation: C_post = C_prior − low-rank update | FOSM Schur complement posterior covariance |
| Data → inference → prediction (Isaac et al.) | Forecast variance yᵀ C_post y with forecast sensitivity vector y |
| Randomized double-pass eigensolver, 2(r+l) solves | Fill Jacobian (n runs), then SVD |
| Likelihood-informed subspace | Super-parameter space re-estimated over an ensemble |

- The saving is the ratio n / (2(r+l)). For a 10^5-parameter model with r ≈ 10^2-10^3 informed directions it is one to two orders of magnitude per Jacobian-equivalent, and it grows with n because r does not.
- A hybrid needs no tangent-linear code: Jᵀ·w from an adjoint solve (MF6-ADJ with a weighted-residual-type performance measure) and J·v from a single finite-difference directional run, exactly as PCGA does. The directional run keeps the model-independent chain of pre- and post-processors intact for half of the products.
- PEST++ already uses randomization in the algebra (redsvd) but not in the sampling of the model. The step from "randomized SVD of an explicit JᵀQJ" to "randomized SVD through operator products" is conceptually small.
- Caveat for practitioners: the rank r is set by the data and noise, so dense transient data sets with small weights-implied noise can push r into the thousands (the seismic case went from 40 to 700 by adding two sources). The saving is not guaranteed.
- The Laplace approximation shares FOSM's weakness: it is a linearisation at one point. Its documented uses pair it with either MCMC correction or prediction-focused checks.

### Gaps
- No published application of randomized low-rank Hessian UQ to a MODFLOW-class regional model was found. Flath's thesis and hIPPYlib's Poisson example are the nearest.
- Ghattas & Willcox (2021), Halko et al. (2011) and Martinsson & Tropp (2020) were cited from memory of their contents, not re-read.
- No source directly compares PCGA with adjoint-based randomized Hessian methods on the same groundwater problem.

## Key question 6: Derivative-based optimal experimental design and data worth

### Takeaway
Adjoint-based optimal experimental design (OED) optimises sensor weights against the trace or determinant of the Laplace posterior covariance using randomized estimators, at a PDE-solve cost reported to be independent of parameter and sensor dimension. PEST/pyEMU data-worth analysis evaluates the same posterior (FOSM) but usually one candidate observation or group at a time and for a specific forecast, from an explicit Jacobian. The transferable pieces are goal-oriented criteria, sparsity-penalised simultaneous selection, and matrix-free evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- A-optimal design for linear problems: minimise the trace of the posterior covariance over sensor weights with sparsifying penalties, using randomized trace estimation and low-rank approximations — [Alexanderian, Petra, Stadler & Ghattas, 2014, "A-optimal design of experiments for infinite-dimensional Bayesian linear inverse problems with regularized ℓ0-sparsification", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 36(5):A2122-A2148](https://doi.org/10.1137/130933381) [V, citation confirmed].
- Nonlinear extension, demonstrated on subsurface flow: the inverse problem infers a log-permeability field in a porous-medium flow model; the design objective is the expected trace of the Laplace-approximation covariance at the MAP point; the bilevel problem is solved by an interior-point quasi-Newton method with adjoint gradients; and "the number of PDE solves required for the evaluation of the OED objective function and its gradient is essentially independent of both the parameter dimension and the sensor dimension" — [Alexanderian, Petra, Stadler & Ghattas, 2016, "A fast and scalable method for A-optimal design of experiments for infinite-dimensional Bayesian nonlinear inverse problems", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 38(1):A243-A272; arXiv:1410.5899](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5899) [V, via search summary].
- Randomized trace and log-determinant estimators underpin A- and D-optimal criteria — [Saibaba, Alexanderian & Ipsen, 2017, "Randomized matrix-free trace and log-determinant estimators", Numer. Math. 137:353-395](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-017-0880-z) [R].
- Goal-oriented design targets the variance of a prediction quantity instead of the whole parameter field — [Attia, Alexanderian & Saibaba, 2018, "Goal-oriented optimal design of experiments for large-scale Bayesian linear inverse problems", Inverse Problems 34:095009](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/aad210) [R].
- Review — [Alexanderian, 2021, "Optimal experimental design for infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems governed by PDEs: a review", Inverse Problems 37:043001](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/abe10c) [R]. Design under model uncertainty — [Alexanderian, Nicholson & Petra, 2022, "Optimal design of large-scale nonlinear Bayesian inverse problems under model uncertainty", arXiv:2211.03952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03952) [V, via search summary]. Expected-information-gain design at scale with low-rank Jacobians — [Wu, Chen & Ghattas, 2023, "A fast and scalable computational framework for large-scale high-dimensional Bayesian optimal experimental design", SIAM/ASA J. Uncertain. Quantif. 11(1):235-261](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1466499) [R].
- Earlier geophysical formulation — [Haber, Horesh & Tenorio, 2008, "Numerical methods for experimental design of large-scale linear ill-posed inverse problems", Inverse Problems 24:055012](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/24/5/055012) [R].
- Ocean observing-system design with adjoint and Hessian machinery: uncertainty reduction in a quantity of interest is evaluated for hypothetical observations before they are collected — [Loose & Heimbach, 2021, "Leveraging uncertainty quantification to design ocean climate observing systems", J. Adv. Model. Earth Syst. 13:e2020MS002386](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020MS002386) [R].
- PEST/pyEMU data worth: change in forecast variance when existing observations are removed or potential ones added, computed by FOSM from a Jacobian that contains rows for the candidate observations — [Dausman, Doherty, Langevin & Sukop, 2010, "Quantifying data worth toward reducing predictive uncertainty", Ground Water 48(5):729-740](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2010.00679.x) [R]; [White, Fienen & Doherty, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2016.08.017) [R]; [PEST help, "Linear analysis"](https://help.pesthomepage.org/linear-analysis.html) [V, page located by search].
- The groundwater adjoint review notes that continuous adjoint sensitivities "can inform both data worth analyses and process understanding" — [Turnadge et al., 2023](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2023/files/turnadge.pdf) [V].

### Inferences
- Same posterior, different search. pyEMU data worth is goal-oriented already (it targets forecast variance) and is effectively a greedy or enumerative search over candidates. The OED literature adds (i) simultaneous optimisation of all sensor weights with a sparsity penalty, which accounts for redundancy between candidates that one-at-a-time ranking ignores, and (ii) matrix-free evaluation so that n can be 10^5-10^6.
- Finite differences and adjoints have complementary strengths here. An FD Jacobian yields sensitivity rows for every model output at once, so adding thousands of candidate observation locations is free once the n runs are done. An adjoint yields one row (one output) for all parameters. For a few forecasts and many parameters the adjoint is ideal for the forecast sensitivity vectors y; for many candidate observations and few parameters FD is the natural tool. A mixed strategy is likely best: adjoint for forecast vectors and for existing-data Hessian products, randomized probing for the candidates.
- For well-siting questions MF6-ADJ already produces a design-relevant map directly: the adjoint state of a head or flux performance measure is the sensitivity to injection at every cell and time, which is a "where would a stress or a measurement matter" field at the cost of one solve.
- OED under model uncertainty (2022) corresponds to pyEMU practice of computing data worth under several prior or structural assumptions; the mathematics literature formalises marginalising over the nuisance parameters.

### Gaps
- No application of adjoint-based sparse OED to a field groundwater monitoring-network design was found.
- No direct numerical comparison of pyEMU data worth with A-optimal sparse design on a common problem was found.
- Quantitative results in Loose & Heimbach (2021) and Wu et al. (2023) could not be read (publisher blocks).

## Key question 7: Optimization under uncertainty and control

### Takeaway
Petroleum reservoir management has two decades of adjoint-based control optimisation (well rates and placement, typically 10^3-10^5 controls) embedded in closed-loop workflows and made robust by averaging gradients over geological realisations; applied mathematics has since added risk measures such as CVaR with rigorous smoothing and trust-region algorithms in ROL. Groundwater management optimisation in PEST++ rests on finite-difference response matrices with FOSM chance constraints, so adjoints would mainly change how many decision variables are affordable.

### Cited Findings

**Risk-averse PDE-constrained optimisation**
- CVaR for PDE-constrained problems with uncertain coefficients, with approximation theory and both primal and dual formulations — [Kouri & Surowiec, 2016, "Risk-averse PDE-constrained optimization using the conditional value-at-risk", SIAM J. Optim. 26(1):365-396 (Matheon preprint)](https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-matheon/files/1365/matheon_preprint_cover_DPK_TMS.pdf) [V, via search summary; journal DOI 10.1137/140954556 R].
- CVaR and other coherent risk measures are nonsmooth; epigraphical regularisation smooths them so that existing derivative-based PDE solvers can be reused — [Kouri & Surowiec, 2020, "Epi-regularization of risk measures", Math. Oper. Res. 45(2):774-795](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormoor/v45y2020i2p774-795.html) [V, via search summary].
- Cost control across samples of the uncertain inputs through adaptive sparse-grid collocation inside a trust-region method — [Kouri, Heinkenschloss, Ridzal & van Bloemen Waanders, 2013, "A trust-region algorithm with adaptive stochastic collocation for PDE optimization under uncertainty", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 35(4):A1847-A1879](https://doi.org/10.1137/120892362) [R].
- ROL packages these methods, but the user must supply forward and adjoint solves — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].

**Reservoir production optimisation and closed-loop management**
- Optimal control of waterflooding with adjoint gradients — [Brouwer & Jansen, 2004, "Dynamic optimization of waterflooding with smart wells using optimal control theory", SPE Journal 9(4):391-402](https://doi.org/10.2118/78278-PA) [R]; review — [Jansen, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compfluid.2010.09.039) [R].
- Closed loop: alternate model updating (history matching) with re-optimisation of controls — [Sarma, Durlofsky, Aziz & Chen, 2006, "Efficient real-time reservoir management using adjoint-based optimal control and model updating", Comput. Geosci. 10:3-36](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-005-9009-z) [R]; [Jansen, Brouwer & Douma, 2009, "Closed loop reservoir management", SPE 119098-MS](https://doi.org/10.2118/119098-MS) [R]; AD-based implementation of both halves in one framework — [Bukshtynov et al., 2015](https://pangea.stanford.edu/departments/ere/dropbox/SUPRI-B/SUPRI-B_publications/2015_bukshtynov_volkov_closed-loop.pdf) [V].
- Robust optimisation: maximise expected net present value over an ensemble of 100 geological realisations, with one adjoint gradient per realisation — [van Essen, Zandvliet, Van den Hof, Bosgra & Jansen, 2009, "Robust waterflooding optimization of multiple geological scenarios", SPE Journal 14(1):202-210](https://doi.org/10.2118/102913-PA) [R].
- Well placement with adjoints, by surrounding each well with pseudo-wells and reading placement gradients from the adjoint — [Zandvliet, Handels, van Essen, Brouwer & Jansen, 2008, "Adjoint-based well-placement optimization under production constraints", SPE Journal 13(4):392-399](https://doi.org/10.2118/105797-PA) [R]; [Sarma & Chen, 2008, "Efficient well placement optimization with gradient-based algorithms and adjoint models", SPE 112257-MS](https://doi.org/10.2118/112257-MS) [R].
- Where simulator source is not available, ensemble-estimated gradients replaced adjoints — [Chen, Oliver & Zhang, 2009, "Efficient ensemble-based closed-loop production optimization", SPE Journal 14(4):634-645](https://doi.org/10.2118/112873-PA) [R].

**Groundwater**
- Adjoint sensitivities were used in remediation design optimisation in the 1980s — [Ahlfeld, Mulvey, Pinder & Wood, 1988, "Contaminated groundwater remediation design using simulation, optimization, and sensitivity theory: 1. Model development", Water Resour. Res. 24(3):431-441](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR024i003p00431) [R].
- PESTPP-OPT solves chance-constrained sequential linear programming; constraint uncertainty comes from FOSM; the response matrix is filled by finite differences or supplied through `base_jacobian()`, and with a supplied Jacobian and residuals the chance-constrained problem can be solved with no model runs — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V]; [White, Fienen, Barlow & Welter, 2018, "A tool for efficient, model-independent management optimization under uncertainty", Environ. Model. Softw. 100:213-221](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2017.11.019) [R].
- One MF6-ADJ solve with stream-aquifer exchange as the performance measure returns the capture fraction for a well at every cell and time; on the San Pedro model (≈115,000 active nodes) this took one forward and one adjoint run — [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V]; [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V]. The repeated-run version of that map is the established capture-fraction method — [Leake, Reeves & Dickinson, 2010, "A new capture fraction method to map how pumpage affects surface water flow", Ground Water 48(5):690-700](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2010.00701.x) [R].

### Inferences
- Management problems are the cleanest fit for adjoints in groundwater: few objectives and constraints (m small), many decision variables (rates by well by stress period, n large), and decision variables that enter through WEL-type packages MF6-ADJ already differentiates. An adjoint row per constraint fills PESTPP-OPT's response matrix in (number of constraints + 1) solves instead of (number of decision variables + 1) runs.
- For linear (confined) or mildly nonlinear models the adjoint-derived response matrix is exact and reusable, which is the superposition/response-function approach generalised to every cell.
- Robust and risk-averse formulations transfer directly on top of a posterior ensemble from IES: N realisations × (1 forward + 1 adjoint per constraint) per optimisation iteration, as in van Essen et al. CVaR replaces PESTPP-OPT's FOSM-based chance constraints with a tail measure that does not assume linearity or Gaussianity, at the price of N-fold cost and a nonsmooth objective that needs the smoothing in Kouri & Surowiec.
- Closed-loop reservoir management (assimilate, re-optimise, repeat) is the same loop as adaptive groundwater management. The reservoir experience suggests the model-update half and the control half can share one adjoint.
- Well placement is discrete; reservoir practice converts it to a continuous problem through pseudo-wells, and MF6-ADJ's adjoint-state map (sensitivity to injection at every cell) is the same information.

### Gaps
- Reported NPV improvements and control dimensions in the reservoir papers were not re-read.
- No groundwater application of CVaR or other risk-averse PDE-constrained formulations with adjoint gradients was found.
- No published coupling of MF6-ADJ to PESTPP-OPT or PESTPP-MOU was found.

## Ranked shortlist of the most promising transfer opportunities for groundwater calibration and uncertainty analysis

### Takeaway
The highest-value transfers are those that keep the PEST++ workflow and replace only what is slow: adjoint rows fed into existing Jacobian consumers first, then scalar-gradient and matrix-free solvers, then randomized low-rank Hessian UQ. Anything requiring a rewritten or fully differentiated simulator ranks last because the documented cost elsewhere is a permanent maintenance team.

### Cited Findings
- Hooks exist today: PESTPP-GLM, its FOSM analysis and PESTPP-OPT read a precomputed Jacobian through `base_jacobian()`; PEST reads external derivatives through JACFILE — [PEST++ user manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md) [V]; [PEST help](https://help.pesthomepage.org/sensitivities.html) [V].
- MF6-ADJ supplies head, flux and weighted-residual performance measures for K, storage, recharge, well and head-dependent-boundary parameters at every cell, at 2.2-4× a forward run, with no MF6 source changes; it lacks GWT, MAW, MVR, UZF and CSUB, and costs one solve per performance measure — [Hayek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025) [V].
- The MF6-ADJ developers' own next steps are quasi-Newton optimisation, RML, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and emulator training — [White, 2025 slides](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf) [V].
- Inexact Gauss-Newton-CG converged in 5,376-7,031 linearised solves from 10^4 to 1.5×10^6 parameters, and low-rank posterior covariance cost about a tenth of the MAP solve — [Isaac et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221) [V].
- Low-rank Hessian cost is 2(r+l) linearised solves — [Villa, Petra & Ghattas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948) [V].
- PCGA reaches about 10^6 unknowns in about 10^2 runs per the developers' slides with no adjoint at all — [Kokkinaki et al., 2018 slides](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~jonghyun/files/TOUGH2018_Kokkinaki.pdf) [V]; [pyPCGA documentation](https://pypcga.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html) [V].
- AD of a legacy Fortran model required hundreds of directives, hand-written wrappers and dedicated regression tests, and the open-source route still ran 2-7× slower than the commercial one — [Gaikwad et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11952) [V].
- A purely linear update degraded operational 4D-Var badly; three or more outer (nonlinear) loops are needed — [Bonavita et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-25-713-2018) [V].

### Inferences
Ranked by expected value to an experienced PEST/PEST++ user, weighing benefit, readiness and risk.

1. **Adjoint rows into the existing PEST++/pyEMU machinery (forecast sensitivities, capture maps, data worth, first-iteration Jacobians).**
   - Why first: everything needed exists (MF6-ADJ, `base_jacobian()`, pyEMU's Jacobian objects). It needs a converter and a chain rule through pilot-point and multiplier pre-processing, not new mathematics.
   - Best targets: forecast sensitivity vectors for FOSM at grid-scale parameterisation (few forecasts, so m is small); response matrices for PESTPP-OPT (few constraints); capture-fraction and "sensitivity to stress anywhere" maps.
   - Limits: one solve per output, so history-matching Jacobians with 10^4+ observations do not benefit; flow only; `base_jacobian()` covers one iteration.

2. **Gradient-based regularised inversion at grid-scale parameterisation with L-BFGS, then matrix-free Gauss-Newton-CG or LSQR-LM.**
   - Why: this is the change every other field made, with cost of roughly 10^2-10^4 solve-equivalents independent of n. L-BFGS needs only the weighted-residual performance measure that MF6-ADJ already has.
   - What must be imported with it: prior-covariance preconditioning (pilot points and Tikhonov replaced or complemented by a covariance operator), continuation on regularisation or scale, and multiple outer iterations. FWI's cycle-skipping history is the warning that exact gradients do not remove local minima.
   - Limits: observation types are restricted to what the adjoint supports (heads and boundary fluxes today); derived observations from post-processors need their own chain rule; non-smooth packages may stall line searches.

3. **Randomized low-rank Gauss-Newton Hessian for Laplace/FOSM uncertainty and super-parameter definition.**
   - Why: it is SVD-assist and pyEMU Schur analysis without the n-run Jacobian, and it scales with the number of data-informed directions. It also produces the subspace for likelihood-informed sampling or for restricting an ensemble method.
   - Low-intrusion variant: Jᵀ·w from the adjoint, J·v from one finite-difference directional run (PCGA-style), so no tangent-linear code is required.
   - Limits: r can reach thousands for rich transient data; still a linearisation; needs a prior covariance that can be applied and square-rooted matrix-free.
   - Note: where no adjoint exists (transport, MAW, UZF), PCGA delivers much of this benefit model-independently and should be benchmarked first.

4. **Adjoint-based management optimisation under uncertainty.**
   - Why: m small and n large is the adjoint's ideal regime; controls enter through supported packages; the reservoir literature provides tested closed-loop and robust (multi-realisation) templates.
   - Path: adjoint-filled response matrices in PESTPP-OPT first; then multi-realisation robust objectives over an IES posterior ensemble; CVaR after that.
   - Limits: nonlinear constraints on SFR/MAW/LAK quantities are only partly supported; discrete decisions need pseudo-well style relaxations.

5. **Sparse, goal-oriented optimal experimental design for monitoring networks.**
   - Why: adds simultaneous, redundancy-aware selection to pyEMU's one-at-a-time data worth, and is already demonstrated on log-permeability inference in porous-medium flow.
   - Limits: depends on item 3 being in place; mixed FD/adjoint strategy needed when candidate observations are numerous.

6. **Stochastic and sketching accelerations: data compression, source encoding for hydraulic tomography, mini-batches of stress periods.**
   - Why: they attack the "many observations" side that weakens adjoint Jacobians, and randomized data reduction has already been published for groundwater.
   - Limits: source encoding needs linearity in the sources; benefits are problem-specific.

7. **Differentiable-by-construction or AD-differentiated simulators (Julia/JAX codes, Enzyme or Tapenade on Fortran).**
   - Why low: highest long-run payoff (transport, unsaturated flow and coupled-process adjoints, second-order adjoints for free) but the MITgcm and Clemo records show the cost is permanent maintenance; new codes forfeit the MODFLOW ecosystem.
   - Sensible near-term role: research test beds (DPFEHM-type) for algorithm development that later feeds items 2-5.

8. **Full-space (SAND/one-shot) methods and MG/OPT.**
   - Why last: they need the simulator's residuals and states exposed inside the optimiser; no path exists through the current MODFLOW API, and reduced-space methods are not yet exhausted.

Cross-cutting conditions for any of these to succeed:
- Keep model-independence where it is cheap to keep. Hybrid schemes (adjoint for Jᵀ, finite-difference directional derivatives for J, ensemble statistics where no adjoint exists) preserve the scripted pre- and post-processing chain that makes PEST practical.
- Verify gradients routinely. A dot-product (adjoint) test and a finite-difference directional check on each new model catch missing-package contributions, CHD masking and switching-boundary artefacts.
- Expect nonlinearity to demand outer iterations and good starting points; do not sell the adjoint as a one-shot linear update.
- Budget for upkeep. The one groundwater adjoint that reached production before 2025 was lost to lack of maintenance.

### Gaps
- No benchmark exists that compares, on one realistic MODFLOW 6 model, (a) PESTPP-GLM with FD Jacobians, (b) PESTPP-GLM with adjoint-supplied Jacobians, (c) L-BFGS or GN-CG on adjoint gradients, (d) PCGA and (e) PESTPP-IES. That benchmark is the single most useful missing piece of evidence for ranking these options.
- The ranking is the researcher's judgement from the cited evidence; no source ranks these transfers.
- State-of-the-art coverage for 2026 is incomplete: verified 2026 items are limited to DJ4Earth (JAMES 2026) and two hydrology preprints, because the session's web-search budget was exhausted before targeted 2026 queries on MF6-ADJ updates, differentiable groundwater simulators and PEST++ developments could run.
