# Optimization and decision-making methods transferable to groundwater calibration and management

Research notes, state of the literature at 13 November 2026. Scope: optimization algorithm families from operations research, machine learning, control, petroleum engineering, energy and water-resources systems analysis that go beyond Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt and evolutionary methods. Adjoint derivation and Bayesian sampling are out of scope. The PEST++ baseline used for comparison is the users manual at version 5.2.27, read from the USGS GitHub master branch on 13 November 2026.

Source notes. The shared web-search quota ran out about two thirds of the way through. After that I verified papers by fetching arXiv, publisher, repository and Crossref DOI records directly. Where I could confirm only the citation and not the content of a paper, the bullet says so. Anything I remember but could not confirm sits under Gaps, never under Cited findings.

## Derivative-free and black-box optimization for expensive simulators

### Takeaway
Benchmarks agree that model-based trust-region methods win on smooth problems at small budgets, and that exploiting least-squares structure (DFO-LS, POUNDERS, DFBGN) is what lets them make progress with fewer runs than one finite-difference Jacobian. For multimodal problems with up to about 30 variables and a few hundred runs, the Regis-Shoemaker radial-basis-function family has the strongest groundwater record of any imported method, including a published hybrid with PEST.

### Cited findings

#### Reviews and benchmarking evidence
- Larson, Menickelly and Wild (2019), "Derivative-free optimization methods", Acta Numerica 28:287-404. The review sorts methods by what is assumed about the black box. It covers deterministic methods for unconstrained nonconvex problems, randomized methods, methods that exploit structure such as nonlinear least squares and nonsmooth compositions, stochastic oracles, and constraints. It concentrates on work from roughly 2009 to 2019. — [Acta Numerica](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492919000060); [arXiv 1904.11585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11585)
- Moré and Wild (2009), "Benchmarking derivative-free optimization algorithms", SIAM J. Optim. 20(1):172-191. The paper introduced data profiles, which report the fraction of problems solved within a budget counted in simplex gradients, that is, groups of n+1 evaluations. It compared a Nelder-Mead code (NMSMAX), a pattern-search code (APPSPACK) and Powell's model-based NEWUOA. NEWUOA performed best on smooth problems, and data profiles for noisy problems looked similar to the smooth ones. — [SIAM J. Optim.](https://doi.org/10.1137/080724083); [preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/1883.pdf)
- Rios and Sahinidis (2013), "Derivative-free optimization: a review of algorithms and comparison of software implementations", J. Global Optim. 56:1247-1293. They ran 22 implementations on 502 problems, convex and nonconvex, smooth and nonsmooth. Within 2,500 evaluations, TOMLAB/MULTIMIN, TOMLAB/GLCCLUSTER, MCS and TOMLAB/LGO gave the best solution quality on average. For refining a near-optimal point, TOMLAB/OQNLP, NEWUOA and TOMLAB/MULTIMIN were best. Global solvers beat local solvers even on convex problems, and every solver degraded as dimension grew. — [J. Global Optim.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10898-012-9951-y)
- In the TuRBO experiments (Eriksson et al. 2019), CMA-ES and BOBYQA were the strongest non-Bayesian baselines on a 14-dimensional problem with 10,000 evaluations and a 60-dimensional problem with 20,000 evaluations. BOBYQA stalled in poor local minima on 200-dimensional Ackley. — [ar5iv 1910.01739](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.01739)

#### Model-based trust-region methods
- Powell's NEWUOA builds quadratic interpolation models for unconstrained problems. BOBYQA adds bounds. NLopt's documentation says BOBYQA largely supersedes NEWUOA and may perform poorly when the objective is not twice differentiable. COBYLA is the only local derivative-free solver in NLopt that takes general nonlinear constraints directly, and an augmented-Lagrangian wrapper (AUGLAG) lets any solver handle them. — [Powell 2006, NEWUOA](https://doi.org/10.1007/0-387-30065-1_16); [NLopt algorithms](https://nlopt.readthedocs.io/en/latest/NLopt_Algorithms/)
- Ragonneau and Zhang (2024), "PDFO: a cross-platform package for Powell's derivative-free optimization solvers", Math. Prog. Comp. 16:535-559. PDFO gives Python and MATLAB access to COBYLA, UOBYQA, NEWUOA, BOBYQA and LINCOA with bug fixes for ill-conditioning and failed function evaluations. The paper reports tests of stability under noise and tolerance to failed evaluations. — [Math. Prog. Comp.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12532-024-00257-9)
- Cartis, Fiala, Marteau and Roberts (2019), "Improving the flexibility and robustness of model-based derivative-free optimization solvers", ACM Trans. Math. Softw. 45(3):32. DFO-LS builds linear regression models of each residual and takes Gauss-Newton steps. It can begin making progress after as few as two evaluations, and the authors report reasonable progress on some medium-scale problems with fewer evaluations than one gradient needs. For noisy objectives they tested sample averaging, regression models and restarts. Restarting when stagnation is detected was cheap and beat the other two. Py-BOBYQA matched or beat existing general solvers on noisy problems. — [ACM TOMS](https://doi.org/10.1145/3338517); [arXiv 1804.00154](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00154)
- DFO-LS documentation, version 1.6.5 dated 23 March 2026. The solver handles bounds, general convex constraints that are never violated during the search, and an optional convex regularizer such as an L1 term. Tikhonov terms go in as extra residuals. The licence is GPL, with commercial terms through NAG. — [DFO-LS docs](https://numericalalgorithmsgroup.github.io/dfols/build/html/index.html)
- Earlier solvers with the same idea are DFLS (Zhang, Conn and Scheinberg 2010, SIAM J. Optim. 20:3555-3576), DFO-GN (Cartis and Roberts 2019, Math. Prog. Comp. 11:631-674) and POUNDERS (Wild 2017, chapter 40 of "Advances and Trends in Optimization with Engineering Applications", distributed in PETSc/TAO). — [DFLS](https://doi.org/10.1137/09075531X); [DFO-GN](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12532-019-00161-7); [POUNDERS](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611974683.ch40)
- Cartis and Roberts (2023), "Scalable subspace methods for derivative-free nonlinear least-squares optimization", Math. Programming 199:461-524. DFBGN takes each step in a random subspace of user-chosen dimension. Per-iteration linear algebra is linear in the problem dimension, the authors prove high-probability iteration bounds, and they report strong performance on large-scale least-squares problems measured in evaluations. — [Math. Programming](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-022-01836-1)

#### Direct search and mesh adaptive direct search
- Audet and Dennis (2006) introduced mesh adaptive direct search (MADS) for constrained problems. NOMAD 4 (Audet, Le Digabel, Rochon Montplaisir and Tribes 2022, ACM TOMS 48(3), Algorithm 1027) is a full redesign with parallel blackbox evaluation, warm and hot restarts and a revised PSD-MADS for larger problems. The authors state that performance matches version 3. — [MADS](https://doi.org/10.1137/040603371); [NOMAD 4](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544489); [arXiv 2104.11627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11627)
- Groundwater application. Fowler et al. (2008), "Comparison of derivative-free optimization methods for groundwater supply and hydraulic capture community problems", Adv. Water Resour. 31:743-757, tested several derivative-free sampling methods new to hydrology, plus a genetic algorithm, on the two community problems defined by Mayer, Kelley and Miller (2002). The authors found the problems hard because the objectives are nonsmooth and nonlinear with many local minima. — [Fowler et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2008.01.010); [Mayer et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708(02)00054-4)
- Reservoir application. Isebor, Durlofsky and Echeverría Ciaurri (2014), Comput. Geosci. 18:463-482, combined particle swarm with MADS for joint optimization of well locations and controls, with a filter for nonlinear constraints. The hybrid consistently beat standalone PSO and standalone MADS, and joint optimization beat the sequential approach. A companion ECMOR paper adds a binary drill or do-not-drill variable per well. — [Comput. Geosci.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-013-9383-x); [IBM record](https://research.ibm.com/publications/a-derivative-free-methodology-with-local-and-global-search-for-the-constrained-joint-optimization-of-well-locations-and-controls)

#### CMA-ES and evolution strategies
- Hansen and Ostermeier (2001), "Completely derandomized self-adaptation in evolution strategies", Evol. Comput. 9:159-195, is the CMA-ES reference. — [Evol. Comput.](https://doi.org/10.1162/106365601750190398)
- Groundwater application. Bayer and Finkel (2004, WRR 40, W06506) compared simple genetic algorithms with derandomized evolution strategies for capture-zone design, a mixed discrete-continuous problem in well positions and rates. Bayer and Finkel (2007, WRR 43, W02410) coupled CMA-ES with groundwater transport models for one-well and five-well pump-and-treat designs with positions and rates as variables, and derived a recommended CMA-ES configuration. — [2004](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003WR002675); [2007](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR004753)
- Reservoir application. Bouzarkouna, Ding and Auger (2012), "Well placement optimization with the covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy and meta-models", Comput. Geosci. 16:75-92. Citation confirmed, content not read. — [Comput. Geosci.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-011-9254-2)
- PESTPP-SQP generates its decision-variable ensemble with a CMA-inspired covariance update (rank-one and rank-mu updates, evolution paths), so one piece of CMA-ES is already in the PEST++ suite. — [PEST++ manual, section 14.2.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)

#### Radial-basis-function surrogate methods
- Regis and Shoemaker (2007), "A stochastic radial basis function method for the global optimization of expensive functions", INFORMS J. Comput. 19:497-509. The method picks each new point from random candidates by weighing the surrogate prediction against distance from evaluated points. On 17 multimodal test problems and a 12-dimensional groundwater bioremediation problem, the multistart local variant was best on most higher-dimensional problems, including the groundwater one. The journal later gave the paper a test-of-time award. — [INFORMS J. Comput.](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.1060.0182); [editor's note 2025](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/ijoc.2025.ed.v37.n4)
- Regis and Shoemaker (2013), "Combining radial basis function surrogates and dynamic coordinate search in high-dimensional expensive black-box optimization" (DYCORS), Eng. Optim. 45:529-555. Candidates come from perturbing only a subset of coordinates of the current best point, and the probability of perturbing each coordinate falls as the budget is used up. — [Eng. Optim.](https://doi.org/10.1080/0305215X.2012.687731); [DyCors implementation notes](https://aquirosr.github.io/DyCors)
- Groundwater calibration. Mugunthan, Shoemaker and Regis (2005, WRR 41, W11427) compared function-approximation methods with heuristic and derivative-based optimizers for calibrating a chlorinated-ethene bioremediation model. In the eight-parameter field case in California, the function-approximation methods beat both other classes in three trials on both objective functions. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR004134)
- Groundwater calibration with PEST. Wan (2015, Cornell PhD thesis) built DYCORS-PEST, a memetic method in which DYCORS decides when to hand over to PEST for local search and when to return to global search. On two groundwater calibration problems with 28 parameters each, the abstract says it "outperforms PEST by a large margin". The same thesis reports that a surrogate mixed-integer method (SO-MI) beat a genetic algorithm and NOMAD on groundwater remediation design, and that the surrogate multi-objective method GOMORS beat AMALGAM, Borg and NSGA-II. — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/775d6507-aa5c-462e-9b2a-b5bfa1087837)
- Groundwater remediation design. Singh (2011, Cornell PhD thesis) applied RBF methods to pump-and-treat optimization at the Umatilla Chemical Depot and Blaine Ammunition Depot Superfund sites. The abstract claims the RBF methods beat traditional and heuristic methods, including DDS, by about two orders of magnitude under limited budgets. — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/32659677-4d1b-4d10-9d8e-5b0526127801)
- Software. Eriksson, Bindel and Shoemaker (2019), "pySOT and POAP: an event-driven asynchronous framework for surrogate optimization". pySOT implements the stochastic RBF strategies in synchronous and asynchronous form. The authors measured close to linear speed-up on 4, 8 and 16 processors and found that the asynchronous advantage grows with the variance of run time. In a 2018 talk abstract the same group claimed their RBF methods consistently beat Gaussian-process expected improvement in 10 or more dimensions. That is the developers' own claim. — [arXiv 1908.00420](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00420); [IPAM abstract](https://www.ipam.ucla.edu/abstract/?tid=14272)
- Related hydrology method. Tolson and Shoemaker (2007) introduced dynamically dimensioned search for 6 to 30 parameters and 1,000 to 10,000 evaluations. On four SWAT calibrations with 14 to 30 parameters it beat shuffled complex evolution, in two cases with 15 to 20 percent of the evaluations. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR004723)

#### Surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms and surrogate reviews
- Jin (2011), "Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation: recent advances and future challenges", Swarm Evol. Comput. 1:61-70, is the standard review. Citation confirmed, content not read. — [Swarm Evol. Comput.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.swevo.2011.05.001)
- Razavi, Tolson and Burn (2012), "Review of surrogate modeling in water resources", WRR 48, W07401, separates response-surface surrogates from lower-fidelity physically based surrogates and gives guidance on framework choices. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR011527)
- Asher, Croke, Jakeman and Peeters (2015), "A review of surrogate models and their application to groundwater modeling", WRR 51:5957-5973, sorts surrogates into data-driven, projection-based and hierarchical. The authors warn that much data-driven work "seemingly ignores major drawbacks" and that only a fraction of the literature emulates fully distributed groundwater models. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR016967)
- Groundwater management. Siade, Cui, Karelse and Hampton (2020), WRR 56, e2019WR026061, cut the number of decision variables with a truncated singular value decomposition, trained a Gaussian process in the reduced space, and resampled adaptively with particle swarm. The surrogate for the Perth regional aquifer system runs in seconds. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019WR026061)
- Earlier groundwater examples include neural network plus genetic algorithm for pump-and-treat under uncertain conductivity (Aly and Peralta 1999), modular neural networks with evolutionary search for coastal pumping (Kourakos and Mantoglou 2009), and surrogate functions for stochastic pump-and-treat management (Baú and Mayer 2006). Citations confirmed. — [Aly and Peralta](https://doi.org/10.1029/98WR02368); [Kourakos and Mantoglou](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2009.01.001); [Baú and Mayer](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2006.01.008)

#### General-purpose software
- Dakota documents pattern search, a NOMAD-based mesh adaptive search, COBYLA, DIRECT, evolutionary algorithms, efficient global optimization and surrogate-based local and global minimization. Its guidance says surrogate-based methods "are often used to reduce the number of actual simulation runs" and suggests using derivative-free methods to find regions of interest before switching to gradient-based methods. — [Dakota 6.20 optimization guide](https://snl-dakota.github.io/docs/6.20.0/users/usingdakota/studytypes/optimization.html)

### Inferences
- A rough budget map follows from the benchmarks. With fewer than n+1 runs available, only DFO-LS in its growing mode, DFBGN and ensemble methods can move at all. With 2 to 20 simplex gradients and a smooth least-squares objective, model-based least-squares solvers are the first choice. With a few hundred runs, up to about 30 variables and suspected multimodality, RBF surrogate search is the first choice. Above a few hundred variables none of the classical derivative-free families is competitive, and ensemble-gradient or subspace methods take over.
- PEST with a finite-difference Jacobian is already a derivative-free Gauss-Newton method. What DFO-LS adds is reuse of old runs across iterations, a start before n+1 runs exist, and restarts for noisy model output. I found no published comparison of PEST or PESTPP-GLM against DFO-LS or POUNDERS on a groundwater model, so the size of the saving is unknown.
- Powell-type solvers are sequential. With several hundred parallel agents, a full parallel Jacobian finishes in the wall time of one run, so a method that saves total runs but proposes one point at a time can lose on wall clock. The case for DFO-LS is strongest when cores are far fewer than parameters or when solver noise corrupts finite differences.
- The DYCORS-PEST result suggests a cheap experiment: wrap PESTPP-GLM as the local phase of an RBF global search for calibrations with tens of parameters, such as reactive transport or zoned models.

## Bayesian optimization

### Takeaway
Bayesian optimization is the most sample-efficient family below about 20 dimensions with budgets of a few hundred runs, and it handles batches, black-box constraints, noise and several objectives. Trust-region variants reach 60 to 200 or more dimensions, but their published budgets are thousands of evaluations. Work from 2024 and 2025 shows the old 20-dimension ceiling was partly an artifact of Gaussian-process initialization. In groundwater the method shows up mostly as Gaussian-process surrogates inside evolutionary search, now including an option in PESTPP-MOU.

### Cited findings

#### Core method and reviews
- Jones, Schonlau and Welch (1998), "Efficient global optimization of expensive black-box functions", J. Global Optim. 13:455-492, introduced kriging with expected improvement. Shahriari et al. (2016), "Taking the human out of the loop: a review of Bayesian optimization", Proc. IEEE 104:148-175, and Frazier (2018), "A tutorial on Bayesian optimization", arXiv 1807.02811, are the standard reviews of acquisition functions, including expected improvement, knowledge gradient and entropy search. — [Jones et al.](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008306431147); [Shahriari et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/JPROC.2015.2494218); [Frazier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02811)
- Eriksson and Jankowiak (2021) quote Frazier's tutorial for the usual limit: successful applications are "often limited to low-dimensional problems, e.g. fewer than twenty dimensions". — [arXiv 2103.00349](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.00349)
- Turner et al. (2021) analysed the NeurIPS 2020 black-box optimization challenge, in which optimizers ran unattended on hidden objectives. The title states the result: "Bayesian optimization is superior to random search for machine learning hyperparameter tuning". — [PMLR 133:3-26](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v133/turner21a.html)

#### High-dimensional variants
- Eriksson, Pearce, Gardner, Turner and Poloczek (2019), "Scalable global optimization via local Bayesian optimization" (TuRBO), NeurIPS. TuRBO fits local Gaussian processes inside trust regions that double after 3 consecutive successes and halve after about d/q failures, where q is the batch size, and restarts when a region shrinks below a minimum. Test problems: 14 dimensions with 10,000 evaluations in batches of 50, 60 dimensions with 20,000 in batches of 100, 12 dimensions with 2,000 in batches of 50, and 200-dimensional Ackley with 10,000 in batches of 100. TuRBO beat the other Bayesian methods and CMA-ES and BOBYQA on all of them. — [ar5iv 1910.01739](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.01739); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/6c990b7aca7bc7058f5e98ea909e924b-Abstract.html)
- Eriksson and Jankowiak (2021), "High-dimensional Bayesian optimization with sparse axis-aligned subspaces" (SAASBO), UAI, PMLR 161:493-503. A sparsity prior on inverse lengthscales lets the model find the few variables that matter. The paper targets hundreds of variables with a few hundred evaluations and reports wins on a 100-dimensional rover problem, a 124-dimensional vehicle design problem and a 388-dimensional SVM problem. Inference uses Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, which is slow. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v161/eriksson21a.html); [Meta research note](https://research.fb.com/blog/2021/07/high-dimensional-bayesian-optimization-with-sparsity-inducing-priors/)
- Wang, Hutter, Zoghi, Matheson and de Freitas (2016), "Bayesian optimization in a billion dimensions via random embeddings", JAIR 55:361-387, is the random-embedding reference. Citation confirmed. — [JAIR](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.4806)
- Hvarfner, Hellsten and Nardi (2024), "Vanilla Bayesian optimization performs great in high dimensions", ICML, PMLR 235. Shifting the log-normal lengthscale prior by log(D)/2, so lengthscales grow like the square root of D, makes a standard Gaussian process with LogEI competitive without any special structure. Tests ran from 124 to 6,392 dimensions (MOPTA08, Lasso-DNA at 180, SVM at 388, MuJoCo Ant at 888, Humanoid at 6,392). The authors say the method works for "dimensionalities into the thousands" and that tailored methods remain warranted when structure is known. The SAASBO baseline was capped at 500 evaluations. — [arXiv 2402.02229](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02229v5)
- Papenmeier, Poloczek and Nardi (2025), "Understanding high-dimensional Bayesian optimization", ICML, PMLR 267:47902-47923. Vanishing gradients caused by Gaussian-process initialization explain many high-dimensional failures. Methods that behave like local search do better, and plain maximum-likelihood lengthscale estimation with a suitable start (their MSR variant) reaches state-of-the-art results. — [arXiv 2502.09198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09198)

#### Constraints, batches and multiple objectives
- Eriksson and Poloczek (2021), "Scalable constrained Bayesian optimization" (SCBO), AISTATS. SCBO uses Thompson sampling inside a trust region with transformed objective and constraints. Results include a 60-dimensional rover problem with 15 constraints, 5,000 evaluations and batches of 100, and the 124-dimensional MOPTA08 problem with 68 constraints, 2,000 evaluations and batches of 10, where SCBO found feasible points in 30 of 30 runs against 11 of 30 for COBYLA. On small engineering problems with 3 to 7 variables and 4 to 11 constraints it led within 100 evaluations. — [ar5iv 2002.08526](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.08526); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08526)
- Gramacy et al. (2016), "Modeling an augmented Lagrangian for blackbox constrained optimization", Technometrics 58:1-11, combine Gaussian-process surrogates, expected improvement and an augmented Lagrangian. The motivating case is a real-data hydrology benchmark where finding the feasible region is the hard part even though the objective is linear. — [Technometrics](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.2015.1014065); [arXiv 1403.4890](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4890)
- Knowles (2006), "ParEGO", IEEE Trans. Evol. Comput. 10:50-66, scalarizes objectives at random and applies expected improvement. Daulton, Balandat and Bakshy (2021), "Parallel Bayesian optimization of multiple noisy objectives with expected hypervolume improvement" (qNEHVI), NeurIPS, treat the Pareto front itself as uncertain under noise and cut the cost of batch hypervolume improvement from exponential to polynomial in batch size. — [ParEGO](https://doi.org/10.1109/TEVC.2005.851274); [qNEHVI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08195)
- Daulton, Eriksson, Balandat and Bakshy (2022), "Multi-objective Bayesian optimization over high-dimensional search spaces" (MORBO), UAI. Coordinated local trust regions gave order-of-magnitude gains in sample efficiency on problems with 146 and 222 parameters, where global-surrogate methods fail beyond a few dozen parameters. — [arXiv 2109.10964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10964)
- Multi-fidelity. Forrester, Sóbester and Keane (2007), "Multi-fidelity optimization via surrogate modelling", Proc. R. Soc. A 463:3251-3269, use co-kriging across levels of analysis. Peherstorfer, Willcox and Gunzburger (2018), SIAM Review 60:550-591, survey multifidelity methods under three strategies: adaptation, fusion and filtering. — [Forrester et al.](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2007.1900); [Peherstorfer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082469)
- Software. BoTorch with Ax ([botorch.org](https://botorch.org), [ax.dev](https://ax.dev)), Trieste ([GitHub](https://github.com/secondmind-labs/trieste)), SMAC3 ([GitHub](https://github.com/automl/SMAC3)), Dragonfly ([GitHub](https://github.com/dragonfly/dragonfly)), pySOT and Dakota's efficient_global method. The TuRBO, SAASBO, SCBO, MORBO and qNEHVI papers all come from the group that maintains BoTorch. — [BoTorch paper, arXiv 1910.06403](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06403)

- CO2 storage. ### Inferences
- A working dimension map for groundwater decisions. Up to about 20 decision variables, standard Gaussian-process methods with batch acquisition need on the order of 100 to 300 full-model runs. From 20 to about 100 variables, trust-region methods work but published budgets are 2,000 to 20,000 runs, which is the same order as an evolutionary run in PESTPP-MOU. Above 100 variables the options are a reduced decision space, as in Siade et al. (2020), or an ensemble-gradient method.
- The 2024 and 2025 results on vanilla Gaussian processes come from machine-learning and control benchmarks with cheap evaluations and budgets of hundreds to thousands. They move the ceiling but do not change the cost of each groundwater run.
- Carrying parameter uncertainty multiplies cost. If each candidate decision needs a stack of realizations, the unit of evaluation is the stack. Noisy acquisition functions allow evaluating each candidate on a random subset of realizations, and multi-fidelity methods allow coarse grids or short stacks early. Neither has been tested on groundwater management that I could find.
- Problems with hundreds of head or flux constraints do not suit one Gaussian process per constraint. The largest verified case is 68 constraints. Constraints would need aggregating, for example into the worst violation per group.
- For calibration, Bayesian optimization fits low-dimensional outer loops, such as regularization weights, variogram parameters or observation-weight schemes, better than the parameter field itself.

### Gaps
- I found no Bayesian optimization studies for mine dewatering, managed aquifer recharge or geothermal well fields. The search quota ended before I could look for geothermal work specifically.
- Evaluation budgets for the SAASBO and MORBO experiments are not in the abstracts I could read.
- Cost-aware and multi-fidelity acquisition functions (expected improvement per unit cost, multi-fidelity knowledge gradient, BOCA) are well known but I did not verify their papers in this session.
- No head-to-head test of BoTorch-style acquisition against PESTPP-MOU exists apart from Macasieb et al. (2025), who compare against particle swarm only.

## Ensemble- and stochastic-gradient methods

### Takeaway
EnOpt and StoSAG are the reservoir community's standard for robust life-cycle optimization with thousands of controls and around 100 geological realizations, at a cost per iteration of one ensemble of runs regardless of the number of controls. PEST++ has already imported the idea: PESTPP-SQP computes the same cross-covariance gradient and cites Chen et al. (2009), Dehdari and Oliver (2012) and Fonseca et al. The open questions are how well the gradient holds up when parameter uncertainty and control perturbations are mixed, and what a groundwater field case shows.

### Cited findings

#### Ensemble-based optimization in reservoir engineering
- Chen, Oliver and Zhang (2009), "Efficient ensemble-based closed-loop production optimization", SPE J. 14(4):634-645. EnOpt takes the gradient of expected net present value from the cross-covariance between random control perturbations and the objective. The authors state that the computation is "nearly independent of the number of control variables", is adjoint-free and works with any simulator. Combined with the ensemble Kalman filter in a closed loop, it raised net present value to a level comparable with optimization under known geology. — [SPE J.](https://doi.org/10.2118/112873-PA)
- Chen and Oliver (2010), "Ensemble-based closed-loop optimization applied to Brugge field", SPE Reserv. Eval. Eng. 13:56-71. The benchmark has 44,550 active cells, 20 producers and 10 injectors with three independently controlled completions each, over a 30-year life. — [SPE REE](https://doi.org/10.2118/118926-PA)
- Dehdari and Oliver (2012), "Sequential quadratic programming for solving constrained production optimization: case study from Brugge field", SPE J. 17(3):874-884. They combined an ensemble gradient with BFGS and used localization to improve the gradient from few realizations. The problem had 84 completion rates at each of 40 time steps (3,360 controls), 1,200 inequality constraints on well liquid rates and 3,360 nonnegativity constraints, plus pressure constraints. Localization and active-constraint handling raised both speed and final net present value. — [SPE J.](https://doi.org/10.2118/141589-PA)
- Fonseca, Chen, Jansen and Reynolds (2017), "A stochastic simplex approximate gradient (StoSAG) for optimization under uncertainty", Int. J. Numer. Methods Eng. 109:1756-1776. Pairing one random control vector with one random geological model, as in the original robust EnOpt, "does not always yield satisfactory results". StoSAG, earlier called modified robust EnOpt, gives much better gradients, and the paper explains why in theory. — [IJNME](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.5342)
- A University of Tulsa thesis reports that in two examples StoSAG produced life-cycle net present value 15 to 60 percent higher than EnOpt. It also builds a lexicographic StoSAG method that maximizes expected value first and then reduces risk, with CVaR among the risk measures compared. — [Tulsa thesis](https://tuprep.utulsa.edu/files/2024/03/Bailian_PhD-9a3adc24e9289bcc.pdf)
- Fonseca et al. (2015), "Ensemble-based multiobjective optimization of on/off control devices under geological uncertainty", SPE Reserv. Eval. Eng. 18:554-563. About 15 inflow control devices on each of three wells were re-expressed as switching times, which turns a discrete problem into a continuous one with fewer controls. Over 100 realizations the mean expected net present value rose 4.2 percent against a pressure-maintenance strategy. A sequential second objective gained 9.2 percent for a 1 percent loss in the first. — [SPE REE](https://doi.org/10.2118/173268-PA)
- Theory linking the methods. Do and Reynolds (2013), "Theoretical connections between optimization algorithms based on an approximate gradient", Comput. Geosci. 17:959-973, and Stordal, Szklarz and Leeuwenburgh (2016), "A theoretical look at ensemble-based optimization in reservoir management", Math. Geosci. 48:399-417. Citations confirmed, content not read. — [Do and Reynolds](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-013-9368-9); [Stordal et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-015-9598-6)
- CO2 storage application. Jeong, Sun, Jeon, Min and Jeong (2020), "Efficient ensemble-based stochastic gradient methods for optimization under geological uncertainty", Front. Earth Sci. 8:108, placed a brine extraction well for pressure management over 20 geological models. Plain EnOpt failed to reach satisfactory objective values because the objective varied so much between models. Their hybrid schemes cut cost by roughly 10 to 50 percent across the examples. — [Front. Earth Sci.](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2020.00108)
- Software. Everest, from Equinor and TNO, is open source under GPL-3.0. Its optimizer package ropt estimates gradients by perturbing controls and fitting a linear regression, combines results over realizations into one objective, and can call SciPy or Dakota. The standalone repository was archived in December 2024 and development moved into Equinor's ert. — [Everest repository](https://github.com/equinor/everest); [ropt](https://pypi.org/project/ropt)
- Benchmark. Fonseca et al. (2020) introduce the OLYMPUS field development optimization challenge in a special issue of Computational Geosciences. Citation confirmed. — [Comput. Geosci.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-020-10003-4)

#### Simultaneous perturbation, random directions and adaptive steps
- Spall (1992), "Multivariate stochastic approximation using a simultaneous perturbation gradient approximation", IEEE Trans. Autom. Control 37:332-341, estimates a gradient from two objective evaluations per iteration whatever the dimension. — [IEEE TAC](https://doi.org/10.1109/9.119632)
- Nesterov and Spokoiny (2017), "Random gradient-free minimization of convex functions", Found. Comput. Math. 17:527-566, show that random-direction methods need at most about n times more iterations than gradient methods, where n is the dimension. — [FoCM](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-015-9296-2)
- Arouri and Sayyafzadeh (2022), "An adaptive moment estimation framework for well placement optimization", Comput. Geosci. 26:957-973, put Adam on top of a stochastic gradient approximation with nonlinear constraints. On a four-vertical-well case and a 20-well case with deviated and horizontal wells it beat steepest descent and generalized pattern search in both efficiency and final value. — [Comput. Geosci.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-022-10135-9)
- Bangerth, Klie, Wheeler, Stoffa and Sen (2006), "On optimization algorithms for the reservoir oil well placement problem", Comput. Geosci. 10:303-319, is the usual reference for SPSA in well placement. Citation confirmed, content not read. — [Comput. Geosci.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-006-9025-7)
- Random-subspace least squares is covered by DFBGN in the first section. — [Cartis and Roberts 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-022-01836-1)

#### How this relates to PEST++
- PESTPP-SQP estimates the gradient as the pseudo-inverse of the decision-variable covariance times the cross-covariance between decision-variable anomalies and objective anomalies, citing Chen et al. (2009), Dehdari and Oliver (2012) and Fonseca et al. (2015). Anomalies are taken about a base realization, the current best estimate, and not about the ensemble mean as in PESTPP-IES. A truncated SVD stabilizes the pseudo-inverse. — [PEST++ manual, section 14.2.2](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- The Hessian update is BFGS with Powell damping by default, with SR1 and a "STOSAG (ensemble-approximated curvature)" option. Steps are accepted by a filter on objective and constraint violation after a line search over fixed multipliers (defaults 0.001 to 1.0), evaluated on a subset of the ensemble. The decision-variable ensemble is regenerated each iteration with a CMA-inspired covariance update. After three consecutive infeasible iterations by default the code switches to an IES-based feasibility recovery. — [PEST++ manual, sections 14.1 to 14.2.4 and control variable table](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Uncertainty in PESTPP-SQP. Each ensemble member holds decision variables and uncertain parameters, and constraints are evaluated at a reliability level set by sqp_risk, at one representative member or at all members. — [PEST++ manual, section 14.2.2](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Macasieb, Knowling and White (2025), MODSIM 2025 extended abstract, describe PEST++SQP as supporting finite-difference and ensemble gradients with an active-set solver, and say SQP "has been proven in the petroleum industry" for very high-dimensional problems. The abstract promises a groundwater management demonstration with many decision variables and nonlinear constraints but gives no numbers. — [MODSIM 2025](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2025/files/A04.macasieb.pdf)
- The GMDSI notebooks repository has tutorials for PESTPP-OPT and PESTPP-MOU (tutorials/part2_08_opt and part2_09_mou) and none for PESTPP-SQP. — [GMDSI notebooks](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks)

### Inferences
- The cost structure is the reason to care. One SQP iteration costs one ensemble plus a short line search, whatever the number of decision variables. PESTPP-OPT needs one run per decision variable per SLP iteration to fill the response matrix. For time-varying rates at many wells, with hundreds to thousands of variables, the ensemble method is the only one in the suite whose cost does not grow with the variable count.
- The StoSAG lesson may apply to PESTPP-SQP. When uncertain parameters vary across the same ensemble that carries the control perturbations, parameter-driven spread in the objective contaminates the gradient. That is the failure Fonseca et al. describe for one-to-one pairing and Jeong et al. observe. The manual does not say how control perturbations are paired with parameter realizations. That detail deserves a direct question to the developers and a test.
- Three reservoir fixes look portable. Localization would zero the sensitivity of a constraint to pumping that happens later in time or far away. Reparameterization, such as switching times or a few schedule shape parameters, shrinks the control space. Adam-style step scaling replaces the fixed list of line-search multipliers.
- Ensemble gradients are averaged, smoothed gradients. That helps with solver noise and small discontinuities such as cells going dry, which is where finite differences in PESTPP-OPT struggle.

### Gaps
- I found no journal paper applying EnOpt or StoSAG to groundwater, geothermal or dewatering problems. The MODSIM 2025 abstract is the only groundwater evidence and it has no results.
- My recollection is that Stordal et al. (2016) show EnOpt to be a special case of a natural evolution strategy with Gaussian mutation, and that Do and Reynolds (2013) relate EnOpt, SPSA and simplex gradients. I could not read either paper, so treat both statements as unverified.
- Variance-reduced stochastic gradient methods from machine learning (SVRG, SAGA) were not searched. In robust optimization the analogue would be sampling a subset of realizations per iteration with a control variate, and I know of no subsurface test.

## Structured nonlinear programming

### Takeaway
Classical groundwater management already is structured programming: a response matrix from superposition, then linear programming, with sequential linearization for mild nonlinearity. GWM and PESTPP-OPT both work this way. The modern equivalent keeps that structure and swaps in stronger parts: algebraic modeling languages, interior-point and SQP solvers, mixed-integer solvers, and reduced-order or learned surrogates managed by a trust region against the full model.

### Cited findings

#### The classical groundwater formulation
- Gorelick (1983), "A review of distributed parameter groundwater management modeling methods", WRR 19:305-319, is the founding review of simulation-optimization for aquifers. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR019i002p00305)
- GWM-2005 (Ahlfeld, Barlow and Mulligan) uses a response-matrix approach. Linear formulations go to the simplex method, nonlinear ones (water-table aquifers, head-dependent boundaries) to sequential linear programming with response coefficients recomputed each iteration, and mixed-binary ones to branch and bound. Decision variables are flow rates, external variables and binary on/off variables. Constraints cover heads, drawdowns, gradients, streamflow and streamflow depletion. — [GWM-2005 summary](https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/mf2005_gwm/Summary_gwm2005.txt); [USGS OFR 2005-1072](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2005/1072/)
- PESTPP-OPT fills its response matrix by finite differences on decision variables and solves the linear program with the COIN-OR CLP simplex library, which "can handle hundreds of thousands of decision-variables". The manual notes that recomputing the matrix each iteration is time-consuming when decision variables are many. With an existing Jacobian and restart file it can re-solve the chance-constrained program with zero model runs, to explore changed bounds, constraints or risk. — [PEST++ manual, sections 8.1.4 and 8.2.14](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- White, Fienen, Barlow and Welter (2018), "A tool for efficient, model-independent management optimization under uncertainty", Environ. Model. Softw. 100:213-221, is the PESTPP-OPT paper, demonstrated on a synthetic integrated surface-water and groundwater model. — [EMS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2017.11.019)
- Zheng and Wang (1999), WRR 35:137-148, paired tabu search for discrete well locations with linear programming for rates, and used superposition so that a perturbed solution is the base solution plus a correction obtained without rerunning the flow model. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/1998WR900032)
- Gorelick and Zheng (2015), "Global change and the groundwater management challenge", WRR 51:3031-3051, review 50 years of simulation-optimization and argue that depletion trajectories, subsidence, ecosystems, seawater intrusion and supply vulnerability call for a new generation of management models. Yeh (2015), Hydrogeol. J. 23:1051-1065, reviews optimization methods for groundwater modeling and management (citation confirmed). — [Gorelick and Zheng](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014WR016825); [Yeh](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10040-015-1260-3)

#### General nonlinear programming solvers
- Wächter and Biegler (2006), "On the implementation of an interior-point filter line-search algorithm for large-scale nonlinear programming", Math. Programming 106:25-57, is the IPOPT reference. Gill, Murray and Saunders (2005), "SNOPT: an SQP algorithm for large-scale constrained optimization", SIAM Review 47:99-131, is the SNOPT reference. Byrd, Nocedal and Waltz (2006) describe KNITRO. Nocedal and Wright (2006), "Numerical Optimization", covers the theory of SQP, interior-point, augmented Lagrangian and filter methods. — [IPOPT](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-004-0559-y); [SNOPT](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036144504446096); [KNITRO](https://doi.org/10.1007/0-387-30065-1_4); [Nocedal and Wright](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5)
- Boyd, Parikh, Chu, Peleato and Eckstein (2011), "Distributed optimization and statistical learning via the alternating direction method of multipliers", Found. Trends Mach. Learn. 3:1-122, is the ADMM reference. — [FnT ML](https://doi.org/10.1561/2200000016)
- Modeling layers. JuMP 1.0 (Lubin et al. 2023, Math. Prog. Comp. 15:581-589) and Pyomo ([pyomo.org](https://www.pyomo.org)) express a problem once and pass it to open or commercial solvers. OMLT ([GitHub](https://github.com/cog-imperial/OMLT)) embeds trained neural networks and tree models as constraints in Pyomo. — [JuMP 1.0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12532-023-00239-3)

#### Optimization with embedded reduced-order or surrogate models
- McPhee and Yeh (2008), "Groundwater management using model reduction via empirical orthogonal functions", J. Water Resour. Plan. Manage. 134:161-170, is an early projection-based reduced model inside a groundwater management problem. Citation confirmed. — [JWRPM](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9496(2008)134:2(161))
- Siade, Putti and Yeh (2012), WRR 48, W06502, linearized the governing equations in the parameters and solved a sequence of quadratic programs on a proper-orthogonal-decomposition reduced model. Snapshots are computed once. The method needs one linearized full-model run per parameter at the first iteration, then only reduced quadratic programs. The test model had about 30,000 nodes and up to 15 conductivity zones. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR011471)
- Reservoir practice. Jansen and Durlofsky (2016), "Use of reduced-order models in well control optimization", Optim. Eng. 18:105-132, review the field. Cardoso and Durlofsky (2010), J. Comput. Phys. 229:681-700, give linearized reduced-order models. Jin, Liu and Durlofsky (2020), J. Pet. Sci. Eng. 192:107273, give a deep-learning surrogate for simulation with time-varying well controls. Citations confirmed. — [Jansen and Durlofsky](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11081-016-9313-6); [Cardoso and Durlofsky](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2009.10.004); [Jin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.petrol.2020.107273)
- Convergence safeguards. Alexandrov, Dennis, Lewis and Torczon (1998), "A trust-region framework for managing the use of approximation models in optimization", Struct. Optim. 15:16-23, and Qian, Grepl, Veroy and Willcox (2017), "A certified trust region reduced basis approach to PDE-constrained optimization", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 39:S434-S460, show how to optimize on a cheap model while checking against the expensive one so that the result converges to the expensive model's optimum. Citations confirmed. — [Alexandrov et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01197433); [Qian et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1081981)
- Optimal control with adjoints is the reservoir baseline against which EnOpt is judged: Brouwer and Jansen (2004), SPE J. 9:391-402; Sarma, Durlofsky, Aziz and Chen (2006), Comput. Geosci. 10:3-36; Jansen (2011) review, Computers and Fluids 46:40-51. Not covered in depth here by assignment. — [Brouwer and Jansen](https://doi.org/10.2118/78278-PA); [Sarma et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-005-9009-z); [Jansen](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compfluid.2010.09.039)

### Inferences
- The response matrix is the most reusable asset a PEST++ user already owns. Once the Jacobian of constraints with respect to decision variables exists, and a stack of constraint anomalies exists, the same numbers can feed formulations PESTPP-OPT does not offer: CVaR objectives, joint chance constraints, Wasserstein-robust constraints, fixed-charge well selection, multi-period smoothing penalties. Each is a linear, mixed-integer or conic program in Pyomo or JuMP and costs no further model runs. An outer SLP loop restores accuracy for mild nonlinearity.
- Most groundwater surrogate-optimization papers optimize on the surrogate and stop. The trust-region model-management literature shows how to keep the full model in the loop with a convergence guarantee. That answers the criticism in Asher et al. (2015).
- Interior-point and SQP solvers need exact first derivatives to work well. With a MODFLOW-class simulator those come from adjoints or automatic differentiation, which other researchers cover, or from an embedded surrogate. Without them, PESTPP-SQP's ensemble gradient is the practical route.

### Gaps
- I found no groundwater applications of ADMM or other proximal splitting methods, and no convex relaxations of unconfined or density-dependent management problems. Both searches were cut short by the quota.
- I did not verify any study that exports a MODFLOW response matrix into Pyomo or JuMP. The idea is an inference from the structure of GWM and PESTPP-OPT.
- Hinze et al. (2009), "Optimization with PDE constraints" ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4020-8839-1)), is the standard text on optimal control of PDEs. Confirmed as a book record only.

## Discrete and mixed-integer problems

### Takeaway
Exact mixed-integer programming is available today for the linear response-matrix case, and GWM has offered it since 2005. For nonlinear simulators the working options are hybrids that separate discrete and continuous variables, surrogate-based mixed-integer search, and reparameterizations that remove the integers. For monitoring design, submodularity theory gives greedy selection a guarantee of 63 percent of the optimum for the right objective, and the greedy data-worth loop in pyEMU is the same algorithm without the certificate.

### Cited findings
- GWM-2005 handles binary on/off variables for wells and external sources and solves mixed-binary linear formulations by branch and bound. — [GWM-2005 summary](https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/mf2005_gwm/Summary_gwm2005.txt)
- McKinney and Lin (1995), "Approximate mixed-integer nonlinear programming methods for optimal aquifer remediation design", WRR 31:731-740, replaced discontinuous fixed costs with polynomial penalty functions so that a nonlinear programming solver could handle them. Including fixed costs changed the design and its cost materially. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/94WR02851)
- Zheng and Wang (1999) split the problem: tabu search over discrete well locations, linear programming over rates. Zheng and Wang (2002), Ground Water 40:258-266, ran evolutionary search with response functions in parallel with trial-and-error design for a large TCE plume at the Massachusetts Military Reservation. — [1999](https://doi.org/10.1029/1998WR900032); [2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2002.tb02653.x)
- Bayer and Finkel (2004) treated well positions and rates as a mixed discrete-continuous problem solved with evolution strategies. — [WRR](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003WR002675)
- Surrogate-based mixed-integer search. Wan (2015) reports that SO-MI beat a genetic algorithm and NOMAD on groundwater remediation design. ### Inferences
- With a response matrix, well siting with fixed costs is a mixed-integer linear program. Open solvers handle thousands of binaries. The PESTPP-OPT sections I read describe only continuous linear programming through CLP, so adding binaries means exporting the matrix to another modeling layer or using GWM.
- The greedy pyEMU loop maximizes reduction in one forecast variance. That objective is not guaranteed submodular, so the 1 minus 1/e bound does not transfer automatically. Krause's online bound can still be computed for any monotone objective after the fact to say how far the greedy set could be from optimal, and lazy evaluation would cut the number of Schur-complement updates. Both are small additions to an existing workflow.
- Switching-time and policy-parameter reformulations are the cheapest way to keep ensemble-gradient or least-squares machinery when the natural decision is on/off.

### Gaps
- I found no study solving a mixed-integer nonlinear program to proven optimality on a full groundwater simulator. The literature I could verify relies on hybrids and heuristics.
- The quota ended before I could search for branch-and-bound with surrogates in subsurface problems.

## Multi-objective optimization

### Takeaway
PESTPP-MOU covers the two-to-three-objective case with NSGA-II and SPEA-2 style selection and now has a Gaussian-process surrogate mode. Published groundwater runs show the cost difference clearly: about 86,000 to 89,000 model runs for a nine-well problem with evolutionary search under uncertainty, against 500 full-model runs for a ten-variable problem with the surrogate mode. Many-objective algorithms built on epsilon-dominance and reference directions (Borg, NSGA-III, MOEA/D) and multi-objective Bayesian optimization are the pieces not yet in the suite.

### Inferences
- For two or three objectives, up to about 20 decision variables and runs of hours, surrogate-assisted or Bayesian multi-objective methods should cut full-model runs by one to two orders of magnitude relative to direct evolutionary search. ## Optimization under uncertainty and robust decision-making

### Takeaway
PEST++'s FOSM chance constraint is the Wagner and Gorelick (1987) design, and its stack option is the multiple-realization method, which operations research calls sample average approximation of a chance constraint. Both are sound and both have known weaknesses. FOSM assumes linearity and normality and was off by a factor of 2.6 in one published seawater-intrusion comparison. The stack quantile has sampling error, treats constraints one at a time, and controls a quantile where a tail-average measure such as CVaR would be convex and better behaved. Stochastic programming supplies sample-size rules, CVaR, joint chance constraints, distributional robustness and recourse. Deep-uncertainty methods cover what has no probability attached.

### Cited findings

#### What PEST++ does
- FOSM chance constraints. PESTPP-OPT computes the posterior variance of each constraint output from the prior parameter covariance, the calibration Jacobian and the sensitivity of the constraint output to parameters (the Schur-complement formula, equations 8.1 to 8.3). It assumes outputs are normally distributed and shifts each constraint by the quantile matching the user's risk value. Risk 0.5 is risk neutral and skips the uncertainty calculation. — [PEST++ manual, sections 8.1.3, 8.1.5 and 8.2.8](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- The manual states the limits: recalculating the parameter Jacobian during the run "constitutes only partial accommodation" of nonlinearity, because parameters are not varied over their posterior range and the equations "assume model linearity with respect to these parameters". — [PEST++ manual, section 8.2.10](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Stack-based chance constraints. A parameter ensemble, possibly from PESTPP-IES, is run at the current decision variables. For each constraint the code sorts that column of the output stack and takes the value at the index matching the risk, for example the 95th of 100 for risk 0.95. This drops the linearity and normality assumptions. When a stack is reused in later iterations, the anomalies about the mean are added to the current constraint value. — [PEST++ manual, section 8.1.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- PESTPP-MOU evaluates the stack at every individual or at one representative point, can reuse it across generations, and moves the distributions between points in decision space by inverse-distance weighting. Running the stack at every individual in every generation means "running the full parameter stacks hundreds if not thousands of times". — [PEST++ manual, section 13.2.2](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- A published comparison. On the island aquifer of Coulon et al. (2024), the stack-based evolutionary analysis allowed 98 m3/d at a 6 percent probability of salinization, where the earlier SLP with FOSM analysis had allowed 260 m3/d. Adding climate projections to 2050 cut allowable pumping at 10 percent probability from 165 to 60 m3/d, and no solution had less than 8 percent probability. — [HESS](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-303-2024)

- Wagner and Gorelick (1987), "Optimal groundwater quality management under parameter uncertainty", WRR 23:1162-1174, coupled nonlinear regression, first-order second-moment analysis and chance-constrained optimization, and checked the first-order assumptions with 2,000 Monte Carlo simulations. - Wagner and Gorelick (1989), WRR 25:2211-2225, introduced the multiple-realization model, which solves one design against many conditional conductivity realizations at once. - Chan (1993), "Robustness of the multiple realization method for stochastic hydraulic aquifer management", WRR 29:3159-3167. Reliability rises monotonically with the number N of stacked realizations and is nearly insensitive to variance, correlation length and boundary conditions. ### Inferences
- How the pieces line up. FOSM chance constraints are a Gaussian, linearized, constraint-by-constraint special case. The stack quantile is a sample approximation of individual chance constraints. Neither bounds the probability that all constraints hold together, which is usually what a regulator means by reliability. Joint reliability can be estimated from the same stack by counting realizations that satisfy every constraint.
- Sample size. Estimating a 95th percentile from 100 realizations is noisy, and optimizing against a sample biases the realized reliability downward. Chan's N/(N+1) rule and scenario theory both say that confident claims at the 95 to 99 percent level need hundreds of realizations, or an independent validation stack. Running the final design through a fresh stack is the cheapest safeguard.
- CVaR fits the existing machinery. With a response matrix and stack anomalies, a CVaR constraint or objective is a linear program with one auxiliary variable per realization (Rockafellar and Uryasev). It penalizes how badly constraints are violated in the tail, which a quantile ignores, and it gives a convex conservative stand-in for a chance constraint (Nemirovski and Shapiro).
- Distributional robustness answers a worry specific to ensemble methods. Posterior ensembles from an iterative smoother can be too narrow. A Wasserstein ball around the stack hedges against that, and in the linear response setting the robust problem remains a linear or conic program.
- The reservoir evidence that robust strategies beat nominal ones by several percent in the mean and by a lot in variance came from adjoint gradients. The same formulation with ensemble gradients is what PESTPP-SQP offers.
- Climate and demand scenarios have no agreed probabilities. The Coulon result shows they can move the answer more than parameter uncertainty does. Robust decision making or adaptive pathways on top of a PESTPP-MOU front is the natural layering: probabilistic risk for parameters, scenario stress-testing for the rest.
- Linear data worth measures variance reduction. Decision-focused value of information measures the improvement in the optimized objective. Feyen and Gorelick did the latter for groundwater in 2005, and with a response matrix and a zero-run re-solve it is cheap to repeat inside PEST++.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the explicit scenario-approach sample-size formula. From memory, a sufficient N grows like (2/epsilon)(ln(1/beta) + d), with d decision variables, violation level epsilon and confidence 1 minus beta. Check against Campi, Garatti and Prandini (2009, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arcontrol.2009.07.001)) before use.
- I found no groundwater application of Wasserstein or moment-based distributionally robust optimization, and no CVaR formulation in the PEST++ manual sections I read.
- From memory, Bayer et al. (2008) introduce "stack ordering", which evaluates the most critical realizations first and skips the rest once a design fails. I could confirm only the citation.
- Whether PESTPP-SQP's "all" chance-point option gives individual or joint constraint reliability is not stated in the manual text I read.
- I found no application of dynamic adaptive policy pathways to groundwater management.

## Control and sequential decision-making

### Takeaway
Reservoir engineering runs optimization in a closed loop with data assimilation and has measured the gain from feedback: a learned policy beat open-loop robust optimization by 15 to 33 percent in net present value in one benchmark study. Groundwater had feedback and dual-control formulations around 1990 and a real-time managed well field in Zurich by 2010, then largely stopped. Stochastic dual dynamic programming is established for conjunctive use where the problem is convex. Deep reinforcement learning for aquifers is at the toy stage.

Rostampour, Jaxa-Rozen, Bloemendal, Kwakkel and Keviczky (2019), Applied Energy 242:624-639, apply distributed stochastic model predictive control to neighbouring storage systems that share subsurface volume. Coordination raised energy savings per unit of subsurface volume without hurting thermal performance, and a companion paper validated the control framework against a MODFLOW simulation. — [Applied Energy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2019.03.110); [TU Delft record](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/probabilistic-energy-management-for-building-climate-comfort-in-s)
- Related water systems. A 2023 TU Delft study applied scenario-based stochastic model predictive control to polder pumping stations under uncertain inflow and hourly electricity prices. — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:248d4da7-4d4d-4c73-8c08-e79228384f01)

#### Direct policy search and reinforcement learning
- Castelletti, Galelli, Restelli and Soncini-Sessa (2010), "Tree-based reinforcement learning for optimal water reservoir operation", WRR 46, W09507, used fitted Q-iteration to avoid the fine state grid that stochastic dynamic programming needs. Giuliani, Castelletti, Pianosi, Mason and Reed (2016), J. Water Resour. Plan. Manage. 142(2):04015050, parameterize the operating policy and search its parameters with a many-objective evolutionary algorithm. — [Castelletti et al.](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008898); [Giuliani et al.](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)WR.1943-5452.0000570)
- Nasir and Durlofsky (2023), "Deep reinforcement learning for optimal well control in subsurface systems with uncertain geology", J. Comput. Phys. 477:111945. They pose closed-loop management as a partially observable decision process and train a policy with proximal policy optimization on an ensemble of prior models from several geological scenarios. The policy maps observed well data to bottom-hole pressure settings. Net present value rose 15 percent in 2D and 33 percent in 3D relative to robust optimization over the prior models, and 4 percent on average relative to traditional closed-loop management, with 76 percent less computation than the latter. A follow-up extends the method across multiple assets. — [JCP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.111945); [arXiv 2203.13375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13375); [arXiv 2207.10376](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10376v1.pdf)
- Groundwater examples are small. Biniyaz, Azmoon and Liu (2022) trained a deep Q-network to set pump rates that hold groundwater near a target in a slope during rainfall and compared it with PID control. A 2024 Frontiers in Water study applies reinforcement learning to a rule-based aquifer management simulation. — [Sensors 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9657859/); [Frontiers in Water 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frwa.2024.1384595/full)
- Adjacent remediation tooling. PNNL's November 2025 report describes a pump-and-treat design tool built on a U-Net emulator of a flow and transport simulator, still in development. — [PNNL report](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/web-based-tools-data-informed-remedy-optimization-software-theory-and-user-guide)

#### Stochastic dual dynamic programming and approximate dynamic programming
- Pereira and Pinto (1991), "Multi-stage stochastic optimization applied to energy planning", Math. Programming 52:359-375, introduced the method, which builds piecewise-linear cost-to-go functions from stage duals and requires convexity and stage-wise independent noise. Shapiro (2011), Eur. J. Oper. Res. 209:63-72, analysed it. Dowson and Kapelevich (2021), INFORMS J. Comput. 33:27-33, provide the open-source SDDP.jl. — [Pereira and Pinto](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01582895); [Shapiro](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2010.08.007); [SDDP.jl](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2020.0987); [review](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/SDDP-Review.pdf)
- Conjunctive use. Macian-Sorribes, Tilmant and Pulido-Velazquez (2017), WRR 53:1407-1423, extended the method with an embedded multireservoir model so that stream-aquifer interaction is represented inside the stochastic optimization of a large surface-groundwater system. — [record](https://core.ac.uk/works/11825086)
- Where convexity fails. Davidsen, Liu, Mo, Rosbjerg and Bauer-Gottwein (2016), HESS 20:771-785, optimized conjunctive use in the Ziya River basin by stochastic dynamic programming. Head-dependent pumping costs make the problem nonconvex, so they solved the one-step subproblems with a genetic algorithm. The minimum cost of ending overdraft came to CNY 5.58 billion per year. — [HESS](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-20-771-2016)
- Powell (2011), "Approximate Dynamic Programming", is the reference text for value-function approximation. — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118029176)

### Inferences
- A fixed pumping schedule from PESTPP-OPT or PESTPP-MOU is an open-loop answer. Under uncertainty, a rule that reacts to observed heads or salinity should do better, and the Nasir and Durlofsky numbers give the size of that effect in one setting. Groundwater responds slowly and is sparsely monitored, so the gain may be smaller, but trigger-level rules are already how many well fields and dewatering systems are run.
- Direct policy search needs no new optimizer. Make the trigger levels and cutback fractions the decision variables, simulate the rule inside the model, and let PESTPP-MOU or PESTPP-SQP search them over the stack. This is the Giuliani et al. approach with a groundwater model in place of a reservoir system model.
- The PEST++ suite has the two halves of closed-loop management in separate programs: sequential data assimilation (the GMDSI tutorial part2_07_da covers PESTPP-DA) and optimization. Cycling them is what reservoir engineers call closed-loop reservoir management, and with a response matrix each control step is a small linear or quadratic program.
- Stochastic dual dynamic programming suits long-horizon conjunctive use when aquifer response enters through linear response functions. Unconfined behaviour, head-dependent costs and on/off thresholds break convexity, as Davidsen et al. found.
- Deep reinforcement learning needs tens of thousands to millions of simulations. It is practical for groundwater only with a fast surrogate, and the surrogate's errors then become the main risk.

### Gaps
- I found no published stochastic model predictive control of a groundwater well field or dewatering system with a distributed model, apart from the thermal storage work. The Zurich papers are about real-time assimilation and control criteria, and I could not read the control details.
- I found nothing on mine dewatering or managed aquifer recharge control within the search budget.
- Training-run counts for Nasir and Durlofsky are not in the abstract.
- A 2025 Scientific Reports paper combining physics-informed networks with reinforcement learning for remediation appeared in one search result. I could not confirm its identifier, so I left it out of the findings.

## Global optimization and non-convexity in calibration

### Takeaway
Three imported ideas address local minima in history matching without resorting to a full global search: restarts and multistart wrapped around a fast local solver, misfit functions that are convex in timing shifts (optimal transport) or resistant to outliers (L1, Huber), and coarse-to-fine or multi-fidelity schemes with a trust-region safeguard. Each exists in hydrogeology or surface hydrology in a small way. None is routine in PEST workflows.

### Cited findings
- Multistart. Rinnooy Kan and Timmer (1987), "Stochastic global optimization methods part II: multi level methods", Math. Programming 39:57-78, is the multi-level single linkage reference, and NLopt implements it around any local solver. Larson and Wild (2018), "Asynchronously parallel optimization solver for finding multiple minima", Math. Prog. Comp. 10:303-332, run many local searches concurrently and return several minima. — [Rinnooy Kan and Timmer](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02592071); [NLopt](https://nlopt.readthedocs.io/en/latest/NLopt_Algorithms/); [Larson and Wild](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12532-017-0131-4)
- Restarts inside a local solver. DFO-LS detects stagnation and restarts automatically, which was its most effective mechanism under noise. — [Cartis et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3338517)
- Basin hopping. Wales and Doye (1997), J. Phys. Chem. A 101:5111-5116, alternate random perturbation with local minimization and accept or reject on the minimized value. — [J. Phys. Chem. A](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp970984n)
- Global search with a local solver inside. The multistart local RBF method of Regis and Shoemaker (2007) and the DYCORS-PEST hybrid of Wan (2015) are the groundwater examples. — [Regis and Shoemaker](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.1060.0182); [Wan](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/775d6507-aa5c-462e-9b2a-b5bfa1087837)
- Optimal-transport misfits in geophysics. Engquist and Froese (2014), Commun. Math. Sci. 12:979-988, proposed the Wasserstein metric for seismic signals. Métivier, Brossier, Mérigot, Oudet and Virieux (2016), Geophys. J. Int. 205:345-377, and Yang, Engquist, Sun and Hamfeldt (2018), Geophysics 83:R43-R62, applied it to full-waveform inversion. Least-squares misfit suffers from cycle skipping, which traps the inversion in local minima. The quadratic Wasserstein metric measures global phase shifts as well as amplitude and is better behaved in convexity and noise sensitivity. — [Engquist and Froese](https://doi.org/10.4310/CMS.2014.v12.n5.a7); [Métivier et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggw014); [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1190/geo2016-0663.1)
- Optimal-transport misfits in hydrology. Magyar and Sambridge (2023), "Hydrological objective functions and ensemble averaging with the Wasserstein distance", HESS 27:991-1010, adapted the distance to hydrographs in two ways and showed in calibration that it accounts for timing errors caused by imprecise rainfall. Code is archived on Zenodo. Ehret and Zehe (2011), HESS 15:877-896, proposed the series distance for the same timing problem. — [Magyar and Sambridge](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-27-991-2023); [Ehret and Zehe](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-15-877-2011)
- Robust norms. Finsterle and Najita (1998), "Robust estimation of hydrogeologic model parameters", WRR 34:2939-2947, tested alternatives to least squares by Monte Carlo on a synthetic experiment with non-Gaussian errors and systematic model errors, then on a gas-pressure-pulse-decay test. Robust estimators reduced bias from noisy data and minor systematic errors. Farquharson and Oldenburg (1998), Geophys. J. Int. 134:213-227, give the general-measure formulation used in geophysical inversion (citation confirmed). — [Finsterle and Najita](https://doi.org/10.1029/98WR02174); [Farquharson and Oldenburg](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-246x.1998.00555.x)
- Sparse or regularized derivative-free least squares. DFO-LS accepts an L1 regularizer, documented in Liu, Lam and Roberts (2024). — [DFO-LS docs](https://numericalalgorithmsgroup.github.io/dfols/build/html/index.html); [arXiv 2407.14915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14915)
- Multiscale continuation. Bunks, Saleck, Zaleski and Chavent (1995), "Multiscale seismic waveform inversion", Geophysics 60:1457-1473, is the reference for solving a sequence of problems from smooth to detailed (citation confirmed). — [Geophysics](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1443880)
- Multilevel and multi-fidelity optimization. Lewis and Nash (2005), SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 26:1811-1837, study multigrid optimization for systems governed by differential equations. March and Willcox (2012), AIAA J. 50:1079-1089, give a provably convergent multi-fidelity method that needs no high-fidelity derivatives. Peherstorfer, Willcox and Gunzburger (2018) survey the field, with the premise that low-fidelity models give speed while the high-fidelity model stays in the loop for accuracy or convergence guarantees. — [Lewis and Nash](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827502407792); [March and Willcox](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.J051125); [Peherstorfer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082469)

### Inferences
- The groundwater data types most prone to cycle-skipping-like local minima are breakthrough curves, pumping-test responses and seasonal head fluctuations, where a timing error makes the least-squares surface flat or multimodal. In one dimension the 1-Wasserstein distance equals the integrated absolute difference between cumulative curves. Cumulative-mass observations and arrival-time quantiles, which PEST users already add as processed observations, are therefore a partial version of the same idea. A full transport misfit could be supplied the same way, as a post-processed observation group.
- Huber or L1 fitting can be had by iteratively reweighting observations between PEST iterations. That is a short pyEMU script, and it targets the outliers and structural misfit that otherwise drive parameter compensation.
- A coarse-to-fine schedule for highly parameterized inversion could vary grid resolution, solver tolerance or pilot-point density. The trust-region rule from the multi-fidelity literature, which accepts a step only if the fine model confirms enough of the predicted decrease, would turn a common ad hoc practice into one with a convergence argument.
- Multistart is nearly free with a parallel run manager. Several GLM solves from different prior realizations also tell the modeller whether the minimum is unique, which bears on how much to trust linear uncertainty analysis.

### Gaps
- I found no groundwater calibration study using an optimal-transport misfit, and none using homotopy or graduated non-convexity under those names. The quota ended before a dedicated search.
- Aliyev and Durlofsky's multilevel field-development optimization with a sequence of upscaled models, which I recall as Math. Geosci. 2017, would be the closest reservoir analogue for coarse-to-fine optimization under uncertainty. The DOI I tried did not resolve, so the reference is unverified.
- Whether iterative-ensemble-smoother posteriors behave like a multistart search in multimodal problems belongs to the Bayesian-methods researchers and is not covered here.

## Ranked shortlist of transfer opportunities

### Takeaway
For management, the highest value for the least effort is to reuse the response matrix and parameter stack that PESTPP-OPT already computes inside richer mathematical programs (CVaR, joint chance, distributionally robust, mixed-integer), followed by hardening PESTPP-SQP with the StoSAG lessons and adopting trust-region Bayesian optimization for small expensive problems. For calibration, the best-supported transfers are structured derivative-free least squares and an RBF global wrapper around PEST, then alternative misfit functions.

### Cited findings

#### Groundwater management and decision optimization, ranked
1. Richer programs on the existing response matrix and stack. A CVaR objective or constraint is a linear program over scenarios, Wasserstein-robust constraints stay convex and often linear, and fixed-charge well selection is a mixed-binary linear program that GWM has solved by branch and bound since 2005. PESTPP-OPT can already re-solve with zero model runs. The Coulon et al. comparison (98 against 260 m3/d) shows why the Gaussian linear shortcut needs a check. — [Rockafellar and Uryasev 2000](https://doi.org/10.21314/JOR.2000.038); [Mohajerin Esfahani and Kuhn 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-017-1172-1); [GWM-2005](https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/mf2005_gwm/Summary_gwm2005.txt); [PEST++ manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/master/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [Coulon et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-303-2024)
2. StoSAG-style robust ensemble-gradient SQP for many decision variables. Reservoir studies handled 3,360 controls with thousands of constraints and showed that robust optimization over 100 realizations beats nominal design. PESTPP-SQP has the algorithm. It lacks published groundwater results, and the pairing of control perturbations with parameter realizations needs checking against the StoSAG analysis. — [Dehdari and Oliver 2012](https://doi.org/10.2118/141589-PA); [van Essen et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.2118/102913-PA); [Fonseca et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.5342); [MODSIM 2025](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2025/files/A04.macasieb.pdf)
3. Surrogate-assisted and Bayesian methods for small, expensive, constrained or multi-objective problems. The PESTPP-MOU surrogate mode cut a three-hour-per-run coastal problem to 500 full runs, an estimated factor of 13. SCBO and qNEHVI offer batch constrained and multi-objective acquisition up to roughly 60 to 120 variables at budgets of thousands. — [Macasieb et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024WR038554); [SCBO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08526); [qNEHVI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08195)
4. Sample-size discipline and out-of-sample checks for stack-based risk. Groundwater's own literature gives N/(N+1) reliability for N stacked realizations and shows reliability depends on N and on log-conductivity variance. Scenario theory gives explicit sample bounds for convex problems. — [Chan 1993](https://doi.org/10.1029/93WR01410); [Feyen and Gorelick 2004](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003WR003003); [Calafiore and Campi 2006](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2006.875041)
5. Feedback policies and closed-loop operation. A learned policy beat open-loop robust optimization by 15 to 33 percent in reservoir tests, direct policy search is established for surface reservoirs, and groundwater had dual-control formulations in 1990 and 1991. — [Nasir and Durlofsky 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.111945); [Giuliani et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)WR.1943-5452.0000570); [Lee and Kitanidis 1991](https://doi.org/10.1029/91WR01307)
6. Deep-uncertainty layering with many-objective robust decision making or adaptive pathways for climate and demand scenarios. — [Kasprzyk et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2012.12.007); [Haasnoot et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2012.12.006)
7. Submodular tools for monitoring design: lazy greedy selection and online optimality bounds added to the existing greedy data-worth loop, and decision-focused value of information. — [Krause et al. 2008](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v9/krause08a.html); [Feyen and Gorelick 2005](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003WR002901)
8. Stochastic dual dynamic programming for long-horizon conjunctive use where the aquifer response is convex. — [Macian-Sorribes et al. 2017](https://core.ac.uk/works/11825086); [Davidsen et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-20-771-2016)
9. Deep reinforcement learning, last, until fast and trusted surrogates exist. — [Nasir and Durlofsky 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.111945)

#### Groundwater calibration, ranked
1. Structured derivative-free least squares (DFO-LS, POUNDERS, DFBGN). These solvers progress with fewer runs than one Jacobian, reuse runs, tolerate noise through restarts, and accept bounds, convex constraints and L1 regularization. NEWUOA-class methods won the benchmark on smooth problems. — [Cartis et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3338517); [Moré and Wild 2009](https://doi.org/10.1137/080724083); [Cartis and Roberts 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-022-01836-1)
2. An RBF global search wrapped around PEST for multimodal problems with up to about 30 parameters. The evidence is from groundwater: 12-dimensional bioremediation, an eight-parameter field calibration and two 28-parameter calibrations. — [Regis and Shoemaker 2007](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.1060.0182); [Mugunthan et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR004134); [Wan 2015](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/775d6507-aa5c-462e-9b2a-b5bfa1087837)
3. Alternative misfits: optimal transport for timing-dominated data, and L1 or Huber norms for outliers and structural error. — [Yang et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1190/geo2016-0663.1); [Magyar and Sambridge 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-27-991-2023); [Finsterle and Najita 1998](https://doi.org/10.1029/98WR02174)
4. Multi-fidelity and coarse-to-fine calibration with a trust-region safeguard. — [Peherstorfer et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082469); [March and Willcox 2012](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.J051125)
5. Bayesian optimization for low-dimensional outer loops such as regularization or weighting hyperparameters. — [Frazier 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02811)
6. Cheap multistart around the local solver to test for multiple minima. — [Larson and Wild 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12532-017-0131-4)

### Inferences
- Management item 1 ranks first because it needs no new model runs and no new simulator coupling. A user with a finished PESTPP-OPT run holds a Jacobian and can generate stack anomalies. Everything else is a small optimization model in Pyomo or JuMP with an open solver. It also fixes three stated weaknesses of the current chance constraints together: tail blindness, constraint-by-constraint reliability, and trust in the posterior ensemble.
- Management item 2 ranks second because it is the only route in the suite whose cost does not grow with the number of decision variables, and reservoir engineering has fifteen years of evidence on what makes it work. The risk is gradient quality when parameter uncertainty is mixed in. A notebook that reproduces the EnOpt versus StoSAG difference on the Freyberg model would settle it and would fill the missing PESTPP-SQP tutorial.
- Management item 3 is ranked third and not first because the gains are proven only up to a few tens of decision variables, and Gaussian-process methods carry setup and tuning cost. Where runs take hours and variables number ten or twenty, it is the best option available now.
- Management items 4 and 6 are procedural changes more than algorithms. They cost little and change how results should be reported: reliability with a validation stack, and performance across named climate and demand scenarios.
- Management item 5 could matter most in the long run, because it changes the product from a schedule to a rule. It ranks fifth because the groundwater evidence is thirty years old or indirect.
- On the calibration side the ranking reflects how directly each method slots into a PEST workflow. Items 1 and 2 are optimizers that call the same model runs. Item 3 changes only the observation processing. Item 4 needs several model fidelities, which few projects maintain.
- Most highly parameterized calibrations with hundreds to thousands of parameters remain the territory of GLM with SVD-assist and the iterative ensemble smoother. The derivative-free methods surveyed here do not displace them. They fit the moderate-dimension, noisy or multimodal cases.

### Gaps
- None of the top-ranked management transfers has a published groundwater field application that I could verify. The ranking rests on reservoir, operations research and machine-learning evidence plus the structure of PEST++.
- The shared web-search quota ran out before I could run targeted searches on geothermal, mine dewatering, managed aquifer recharge, ADMM, convex relaxation, optimal-transport misfits in groundwater, and multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization. Absence of findings in those areas reflects the cut-off as much as the literature.
- Quantified run savings from DFO-LS, DFBGN or POUNDERS against PESTPP-GLM on a groundwater model do not exist in the sources I found. A benchmark on the Freyberg tutorial model would be the first.
