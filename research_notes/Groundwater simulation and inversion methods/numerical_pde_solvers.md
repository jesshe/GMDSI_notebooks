## Discretization: what do MPFA, mimetic, virtual element, mixed and hybrid FE, HDG, nonlinear two-point, cut-cell, Voronoi and isogeometric methods offer compared with MODFLOW's two-point control-volume finite difference?

### Takeaway
The two-point flux used by MODFLOW is consistent only on K-orthogonal grids. Reservoir simulation and applied mathematics supply consistent replacements, and MODFLOW 6 already ships one of them (XT3D). Published comparisons agree on three points. The consistent schemes agree closely with each other and differ from TPFA on skewed or anisotropic problems. They cost a wider stencil or extra face unknowns. Linear consistent schemes lose monotonicity, which only nonlinear schemes recover. The 2025 SPE11 CO2 benchmark adds a sobering fourth point: grid resolution and analyst choices moved results more than the choice of scheme or grid type.

### Cited Findings

#### Baseline in groundwater codes
- MODFLOW-USG uses a control volume finite difference (CVFD) formulation "in which a cell can be connected to an arbitrary number of adjacent cells". Its Ghost Node Correction package exists "to improve accuracy of the CVFD formulation for irregular grid-cell geometries or nested grids". [Panday et al. (2013), "MODFLOW-USG version 1: An unstructured grid version of MODFLOW for simulating groundwater flow and tightly coupled processes using a control volume finite-difference formulation", USGS Techniques and Methods 6-A45](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A45) [V]
- The XT3D option of MODFLOW 6 is a multi-point scheme. It interpolates head gradients, applies the full 3D conductivity tensor, and enforces flux continuity at the cell interface. The flow expression involves "neighbors of neighbors", so the matrix stencil grows. An RHS suboption keeps the original stencil by lagging the extra terms. USGS states that XT3D "is typically more computationally intensive than the conductance-based formulation", with or without ghost nodes, and that it can replace the GNC package. [Provost, Langevin and Hughes (2017), "Documentation for the XT3D option in the Node Property Flow (NPF) Package of MODFLOW 6", USGS Techniques and Methods 6-A56](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A56) [V]
- Bardot and co-authors benchmarked MODFLOW on dipping, anisotropic sedimentary structures. With versions before MODFLOW 6, "effective conductivity for a 30° dipping layer with a 100:1 conductivity ratio is reduced to only 2% of its inputted value". MODFLOW 6 with XT3D and flexible discretization was "far superior", but accuracy was still lost on vertically offset grids when the offset was large. [Bardot et al. (2023), "Revisiting MODFLOW's capability to model flow through sedimentary structures", Groundwater 61:663-673](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13273) [V]
- XT3D alone does not fix steeply dipping layers. A 2025 Groundwater paper shows that inaccurate flows in a dipping-aquifer benchmark came from "the limited cell connectivity inherent in the most commonly used discretization packages", and that XT3D reaches the expected accuracy only with the unstructured DISU grid type and at least two model layers per aquifer. [Provost et al. (2025), "Accurate Simulation of Flow through Dipping Aquifers with MODFLOW 6 Using Enhanced Cell Connectivity", Groundwater 63:399-408](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13459) [V]
- MODFLOW 6 now consolidates the unstructured grids of MODFLOW-USG and the Newton-Raphson formulation of MODFLOW-NWT in one code with multi-model coupling. [Langevin et al. (2024), "MODFLOW as a Configurable Multi-Model Hydrologic Simulator", Groundwater 62:111-123](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13351) [V]
- MODFLOW 6 represents pinched-out layers through IDOMAIN pass-through cells that connect the cells above and below, and its DISV and DISU packages take Voronoi and other unstructured cells. [Langevin et al. (2017), "Documentation for the MODFLOW 6 Groundwater Flow Model", USGS Techniques and Methods 6-A55](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A55) [B]
- The integrated finite difference method behind the TOUGH family is the same two-point idea on arbitrary polyhedra and dates from 1976. [Narasimhan and Witherspoon (1976), "An integrated finite difference method for analyzing fluid flow in porous media", Water Resources Research 12:57-64](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR012i001p00057) [B]
- Lie's reservoir simulation textbook states that the two-point scheme "is only consistent for certain combinations of grids and permeability tensors K". [Lie (2019), "An Introduction to Reservoir Simulation Using MATLAB/GNU Octave", Cambridge University Press](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108591416) [S]
- For transport, MODFLOW 6.7.0 (February 2026) added a UTVD advection scheme that "typically improves accuracy and reduces numerical dispersion more than the existing TVD scheme" on unstructured grids or when flow is poorly aligned with the grid. [USGS (2026), "MODFLOW 6 release notes, version 6.7.0, February 6, 2026", MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6 repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex) [V]

#### Multipoint flux approximation (MPFA-O, MPFA-L, AvgMPFA)
- What it is. MPFA builds each face flux from pressures in all cells around a grid vertex by imposing flux and pressure continuity inside an interaction region. It is consistent for full tensors on non-K-orthogonal grids. On logically Cartesian grids the O-method gives a 9-point stencil in 2D and a 27-point stencil in 3D, against 5 and 7 points for TPFA. [Aavatsmark (2002), "An Introduction to Multipoint Flux Approximations for Quadrilateral Grids", Computational Geosciences 6:405-432](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1021291114475) [B]
- The flux-continuous control-volume distributed family from Edwards and Rogers is the parallel line of development for general tensors. [Edwards and Rogers (1998), "Finite volume discretization with imposed flux continuity for the general tensor pressure equation", Computational Geosciences 2:259-290](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1011510505406) [B]
- The L-method uses fewer cells per sub-flux than the O-method and stays monotone over a wider range of grid skewness and anisotropy. [Aavatsmark et al. (2008), "A compact multipoint flux approximation method with improved robustness", Numerical Methods for Partial Differential Equations 24:1329-1360](https://doi.org/10.1002/num.20320) [B]
- Monotonicity is conditional. Nordbotten and co-authors derived conditions on grid and tensor under which a control-volume scheme has a monotone matrix. Keilegavlen and co-authors showed those conditions are also necessary, so no linear nine-point scheme stays monotone for every anisotropy ratio and skewness. [Nordbotten, Aavatsmark and Eigestad (2007), "Monotonicity of control volume methods", Numerische Mathematik 106:255-288](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-006-0060-z) [B] [Keilegavlen, Nordbotten and Aavatsmark (2009), "Sufficient criteria are necessary for monotone control volume methods", Applied Mathematics Letters 22:1178-1180](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aml.2009.01.048) [B]
- Convergence theory comes from the link to mixed finite elements. Wheeler and Yotov showed a mixed method with a special quadrature reduces to a cell-centred MPFA stencil. Arbogast, Wheeler and Yotov did the same earlier for tensor coefficients on rectangular grids. [Wheeler and Yotov (2006), "A Multipoint Flux Mixed Finite Element Method", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 44:2082-2106](https://doi.org/10.1137/050638473) [B] [Arbogast, Wheeler and Yotov (1997), "Mixed Finite Elements for Elliptic Problems with Tensor Coefficients as Cell-Centered Finite Differences", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 34:828-852](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036142994262585) [B]
- Quasi-positive variants with full pressure support aim to reduce spurious oscillations for strong anisotropy. [Edwards and Zheng (2008), "A quasi-positive family of continuous Darcy-flux finite-volume schemes with full pressure support", Journal of Computational Physics 227:9333-9364](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2008.05.028) [B]
- Maturity and code. MPFA is a 20-year-old production method. Open implementations exist in MRST [Lie (2019), "An Introduction to Reservoir Simulation Using MATLAB/GNU Octave", Cambridge University Press](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108591416) [B], DuMux [Koch et al. (2021), "DuMux 3 - an open-source simulator for solving flow and transport problems in porous media with a focus on model coupling", Computers & Mathematics with Applications 81:423-443](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2020.02.012) [B] and PorePy [Keilegavlen et al. (2021), "PorePy: an open-source software for simulation of multiphysics processes in fractured porous media", Computational Geosciences 25:243-265](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-020-10002-5) [B]. Groundwater status: XT3D in MODFLOW 6 is the existing application. [Provost, Langevin and Hughes (2017)](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A56) [V]

#### Mimetic finite differences, virtual elements and hybrid schemes
- What they are. Mimetic finite differences build discrete gradient and divergence operators that keep conservation and duality on general polyhedral cells. In hybrid form the unknowns sit on faces. [Lipnikov, Manzini and Shashkov (2014), "Mimetic finite difference method", Journal of Computational Physics 257:1163-1227](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2013.07.031) [B] [Brezzi, Lipnikov and Shashkov (2005), "Convergence of the Mimetic Finite Difference Method for Diffusion Problems on Polyhedral Meshes", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 43:1872-1896](https://doi.org/10.1137/040613950) [B]
- The virtual element method generalizes finite elements to polygons and polyhedra without explicit shape functions. A mixed version exists for second-order elliptic problems. [Beirão da Veiga et al. (2013), "Basic principles of virtual element methods", Mathematical Models and Methods in Applied Sciences 23:199-214](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218202512500492) [B] [Beirão da Veiga et al. (2016), "Mixed virtual element methods for general second order elliptic problems on polygonal meshes", ESAIM: Mathematical Modelling and Numerical Analysis 50:727-747](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2015067) [B]
- Close relatives are the SUSHI hybrid finite volume scheme and hybrid high-order methods. Droniou's review places all of them in one framework and discusses the trade-off between coercivity and monotonicity. [Eymard, Gallouet and Herbin (2010), "Discretization of heterogeneous and anisotropic diffusion problems on general nonconforming meshes SUSHI: a scheme using stabilization and hybrid interfaces", IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis 30:1009-1043](https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/drn084) [B] [Di Pietro, Ern and Lemaire (2014), "An Arbitrary-Order and Compact-Stencil Discretization of Diffusion on General Meshes Based on Local Reconstruction Operators", Computational Methods in Applied Mathematics 14:461-472](https://doi.org/10.1515/cmam-2014-0018) [B] [Droniou (2014), "Finite volume schemes for diffusion equations: Introduction to and review of modern methods", Mathematical Models and Methods in Applied Sciences 24:1575-1619](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218202514400041) [B]
- Groundwater status. Amanzi-ATS uses mimetic finite differences for coupled surface and subsurface flow on unstructured polyhedral meshes, so a hydrology application exists. [Coon et al. (2020), "Coupling surface flow and subsurface flow in complex soil structures using mimetic finite differences", Advances in Water Resources 144:103701](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2020.103701) [B]
- Open implementations are in MRST (mimetic and VEM modules) [Lie (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108591416) [B] and PorePy [Keilegavlen et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-020-10002-5) [B].

#### Nonlinear two-point flux approximation (NTPFA)
- What it is. The face flux keeps a two-point form, but the transmissibilities depend on the solution. This restores consistency and guarantees non-negative solutions on general meshes and tensors. Le Potier introduced it for triangles, Lipnikov and co-authors gave an interpolation-free polygonal version, and Terekhov and co-authors extended it to heterogeneous anisotropic media. [Le Potier (2005), "Schéma volumes finis monotone pour des opérateurs de diffusion fortement anisotropes sur des maillages de triangles non structurés", Comptes Rendus. Mathématique 341:787-792](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2005.10.010) [B] [Lipnikov, Svyatskiy and Vassilevski (2009), "Interpolation-free monotone finite volume method for diffusion equations on polygonal meshes", Journal of Computational Physics 228:703-716](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2008.09.031) [B] [Terekhov, Mallison and Tchelepi (2017), "Cell-centered nonlinear finite-volume methods for the heterogeneous anisotropic diffusion problem", Journal of Computational Physics 330:245-267](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2016.11.010) [B]
- Nikitin and co-authors report a nonlinear TPFA for diffusion and two-phase flow that is monotone, less sensitive to grid distortion than linear TPFA, and sparser than MPFA. [Nikitin, Terekhov and Vassilevski (2014), "A monotone nonlinear finite volume method for diffusion equations and multiphase flows", Computational Geosciences 18:311-324](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-013-9387-6) [S]
- Schneider and co-authors applied it to corner-point grids with non-planar, non-matching and degenerate faces. They state that cell-centred TPFA "introduces large face-flux errors on unstructured grids". [Schneider et al. (2018), "Monotone nonlinear finite-volume method for challenging grids", Computational Geosciences 22:565-586](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9710-8) [S]
- Limitation. The discrete system is nonlinear even for a linear PDE, so every solve needs Picard or Newton iterations. The basic schemes preserve positivity but not the full discrete maximum principle. [Droniou (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218202514400041) [B]

#### Comparative evidence on accuracy, monotonicity and cost
- Klemetsdal and co-authors compared linear and nonlinear TPFA, MPFA-O, mimetic and virtual element methods on polyhedral grids for accuracy, monotonicity and cost, where cost meant degrees of freedom, sparsity and condition number. [Klemetsdal et al. (2020), "A Comparison of Consistent Discretizations for Elliptic Problems on Polyhedral Grids", Springer Proceedings in Mathematics & Statistics 585-594](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-43651-3_55) [S]
- Raynaud and co-authors benchmarked MPFA-O, mimetic, NTPFA, TPFA and AvgMPFA on grids from commercial gridding tools to pick a scheme for a commercial simulator. They "observe distinctively different behavior with the inconsistent two-point method and the consistent methods, which agree closely in terms of accuracy of the response". They also propose static indicators that measure the non-consistency of TPFA per cell before any run, and dynamic indicators based on idealized tracer simulations. [Raynaud et al. (2021), "Toward Accurate Reservoir Simulations on Unstructured Grids: Design of Simple Error Estimators and Critical Benchmarking of Consistent Discretization Methods for Practical Implementation", SPE Journal 26:4109-4127](https://doi.org/10.2118/203920-PA) [V]
- Holme and co-authors ran the first systematic grid and scheme study on SPE11 case B. They combined Cartesian, cut-cell, PEBI, hybrid and Delaunay grids with TPFA, MPFA, AvgMPFA and NTPFA in MRST and JutulDarcy. Predictions of overall plume migration and pointwise pressure build-up varied modestly across grids and schemes. The resolution of dissolution fingers changed a lot. [Holme et al. (2025), "Grid-Orientation Effects in the 11th SPE Comparative Solution Project Using Unstructured Grids and Consistent Discretizations", SPE Reservoir Simulation Conference, paper SPE-223885-MS](https://doi.org/10.2118/223885-MS) [V]
- The SPE11 results paper analysed submissions from 18 of 45 registered groups. It found "a strong dependence on grid resolution" in all three cases. It also found that "the choice of simulator, grid type, and a two-times grid refinement in each direction generally matter less (or at least not more) than the variability among participating groups", and that 80% of submitted data were corrected after the deadline. Five submissions used non-orthogonal grids and two replaced TPFA with a consistent multipoint method. [Nordbotten et al. (2025), "Benchmarking CO2 storage simulations: results from the 11th Society of Petroleum Engineers Comparative Solution Project", International Journal of Greenhouse Gas Control 148:104519](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijggc.2025.104519) [V]
- The benchmark definition and the earlier FluidFlower validation study give the test problems. [Nordbotten et al. (2024), "The 11th Society of Petroleum Engineers Comparative Solution Project: Problem Definition", SPE Journal 29:2507-2524](https://doi.org/10.2118/218015-PA) [B] [Flemisch et al. (2024), "The FluidFlower validation benchmark study for the storage of CO2", Transport in Porous Media 151:865-912](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11242-023-01977-7) [B]
- The FVCA6 3D benchmark compared many schemes for anisotropic diffusion on general grids. [Eymard et al. (2011), "3D Benchmark on Discretization Schemes for Anisotropic Diffusion Problems on General Grids", Springer Proceedings in Mathematics 895-930](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-20671-9_89) [B]
- Mlacnik and Durlofsky attacked the problem from the grid side by optimizing unstructured grids to improve monotonicity for strongly anisotropic coefficients. [Mlacnik and Durlofsky (2006), "Unstructured grid optimization for improved monotonicity of discrete solutions of elliptic equations with highly anisotropic coefficients", Journal of Computational Physics 216:337-361](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2005.12.007) [B]

#### Mixed, mixed-hybrid, HDG and enriched Galerkin finite elements
- Mixed finite elements approximate head and flux together and give continuous normal fluxes. Hybridization reduces the saddle-point system to a symmetric positive definite system in face unknowns. Younes, Ackerer and Delay review the method for diffusion-type equations in hydrogeology, so groundwater use is established. [Younes, Ackerer and Delay (2010), "Mixed finite elements for solving 2-D diffusion-type equations", Reviews of Geophysics 48:RG1004](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008RG000277) [B]
- Durlofsky compared control-volume finite elements with lowest-order mixed elements on triangles. For moderate heterogeneity the control-volume method gave more accurate flow per unknown. For discontinuous permeability such as sand and shale, the mixed method approximated flow "more accurately and more realistically" with the same number of unknowns. [Durlofsky (1994), "Accuracy of mixed and control volume finite element approximations to Darcy velocity and related quantities", Water Resources Research 30:965-973](https://doi.org/10.1029/94WR00061) [V]
- Hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin methods unify DG, mixed and continuous Galerkin under one hybrid framework with face unknowns. [Cockburn, Gopalakrishnan and Lazarov (2009), "Unified Hybridization of Discontinuous Galerkin, Mixed, and Continuous Galerkin Methods for Second Order Elliptic Problems", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 47:1319-1365](https://doi.org/10.1137/070706616) [B]
- A performance study on Poisson problems found that high-order continuous elements with matrix-free multigrid gave the best time to solution for smooth solutions, and that matrix-free solvers were "up to an order of magnitude" faster than the best matrix-based ones, including matrix-based HDG. [Kronbichler and Wall (2018), "A Performance Comparison of Continuous and Discontinuous Galerkin Methods with Fast Multigrid Solvers", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 40:A3423-A3448](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M110455X) [V]
- Enriched Galerkin adds piecewise constants to continuous elements. It is locally conservative with fewer unknowns than DG, and its solver cost is "roughly that of CG". [Lee, Lee and Wheeler (2016), "A Locally Conservative Enriched Galerkin Approximation and Efficient Solver for Elliptic and Parabolic Problems", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 38:A1404-A1429](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1041109) [V]
- Firedrake automates hybridization and static condensation as runtime preconditioners, developed for weather models. [Gibson et al. (2020), "Slate: extending Firedrake's domain-specific abstraction to hybridized solvers for geoscience and beyond", Geoscientific Model Development 13:735-761](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-13-735-2020) [V]

#### Cut-cell, embedded boundary, Voronoi grids and isogeometric analysis
- The Cartesian embedded boundary method of Johansen and Colella cuts a regular grid by the domain boundary and keeps second-order accuracy for Poisson problems. [Johansen and Colella (1998), "A Cartesian Grid Embedded Boundary Method for Poisson's Equation on Irregular Domains", Journal of Computational Physics 147:60-85](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1998.5965) [B]
- Chombo-Crunch uses embedded boundaries for pore-scale reactive transport and was coupled to GEOS for fracture evolution runs on the Frontier exascale machine. [Trebotich et al. (2024), "A multiphysics coupling framework for exascale simulation of fracture evolution in subsurface energy applications", Frontiers in High Performance Computing 2:1416727](https://doi.org/10.3389/fhpcp.2024.1416727) [V]
- Cut-cell grids that conform to facies boundaries were one of the grid types tested on SPE11 case B. [Holme et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.2118/223885-MS) [V]
- Voronoi (PEBI) grids that conform to faults, fractures and wells can be generated automatically with the MRST upr module. [Berge, Klemetsdal and Lie (2019), "Unstructured Voronoi grids conforming to lower dimensional objects", Computational Geosciences 23:169-188](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-018-9790-0) [B]
- Groundwater status for unstructured cells. MODFLOW-USG supports "nested grids and grids based on prismatic triangles, rectangles, hexagons, and other cell shapes". [Panday et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A45) [V]
- Isogeometric analysis uses the spline basis of CAD geometry as the finite element basis. It was built for smooth geometry and smooth fields in mechanics. [Hughes, Cottrell and Bazilevs (2005), "Isogeometric analysis: CAD, finite elements, NURBS, exact geometry and mesh refinement", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 194:4135-4195](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2004.10.008) [B]

#### Lessons from fusion plasma and weather modelling
- Magnetized plasma heat transport has parallel conductivity many orders of magnitude above perpendicular conductivity. Günter and co-authors showed that grids not aligned with the field cause numerical perpendicular diffusion and proposed a symmetric finite difference scheme that limits it. [Günter et al. (2005), "Modelling of heat transport in magnetised plasmas using non-aligned coordinates", Journal of Computational Physics 209:354-370](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2005.03.021) [B]
- Sharma and Hammett showed that centred differencing of anisotropic conduction can drive heat from cold to hot cells and produce negative temperatures, and that slope limiters restore monotonicity at some cost in accuracy. [Sharma and Hammett (2007), "Preserving monotonicity in anisotropic diffusion", Journal of Computational Physics 227:123-142](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2007.07.026) [B]
- Later work compares finite difference schemes for extreme anisotropy and builds asymptotic-preserving formulations that stay well conditioned as the anisotropy ratio grows. [van Es, Koren and de Blank (2014), "Finite-difference schemes for anisotropic diffusion", Journal of Computational Physics 272:526-549](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2014.04.046) [B] [Degond et al. (2012), "An asymptotic-preserving method for highly anisotropic elliptic equations based on a Micro-Macro decomposition", Journal of Computational Physics 231:2724-2740](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2011.11.040) [B]
- Weather prediction moved to compatible (mimetic) mixed finite elements on non-orthogonal global grids. The Met Office LFRic model needs a bespoke multigrid preconditioner for the pressure correction because the mimetic discretization "makes the construction of efficient solvers challenging as compared to models using standard finite-difference and finite-volume methods". [Cotter (2023), "Compatible finite element methods for geophysical fluid dynamics", Acta Numerica 32:291-393](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492923000028) [B] [Maynard, Melvin and Müller (2020), "Multigrid preconditioners for the mixed finite element dynamical core of the LFRic atmospheric model", Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society 146:3917-3936](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3880) [V]

### Inferences
- The MPFA transfer to groundwater has in effect happened through XT3D. What MODFLOW lacks relative to reservoir practice is a monotone option (NTPFA or a limiter), a pre-run error indicator of the Raynaud type that tells the user where TPFA is inconsistent, and linear solver support tuned for the wider, nonsymmetric XT3D stencil.
- For layered models with mild dip and conductivity tensors aligned with layers, TPFA error is small and consistent schemes will not pay for their cost. They pay off for dipping anisotropy, layer offsets at faults and pinch-outs, and unstructured grids that are not K-orthogonal. Provost et al. (2025) show that connectivity must be fixed first.
- Monotonicity matters more for transport, density-dependent flow and reactive chemistry than for head-only models, because small negative or overshooting values feed nonlinear terms. That is the same lesson the fusion community drew.
- SPE11 suggests that a groundwater modeller gets more from grid refinement and workflow quality control than from a more elaborate flux scheme, unless the grid is badly non-orthogonal.
- Mixed-hybrid elements are the natural choice when velocity accuracy across sharp conductivity contrasts drives the result, for example particle tracking through sand and clay sequences. The price is roughly three face unknowns per hexahedral cell instead of one cell unknown.
- Isogeometric and high-order HDG methods fit poorly with cellwise-constant conductivity fields from geostatistics. Their advantage depends on smooth solutions, which heterogeneous aquifers do not have.

## Nonlinear solvers: what techniques handle variably saturated, unconfined and multiphase nonlinearity elsewhere, and what gains are reported?

### Takeaway
Other fields treat the nonlinear solver as a research object in its own right. The useful results for groundwater are an adaptive switch between the L-scheme and Newton for Richards' equation that is as fast as Newton and as reliable as Picard, Anderson acceleration of fixed-point iterations, variable switching and reparametrization for dry soils, and nonlinear domain decomposition, which is now a user option in the open-source OPM Flow simulator. Reported gains are mostly in avoided time-step cuts and wasted iterations. They reach an order of magnitude on hard cases and are zero or negative when plain Newton already converges in a few iterations.

### Cited Findings

#### Baseline in groundwater codes
- MODFLOW-NWT replaced discrete cell drying and rewetting by a smooth, upstream-weighted conductance function so that Newton derivatives stay continuous. The Newton matrix is asymmetric, so the code ships GMRES and Orthomin/BiCGSTAB solvers. [Niswonger, Panday and Ibaraki (2011), "MODFLOW-NWT, a Newton formulation for MODFLOW-2005", USGS Techniques and Methods 6-A37](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A37) [V]
- MODFLOW-USG carries the same Newton formulation and adds a sparse matrix solver package with "several methods for resolving nonlinearities". [Panday et al. (2013), "MODFLOW-USG version 1: An unstructured grid version of MODFLOW for simulating groundwater flow and tightly coupled processes using a control volume finite-difference formulation", USGS Techniques and Methods 6-A45](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A45) [V]
- Recent MODFLOW 6 releases keep patching the Newton path case by case. Version 6.7.0 added a HIGHEST_CELL_SATURATION option "to prevent flow from leaving a dry cell". Version 6.8.0 added an implicit lake formulation that "typically converges in far fewer outer iterations", needs BiCGSTAB because the matrix becomes asymmetric, and added damping of well-head oscillations under Newton under-relaxation. [USGS (2026), "MODFLOW 6 release notes, version 6.7.0, February 6, 2026", MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6 repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex) [V] [USGS (2026), "MODFLOW 6 release notes, version 6.8.0, September 2, 2026", MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6 repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex) [V]
- Celia's modified Picard iteration on the mixed form of Richards' equation remains the reference mass-conservative scheme. Lehmann and Ackerer compared iterative schemes for the same equation in 1998. [Celia, Bouloutas and Zarba (1990), "A general mass-conservative numerical solution for the unsaturated flow equation", Water Resources Research 26:1483-1496](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR026i007p01483) [B] [Lehmann and Ackerer (1998), "Comparison of Iterative Methods for Improved Solutions of the Fluid Flow Equation in Partially Saturated Porous Media", Transport in Porous Media 31:275-292](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1006555107450) [B]
- Paniconi and Putti compared Picard and Newton on eight variably saturated test cases in one to three dimensions. Picard was often adequate, Newton was needed when Picard failed or crawled, and a mixed Picard-Newton start reduced Newton's sensitivity to the initial guess. [Paniconi and Putti (1994), "A comparison of Picard and Newton iteration in the numerical solution of multidimensional variably saturated flow problems", Water Resources Research 30:3357-3374](https://doi.org/10.1029/94WR02046) [V]
- Two reviews of Richards solvers conclude that no single linearization is reliable across dry initial conditions, layered soils and large time steps. [Farthing and Ogden (2017), "Numerical Solution of Richards' Equation: A Review of Advances and Challenges", Soil Science Society of America Journal 81:1257-1269](https://doi.org/10.2136/sssaj2017.02.0058) [B] [Zha et al. (2019), "Review of numerical solution of Richardson-Richards equation for variably saturated flow in soils", WIREs Water 6:e1364](https://doi.org/10.1002/wat2.1364) [B]
- The first integrated hydrology intercomparison set up surface-subsurface benchmarks in 2014. [Maxwell et al. (2014), "Surface-subsurface model intercomparison: A first set of benchmark results to diagnose integrated hydrology and feedbacks", Water Resources Research 50:1531-1549](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013WR013725) [B]
- Its second phase, IH-MIP2, ran ATS, Cast3M, CATHY, GEOtop, HydroGeoSphere, MIKE-SHE and ParFlow on common benchmarks and found generally good agreement, with differences tied to ponding and overland flow treatment. [Kollet et al. (2017), "The integrated hydrologic model intercomparison project, IH-MIP2: A second set of benchmark results to diagnose integrated hydrology and feedbacks", Water Resources Research 53:867-890](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR019191) [V]

#### L-scheme, modified L-scheme and adaptive switching to Newton
- The L-scheme replaces the derivative of saturation by a constant L at least as large as its maximum. It needs no derivatives of the hydraulic functions, converges linearly from any starting guess under a mild condition, and gives better-conditioned linear systems than Newton. It came from mixed finite element analysis of Richards' equation. [Pop, Radu and Knabner (2004), "Mixed finite elements for the Richards' equation: linearization procedure", Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics 168:365-373](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cam.2003.04.008) [B]
- List and Radu compared Newton, Picard, Picard-Newton and the L-scheme for Richards' equation, recorded convergence, run time and condition numbers, and proposed starting with L-scheme iterations before switching to Newton. [List and Radu (2016), "A study on iterative methods for solving Richards' equation", Computational Geosciences 20:341-353](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-016-9566-3) [V]
- The modified L-scheme adds the local derivative back in and speeds up convergence at small time steps while keeping the stabilization. [Mitra and Pop (2019), "A modified L-scheme to solve nonlinear diffusion problems", Computers & Mathematics with Applications 77:1722-1738](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2018.09.042) [B]
- Stokke and co-authors derived a posteriori indicators that decide at every iteration whether to switch from the L-scheme to Newton or back. In their tests Newton diverged on finer meshes and larger time steps while the L-scheme always converged. The adaptive scheme "is as fast as Newton's method when it converges, and is significantly more robust". In one benchmark the plain L-scheme needed about 373% of the adaptive scheme's computing time, including the cost of the indicators. [Stokke et al. (2023), "An adaptive solution strategy for Richards' equation", Computers & Mathematics with Applications 152:155-167](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2023.10.020) [V]
- A January 2026 preprint from the same group generalizes the idea to adaptive switching, adaptive time stepping and adaptive tuning of stabilization parameters for two-phase flow, surfactant transport and poroelasticity. It is not peer reviewed yet. [Stokke, Kumar and Radu (2026), "Convergent adaptive iterative schemes for solving multi-physics problems", arXiv preprint 2601.16640](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.16640) [V]
- Other globally convergent linearizations for Richards' equation exist, and L-type schemes have been extended to coupled flow and reactive or surfactant transport. [Albuja and Ávila (2021), "A family of new globally convergent linearization schemes for solving Richards' equation", Applied Numerical Mathematics 159:281-296](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apnum.2020.09.012) [B] [Illiano, Pop and Radu (2021), "Iterative schemes for surfactant transport in porous media", Computational Geosciences 25:805-822](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-020-09949-2) [B]
- A linear domain decomposition method built on the L-scheme solves Richards' equation subdomain by subdomain without Newton. It was more stable than Newton at comparable computing time. [Seus et al. (2018), "A linear domain decomposition method for partially saturated flow in porous media", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 333:331-355](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.01.029) [S]

#### Anderson acceleration and nonlinear Krylov methods
- Anderson acceleration combines the last few fixed-point iterates to extrapolate a better one. For linear problems it is essentially equivalent to GMRES. [Walker and Ni (2011), "Anderson Acceleration for Fixed-Point Iterations", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 49:1715-1735](https://doi.org/10.1137/10078356X) [B]
- Lott and co-authors applied Anderson acceleration to modified Picard iteration for variably saturated flow. They compared it with plain modified Picard and with Newton on a 2D steady problem and a 1D Richards problem. Acceleration improved speed and reliability, and in some cases it beat Newton without any derivative computation. [Lott et al. (2012), "An accelerated Picard method for nonlinear systems related to variably saturated flow", Advances in Water Resources 38:92-101](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.12.013) [S]
- Anderson acceleration also stabilizes fixed-stress splitting for unsaturated poroelasticity. [Both et al. (2019), "Anderson accelerated fixed-stress splitting schemes for consolidation of unsaturated porous media", Computers & Mathematics with Applications 77:1479-1502](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2018.07.033) [B]
- It reduces the sensitivity of the L-scheme to the choice of the stabilization constant. [Stokke et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2023.10.020) [S]
- PETSc exposes Anderson, nonlinear GMRES, full approximation scheme multigrid and nonlinear preconditioning as composable solver objects. SUNDIALS KINSOL offers Anderson-accelerated fixed-point iteration. [Brune et al. (2015), "Composing Scalable Nonlinear Algebraic Solvers", SIAM Review 57:535-565](https://doi.org/10.1137/130936725) [B] [Gardner et al. (2022), "Enabling New Flexibility in the SUNDIALS Suite of Nonlinear and Differential/Algebraic Equation Solvers", ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 48:1-24](https://doi.org/10.1145/3539801) [B]

#### Primary variable switching and reparametrization
- Forsyth, Wu and Pruess introduced primary variable switching between pressure and saturation for infiltration into very dry heterogeneous soils. [Forsyth, Wu and Pruess (1995), "Robust numerical methods for saturated-unsaturated flow with dry initial conditions in heterogeneous media", Advances in Water Resources 18:25-38](https://doi.org/10.1016/0309-1708%2895%2900020-J) [B]
- Diersch and Perrochet implemented and analysed the technique for unsaturated-saturated flow in the FEFLOW setting, so a groundwater application exists. [Diersch and Perrochet (1999), "On the primary variable switching technique for simulating unsaturated-saturated flows", Advances in Water Resources 23:271-301](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2898%2900057-8) [B]
- Krabbenhøft proposed an alternative that keeps one variable set and avoids the switching logic. [Krabbenhøft (2007), "An alternative to primary variable switching in saturated-unsaturated flow computations", Advances in Water Resources 30:483-492](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2006.04.009) [B]
- Brenner and Cancès replace the switch by a smooth parametrization of the pressure-saturation graph. They prove local quadratic convergence of Newton under non-degeneracy conditions and give "numerical evidences of the efficiency". Bassetto and co-authors extend the comparison to heterogeneous media with finite volumes. [Brenner and Cancès (2017), "Improving Newton's Method Performance by Parametrization: The Case of the Richards Equation", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 55:1760-1785](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1083414) [V] [Bassetto et al. (2022), "On several numerical strategies to solve Richards' equation in heterogeneous media with finite volumes", Computational Geosciences 26:1297-1322](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-022-10150-w) [B]
- Casulli and Zanolli's nested Newton method for the mixed form of Richards' equation obtains "the quadratic convergence rate ... for any time step size and for all flow regimes" given a suitable initial guess. [Casulli and Zanolli (2010), "A Nested Newton-Type Algorithm for Finite Volume Methods Solving Richards' Equation in Mixed Form", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 32:2255-2273](https://doi.org/10.1137/100786320) [V]
- In multiphase flow, complementarity formulations treat phase appearance and disappearance as a constraint handled by a semi-smooth Newton method instead of a switch. [Lauser et al. (2011), "A new approach for phase transitions in miscible multi-phase flow in porous media", Advances in Water Resources 34:957-966](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.04.021) [B]

#### Safeguarded Newton: flux-function inflection points and trust regions
- Jenny, Tchelepi and Lee showed that Newton for transport with S-shaped fractional flow converges for any time step if saturation updates are not allowed to cross the inflection point of the flux function. [Jenny, Tchelepi and Lee (2009), "Unconditionally convergent nonlinear solver for hyperbolic conservation laws with S-shaped flux functions", Journal of Computational Physics 228:7497-7512](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2009.06.032) [B]
- Wang and Tchelepi generalized this to trust regions delimited by inflection, unit-flux and end points. They report reductions of total Newton iterations by more than an order of magnitude against standard safeguarded Newton solvers, with tests on SPE10. [Wang and Tchelepi (2013), "Trust-region based solver for nonlinear transport in heterogeneous porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 253:114-137](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2013.06.041) [S]
- Li and Tchelepi showed the trust regions should come from the numerical flux, and Møyner removed the need to specify inflection points in advance. [Li and Tchelepi (2015), "Nonlinear analysis of multiphase transport in porous media in the presence of viscous, buoyancy, and capillary forces", Journal of Computational Physics 297:104-131](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2015.04.057) [S] [Møyner (2017), "Nonlinear solver for three-phase transport problems based on approximate trust regions", Computational Geosciences 21:999-1021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9660-1) [S]
- Implicit hybrid upwinding makes the numerical flux differentiable when buoyancy reverses flow direction and improves Newton convergence. [Hamon, Mallison and Tchelepi (2016), "Implicit Hybrid Upwind scheme for coupled multiphase flow and transport with buoyancy", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 311:599-624](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2016.08.009) [B]

#### Continuation methods: pseudo-transient and homotopy
- Pseudo-transient continuation reaches a steady state through a sequence of growing artificial time steps and has a convergence theory. Farthing and co-authors applied it to steady variably saturated groundwater flow, so a groundwater application exists. [Kelley and Keyes (1998), "Convergence Analysis of Pseudo-Transient Continuation", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 35:508-523](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036142996304796) [B] [Farthing et al. (2003), "Efficient steady-state solution techniques for variably saturated groundwater flow", Advances in Water Resources 26:833-849](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2803%2900076-9) [B]
- The continuation-Newton method of Younis and co-authors treats a time step as a homotopy path so that "no iterations are wasted and a solution is always attainable", and solves each linear step only on the cells that will change. [Younis, Tchelepi and Aziz (2010), "Adaptively Localized Continuation-Newton Method-Nonlinear Solvers That Converge All the Time", SPE Journal 15:526-544](https://doi.org/10.2118/119147-PA) [V]
- Dissipation-based continuation adds numerical diffusion controlled by a homotopy parameter. In one-dimensional transport tests it cut total iterations "by more than an order of magnitude for most cases" against standard Newton, which suffered many time-step cuts. [Jiang and Tchelepi (2018), "Dissipation-based continuation method for multiphase flow in heterogeneous porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 375:307-336](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2018.08.044) [V]

#### Nonlinear preconditioning, nonlinear domain decomposition and nonlinear multigrid
- ASPIN solves local nonlinear problems on subdomains and applies Newton to the preconditioned global residual, which balances nonlinearities that are strong only locally. [Cai and Keyes (2002), "Nonlinearly Preconditioned Inexact Newton Algorithms", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 24:183-200](https://doi.org/10.1137/S106482750037620X) [B]
- RASPEN is the restricted, exact variant. It "already converges when used as an iterative solver, in contrast to ASPIN" and admits a coarse correction by nonlinear multigrid. The authors tested it on a Forchheimer problem and nonlinear diffusion. [Dolean et al. (2016), "Nonlinear Preconditioning: How to Use a Nonlinear Schwarz Method to Precondition Newton's Method", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 38:A3357-A3380](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M102887X) [V]
- MSPIN splits by physical field instead of subdomain and was "significantly more robust than Newton methods based on global linearizations" in its tests. [Liu and Keyes (2015), "Field-Split Preconditioned Inexact Newton Algorithms", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 37:A1388-A1409](https://doi.org/10.1137/140970379) [V]
- Skogestad and co-authors brought ASPIN to porous media flow. [Skogestad, Keilegavlen and Nordbotten (2013), "Domain decomposition strategies for nonlinear flow problems in porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 234:439-451](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2012.10.001) [B]
- Klemetsdal and co-authors studied ASPEN for immiscible and compositional flow and concluded that it is "a very robust and scalable alternative" to Newton for fully implicit schemes. [Klemetsdal et al. (2022), "A numerical study of the additive Schwarz preconditioned exact Newton method (ASPEN) as a nonlinear preconditioner for immiscible and compositional porous media flow", Computational Geosciences 26:1045-1063](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-021-10090-x) [V]
- The field-scale follow-up gives the honest balance: "ASPEN is outperformed by Newton when this method works well and converges in a few iterations", but it "avoids time-step cuts and has significantly lower runtimes in time steps where Newton struggles". The authors recommend switching adaptively between the two. [Møyner et al. (2024), "Nonlinear domain-decomposition preconditioning for robust and efficient field-scale simulation of subsurface flow", Computational Geosciences 28:241-251](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-023-10215-4) [V]
- OPM Flow exposes this as a user choice. Its source registers the option "Choose nonlinear solver. Valid choices are newton or nldd", with parameters for local iterations and subdomain overlap. [OPM project (2026), "opm-simulators source, BlackoilModelParameters.cpp, NonlinearSolver option", GitHub](https://github.com/OPM/opm-simulators/blob/master/opm/simulators/flow/BlackoilModelParameters.cpp) [V]
- The developers' own timings are modest. On the Norne field model with 45,000 cells and six MPI processes, total run time fell from 156.1 s with Newton to 122.3 s with 42 subdomains and 114.2 s with 200 subdomains. On the 70,000-cell Drogon model it fell from 59.1 s to 52.1 s. The slides add that gains are "not (yet) universally" seen across models and that NLDD "reduces work done in global linear solves, and increases work in assembly". [Rasmussen and Møyner (2024), "Non-linear domain decomposition: NLDD implementation in OPM Flow", OPM meeting Oslo, April 2024, slides](https://opm-project.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/rasmussen-nldd-opm-oslo2024.pdf) [V]
- A distributed-memory version was presented at ECMOR 2024. Two 2025 journal papers report that the pressure-ordered Gauss-Seidel sweep does not carry across MPI processes, which may limit scaling, and that NLDD with local solves switched on by mobility change was the most effective of several Newton accelerations on oil, CO2 storage and geothermal models. [Rasmussen et al. (2024), "Distributed Field-Scale Parallel Nonlinear Domain Decomposition Solver", ECMOR 2024 conference paper](https://doi.org/10.3997/2214-4609.202437058) [B] [SINTEF publication record (2025) of a Computational Geosciences 29(4) paper on NLDD for distributed OPM Flow runs](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/019c0f6fdfcd-bf30d83b-81fb-48a1-ae20-75d750791dbe/) [S] [SINTEF publication record (2025) of an SPE Journal paper comparing adaptive relaxation and NLDD preconditioning for Newton](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/019bc17052fa-3556201b-29f7-457a-8757-5116b954b363/) [S]
- Nonlinear multigrid (FAS) applied to a black-oil model outperformed Newton-based solvers "without loss of robustness" and used much less memory in a feasibility study. A later method uses spectral coarsening for heterogeneous nonlinear diffusion. [Christensen et al. (2016), "Nonlinear Multigrid for Reservoir Simulation", SPE Journal 21:888-898](https://doi.org/10.2118/178428-PA) [V] [Lee et al. (2020), "Nonlinear multigrid based on local spectral coarsening for heterogeneous diffusion problems", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 372:113432](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2020.113432) [B]

#### Sequential-implicit, adaptive-implicit, localized solvers and operator-based linearization
- Sequential fully implicit schemes solve pressure and transport in turn and iterate to the fully implicit answer. They allow a specialized solver for each sub-problem. [Jenny, Lee and Tchelepi (2006), "Adaptive fully implicit multi-scale finite-volume method for multi-phase flow and transport in heterogeneous porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 217:627-641](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2006.01.028) [B] [Moncorgé, Tchelepi and Jenny (2018), "Sequential fully implicit formulation for compositional simulation using natural variables", Journal of Computational Physics 371:690-711](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2018.05.048) [B]
- Their outer loop converges slowly with strong coupling and benefits from nonlinear acceleration. A sequential scheme for geothermal problems treats the pressure-enthalpy coupling in the same way. [Jiang and Tchelepi (2019), "Nonlinear acceleration of sequential fully implicit (SFI) method for coupled flow and transport in porous media", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 352:246-275](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2019.04.030) [B] [Wong, Horne and Tchelepi (2018), "Sequential implicit nonlinear solver for geothermal simulation", Journal of Computational Physics 368:236-253](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2018.04.043) [B]
- The adaptive implicit method treats only cells with high flow rates implicitly and dates from 1983. [Thomas and Thurnau (1983), "Reservoir Simulation Using an Adaptive Implicit Method", Society of Petroleum Engineers Journal 23:759-768](https://doi.org/10.2118/10120-PA) [B]
- Reordering cells along the flow direction turns the transport Jacobian into a triangular system that can be solved cell by cell. Potential-based reduced Newton and nonlinear Gauss-Seidel solvers exploit this. [Kwok and Tchelepi (2007), "Potential-based reduced Newton algorithm for nonlinear multiphase flow in porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 227:706-727](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2007.08.012) [B] [Natvig and Lie (2008), "Fast computation of multiphase flow in porous media by implicit discontinuous Galerkin schemes with optimal ordering of elements", Journal of Computational Physics 227:10108-10124](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2008.08.024) [B] [Klemetsdal et al. (2020), "Efficient reordered nonlinear Gauss-Seidel solvers with higher order for black-oil models", Computational Geosciences 24:593-607](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-019-09844-5) [B]
- Operator-based linearization tabulates the nonlinear terms of the residual in the space of primary variables and interpolates them, which simplifies Jacobian assembly and suits GPUs. It is the basis of the open-source open-DARTS simulator and was applied to geothermal models. [Voskov (2017), "Operator-based linearization approach for modeling of multiphase multi-component flow in porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 337:275-288](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2017.02.041) [B] [Khait and Voskov (2018), "Operator-based linearization for efficient modeling of geothermal processes", Geothermics 74:7-18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2018.01.012) [B] [Voskov et al. (2024), "open Delft Advanced Research Terra Simulator (open-DARTS)", Journal of Open Source Software 9:6737](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.06737) [B]

#### Adaptive stopping criteria from a posteriori error estimates
- Ern and Vohralík derived estimates that split the total error into discretization, linearization and algebraic parts. They stop the linear and nonlinear iterations when their part no longer affects the total error and report "important computational savings". [Ern and Vohralík (2013), "Adaptive Inexact Newton Methods with A Posteriori Stopping Criteria for Nonlinear Diffusion PDEs", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 35:A1761-A1791](https://doi.org/10.1137/120896918) [V]
- The approach was extended to two-phase flow and, in 2024, to Richards' equation. [Vohralík and Wheeler (2013), "A posteriori error estimates, stopping criteria, and adaptivity for two-phase flows", Computational Geosciences 17:789-812](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-013-9356-0) [B] [Mitra and Vohralík (2024), "A posteriori error estimates for the Richards equation", Mathematics of Computation 93:1053-1096](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3932) [B]

#### Free-boundary and complementarity formulations from glaciology
- The shallow ice equation is a degenerate nonlinear diffusion with a moving margin, mathematically close to unconfined flow with drying cells. Jouvet and Bueler pose it as an obstacle problem, and Bueler gives stable finite volume element schemes for it. [Jouvet and Bueler (2012), "Steady, Shallow Ice Sheets as Obstacle Problems: Well-Posedness and Finite Element Approximation", SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 72:1292-1314](https://doi.org/10.1137/110856654) [B] [Bueler (2016), "Stable finite volume element schemes for the shallow-ice approximation", Journal of Glaciology 62:230-242](https://doi.org/10.1017/jog.2015.3) [B]
- PETSc solves such variational inequalities with reduced-space and semi-smooth Newton methods. [Benson and Munson (2006), "Flexible complementarity solvers for large-scale applications", Optimization Methods and Software 21:155-168](https://doi.org/10.1080/10556780500065382) [B] [Bueler (2020), "PETSc for Partial Differential Equations: Numerical Solutions in C and Python", SIAM](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611976311) [B]
- Groundwater status. Seepage faces have been posed as variational inequalities. Ashby and co-authors solve steady variably saturated flow with seepage faces this way and add goal-oriented mesh adaptation for the flux across the seepage face. Jiang and co-authors give a variational inequality formulation for unconfined seepage through fracture networks. [Ashby et al. (2021), "Adaptive modelling of variably saturated seepage problems", The Quarterly Journal of Mechanics and Applied Mathematics 74:55-81](https://doi.org/10.1093/qjmam/hbab001) [V] [Jiang et al. (2012), "A new variational inequality formulation for unconfined seepage flow through fracture networks", Science China Technological Sciences 55:3090-3101](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11431-012-4904-8) [S]

#### Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov and multiphysics frameworks
- Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov methods need only residual evaluations and came from computational physics and nuclear engineering. [Knoll and Keyes (2004), "Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov methods: a survey of approaches and applications", Journal of Computational Physics 193:357-397](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2003.08.010) [B]
- MOOSE packages them for multiphysics and includes the PorousFlow module for coupled flow, heat, chemistry and mechanics in porous media, developed with groundwater and CO2 storage applications in mind. [Permann et al. (2020), "MOOSE: Enabling massively parallel multiphysics simulation", SoftwareX 11:100430](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.softx.2020.100430) [B] [Wilkins, Green and Ennis-King (2021), "An open-source multiphysics simulation code for coupled problems in porous media", Computers & Geosciences 154:104820](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2021.104820) [B]
- ParFlow has used a Newton-Krylov solver with multigrid preconditioning for heterogeneous variably saturated flow since 2001. [Jones and Woodward (2001), "Newton-Krylov-multigrid solvers for large-scale, highly heterogeneous, variably saturated flow problems", Advances in Water Resources 24:763-774](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2800%2900075-0) [B]

### Inferences
- MODFLOW's Newton option with upstream weighting, delta-bar-delta under-relaxation and backtracking is a heuristic safeguard of the same kind that reservoir simulators used before trust-region and nonlinear-preconditioning work. The release notes show convergence problems being fixed package by package, which suggests room for a more systematic approach.
- The lowest-cost transfer is Anderson acceleration of the outer Picard loop. It needs only stored iterates and a small least-squares solve, and it applies unchanged to couplings such as lakes, wells, streams and subsidence that MODFLOW handles by outer iteration.
- The L-scheme to Newton switch is the most promising idea for Richards-type options in HydroGeoSphere, FEFLOW or an unsaturated-zone extension of MODFLOW. It needs an a posteriori estimator, which is cheap to compute for finite volume schemes.
- Flux-function trust regions target S-shaped fractional flow curves and do not carry over directly to single-phase unconfined flow. The underlying principle does carry over: limit each Newton update at known kinks, which for groundwater are the cell top and bottom, drain and river switching elevations, and the pumping-reduction threshold.
- Nonlinear domain decomposition fits MODFLOW 6 structurally, because multi-model simulations already define subdomains coupled through exchanges and the parallel build already partitions the grid. It should help when a small region such as a dewatering mine pit or a perched zone controls convergence of a large model.
- Complementarity solvers offer a cleaner treatment of seepage faces, drains and dry cells than switching boundary conditions between iterations.
- None of this pays off for confined, linear models, and nonlinear preconditioning slows down runs in which Newton converges in two to four iterations.

### Gaps
- I could not read the abstracts or text of Lott et al. (2012), Farthing et al. (2003), Wang and Tchelepi (2013) or Lee et al. (2020), so no numbers from them appear above. A search in this session found no 2024 or 2025 benchmark that compares Newton, Picard, L-scheme and trust-region solvers for Richards' equation on common problems.
- None of MODFLOW, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere documents an L-scheme option in the sources I could reach.
- I found no published test of ASPEN or NLDD, L-scheme switching, or a posteriori stopping criteria inside MODFLOW, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere.
- The Møyner et al. (2024) paper reports runtimes for field models, but Springer blocked the full text. The run times quoted above come from the developers' 2024 slides. SINTEF's pages for the two 2025 NLDD papers were also blocked, so I cite their repository records without titles.
- I did not find a recent benchmark intercomparison for Richards solvers that ranks linearization schemes on common problems. IH-MIP2 compares models, not solvers.

## Linear solvers and HPC: what is the state of algebraic multigrid, multistage preconditioners, matrix-free multigrid, GPU and performance-portable solvers, mixed precision, and exascale subsurface codes?

### Takeaway
Algebraic multigrid is the workhorse for Darcy-type pressure systems outside groundwater practice. A 2002 test showed AMG up to 140 times faster than MODFLOW's PCG solver, yet the release notes for parallel MODFLOW 6 still describe incomplete factorization preconditioners. GPU ports of AMG give roughly 2 to 8 times over many-core CPU nodes for large problems, ParFlow reports 10 to 30 times, and all sources warn that small models run slower on GPUs. Parallel MODFLOW 6 arrived in 2024 on top of PETSc, and its published global application scaled to 278 million cells but with a one-level preconditioner whose iteration counts grew by a third to a half under partitioning.

### Cited Findings

#### Algebraic multigrid libraries
- Classical AMG and its theory are reviewed by Stüben and by Xu and Zikatanov. Smoothed aggregation is the main alternative coarsening. - Groundwater evidence, dated 2002. Detwiler and co-authors linked AMG into MODFLOW-2000 and found CPU times "up to 140 times faster" than the PCG2 solver, at the cost of up to nine times more memory in 3D and four times in 2D. For steady transport AMG was up to 17 times faster than ILU-preconditioned GMRES with 20% more memory. [Detwiler et al. (2002), "Comparison of an Algebraic Multigrid Algorithm to Two Iterative Solvers Used for Modeling Ground Water Flow and Transport", Groundwater 40:267-272](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2002.tb02654.x) [V]
- USGS released that link as the LMG package and later added a geometric multigrid package, GMG. [Mehl and Hill (2001), "MODFLOW-2000, the U. S. - A 2014 rebuild of MODFLOW on the JASMIN structured-mesh infrastructure gives a second groundwater data point. [Cheng, Mo and Shao (2014), "Accelerating groundwater flow simulation in MODFLOW using JASMIN-based parallel computing", Groundwater 52:194-205](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12047) [V]
- AMG preconditioners with sparse approximate inverse smoothers were tested on GPU clusters with a groundwater modelling case supplied by the Jülich Supercomputing Centre. [Abdullahi Hassan et al. (2019), "Efficient Algebraic Multigrid Preconditioners on Clusters of GPUs", Parallel Processing Letters 29:1950001](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129626419500014) [V]
- hypre BoomerAMG is the reference parallel AMG and scales past 100,000 cores. [Henson and Yang (2002), "BoomerAMG: A parallel algebraic multigrid solver and preconditioner", Applied Numerical Mathematics 41:155-177](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-9274%2801%2900115-5) [B] [Baker et al. - The Exascale Computing Project reported that hypre's AMG-preconditioned CG on a GPU ran 1.6 to 3.2 times faster than the same code on 640 IBM Power 9 cores for a coupled 3D Poisson benchmark, that the solve phase alone ran up to about 7 to 8 times faster, and that gains depend strongly on the PDE and shrink on coarse levels. [Farber (2021), "A new approach in the hypre library brings performant GPU-based algebraic multigrid to exascale supercomputers and the general HPC community", Exascale Computing Project highlight, 19 April 2021](https://www.exascaleproject.org/highlight/a-new-approach-in-the-hypre-library-brings-performant-gpu-based-algebraic-multigrid-to-exascale-supercomputers-and-the-general-hpc-community/) [V]
- NVIDIA AmgX offers classical and aggregation AMG with Krylov solvers on GPUs and "achieves 2-5x speedup on a single GPU against a competitive implementation on the CPU". - AMGCL is a header-only C++ library with OpenMP, CUDA, OpenCL and MPI backends. [Demidov (2019), "AMGCL: An Efficient, Flexible, and Extensible Algebraic Multigrid Implementation", Lobachevskii Journal of Mathematics 40:535-546](https://doi.org/10.1134/S1995080219050056) [B] [Demidov, "AMGCL documentation, Benchmarks", readthedocs](https://amgcl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/benchmarks.html) [V]

#### Constrained pressure residual and multistage preconditioners
- CPR extracts a near-elliptic pressure system from the coupled Jacobian, solves it approximately in a first stage, and applies a cheap global smoother such as ILU in a second stage. It dates from 1983. [Wallis (1983), "Incomplete Gaussian Elimination as a Preconditioning for Generalized Conjugate Gradient Acceleration", SPE Reservoir Simulation Symposium, paper SPE-12265-MS](https://doi.org/10.2118/12265-MS) [B]
- Gries and co-authors explain why naive CPR-AMG fails on some industrial cases and give a left and right preconditioning recipe that makes the pressure block suitable for AMG. They show it on ill-conditioned industrial problems. [Gries et al. (2014), "Preconditioning for Efficiently Applying Algebraic Multigrid in Fully Implicit Reservoir Simulations", SPE Journal 19:726-736](https://doi.org/10.2118/163608-PA) [V]
- Bui, Elman and Moulton compare black-box AMG, a CPR-AMG variant and a block factorization preconditioner for fully implicit two-phase flow. The block methods were the most reliable across capillary regimes and the block factorization scaled optimally with problem size. [Bui, Elman and Moulton (2017), "Algebraic Multigrid Preconditioners for Multiphase Flow in Porous Media", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 39:S662-S680](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082652) [V]
- Extensions add temperature to the first stage (CPTR) and handle thermal, compositional and reactive flow. [Roy et al. (2020), "A Constrained Pressure-Temperature Residual (CPTR) Method for Non-Isothermal Multiphase Flow in Porous Media", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 42:B1014-B1040](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1292023) [V] [Cremon, Castelletto and White (2020), "Multi-stage preconditioners for thermal-compositional-reactive flow in porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 418:109607](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2020.109607) [B]
- hypre's multigrid reduction (MGR) framework generalizes CPR algebraically and is the solver strategy in GEOS for every multiphysics problem. [Bui et al. (2020), "A Scalable Multigrid Reduction Framework for Multiphase Poromechanics of Heterogeneous Media", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 42:B379-B396](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1256117) [V] [Bui et al. (2021), "Multigrid reduction preconditioning framework for coupled processes in porous and fractured media", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 387:114111](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2021.114111) [B] [Settgast et al. (2024), "GEOS: A performance portable multi-physics simulation framework for subsurface applications", Journal of Open Source Software 9:6973](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.06973) [V]

#### Multigrid that exploits layered, extruded and anisotropic meshes
- Tuminaro and co-authors built a multigrid for meshes that are a 2D unstructured mesh extruded in a thin third direction. It semi-coarsens in the thin direction with matrix-dependent transfers until one layer is left, then applies standard AMG. The motivation was ice sheets, where layer spacing is far smaller than horizontal spacing. [Tuminaro et al. (2016), "A Matrix Dependent/Algebraic Multigrid Approach for Extruded Meshes with Applications to Ice Sheet Modeling", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 38:C504-C532](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1040839) [V]
- Brown and co-authors report textbook multigrid efficiency, one order of magnitude residual reduction per iteration, for 3D hydrostatic ice flow on high-aspect domains with strong anisotropy. [Brown, Smith and Ahmadia (2013), "Achieving Textbook Multigrid Efficiency for Hydrostatic Ice Sheet Flow", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 35:B359-B375](https://doi.org/10.1137/110834512) [V]
- Müller and Scheichl solved an atmospheric pressure-correction equation with more than 10^10 unknowns on 65,536 cores. Their tensor-product geometric multigrid "exploits the strong vertical anisotropy", and, unlike one-level methods, it "is robust with respect to parameter variations". [Müller and Scheichl (2014), "Massively parallel solvers for elliptic partial differential equations in numerical weather and climate prediction", Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society 140:2608-2624](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2327) [V]
- The Met Office confirmed the same approach for the LFRic model and credits multigrid's scalability to "its avoidance of expensive global reduction operations". [Maynard, Melvin and Müller (2020), "Multigrid preconditioners for the mixed finite element dynamical core of the LFRic atmospheric model", Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society 146:3917-3936](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3880) [V]
- Black-box multigrid with operator-induced interpolation handled discontinuous coefficients on structured grids as early as 1982 and was later used for numerical homogenization. [Dendy (1982), "Black box multigrid", Journal of Computational Physics 48:366-386](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991%2882%2990057-2) [B] [Moulton, Dendy and Hyman (1998), "The Black Box Multigrid Numerical Homogenization Algorithm", Journal of Computational Physics 142:80-108](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1998.5911) [B]

#### Matrix-free geometric multigrid
- deal.II's matrix-free framework evaluates operators cell by cell with sum factorization and avoids storing the matrix. [Kronbichler and Kormann (2012), "A generic interface for parallel cell-based finite element operator application", Computers & Fluids 63:135-147](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compfluid.2012.04.012) [B] [Arndt et al. [Kronbichler and Wall (2018), "A Performance Comparison of Continuous and Discontinuous Galerkin Methods with Fast Multigrid Solvers", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 40:A3423-A3448](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M110455X) [V]
- deal.II also provides parallel geometric multigrid on adaptively refined meshes with local smoothing. MFEM and the CEED project carry the same ideas to GPUs for high-order elements. [Clevenger et al. (2021), "A Flexible, Parallel, Adaptive Geometric Multigrid Method for FEM", ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 47:1-27](https://doi.org/10.1145/3425193) [V] [Anderson et al. #### Domain decomposition with robust coarse spaces

(2014), "Abstract robust coarse spaces for systems of PDEs via generalized eigenproblems in the overlaps", Numerische Mathematik 126:741-770](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-013-0576-y) [B] [Jolivet et al. - GEOS uses RAJA, CHAI and Umpire for portability and hypre for all linear solves. On Frontier its mechanics solver weak-scaled to 32,768 GPUs and 81.3 x 10^9 degrees of freedom, and compositional flow scaled to 2,048 GPUs. [Settgast et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.06973) [V]
- OPM Flow's GPU path is the best documented open example. A 2023 evaluation of BiCGStab with ILU0 on several GPU libraries found that a GPU "can accelerate a single dual-threaded MPI process up to 5.6 times, and that it can compare with around 8 dual-threaded MPI processes", on models from 50,000 to one million cells. The GPU won only from about two million cells upward and was 1.17 times faster per linear solve at three million cells. [ACROSS project (2024), "Progress of GPU acceleration in OPM Flow", blog post, 26 February 2024](https://www.acrossproject.eu/progress-of-gpu-acceleration-in-opm-flow/) [V]
- The GPU linear solver layer was published as gpu-ISTL in 2025. ### Inferences
- The strongest single transfer in these notes is to make AMG-preconditioned Krylov the normal choice for large MODFLOW models. The evidence is 24 years old, the PETSc link now exists in Extended MODFLOW, and GLOBGM's iteration growth under a one-level Schwarz preconditioner is the textbook symptom that a multilevel method removes.
- Regional groundwater grids are thin, layered and extruded, exactly the mesh class for which ice-sheet and weather modellers built semi-coarsening and tensor-product multigrid. Line relaxation in the vertical plus AMG on the collapsed 2D problem should beat black-box AMG on models with large vertical anisotropy and thin layers.
- Dry or inactive cells, strong heterogeneity and the nonsymmetric Newton and XT3D matrices are the cases where black-box AMG can stall. The CPR literature shows the remedy is a physics-aware first stage, which for groundwater means a multilevel solve on the symmetric conductance part and a cheap smoother for the rest.
- GPUs make sense for groundwater only above about a million cells and only if assembly also runs on the device. Below that size, a faster algorithm on a CPU is the better investment.
- Memory is the price of AMG, up to nine times in the 2002 study. That matters for ensemble runs on one machine, where many small models compete for memory bandwidth. In that setting single precision or mixed precision may give more throughput than GPU offload.
- For ensembles of models that share a grid, AMG setup can be reused across realizations when conductivity changes are modest. I have not seen that measured for groundwater.

### Gaps
- I could not find a peer-reviewed scalability study of the PETSc-based Extended MODFLOW 6. The only published numbers are from the earlier GLOBGM prototype.
- I could not read Falgout et al. (2021) or Jolivet et al. (2013), so the hypre GPU numbers come from an Exascale Computing Project news article and no numbers are given for GenEO.
- I found no published test of AmgX, AMGCL, Ginkgo or hypre GPU solvers on MODFLOW, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere matrices.
- I found GPU results for OPM Flow and PFLOTRAN-OGS but none for Amanzi-ATS, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere, and no GPU benchmark of MODFLOW newer than 2013 to 2014.
- I have no source that measures mixed-precision solvers on groundwater problems, and no verified numbers for GEOS compositional flow on GPUs against CPUs.

## Time integration: adaptive and high-order implicit stepping, exponential and Krylov methods, Laplace-transform methods, multirate stepping and parallel-in-time

### Takeaway
Groundwater codes still mostly use backward Euler with user-set or heuristic step control. Three mature ideas from elsewhere apply directly. Error-controlled variable-order implicit integrators, available in SUNDIALS and PETSc, have been shown on Richards problems since 1997. For linear confined flow, Krylov, rational Krylov and Laplace-transform methods replace hundreds of time steps by a handful of solves, and groundwater papers demonstrated them between 1989 and 1991 before the field dropped them. Parallel-in-time methods work for diffusion but break even only at a few hundred processors and gave 6 to 10 times at 4,096 processors in the reference study, so they are a low priority for typical groundwater models.

### Cited Findings

#### Baseline in groundwater codes
- MODFLOW 6's adaptive time stepping is driven by package-specific criteria and solver failure. Version 6.6.0 added ATS_PERCEL, which limits the fraction of a cell a water particle can cross in one step for transport. Version 6.8.0 added ATS_COURANT for kinematic-wave stream routing, and its notes refer to "discarded/retried time step solve attempts". None of these controls estimates the local truncation error of the head solution. [USGS (2024), "MODFLOW 6 release notes, version 6.6.0, December 2024", MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6 repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.6.0.tex) [V] [USGS (2026), "MODFLOW 6 release notes, version 6.8.0, September 2, 2026", MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6 repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex) [V]

#### Error-controlled adaptive and higher-order implicit stepping
- Tocci, Kelley and Miller solved the pressure-head form of Richards' equation by the method of lines with a variable-order, variable-step BDF integrator and found it more accurate and cheaper than fixed low-order stepping. [Tocci, Kelley and Miller (1997), "Accurate and economical solution of the pressure-head form of Richards' equation by the method of lines", Advances in Water Resources 20:1-14](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2896%2900008-5) [B]
- Kavetski and co-authors added truncation-error control to a mass-conservative mixed-form Richards solver. Miller and co-authors combined temporal and spatial adaptivity. [Kavetski, Binning and Sloan (2001), "Adaptive time stepping and error control in a mass conservative numerical solution of the mixed form of Richards equation", Advances in Water Resources 24:595-605](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2800%2900076-2) [B] [Miller, Abhishek and Farthing (2006), "A spatially and temporally adaptive solution of Richards' equation", Advances in Water Resources 29:525-545](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2005.06.008) [B]
- Higher-order time integration was also tested with mixed finite elements for variably saturated flow and for two-phase flow. [Farthing, Kees and Miller (2003), "Mixed finite element methods and higher order temporal approximations for variably saturated groundwater flow", Advances in Water Resources 26:373-394](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2802%2900187-2) [B] [Kees and Miller (2002), "Higher order time integration methods for two-phase flow", Advances in Water Resources 25:159-177](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2801%2900054-9) [B]
- D'Haese and co-authors compared heuristic step control, which reacts to nonlinear iteration counts, with adaptive control based on truncation error. The heuristic method won under strongly nonlinear conditions. The adaptive method won when accuracy requirements were strict. They recommend a mixed strategy. [D'Haese et al. (2007), "Assessment of adaptive and heuristic time stepping for variably saturated flow", International Journal for Numerical Methods in Fluids 53:1173-1193](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.1369) [V]
- SUNDIALS provides variable-order BDF (CVODE, IDA), additive Runge-Kutta (ARKODE) and Anderson-accelerated nonlinear solvers behind one interface, with GPU-capable vectors in recent versions. [Hindmarsh et al. (2005), "SUNDIALS: suite of nonlinear and differential/algebraic equation solvers", ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 31:363-396](https://doi.org/10.1145/1089014.1089020) [B] [Gardner et al. (2022), "Enabling New Flexibility in the SUNDIALS Suite of Nonlinear and Differential/Algebraic Equation Solvers", ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 48:1-24](https://doi.org/10.1145/3539801) [B]
- The SUMMA land model was rebuilt on SUNDIALS to separate process equations from their numerical solution, and passes a set of synthetic "laugh tests" for soil water and snow. The authors "posit that some land models may have difficulty in solving these benchmark problems". [Clark et al. (2021), "The numerical implementation of land models: Problem formulation and laugh tests", Journal of Hydrometeorology 22:1627-1648](https://doi.org/10.1175/JHM-D-20-0175.1) [V]
- Step-size selection can be treated as a control problem. PI controllers give smoother step sequences than the classical controller. [Söderlind (2002), "Automatic Control and Adaptive Time-Stepping", Numerical Algorithms 31:281-310](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1021160023092) [B]

- Multirate infinitesimal methods advance slow and fast components with different step sizes and are available in SUNDIALS. [Sandu (2019), "A Class of Multirate Infinitesimal GARK Methods", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 57:2300-2327](https://doi.org/10.1137/18M1205492) [B] [Chinomona and Reynolds (2021), "Implicit-Explicit Multirate Infinitesimal GARK Methods", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 43:A3082-A3113](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1354349) [B]
- A conservative multirate method combined with multiscale basis functions lets only the cells near saturation fronts take small steps in multiphase porous media flow. [Delpopolo Carciopolo et al. - Super-time-stepping with Runge-Kutta-Legendre stages extends the stable step of explicit schemes for parabolic problems and suits GPUs, since it needs no linear solve. [Meyer, Balsara and Aslam (2014), "A stabilized Runge-Kutta-Legendre method for explicit super-time-stepping of parabolic and mixed equations", Journal of Computational Physics 257:594-626](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2013.08.021) [B]

#### Exponential integrators and Krylov matrix-function methods
- Exponential integrators solve the linear part of a stiff system exactly through the matrix exponential and need only matrix-vector products when combined with Krylov approximations. [Hochbruck and Ostermann (2010), "Exponential integrators", Acta Numerica 19:209-286](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492910000048) [B]
- Algorithms and software exist for the action of the matrix exponential on a vector, including adaptive Krylov codes. [Al-Mohy and Higham (2011), "Computing the Action of the Matrix Exponential, with an Application to Exponential Integrators", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 33:488-511](https://doi.org/10.1137/100788860) [V] [Niesen and Wright (2012), "Algorithm 919: a Krylov subspace algorithm for evaluating the phi-functions appearing in exponential integrators", ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 38:1-19](https://doi.org/10.1145/2168773.2168781) [B] [Gaudreault, Rainwater and Tokman (2018), "KIOPS: A fast adaptive Krylov subspace solver for exponential integrators", Journal of Computational Physics 372:236-255](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2018.06.026) [B]
- Groundwater evidence, dated. Dunbar and Woodbury used the Lanczos algorithm in 1989 to reduce the finite element groundwater flow equations to a small tridiagonal system and reported an efficient solution for long simulations, including aquifers with large conductivity contrasts. The Arnoldi version for transport gave "an impressive 613% increase in speed over the conventional Crank-Nicolson scheme" on the Borden landfill problem. [Dunbar and Woodbury (1989), "Application of the Lanczos Algorithm to the solution of the groundwater flow equation", Water Resources Research 25:551-558](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR025i003p00551) [V] [Woodbury, Dunbar and Nour-Omid (1990), "Application of the Arnoldi Algorithm to the solution of the advection-dispersion equation", Water Resources Research 26:2579-2590](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR026i010p02579) [V]
- Carr, Moroney and Turner applied an exponential Euler method with Krylov approximation to Richards' equation and compared it with variable-step BDF using Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov. [Carr, Moroney and Turner (2011), "Efficient simulation of unsaturated flow using exponential time integration", Applied Mathematics and Computation 217:6587-6596](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amc.2011.01.041) [S]
- Exponential integrators were also used for advection-dominated reactive transport in heterogeneous media. [Tambue, Lord and Geiger (2010), "An exponential integrator for advection-dominated reactive transport in heterogeneous porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 229:3957-3969](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2010.01.037) [B]
- Rational Krylov methods use shifted solves instead of matrix-vector products and converge much faster for diffusion operators. In transient electromagnetic geophysics, which is governed by the same parabolic operator, optimized poles gave "convergence within an a priori determined number of iterations independent of mesh size and conductivity structure", and reusing a few poles with direct solvers made the method faster than earlier schemes. [Güttel (2013), "Rational Krylov approximation of matrix functions: Numerical methods and optimal pole selection", GAMM-Mitteilungen 36:8-31](https://doi.org/10.1002/gamm.201310002) [B] [Börner, Ernst and Güttel (2015), "Three-dimensional transient electromagnetic modelling using Rational Krylov methods", Geophysical Journal International 202:2025-2043](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggv224) [V] [Druskin and Knizhnerman (1998), "Extended Krylov Subspaces: Approximation of the Matrix Square Root and Related Functions", SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications 19:755-771](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479895292400) [B]
- A 2025 preprint by Börner and Güttel uses rational approximants of the exponential that share one denominator for all output times. The number of shifted linear systems, which can be solved in parallel, "does not depend on the number of required time channels nor the spatial discretization". [Börner and Güttel (2025), "Fast parallel transient electromagnetic modeling using a uniform-in-time approximation to the exponential", arXiv preprint 2506.11657](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2506.11657) [V]

#### Laplace-transform and semi-analytical time treatment
- Sudicky's Laplace Transform Galerkin method removes the time derivative from the finite element equations, solves a set of independent complex-valued systems, and inverts numerically. It gave solutions "essentially devoid of numerical dispersion for grid Peclet numbers in excess of 30", and because the transformed systems are independent it suits parallel computers. It also suits dual-porosity and aquifer-aquitard problems. [Sudicky (1989), "The Laplace Transform Galerkin Technique: A time-continuous finite element theory and application to mass transport in groundwater", Water Resources Research 25:1833-1846](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR025i008p01833) [V]
- The Laplace transform finite difference method needs "no more than eight matrix solutions" per output time regardless of how far that time is from the initial condition, where a standard scheme "may require several hundred time steps". [Moridis and Reddell (1991), "The Laplace Transform Finite Difference Method for Simulation of Flow Through Porous Media", Water Resources Research 27:1873-1884](https://doi.org/10.1029/91WR01190) [V]
- Sheen, Sloan and Thomée gave the parallel version with quadrature along a contour, and Weideman and Trefethen optimized parabolic and hyperbolic contours. [Sheen, Sloan and Thomée (2003), "A parallel method for time discretization of parabolic equations based on Laplace transformation and quadrature", IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis 23:269-299](https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/23.2.269) [B] [Weideman and Trefethen (2007), "Parabolic and hyperbolic contours for computing the Bromwich integral", Mathematics of Computation 76:1341-1356](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-07-01945-X) [B]
- Inversion algorithms are reviewed by Kuhlman, and the de Hoog algorithm is the usual choice. [Kuhlman (2013), "Review of inverse Laplace transform algorithms for Laplace-space numerical approaches", Numerical Algorithms 63:339-355](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11075-012-9625-3) [B] [de Hoog, Knight and Stokes (1982), "An Improved Method for Numerical Inversion of Laplace Transforms", SIAM Journal on Scientific and Statistical Computing 3:357-366](https://doi.org/10.1137/0903022) [B]
- TTim applies the same idea to transient multi-layer analytic element models. [Bakker (2013), "Semi-analytic modeling of transient multi-layer flow with TTim", Hydrogeology Journal 21:935-943](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10040-013-0975-2) [B]
- Groundwater status of the modern form. Bakhos, Saibaba and Kitanidis combined a Laplace-transform exponential integrator with a Krylov method for shifted systems for the forward problem of transient hydraulic tomography, and report transient solutions at several times for roughly the cost of one steady solve. [Bakhos, Saibaba and Kitanidis (2015), "A fast algorithm for parabolic PDE-based inverse problems based on Laplace transforms and flexible Krylov solvers", Journal of Computational Physics 299:940-954](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2015.07.007) [B] [Bakhos, Saibaba and Kitanidis (2013), "Fast solvers for transient hydraulic tomography based on Laplace transform", AGU Fall Meeting abstract H51G-1283](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013AGUFM.H51G1283B) [S]

#### Parallel-in-time methods
- Parareal iterates between a cheap coarse propagator and parallel fine propagators on time slices. Its convergence for parabolic problems is well understood. [Lions, Maday and Turinici (2001), "Résolution d'EDP par un schéma en temps «pararéel »", Comptes Rendus de l'Académie des Sciences - Series I - Mathematics 332:661-668](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0764-4442%2800%2901793-6) [B] [Gander and Vandewalle (2007), "Analysis of the Parareal Time-Parallel Time-Integration Method", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 29:556-578](https://doi.org/10.1137/05064607X) [B] [Gander (2015), "50 Years of Time Parallel Time Integration", Contributions in Mathematical and Computational Sciences 69-113](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-23321-5_3) [B]
- MGRIT generalizes Parareal to a multilevel, non-intrusive algorithm and is implemented in XBraid. For the heat equation on a 129 x 129 x 16,385 space-time grid, the crossover against sequential stepping was at about 256 processors and the speedup at 4,096 processors was up to 10. For a 3D case on a 33^3 x 4,097 grid the speedup at 4,096 processors was up to 6. The authors estimate that their model problem needs about 50 times as many processors to break even. Sequential stepping was "both faster and uses less memory" at small processor counts. [Falgout et al. (2014), "Parallel Time Integration with Multigrid", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 36:C635-C661](https://doi.org/10.1137/130944230) [V]
- For nonlinear parabolic problems, MGRIT reached scaling comparable to the linear case only after changes such as better initial guesses for the nonlinear solver and delayed spatial coarsening. The same paper cites linear speedups of up to 50. [Falgout et al. (2017), "Multigrid Reduction in Time for Nonlinear Parabolic Problems: A Case Study", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 39:S298-S322](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082330) [V]
- PFASST couples spectral deferred corrections with a parallel-in-time sweep and has run on hundreds of thousands of cores for particle problems. [Emmett and Minion (2012), "Toward an efficient parallel in time method for partial differential equations", Communications in Applied Mathematics and Computational Science 7:105-132](https://doi.org/10.2140/camcos.2012.7.105) [B] [Speck et al. (2012), "A massively space-time parallel N-body solver", 2012 International Conference for High Performance Computing, Networking, Storage and Analysis 1-11](https://doi.org/10.1109/SC.2012.6) [B]
- Subsurface applications are few. Garrido and co-authors applied a Parareal-type scheme in a reservoir simulator in 2005. Miyagi and co-authors put Parareal into TOUGH2 and cut the run time of a radial injection model to one-eighth of the serial run. [Garrido, Espedal and Fladmark (2005), "A Convergent Algorithm for Time Parallelization Applied to Reservoir Simulation", Domain Decomposition Methods in Science and Engineering, Lecture Notes in Computational Science and Engineering 40](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-26825-1_48) [B] [Miyagi, Yamamoto, Iizuka and Ono (2021), "Adaptation of parallel in time integration algorithm to groundwater simulations", Transactions of the Japan Society for Computational Engineering and Science](https://doi.org/10.11421/jsces.2021.20210013) [V]
- A 2025 poster from the UG4 group reports, for a surface and subsurface porous media application, a relative speedup of 4.94 with two-level Parareal on 8,192 cores and 14.62 with MGRIT on 65,536 cores. This is a poster, not a reviewed paper. [Goethe University Frankfurt UG4 group (2025), poster on parallel-in-time porous media simulation, NIC symposium](https://msqc.cgi-host6.rz.uni-frankfurt.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PosterNIC2_A3.pdf) [S]
- A January 2026 preprint analyses Parareal for quasi-static poroelasticity and derives time-step restrictions for convergence. [Jimenez-Ciga et al. (2026), "Parareal algorithm for coupled elliptic-parabolic problems", arXiv preprint 2601.15191](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.15191) [V]
- Surveys list applications and a unified convergence analysis. A methodological paper warns against inflated speedup claims, for example comparisons against a poorly chosen serial baseline. [Ong and Schroder (2020), "Applications of time parallelization", Computing and Visualization in Science 23:11](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00791-020-00331-4) [B] [Gander et al. (2023), "A Unified Analysis Framework for Iterative Parallel-in-Time Algorithms", SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 45:A2275-A2303](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1487163) [B] [Götschel et al. (2021), "Twelve Ways to Fool the Masses When Giving Parallel-in-Time Results", Springer Proceedings in Mathematics & Statistics 81-94](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-75933-9_4) [B]

### Inferences
- A groundwater code that exposes its residual and Jacobian through an interface such as the MODFLOW API could hand time integration to SUNDIALS or PETSc TS and gain error control, variable order and PI step controllers without new numerical development. The gain is largest for Richards-type and strongly transient problems, where D'Haese et al. show heuristic control is fragile at tight tolerances.
- Laplace-transform and rational Krylov methods fit a specific but common case: linear, confined or linearized models with time-invariant coefficients and many output times or many forcing scenarios. They do not apply when conductance depends on head, when boundary conditions switch, or when cells dry.
- Rational Krylov time integration and projection-based model reduction are the same mathematics. The few shifted solves that integrate the system also give a reduced model for repeated forcing scenarios, which is why this item appears again under reduced-order models.
- Parallel-in-time is unattractive for calibration and uncertainty workloads. Those are already embarrassingly parallel across model runs, so spare processors are better spent on more runs than on a 6 to 10 times gain that needs thousands of cores.
- Multirate stepping matters for coupled groundwater and surface-water or transport models, where MODFLOW today forces one step size on all coupled models.

## Multiscale and upscaling: multiscale finite volume and finite element methods, numerical homogenization, adaptive mesh refinement, embedded fractures and conduit coupling

### Takeaway
Multiscale methods replace static upscaling by numerically computed basis functions that keep fine-scale heterogeneity inside a coarse solve. After 15 years of development the restriction-smoothed basis variant (MsRSB) reached a commercial reservoir simulator, where its developers report about one order of magnitude speedup, far below the thousandfold gains first claimed. Used as iterative solvers, multiscale methods perform about as well as algebraic multigrid, so their distinct value is reusable basis functions for many runs on one grid. Embedded discrete fracture models and block-structured adaptive mesh refinement are mature and have direct groundwater analogues in MODFLOW-USG's connected linear networks and MODFLOW's local grid refinement.

#### Upscaling background

- Spectral (FFT-based) solvers compute effective properties of periodic heterogeneous cells quickly. Dykaar and Kitanidis used a spectral approach for effective hydraulic conductivity in 1992, and the Moulinec-Suquet FFT scheme from composite mechanics is the modern form. #### Multiscale finite element and mixed multiscale methods
- The multiscale finite element method of Hou and Wu builds coarse basis functions by solving local fine-scale problems, so the coarse system inherits subgrid heterogeneity. [Hou and Wu (1997), "A Multiscale Finite Element Method for Elliptic Problems in Composite Materials and Porous Media", Journal of Computational Physics 134:169-189](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1997.5682) [B]
- Mixed multiscale methods do the same for flux basis functions and give locally conservative fine-scale velocities. Aarnes showed how to use them for speed or for accuracy in reservoir simulation, and later work with Krogstad and Lie extended them to corner-point grids through mimetic subgrid solvers. [Chen and Hou (2002), "A mixed multiscale finite element method for elliptic problems with oscillating coefficients", Mathematics of Computation 72:541-576](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-02-01441-2) [B] [Aarnes (2004), "On the Use of a Mixed Multiscale Finite Element Method for GreaterFlexibility and Increased Speed or Improved Accuracy in Reservoir Simulation", Multiscale Modeling & Simulation 2:421-439](https://doi.org/10.1137/030600655) [B] [Aarnes, Krogstad and Lie (2008), "Multiscale mixed/mimetic methods on corner-point grids", Computational Geosciences 12:297-315](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-007-9072-8) [B]
- Groundwater status. Ye, Xue and Xie applied the multiscale finite element method to five 2D and two 3D groundwater flow problems and reported that it "significantly" reduced computational effort and improved accuracy against conventional finite elements. He and Ren built a locally conservative finite volume version and tested it on lognormal conductivity fields. A triple-grid variant targets 3D regional flow. [Ye, Xue and Xie (2004), "Application of the multiscale finite element method to flow in heterogeneous porous media", Water Resources Research 40:W09202](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003WR002914) [V] [He and Ren (2005), "Finite volume multiscale finite element method for solving the groundwater flow problems in heterogeneous porous media", Water Resources Research 41:W10417](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004WR003934) [V] [Xie et al. #### Multiscale finite volume, algebraic multiscale and MsRSB
- The multiscale finite volume method of Jenny, Lee and Tchelepi solves local problems on a dual coarse grid to get basis functions and reconstructs a conservative fine-scale velocity. [Jenny, Lee and Tchelepi (2003), "Multi-scale finite-volume method for elliptic problems in subsurface flow simulation", Journal of Computational Physics 187:47-67](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9991%2803%2900075-5) [B]
- Iterating the method with a smoother removes the localization error and turns it into a convergent linear solver. [Hajibeygi et al. (2008), "Iterative multiscale finite-volume method", Journal of Computational Physics 227:8604-8621](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2008.06.013) [B]
- The two-stage algebraic multiscale solver combines a multiscale coarse stage with a local ILU or Schwarz stage. On strongly heterogeneous cases with large grid aspect ratios it was "comparable to state-of-the-art algebraic multigrid methods" and scaled nearly linearly with problem size. [Zhou and Tchelepi (2012), "Two-Stage Algebraic Multiscale Linear Solver for Highly Heterogeneous Reservoir Models", SPE Journal 17:523-539](https://doi.org/10.2118/141473-PA) [V]
- Algebraic formulations followed for incompressible and compressible flow, and a parallel version solved a 128-million-cell pressure problem with a ninefold speedup on 12 cores. [Wang, Hajibeygi and Tchelepi (2014), "Algebraic multiscale solver for flow in heterogeneous porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 259:284-303](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2013.11.024) [B] [Ţene, Wang and Hajibeygi (2015), "Adaptive algebraic multiscale solver for compressible flow in heterogeneous porous media", Journal of Computational Physics 300:679-694](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2015.08.009) [B] [Manea, Sewall and Tchelepi (2016), "Parallel Multiscale Linear Solver for Highly Detailed Reservoir Models", SPE Journal 21:2062-2078](https://doi.org/10.2118/173259-PA) [V]
- MsRSB computes basis functions by restricted smoothing iterations instead of local solves, which makes it work on unstructured grids with faults, inactive cells and high contrast. [Møyner and Lie (2016), "A multiscale restriction-smoothed basis method for high contrast porous media represented on unstructured grids", Journal of Computational Physics 304:46-71](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2015.10.010) [B]
- Industrial result. MsRSB is implemented in a commercial simulator. Its developers write that "the multiscale method may give an order-of-magnitude speedup, which is significant, but less than the theoretical O(10^3) speedup that was indicated for simple flow problems". [Lie et al. (2017), "Successful application of multiscale methods in a real reservoir simulator environment", Computational Geosciences 21:981-998](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9627-2) [V]
- The companion paper reports that the multiscale solver handled very large real-field black-oil models "an order of magnitude faster than the current commercial version of the solver". [Kozlova et al. (2016), "A Real-Field Multiscale Black-Oil Reservoir Simulator", SPE Journal 21:2049-2061](https://doi.org/10.2118/173226-PA) [V]
- The vendor's 2022 release notes quote an average speedup of 1.9 times and best cases above 5 times on a range of realistic models. These are vendor numbers for a whole release. [SLB (2022), "INTERSECT 2022.1 release news", SLB software news page](https://www.software.slb.com/software-news/software-top-news/intersect/intersect-2022-1) [S]

- Generalized multiscale finite elements enrich each coarse block with several basis functions from local spectral problems, which handles channels and high contrast that defeat one basis function per block. ### Inferences
- For a single forward run, multiscale solvers offer little beyond what AMG gives, as Zhou and Tchelepi's comparison says. Their case for groundwater rests on ensembles: basis functions computed once per conductivity realization can be reused across all stress periods and forcing scenarios, and updated locally when conductivity changes locally.
- An approximate multiscale solution with a reconstructed conservative velocity field is a natural low-fidelity model for multilevel or multifidelity Monte Carlo. It keeps the fine heterogeneity that static upscaling loses.
- The embedded fracture idea and the MODFLOW-USG connected linear network are the same construction: a lower-dimensional feature exchanges flow with the cells it crosses through a computed conductance. The reservoir literature adds two things groundwater codes lack, a systematic transfer coefficient for arbitrary orientation and the projection correction for barriers such as clay-filled faults.
- Dynamic adaptive refinement pays for moving fronts, which in groundwater means plumes, saltwater interfaces and wetting fronts. It does not pay for head solutions in regional flow, which are smooth away from wells and can be handled by static refinement.
- High-contrast channelized aquifers are where one basis function per coarse block fails and spectral enrichment of the GMsFEM or GenEO type is needed.

- I could not read the two fracture benchmark papers beyond their abstracts, so the notes do not say which methods failed on which case. My statement that the standard embedded model fails for blocking fractures is from memory of the pEDFM paper.
- I found no multiscale finite volume or MsRSB implementation coupled to MODFLOW, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere, and no groundwater study that measures basis-function reuse across an ensemble. A search in this session for MsFV or MsRSB applied to aquifer models returned only reservoir material.
- I found no source that couples an embedded discrete fracture model with MODFLOW for karst or fractured aquifers.

## Projection-based reduced-order models: POD-Galerkin, POD-DEIM, reduced basis, DMD, operator inference, Krylov and balanced truncation, hyper-reduction

### Takeaway
Projection-based reduction has been demonstrated on MODFLOW-type models since 2004 and works very well for linear confined flow with fixed boundary types, where a regional model ran about 1,000 times faster after reduction. Unconfined flow needs hyper-reduction such as DEIM, which a UCLA group implemented for MODFLOW, and an independent test found it struggles with head-dependent river boundaries. Reservoir simulation reports speedups from about 10 for plain POD up to 800 for trajectory piecewise linearization, with training costs of a few full runs and accuracy that degrades away from the training controls. Reduction with respect to a spatially distributed random conductivity field is the hard case, and the number of basis functions grows with the dimension of the parameter field.

### Inferences
- The reliable groundwater use case is narrow and common: a linear or linearized confined model, fixed boundary types, and many runs that differ in pumping, recharge or a few zone parameters. Management optimization and scenario screening fit it. Siade et al.'s factor of 1,000 is for exactly this case.
- For calibration with hundreds or thousands of distributed parameters, a global POD basis must span the parameter space, and the basis size grows until the reduced model is no longer cheap. Pasetto et al. needed greedy sampling and an error estimator to control this. Localized bases or multifidelity estimators look like better fits than one global basis.
- Head-dependent boundaries such as rivers, drains and evapotranspiration are switches, and they break both the linear subspace assumption and DEIM's smooth interpolation. This matches the Gosses et al. finding and the Kolmogorov barrier argument. Any MODFLOW reduced model should treat these cells explicitly instead of projecting them.
- Intrusive projection needs the system matrices at every step. The MODFLOW API makes that possible without changing the Fortran source, which lowers the barrier that the UCLA work faced when it had to modify MODFLOW itself.
- Operator inference and DMD need only snapshot output, so they can be built from existing model runs. Their weakness is the same as POD's outside the training range, and neither comes with the error bounds of certified reduced basis methods.
- Plain POD-Galerkin projection of a finite volume model does not conserve mass cell by cell. If the reduced heads feed transport or particle tracking, a flux-conservative variant is needed, as Yang et al. point out.

- I could not read Boyce et al. (2015) or Stanko et al. (2016) directly. The dimension reductions quoted come from search summaries of their abstracts, and I have no verified run-time speedups for the unconfined MODFLOW reduced models.
- I found no certified reduced basis study, with rigorous error bounds, for a regional groundwater model with realistic boundary packages. The certified examples above use idealized domains.

## Other formulations: analytic and boundary element methods, fast multipole and hierarchical matrices, stochastic Galerkin and collocation, lattice Boltzmann, particle and meshfree methods, semi-analytical solvers

### Takeaway
Most of these formulations fill niches. Analytic elements are a groundwater-native method that borrowed fast multipole ideas and now handles multi-layer transient flow through Laplace transforms. Stochastic Galerkin and sparse-grid collocation beat Monte Carlo only when a few random dimensions describe the conductivity field, and the multilevel Monte Carlo family is the more general remedy. Lattice Boltzmann, SPH and meshfree collocation have groundwater demonstrations but no evidence of an advantage over finite volumes for regional Darcy flow. Their real use is pore-scale flow, conduits and transport without numerical dispersion.

- For Richards' equation with random soil parameters, a multilevel estimator that solves simpler nonlinear problems on coarse levels and the hard one only on the finest level gave computational savings over plain Monte Carlo. The forward solver was modified Picard with cell-centred multigrid. [Kumar et al. (2020), "A parametric acceleration of multilevel Monte Carlo convergence for nonlinear variably saturated flow", Computational Geosciences 24:311-331](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-019-09922-8) [V]
- Quasi-Monte Carlo methods have been analysed and applied for lognormal conductivity fields with many random dimensions. [Graham et al. (2011), "Quasi-Monte Carlo methods for elliptic PDEs with random coefficients and applications", Journal of Computational Physics 230:3668-3694](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2011.01.023) [B]
- Ballio and Guadagnini give a procedure for assessing the convergence of Monte Carlo statistics in groundwater simulations. [Ballio and Guadagnini (2004), "Convergence assessment of numerical Monte Carlo simulations in groundwater hydrology", Water Resources Research 40:W04603](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003WR002876) [B]

#### Lattice Boltzmann methods
- Lattice Boltzmann models exist for Darcy-scale and Brinkman-type flow in porous media and for variably saturated flow with anisotropy. [Guo and Zhao (2002), "Lattice Boltzmann model for incompressible flows through porous media", Physical Review E 66:036304](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.66.036304) [B] [Ginzburg (2006), "Variably saturated flow described with the anisotropic Lattice Boltzmann methods", Computers & Fluids 35:831-848](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compfluid.2005.11.001) [B]
- For karst, lattice Boltzmann simulations capture inertial conduit flow and eddies that Darcy-based models cannot, and reproduce two-region breakthrough behaviour. [Anwar and Sukop (2009), "Lattice Boltzmann Models for Flow and Transport in Saturated Karst", Groundwater 47:401-413](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2008.00514.x) [V]

#### Particle and meshfree methods
- Random walk particle tracking solves advection-dispersion without numerical dispersion and is reviewed by Salamon and co-authors. Benson and co-authors compare Eulerian and Lagrangian schemes for transport with nonlinear reactions. [Salamon, Fernàndez-Garcia and Gómez-Hernández (2006), "A review and numerical assessment of the random walk particle tracking method", Journal of Contaminant Hydrology 87:277-305](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconhyd.2006.05.005) [B] [Benson et al. (2017), "A comparison of Eulerian and Lagrangian transport and non-linear reaction algorithms", Advances in Water Resources 99:15-37](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2016.11.003) [B]
- Groundwater codes already use particles for advection. MODPATH 7 does this for MODFLOW, and MODFLOW 6.5.0 added a built-in particle tracking model. [Pollock (2016), "User guide for MODPATH Version 7: a particle-tracking model for MODFLOW", USGS Open-File Report 2016-1086](https://doi.org/10.3133/ofr20161086) [B] [USGS (2024), "MODFLOW 6 release notes, version 6.5.0, May 23, 2024", MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6 repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.5.0.tex) [V]
- Smoothed particle hydrodynamics has been used for solute transport in heterogeneous media and compared with Eulerian schemes in highly heterogeneous formations. A review covers its use for pore-scale multiphase flow and reactive transport. [Herrera, Massabó and Beckie (2009), "A meshless method to simulate solute transport in heterogeneous porous media", Advances in Water Resources 32:413-429](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2008.12.005) [B] [Boso, Bellin and Dumbser (2013), "Numerical simulations of solute transport in highly heterogeneous formations: A comparison of alternative numerical schemes", Advances in Water Resources 52:178-189](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2012.08.006) [B] [Tartakovsky et al. (2016), "Smoothed particle hydrodynamics and its applications for multiphase flow and reactive transport in porous media", Computational Geosciences 20:807-834](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-015-9468-9) [B]
- Radial basis function finite differences and generalized finite differences replace the mesh by scattered nodes. Demonstrations exist for Richards' equation and for unconfined groundwater flow. [Fornberg and Flyer (2015), "Solving PDEs with radial basis functions", Acta Numerica 24:215-258](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492914000130) [B] [Chávez-Negrete, Domínguez-Mota and Santana-Quinteros (2018), "Numerical solution of Richards' equation of water flow by generalized finite differences", Computers and Geotechnics 101:168-175](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compgeo.2018.05.003) [B] [Meenal and Eldho (2011), "Simulation of groundwater flow in unconfined aquifer using meshfree point collocation method", Engineering Analysis with Boundary Elements 35:700-707](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enganabound.2010.12.003) [B]

#### Spectral methods
- Fourier spectral solvers are confined to periodic or simple domains and serve mainly to compute effective conductivity of heterogeneous blocks. [Dykaar and Kitanidis (1992), "Determination of the effective hydraulic conductivity for heterogeneous porous media using a numerical spectral approach: 1. Method", Water Resources Research 28:1155-1166](https://doi.org/10.1029/91WR03084) [B] [Moulinec and Suquet (1998), "A numerical method for computing the overall response of nonlinear composites with complex microstructure", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering 157:69-94](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0045-7825%2897%2900218-1) [B]

### Inferences
- Analytic elements remain the best tool for fast regional screening and for supplying boundary conditions to a local MODFLOW model. Fast multipole acceleration matters only for models with thousands of elements.
- Stochastic Galerkin is a poor fit for existing groundwater simulators because it is intrusive and its coupled system grows with the number of random dimensions. Geostatistical conductivity fields with short correlation lengths need hundreds of Karhunen-Loève terms, which defeats sparse grids as well. Non-intrusive collocation works for a handful of uncertain zone parameters.
- Multilevel and multifidelity Monte Carlo are the transferable ideas for uncertainty propagation, because they wrap any forward solver and get their coarse levels from coarser grids, multiscale solvers or reduced models. Their link to ensemble-based history matching belongs to the inverse-methods notes.
- Lattice Boltzmann, SPH and meshfree collocation solve a problem regional groundwater flow does not have. Finite volumes on layered grids are cheap, conservative and well served by multigrid. The particle methods earn their place in transport, where grid-based schemes smear fronts.
- Hierarchical matrices matter to groundwater less for flow than for dense covariance matrices in geostatistics, which again belongs to the inverse-methods notes.

### Gaps
- I could not read Cliffe et al. (2011), Graham et al. (2011) or Müller et al. (2013) directly, so I give no cost-reduction numbers for multilevel or quasi-Monte Carlo.
- I found no quantitative comparison of lattice Boltzmann, SPH or RBF-FD against finite volume solvers for regional groundwater flow at equal accuracy.
- I did not verify the current state of open analytic element codes such as TimML, TTim and AnAqSim beyond the cited papers.
- Boundary element methods for groundwater have an older literature that I did not cover, because I could not confirm sources.

## Ranked shortlist: which transfers to groundwater flow and transport simulators look most promising?

### Takeaway
The best transfers are the ones with old, strong evidence and low integration cost: multilevel linear solvers, better-safeguarded nonlinear iteration, and model reduction for linear many-query problems. GPU ports, multiscale solvers and adaptive refinement pay only for very large or front-dominated models. Parallel-in-time, stochastic Galerkin and meshfree methods are poor bets for regional groundwater flow. The ranking below is my judgement. It weighs reported gain, how directly the evidence applies to groundwater, and how much code change MODFLOW 6, MODFLOW-USG, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere would need.

### Cited Findings
These are the evidence items the ranking leans on. Each is documented in the sections above.
- AMG in MODFLOW-2000 ran up to 140 times faster than PCG2 with four to nine times the memory. [Detwiler et al. (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2002.tb02654.x) [V]
- An AMG-based parallel rebuild of MODFLOW on 40 cores ran nine times faster than MODFLOW's GMG solver on a heterogeneous aquifer. [Cheng, Mo and Shao (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12047) [V]
- Parallel MODFLOW 6 with a one-level Schwarz preconditioner needed 34 to 58% more iterations after partitioning, and reached 62% efficiency on 224 cores for 168 million cells. [Verkaik et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-17-275-2024) [V]
- Multigrid built for thin, vertically anisotropic meshes scaled to more than 10^10 unknowns in weather prediction and was designed for extruded ice-sheet meshes. [Müller and Scheichl (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2327) [V] [Tuminaro et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1040839) [V]
- MODFLOW 6 has had an MPI and PETSc build since version 6.5.0 of May 2024. [USGS (2024)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.5.0.tex) [V]
- An adaptive L-scheme and Newton switch for Richards' equation matched Newton's speed where Newton converged and kept converging where Newton failed. The plain L-scheme cost about 373% of its run time in one test. [Stokke et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2023.10.020) [V]
- Nonlinear domain decomposition loses to Newton on easy steps and wins where Newton cuts time steps. It is a user option in OPM Flow, where the developers measured run-time reductions of 12 to 27% on two open field models. [Møyner et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-023-10215-4) [V] [OPM project (2026)](https://github.com/OPM/opm-simulators/blob/master/opm/simulators/flow/BlackoilModelParameters.cpp) [V] [Rasmussen and Møyner (2024)](https://opm-project.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/rasmussen-nldd-opm-oslo2024.pdf) [V]
- A posteriori stopping criteria gave "important computational savings" by ending linear and nonlinear iterations once their error no longer mattered. [Ern and Vohralík (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1137/120896918) [V]
- A reduced model of a confined regional aquifer ran about 1,000 times faster than the full model. POD and POD-DEIM lost accuracy or speed with river and variable-head boundaries. [Siade, Putti and Yeh (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008792) [V] [Gosses, Nowak and Wöhling (2017)](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2017/EGU2017-7925.pdf) [V]
- The Laplace transform finite difference method needs at most eight matrix solves per output time, and rational Krylov time integration converges independently of mesh size and conductivity structure. [Moridis and Reddell (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1029/91WR01190) [V] [Börner, Ernst and Güttel (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggv224) [V]
- Heuristic time-step control is fragile when accuracy demands are strict, and truncation-error control is fragile under strong nonlinearity. [D'Haese et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.1369) [V]
- Consistent flux schemes agree with each other and differ from TPFA on non-K-orthogonal grids, and cheap static indicators locate the inconsistency before a run. XT3D needs adequate cell connectivity to deliver its accuracy. [Raynaud et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.2118/203920-PA) [V] [Provost et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13459) [V]
- In SPE11, analyst choices and grid resolution moved results more than scheme or grid type. [Nordbotten et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijggc.2025.104519) [V]
- A multiscale solver in a commercial simulator gave about one order of magnitude, and as a linear solver the method is comparable to AMG. [Lie et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9627-2) [V] [Zhou and Tchelepi (2012)](https://doi.org/10.2118/141473-PA) [V]
- GPU gains are 1.6 to 3.2 times for hypre AMG against 640 CPU cores, 2 to 5 times for AmgX, 2 to 8 times for a MODFLOW CG solver, and 10 to 30 times for ParFlow, with slower runs on small problems. In OPM Flow a GPU with a simple preconditioner overtook a 16-core CPU with CPR-AMG only above about two million cells. [Farber (2021)](https://www.exascaleproject.org/highlight/a-new-approach-in-the-hypre-library-brings-performant-gpu-based-algebraic-multigrid-to-exascale-supercomputers-and-the-general-hpc-community/) [V] [Naumov et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1137/140980260) [V] [Hughes and White (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12004) [V] [Piotrowski et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2023-1079) [V] [ParFlow developers](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/parflow/parflow/master/README-GPU.md) [V] [ACROSS project (2024)](https://www.acrossproject.eu/progress-of-gpu-acceleration-in-opm-flow/) [V]
- Single precision gave ECMWF about 40% more throughput with no noticeable loss of forecast accuracy. [Váňa et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-16-0228.1) [V]
- Parallel-in-time integration of the heat equation broke even at about 256 processors and gave 6 to 10 times at 4,096. [Falgout et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1137/130944230) [V]

### Inferences
Ranked from most to least promising.

1. Multilevel linear solvers as the default for large models. Make AMG-preconditioned CG or BiCGSTAB the standard choice above roughly 10^5 to 10^6 cells, through the PETSc and hypre link that Extended MODFLOW already has, and add a variant that semi-coarsens the vertical direction first for thin layered grids. The evidence is the oldest and largest in these notes, and the GLOBGM iteration growth shows the current one-level preconditioner is the bottleneck at scale. Integration cost is low because the library link exists. It does not pay for small models, and the memory overhead hurts when many ensemble members share one node.

2. Safeguarded nonlinear iteration. In order of effort: Anderson acceleration of the outer Picard loop, a posteriori stopping criteria that stop over-solving the linear system early in the nonlinear iteration, the L-scheme to Newton switch for Richards-type and unsaturated options, and nonlinear domain decomposition on MODFLOW 6 multi-model simulations. Convergence failure and tiny time steps cost groundwater modellers more wall time than slow linear algebra does, and they break calibration runs outright. The first two items need only stored iterates and residual norms. The gain is zero for confined linear models.

3. Model reduction and transform methods for linear many-query problems. For confined or linearized models with fixed boundary types, POD-Galerkin, rational Krylov and Laplace-domain solves replace long transient runs by a few solves or a system of order 10 to 100. Management optimization, scenario screening and pumping-test interpretation fit this. The MODFLOW API gives access to matrices without source changes, and pyMOR supplies the algorithms. Head-dependent boundaries and unconfined conditions cut the gain sharply, so this is a tool for a defined model class and not a general accelerator.

4. Error-controlled time integration from a library. Hand transient integration to SUNDIALS or PETSc TS for variable-order implicit stepping with truncation-error control and PI step controllers, combined with the existing convergence-based heuristics as D'Haese et al. recommend. The gain is fewer wasted steps and a stated accuracy in time, which matters for transport and for Richards problems. It needs the code to expose residual and Jacobian callbacks.

5. Discretization diagnostics before new schemes. Add static K-orthogonality indicators and tracer-based dynamic indicators to pre-processing tools such as FloPy, so users know where the two-point flux is inconsistent and where XT3D or more connectivity is needed. Then consider a monotone nonlinear two-point option for models where heads drive transport or density. SPE11 says the scheme is rarely the largest error, which is why diagnostics rank above new schemes.

6. Multiscale basis reuse and multilevel Monte Carlo for ensembles. Multiscale solvers give about ten times in reservoir practice and no advantage over AMG for one solve. Their groundwater value is in ensembles on a fixed grid, where basis functions are reused across time steps and scenarios, and as the coarse level of a multilevel or multifidelity estimator. Integration cost is high and no groundwater code has it, so this is a research project with MRST as the prototype platform.

7. Embedded fracture and conduit models with the projection correction. The embedded discrete fracture model generalizes what MODFLOW-USG's connected linear network already does, adds a systematic transfer coefficient for arbitrary orientation, and with the projection variant handles barrier faults. It lets structured or layered grids carry faults, fracture zones and karst conduits without regridding. The effort is moderate and local to one package.

8. GPU offload and reduced precision, for large models only. GPU gains of 2 to 8 times for AMG and up to 30 times for a structured-grid code are real, but all sources report losses on small problems, and assembly must move to the device as well. A cheaper experiment is single or mixed precision in the linear solver for ensemble runs, where memory bandwidth limits throughput.

9. Complementarity formulations for seepage faces, drains and dry cells. Glaciology solves the mathematically similar margin problem as a variational inequality with reduced-space or semi-smooth Newton methods, which PETSc provides. This could replace boundary-condition switching between iterations. The evidence is by analogy only, so it ranks low despite moderate effort.

10. Dynamic adaptive mesh refinement for transport fronts. AMReX and p4est are mature, but they demand a different grid data structure from the one MODFLOW uses, and regional head fields do not need moving refinement. This is worth it only for plume, saltwater-interface and wetting-front problems.

11. Parallel-in-time integration. The break-even point of a few hundred processors and the modest speedups make it a poor use of cores that could run more ensemble members instead.

Not recommended for regional flow: stochastic Galerkin, because it is intrusive and scales badly with random dimension; lattice Boltzmann, SPH and meshfree collocation, because finite volumes with multigrid are already cheap and conservative; isogeometric and high-order HDG methods, because heterogeneous aquifers lack the smoothness those methods exploit.
