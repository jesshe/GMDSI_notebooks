## 1. What PDE forms, discretizations, nonlinear schemes and linear solvers does each simulator use, and what are the documented performance and robustness pain points?

### Takeaway

All the mainstream codes use low-order, fully implicit schemes (two-point-flux control-volume finite difference, cell-centred finite volume, or linear Galerkin and control-volume finite elements) with Picard or Newton outer loops and ILU- or multigrid-preconditioned Krylov inner solvers. The documented pain points are the same ones practitioners complain about: dry cells and steady-state Newton convergence, Richards-equation robustness, anisotropy and grid-orientation error on unstructured grids, forward runs of minutes to hours, parallel speedup that only pays off above roughly 1e5 to 1e6 cells, and no production GPU path outside a beta option in ParFlow.

### Cited findings

#### MODFLOW 6

- Release sequence. 6.4.4 (13 Feb 2024), 6.5.0 (23 May 2024), 6.6.0 (19 Dec 2024), 6.6.1 (10 Feb 2025), 6.6.2 (12 May 2025), 6.6.3 (29 Sep 2025), 6.7.0 (6 Feb 2026), 6.8.0 (2 Sep 2026), 6.8.1 (23 Sep 2026), 6.8.2 (30 Oct 2026). The develop branch identifies itself as 6.9.0.dev0. [V] [MODFLOW 6 releases](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/releases), [version.txt on develop](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/version.txt)
- The GWF model uses a generalized control-volume finite-difference (CVFD) formulation in which a cell can connect to any number of neighbours, on DIS, DISV or DISU grids. [M] [Langevin, Hughes, Banta, Niswonger, Panday, Provost 2017, Documentation for the MODFLOW 6 Groundwater Flow Model, USGS TM 6-A55](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A55)
- MODFLOW 6 "consolidates popular capabilities available in MODFLOW variants, such as the unstructured grid support in MODFLOW-USG, the Newton-Raphson formulation in MODFLOW-NWT, and the support for partitioned stress boundaries in MODFLOW-CDSS", and its architecture "supports multiple model instances and multiple types of models within a single simulation". [V, abstract] [Langevin, Hughes, Provost, Russcher, Panday 2023/2024, MODFLOW as a configurable multi-model hydrologic simulator, Groundwater 62(1):111-123](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13351)
- Unconfined flow has two formulations. The Standard formulation is a Picard iteration with optional rewetting. The Newton-Raphson formulation uses upstream weighting of the saturated thickness so cells stay in the solution when dry. [M] [TM 6-A55](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A55)
- Dry-cell behaviour under Newton was still being adjusted in 2026. Version 6.7.0 added HIGHEST_CELL_SATURATION, which "is intended to prevent flow from leaving a dry cell", applies only with Newton, and with the AMT-HMK averaging option reproduces "the same horizontal conductance as MODFLOW-USG when upstream weighting is used (LAYCON=4)". [V] [MF6 release notes v6.7.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex)
- XT3D is the option for full three-dimensional anisotropy and for grids that violate the CVFD geometric requirements. It widens the stencil to neighbours of neighbours and makes the matrix asymmetric when solved implicitly. The older ghost-node correction (GNC) is the alternative for nested grids. [M] [Provost, Langevin, Hughes 2017, Documentation for the XT3D option in the NPF Package of MODFLOW 6, USGS TM 6-A56](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A56)
- "GNC is not supported on flow exchanges when running in parallel or when the XT3D option is enabled on the exchange." [V] [MF6 release notes v6.5.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.5.0.tex)
- The Iterative Model Solution (IMS) linear solver offers two accelerators, "CG - preconditioned conjugate gradient method" and "BICGSTAB - preconditioned bi-conjugate gradient stabilized method". Preconditioners are ILU(0), MILU(0), ILUT and MILUT, set through level of fill, drop tolerance and a relaxation factor. Reordering is reverse Cuthill-McKee or minimum degree. Scaling is diagonal or L2-norm. [V] [sln-ims.dfn](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/mf6io/mf6ivar/dfn/sln-ims.dfn)
- Nonlinear controls in IMS are under-relaxation (off by default, described as "an effective tool for highly nonlinear models when there are large and often counteracting changes in the calculated dependent variable between successive outer iterations"), residual-based backtracking (BACKTRACKING_NUMBER, "a value of 10 works well for most problems"), and pseudo-transient continuation. [V] [sln-ims.dfn](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/mf6io/mf6ivar/dfn/sln-ims.dfn). The under-relaxation variants are simple, Cooley and delta-bar-delta. [M] [Hughes, Langevin, Banta 2017, Documentation for the MODFLOW 6 framework, USGS TM 6-A57](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A57)
- Pseudo-transient continuation (PTC) applies only to steady-state stress periods under Newton and is on by default. The input guide warns "In some cases, however, PTC can worsen the convergence behavior, especially when the initial conditions are similar to the solution." [V] [sln-ims.dfn](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/mf6io/mf6ivar/dfn/sln-ims.dfn)
- PTC still had a correctness bug in multi-model runs until 6.8.2. The PTC factor "was calculated from the residuals of the first model in the solution, or of the first model on each process in a parallel run", so the iteration history depended on model order and process layout. [V] [MF6 release notes v6.8.2](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.2.tex)
- A convergence-check bug lived from 6.4.2 to 6.6.3. "In cases where the linear solver failed to converge, or when it did converge but the STRICT option was active and convergence did not occur on the first iteration, the solution was erroneously considered for overall convergence." [V] [MF6 release notes v6.6.3](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.6.3.tex)
- Coupling of advanced packages by substitution iteration is a known convergence weak spot. Version 6.8.0 added an IMPLICIT option to the Lake Package that "solves each lake stage as an additional unknown in the groundwater flow matrix", "typically converges in far fewer outer iterations and can solve problems for which the default formulation fails to converge", and "makes the coefficient matrix asymmetric and therefore requires the BICGSTAB linear acceleration". [V] [MF6 release notes v6.8.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex)
- The unsaturated zone in MODFLOW 6 is the UZF package, a one-dimensional vertical kinematic-wave approximation. MODFLOW 6 has no three-dimensional Richards solver. [M] [TM 6-A55](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A55)
- Transport (GWT) "solves the solute transport equation using numerical methods and a generalized CVFD approach, which can be used with regular MODFLOW grids or with unstructured grids." [V] [MODFLOW 6 README](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/README.md), documented in [Langevin, Provost, Panday, Hughes 2022, USGS TM 6-A61](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A61) [M]
- Numerical dispersion and grid-orientation error on unstructured grids are acknowledged in the 2026 notes. A new UTVD advection scheme "typically improves accuracy and reduces numerical dispersion more than the existing TVD scheme when simulating advective transport either on unstructured grids, or when the dominant flow direction is poorly aligned with the grid." [V] [MF6 release notes v6.7.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex)
- Parallel MODFLOW 6 is domain decomposition over multiple models linked by GWF-GWF exchanges, built on MPI and PETSc. "A special extended version of MODFLOW can be compiled to simulate multiple models in parallel. This extended version of MODFLOW 6 is based on the Message Passing Interface (MPI) and the Portable, Extensible Toolkit for Scientific Computation (PETSc) libraries." [V] [MF6 release notes v6.5.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.5.0.tex)
- Parallel limits stated in the user guide. "It is not possible to run multiple instances of a model type on the same spatial domain in parallel." Supported process models are GWF, GWT and GWE. GNC on exchanges is unsupported, with XT3D suggested as the workaround. FloPy's Mf6Splitter does the partitioning. [V] [Extended MODFLOW User Guide wiki](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/wiki/Extended-MODFLOW-User-Guide)
- The wiki still lists mover transport across parallel exchanges as unsupported, but 6.8.0 says "GWT-GWT or GWE-GWE exchanges that also include MVT or MVE connections, respectively, is now supported for simulations run in parallel mode." The release note is newer, so I read the wiki as stale on this point. [V] [MF6 release notes v6.8.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex); contradicted by [Extended MODFLOW User Guide wiki](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/wiki/Extended-MODFLOW-User-Guide)
- Developer guidance on when parallel pays off. Joseph Hughes (USGS), 3 April 2026: "A rule of thumb is parallel simulations are not practical unless the number of cells is at least 100,000 to 1,000,000." "The only fair parallel runtime comparison is running the model on 1 core with the PETSc solver." IMS "has been tuned over the years for groundwater problems". The user who prompted this had a 20,000-cell density-dependent GWF plus GWT model that took 2 min 49 s serial with IMS, 6 min 26 s on one core with PETSc, and 4 min 17 s on two cores with PETSc. This is a forum anecdote, not a benchmark. [V] [MODFLOW users group thread, April 2026](https://groups.google.com/g/modflow/c/kRmPZP05iNw)
- Parallel robustness fixes through 2025 and 2026 include an MPI integer overflow for subdomains of "~20M nodes or more" (6.6.2), a PETSc linear-solver failure that halted the program (6.6.3), and an adaptive-time-stepping deadlock when the transport time step differed across processes (6.8.0). [V] [v6.6.2](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.6.2.tex), [v6.6.3](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.6.3.tex), [v6.8.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex)
- None of the release notes from 6.5.0 to 6.8.2 mentions GPU support. [V, by absence] [MF6 release notes directory](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/tree/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous)
- GPU work on MODFLOW exists only as an older research solver. Hughes and White built an unstructured preconditioned conjugate gradient (UPCG) solver for MODFLOW-2005-era code with Jacobi, ILU(0), MILU(0) and polynomial preconditioners in which "all basic linear algebra operations are performed on the GPGPU". [V, abstract] [Hughes, White 2013, Use of general purpose graphics processing units with MODFLOW, Groundwater 51(6):833-846](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12004)
- Large-scale parallel MODFLOW precedents. The Netherlands Hydrological Instrument prototype parallelized the conjugate gradient solver with MPI using an additive Schwarz preconditioner and an optional additive coarse-grid correction. [M] [Verkaik, Hughes, van Walsum, Oude Essink, Lin, Bierkens 2021, Environmental Modelling & Software 143:105092](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2021.105092). The 30 arcsec global model GLOBGM reports a speedup of 138 on 224 cores for its largest (Afro-Eurasia) submodel. [V, via search extract of the paper] [Verkaik, Sutanudjaja, Oude Essink, Lin, Bierkens 2024, GMD 17:275-300](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-17-275-2024)

#### MODFLOW-USG and USG-Transport

- MODFLOW-USG version 1 is a CVFD unstructured-grid code with the ghost-node correction package, the Connected Linear Network (CLN) process, and a Sparse Matrix Solver package that offers Newton-Raphson with upstream weighting and the PCGU and xMD linear solvers. [M] [Panday, Langevin, Niswonger, Ibaraki, Hughes 2013, USGS TM 6-A45](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A45)
- Development since then sits with GSI Environmental, outside the USGS release channel. The GSI page lists a MODFLOW-USG download named "Version-2.8.1" under a 2026/08 path and a USG-Transport download "USG_T_Version-2.5.0-2" uploaded April 2025. Listed capabilities include "Unsaturated zone flow (Richards equation) and transport", dual porosity, density dependence, matrix diffusion, heat transport, turbulent conduit flow, time-varying material properties and automated time stepping. [V] [GSI Environmental MODFLOW-USG page](https://www.gsienv.com/software/modflow-usg/)

#### MODFLOW-NWT and MT3D-USGS

- MODFLOW-NWT is a Newton formulation of MODFLOW-2005 with upstream weighting (UPW package) and two asymmetric-matrix solvers, GMRES and xMD. [M] [Niswonger, Panday, Ibaraki 2011, USGS TM 6-A37](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A37)
- The dry-cell and rewetting problem that motivated NWT has its own literature. [M] [Doherty 2001, Groundwater 39(6):863-869](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2001.tb02474.x); [Painter, Başağaoğlu, Liu 2008, Groundwater 46(6):873-881](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2008.00483.x); [Keating, Zyvoloski 2009, Groundwater 47(4):569-579](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2009.00555.x); [Bedekar, Niswonger, Kipp, Panday, Tonkin 2012, Groundwater 50(2):187-198](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2011.00829.x); [Hunt, Feinstein 2012, Groundwater 50(5):659-663](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2012.00976.x)
- A production MODFLOW-NWT model (Mississippi Alluvial Plain, 13 layers, 1,777 adjustable parameters) took more than 6 hours per forward run on a desktop. [V] [Hunt, White, Duncan, Haugh, Doherty 2021, Groundwater 59(6):788-798](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)
- MT3D-USGS is the USGS continuation of MT3DMS with added packages for streams, lakes, the unsaturated zone and contaminant treatment systems, used with MODFLOW-2005 and MODFLOW-NWT. [M] [Bedekar, Morway, Langevin, Tonkin 2016, USGS TM 6-A53](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A53). MODFLOW 6 borrowed from it in 2026, when a DEPENDENT_VARIABLE_SCALING option for GWT and GWE "applies the same scaling approach used in the MT3DMS GCG package". [V] [MF6 release notes v6.7.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex)

#### FEFLOW

- FEFLOW is a Galerkin finite-element code for variably saturated flow, mass and heat transport. It solves Richards' equation with several formulations and a primary-variable-switching technique, offers Picard and Newton iteration, and uses predictor-corrector adaptive time stepping. Linear solvers include PCG and BiCGSTAB-type methods and the algebraic multigrid package SAMG. [M] [Diersch 2014, FEFLOW: Finite Element Modeling of Flow, Mass and Heat Transport in Porous and Fractured Media, Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-38739-5)
- DHI's version history lists "Control-Volume Finite Element Method" and "3D Unstructured Meshing" as new in FEFLOW 7.0, "New SAMG solver version" in 7.2 (December 2018), "Improvements in FEFLOW's Numerics" in 7.5 (November 2021), FEFLOW 10.0 in November 2024, and FEFLOW 11 in January 2026 with "Performance improvements" and no solver detail. FePEST is mentioned from 7.0 on, with "Enhanced features for FePEST parallelization" in 8.0. The page says nothing about GPUs. [V] [DHI knowledge base KA-01203, FEFLOW version history](https://support.dhigroup.com/knowledgebase/article/KA-01203/en-us)

#### HydroGeoSphere

- HydroGeoSphere solves the three-dimensional Richards equation for the subsurface and the two-dimensional diffusion-wave equation for surface water in one globally implicit system, with control-volume finite elements, Newton-Raphson linearization and adaptive time stepping. [M] [Brunner, Simmons 2012, HydroGeoSphere: a fully integrated, physically based hydrological model, Groundwater 50(2):170-176](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2011.00882.x). The equation set is confirmed in [Delottier, Doherty, Brunner 2023, GMD 16:4213-4231](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023) [V]
- Parallelism is shared-memory OpenMP over domain partitions. The published scheme parallelizes Jacobian assembly, uses multi-block node reordering for the LU solve, and privatizes parameters, with a preconditioned BiCGSTAB solver. [M, plus search extract] [Hwang, Park, Sudicky, Forsyth 2014, Environmental Modelling & Software 61:39-58](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2014.06.024)
- Aquanty's own guidance says "parallel efficiency degrades with increasing CPU count" and that the best efficiency was found at "around 100,000 nodes per CPU". [V] [Aquanty, HGS parallelization best practices, 2016](https://www.aquanty.com/blog/2016/2/18/hgs-parallelization-best-practices)
- Robustness under random parameter fields is poor enough to affect inversion. In an ensemble-smoother run with a 204,000-cell HGS model (about 10 minutes per run), 30 of 100 realizations were abandoned for slow convergence. [V] [Delottier, Doherty, Brunner 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023)

#### ParFlow

- ParFlow "solves the Richards equation for three-dimensional variably saturated groundwater flow and the two-dimensional kinematic wave approximation of the shallow water equations for overland flow" using "a conservative centered finite-difference scheme", backward Euler time stepping, an inexact Newton-Krylov solver through KINSOL, and GMRES preconditioned with the PFMG multigrid from hypre. It uses a structured orthogonal or terrain-following grid "specifically for its parallel performance". [V] [Kuffour, Engdahl, Woodward, Condon, Kollet, Maxwell 2020, GMD 13:1373-1397](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-13-1373-2020)
- The same paper states the solver weak point. "These high aspect ratios produce numerical anisotropy in the problem, which can cause the multigrid algorithms to converge slowly", and surface coupling adds non-structured matrix entries that reduce multigrid effectiveness. It also cites a run with "over 8.1 billion unknowns". [V] [Kuffour et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-13-1373-2020)
- The solver lineage is Newton-Krylov-multigrid for heterogeneous variably saturated flow, with the free-surface overland boundary condition added later. [M] [Jones, Woodward 2001, Advances in Water Resources 24:763-774](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708(00)00075-0); [Kollet, Maxwell 2006, Advances in Water Resources 29:945-958](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2005.08.006)
- GPU status in the repository today. "Parflow GPU support is still in beta and there may be issues with it." It is "compatible only with NVIDIA GPUs", "is often slower for test cases and small problems due to initialization overhead", and "Currently, the only preconditioner resulting in a good performance is MGSemi, when applied to spinup problems." [V] [ParFlow README-GPU.md](https://github.com/parflow/parflow/blob/master/README-GPU.md)
- The GPU port is an application-side adaptor layer for compute kernels and memory management with CUDA and Kokkos back ends. [V, abstract] [Hokkanen, Kollet, Kraus, Herten, Hrywniak, Pleiter 2021, Computational Geosciences 25:1579-1590](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-021-10051-4). An earlier conference abstract reported up to 20 times speedup on a node with 2 Skylake CPUs and 4 V100 GPUs relative to the original code. [V, via search extract] [EGU2020-12904](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-12904.html)

#### PFLOTRAN

- PFLOTRAN is a finite-volume, fully implicit, massively parallel flow and reactive transport code built on PETSc Newton-Krylov solvers with MPI domain decomposition on structured and unstructured grids. [M] [Hammond, Lichtner, Mills 2014, Evaluating the performance of parallel subsurface simulators: an illustrative example with PFLOTRAN, WRR 50:208-228](https://doi.org/10.1002/2012WR013483)
- The development documentation is titled "PFLOTRAN v6.0 documentation". [V] [documentation.pflotran.org](https://documentation.pflotran.org/)
- Standard Newton fails on strongly nonlinear cases, which is why the code gained Newton trust-region solvers (NTR and NTRDC, Newton trust-region dogleg-Cauchy). The 2021 Sandia and PNNL paper introduced NTRDC "to resolve extreme nonlinearities in the models". [V, via search extract] [Park and co-authors 2021, Linear and nonlinear solvers for simulating multiphase flow within large-scale engineered subsurface systems, OSTI 1828787](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1828787); [PFLOTRAN Newton solver card](https://documentation.pflotran.org/user_guide/cards/subsurface/newton_solver_card.html)
- A follow-up paper reports a repository-scale case that did not finish under standard Newton on 144 cores within 96 hours, and high-temperature cases that "did not converge with NR, but they do complete successfully with the trust-region methods". These are multiphase GENERAL-mode problems, not Richards-mode groundwater problems. [V, via search extract; author list not checked] [Newton trust-region methods with primary variable switching for simulating high temperature multiphase porous media flow, OSTI 1995950](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1995950)

#### iMOD

- The current Deltares suite is iMOD Python ("iMOD Python 1.1.0 is released", dated 2026-08-10), which builds MODFLOW 6 models, the iMOD Coupler, which "currently supports a coupling to MetaSWAP and RIBASIM", and a QGIS plugin. The numerical kernel for new work is therefore MODFLOW 6. [V] [iMOD Suite documentation](https://deltares.github.io/iMOD-Documentation/)

#### Richards equation and integrated models in general

- "The numerical solution of Richards' equation remains challenging." [V, abstract] [Farthing, Ogden 2017, SSSAJ 81(6):1257-1269](https://doi.org/10.2136/sssaj2017.02.0058)
- "The robustness and the efficiency of RRE-based models can deteriorate rapidly when certain unfavorable conditions are met. These demerits of RRE hinder its application on large-scale vadose zone hydrology problems and uncertainty quantification, both of which requires many runs of the prediction model." [V, abstract] [Zha, Yang, Zeng, Tso, Zeng, Shi 2019, WIREs Water 6(5):e1364](https://doi.org/10.1002/wat2.1364)
- The integrated-model intercomparison IH-MIP2 ran ATS, Cast3M, CATHY, GEOtop, HydroGeoSphere, MIKE-SHE and ParFlow on common benchmarks. [V, abstract] [Kollet et al. 2017, WRR 53:867-890](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR019191)

#### How simulator numerics leak into calibration

- Finite-difference derivatives need tight solver convergence. "Convergence criteria of iterative solvers employed by a model should be set tightly", and "it may be necessary to limit the use of model numerical devices such as adaptive time stepping if it is felt that they may unwittingly contribute alterations to model outputs that do not arise exclusively from alterations to the values of model parameters". [V] [PEST++ users manual v5.2.28, section 1.4.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Random parameter fields trigger failures. "Use of an ensemble of random parameter fields is likely to trigger occasional model run failures, or occasional simulations whose run times are excessively long." [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.4](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- "Complex models are often difficult to calibrate because of their long run times and sometimes questionable numerical stability." [V, abstract] [Doherty, Christensen 2011, WRR 47:W12534](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010763)

### Inferences

- None of the surveyed production codes ships a higher-order or mimetic flux scheme. On general unstructured grids MODFLOW 6 offers XT3D (wider stencil, asymmetric matrix) or GNC (not usable with XT3D or in parallel). A consistent flux approximation that stays symmetric and parallel-friendly is an open slot.
- Preconditioning is the common weak link. MODFLOW 6 serial relies on ILU-family preconditioners, the PETSc route did not beat tuned IMS on a small problem, ParFlow's multigrid degrades with aspect-ratio anisotropy, and HGS iteration counts rise with partition count.
- The pattern of 2025 to 2026 fixes (PTC factor, convergence check, HIGHEST_CELL_SATURATION, implicit LAK) says that nonlinear robustness for unconfined, steady-state and package-coupled problems is still being repaired case by case, not solved by a general globalization strategy.
- Parallel MODFLOW 6 helps large single runs. It does not help the typical calibration workload of hundreds of independent 10-minute runs, which PEST and PEST++ already distribute at run level.

### Gaps

- I found no peer-reviewed strong or weak scaling study of the PETSc-based parallel MODFLOW 6. The evidence is release notes, the wiki, a users-group post and the pre-PETSc Verkaik papers.
- I could not retrieve the PETSc Krylov and preconditioner choices exposed to MODFLOW 6 users. The wiki page I fetched does not list them.
- FEFLOW numerics come from the 2014 book, not from FEFLOW 10 or 11 documentation, which I could not open. Whether FEFLOW 11 added any GPU or distributed-memory solver is unverified.
- The current HydroGeoSphere release number and any 2025 to 2026 solver changes are unverified.
- The PFLOTRAN release version and date are unverified. I saw only the "v6.0" development documentation title. I recall a PFLOTRAN rule of thumb of about 10,000 degrees of freedom per core but could not confirm it. [U]
- iMOD 5 and iMODFLOW (MODFLOW-2005 based, with the PKS parallel Krylov solver) were not on the page I read. [U]
- The relationship between GSI's "MODFLOW-USG 2.8.1" and "USG-Transport 2.5.0" downloads is unclear from the page.

## 2. What recent developments exist in MODFLOW 6?

### Takeaway

From 2024 to late 2026 MODFLOW 6 added energy transport (GWE), particle tracking (PRT), an "extended" executable with PETSc parallelism and NetCDF, adaptive time stepping driven by transport and stream routing, and a run of robustness options. Exact sensitivities exist only through MF6-ADJ, an external Python adjoint that reads the solution through the MODFLOW API, covers a subset of GWF packages, and costs one extra linear solve sequence per performance measure. I found no automatic-differentiation or differentiable build of MODFLOW.

### Cited findings

- The MODFLOW API implements the Basic Model Interface with an extended model interface (XMI) that lets an external program control the simulation inside a time step and read or write internal arrays. [M] [Hughes, Russcher, Langevin, Morway, McDonald 2022, The MODFLOW Application Programming Interface for simulation control and software interoperability, Environmental Modelling & Software 148:105257](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2021.105257)

The package is mf6adj on PyPI and conda-forge, source at github.com/INTERA-Inc/mf6adj. [V] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025), [open-access copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/), [mf6adj documentation](https://mf6adj.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)
- MF6-ADJ formulation. The forward residual is r_k = A_k h_k - b_k = 0 at time step k. For a performance measure J = sum of F_k, the adjoint system J_k^T lambda_(k-1) = (db_k/dh_k)^T lambda_k - (dF_k/dh_k)^T is solved backward in time from lambda_N = 0, and dJ/d alpha = sum over k of [dF_k/d alpha + lambda_(k-1)^T (dA_k/d alpha h_k - db_k/d alpha)]. "The matrix of the adjoint system is the transpose of that of the forward problem at the convergent iteration." [V] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
- MF6-ADJ implementation. It "leverages the MODFLOW Application Programming Interface (API) to interact with the forward groundwater flow solution" without changing MODFLOW source. One forward run stores heads, saturations and the Jacobian or conductance matrix in HDF5, and the adjoint solves run from that file in Python. It supports confined and unconfined flow, structured and unstructured grids, and both the Standard and Newton formulations. [V] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
- MF6-ADJ scope. Parameters are horizontal and vertical K, storage, well rates, recharge, and GHB head and conductance, through the NPF, STO, WEL, RCH and GHB packages. Performance measures are heads, weighted sums of squared head residuals, and boundary fluxes. [V] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
- MF6-ADJ limits. "The initial release of MF6-ADJ does not yet support all advanced MF6 GWF functionality." MAW, MVR, CSUB and UZF are unsupported. It is "limited to the GWF model", and transport would "require substantial further development". "CHD cells enforce a fixed head value, which leads to a zero adjoint state at those locations." "The overall cost increases linearly with the number of PMs." [V] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
- MF6-ADJ results. Against an analytical adjoint the RMSE was 6e-4. For a Freyberg model with 17,625 parameters the forward run took about 10 s and the adjoint about 40 s, where perturbation would need 17,626 runs. For the San Pedro model (115,531 active cells) forward plus adjoint took about 30 s against an estimated 346,596 perturbation runs. The abstract claims "speedups ranging from hundreds to tens of thousands of times depending on grid discretization". The paper reports no timing against a PEST or PEST++ calibration. [V] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
- The authors note that the earlier MODFLOW-2005 adjoint was intrusive and incompatible with later MODFLOW versions. [V, via search extract] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025)
- The closest differentiable groundwater simulator I found is not MODFLOW. DPFEHM is a Julia code that solves single-phase groundwater flow with automatic differentiation, and its paper notes that FEHM and PFLOTRAN support more physics "but lack automatic differentiation". [V, via search extract] [O'Malley, Greer, Pachalieva, Hao, Harp, Vesselinov 2023, DPFEHM: a differentiable subsurface physics simulator, JOSS 8(90):4560](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.04560)

### Inferences

- Because MF6-ADJ cost grows with the number of performance measures, the cheap product is the gradient of a scalar objective (one adjoint solve), not the full observation-by-parameter Jacobian that GLM and FOSM consume. Methods that need only gradients or Jacobian-vector and transpose-vector products would fit what the adjoint can supply. This is my reading, not a claim in the paper.
- The API already exposes the assembled matrix, right-hand side and solution at run time. That is the access point any new exact-derivative or matrix-free method for MODFLOW 6 would use.
- Transport, advanced packages, multi-model and parallel adjoints are all outside the released MF6-ADJ scope, so calibration against concentrations, stream flows through SFR routing, or lake stages still depends on finite differences or ensembles.

## 3. What algorithms do PEST and PEST++ implement, and what are the documented limitations?

### Takeaway

The PEST family covers three algorithm classes: Jacobian-based regularized Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt with subspace and Tikhonov regularization plus linear (FOSM) uncertainty; ensemble Kalman-type updates in batch (PESTPP-IES) and sequential (PESTPP-DA) form; and optimization under uncertainty by linear programming, evolutionary search and, since mid-2026, ensemble-gradient SQP. The tools' own documentation states the limits plainly: finite-difference Jacobians cost one run per parameter and need clean numerics, the ensemble update is derived under linear-Gaussian assumptions and loses variance over iterations, spurious correlations need localization that has its own memory limits, categorical geology is not preserved, and structural error is set aside by assumption.

#### Versions and status

[V] [usgs/pestpp releases](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/releases)
- The reference report is still White, Hunt, Fienen, Doherty 2020, "Approaches to Highly Parameterized Inversion: PEST++ Version 5", USGS TM 7-C26. [V, citation in README] [TM 7-C26](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm7C26)

#### Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt, SVD, Tikhonov, SVD-assist (PEST, PEST_HP, PESTPP-GLM)

- PESTPP-GLM "implements theory and methodologies that are programmed into PEST" through "repeated linearization of the inverse problem over successive iterations". It uses a Marquardt lambda, truncated SVD (the default package is the randomized "redsvd", which solves "only ... to the number of singular components needed") and Tikhonov regularization. It can also run differential evolution. [V] [PEST++ users manual, chapter 6](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Derivatives are finite differences. "When using finite parameter differences to fill a Jacobian matrix, the number of required model runs is equal to the number of parameters." "If numbers calculated by a model are not continuous functions of the model's parameters or decision variables this process fails." [V] [PEST++ users manual, sections 9.1.4 and 1.4.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- SVD-assist estimates "super parameters", coefficients of the leading eigenvectors of the base-parameter normal matrix, while Tikhonov constraints act on base parameters. [V, abstract] [Tonkin, Doherty 2005, WRR 41:W10412](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR003995)
- SVD-assist costs. "A Jacobian matrix based on the full parameter set k must be calculated prior to commencement of the super parameter inversion process." "For moderately to highly nonlinear models, super parameter redefinition may be required every few iterations." "As of PEST++ version 5.2.25, the automated internal SVD-assist functionality in PESTPP-GLM has been removed." [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 6.2.6](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- The PEST++ README still says pestpp-glm does "on-the-fly subspace reparameterization, effectively reproducing the SVD-Assist methodology of PEST". The manual is newer and says the feature was removed. [V] [PEST++ README](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/README.md); contradicted by [PEST++ users manual, section 6.2.6](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- A failed run hurts Jacobian-based methods more than ensembles. "For the latter programs a failed model run during finite-difference derivatives calculation may lead to an empty column of the Jacobian matrix." [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.2.7](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Pilot points with Tikhonov regularization are the standard spatial parameterization. [M] [Doherty 2003, Groundwater 41(2):170-177](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2003.tb02580.x); [Fienen, Muffels, Hunt 2009, Groundwater 47(6):835-844](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2009.00579.x); [Doherty, Hunt 2010, USGS SIR 2010-5169](https://doi.org/10.3133/sir20105169)
- Measured cost on a real model. With 1,777 parameters and a 6-hour run, the full-Jacobian approach needed "over 25 times" the forward runs of the efficient methods. IES and a randomized-Jacobian GLM each reached the target fit in two iterations with 300 realizations, "in less total forward runs than the number of parameters". The "simultaneous increments" shortcut failed to reach the fit. [V] [Hunt, White, Duncan, Haugh, Doherty 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)

#### Linear (FOSM) analysis and null-space Monte Carlo

- FOSM in PESTPP-GLM, pyEMU and the PEST PREDUNC utilities is Schur-complement conditioning. In partitioned form the posterior covariance is C22 - C21 C11^-1 C12. [V] [GMDSI notebooks, intro to EVA and DSI](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks); pyEMU describes "Schur's complement for conditional uncertainty propagation (a.k.a. Bayes Linear estimation) (the foundation of the PREDUNC suite from PEST) and error variance analysis (the foundation of the PREDVAR suite of PEST)". [V] [pyEMU README](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/README.md)
- FOSM assumptions as the manual lists them. "The relationship between model outputs and parameters is linear; prior parameter uncertainties (and hence posterior parameter uncertainties) are described by multi-Gaussian probability distributions; measurement noise is also Gaussian." PESTPP-GLM adds a heuristic "that the standard deviation of measurement noise associated with each observation is proportional current observation residual". [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 6.2.8](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Null-space Monte Carlo calibrates once, projects the difference between each stochastic field and the calibrated field onto the calibration null space, then recalibrates each field cheaply with super parameters. [V, abstract] [Tonkin, Doherty 2009, WRR 45:W00B10](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007WR006678)
- NSMC was compared with DREAM on a surrogate of a CPU-intensive model before being applied to the full model. [V, abstract] [Keating, Doherty, Vrugt, Kang 2010, WRR 46:W10517](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008584)
- pyEMU implements FOSM, data worth, ensemble generation and the PstFrom interface builder, which targets "PEST(++) interfaces in the 100,000 to 1,000,000 parameter range". [V] [pyEMU README](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/README.md); [White, Fienen, Doherty 2016, Environmental Modelling & Software 85:217-228](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2016.08.017) [M]; [White, Hemmings, Fienen, Knowling 2021, Environmental Modelling & Software 139:105022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2021.105022) [M]

#### Iterative ensemble smoother (PESTPP-IES)

- Algorithms. "PESTPP-IES includes two different solution techniques: The standard Kalman update (Evensen, 2003) (including the iterative Multiple Data Assimilation scheme of Emerick and Reynolds (2013)) and the ensemble form of the Gauss-Levenburg-Marquardt equation (Chen and Oliver, 2013)", with the GLM form the default. In MDA the inflation factors are "chosen such that the sum of the inverse of these factors sums to 1". [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md). Underlying papers: [Chen, Oliver 2013, Computational Geosciences 17:689-703](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-013-9351-5) [M]; [Emerick, Reynolds 2013, Computers & Geosciences 55:3-15](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2012.03.011) [M]; [White 2018, Environmental Modelling & Software 109:191-201](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2018.06.009) [M]
- Why it is cheap. The ensemble supplies an approximate Jacobian, so "the number of model runs required ... can be far fewer than the number of parameters". Parameter counts "can thus rise to the hundreds of thousands, or even millions, with little extra computational cost." [V] [PEST++ users manual, sections 9.1.2 and 9.1.4](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Linear-Gaussian basis. "Where a model is highly nonlinear, this characterization may be flawed because its Bayes-theorem-derived parameter adjustment equations assume model linearity." [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Doherty's help pages are blunter. With strong nonlinearity or large datasets "its performance may suffer"; "there can be no guarantee that its uncertainty limits are either too broad or too narrow"; and, quoting Evensen and co-authors, "nobody knows precisely what distribution the method samples in the nonlinear case." [V] [PEST help, PESTPP-IES](https://help.pesthomepage.org/pestpp-ies.html)
- Fit and bias costs of ensemble derivatives. "It may not always be possible to achieve as good a fit with the calibration dataset ... as that which can be achieved through adjustment of a single parameter field using partial derivatives calculated through finite parameter differences." Limited ensembles do "not forestall inadvertent entrainment of null space parameter components", which "may introduce bias to some ensemble members". [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.4](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Variance loss. "Both the mean and (co)variance of the parameter ensemble can change, with the latter usually decreasing substantially each iteration ... this is especially true in highly nonlinear problems ... and in cases where the prior simulated ensemble has a consistent bias compared to observed counterparts. Spurious correlations exacerbate this problem." The remedy offered is covariance reinflation, which subtracts the prior mean from the prior ensemble and adds the current mean, controlled by ies_n_iter_reinflate. [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.15](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- The same point from the USGS side. "Using an ensemble size that is much smaller than the number of parameters can result in spurious correlation", collapse can lead to underestimated posterior variance, and "an ensemble size between 50 and 150 realizations is appropriate for most environmental modeling applications". The authors used 300 to avoid collapse. [V] [Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)
- Localization. PESTPP-IES accepts a user localization matrix and offers "correlation-based, automatic adaptive localization", which compares each parameter-observation correlation against N-1 circularly shifted background correlations. "The automatic adaptive localization process can be computationally demanding", and "if the number of non-zero-weighted observations is greater than about 20,000, this matrix will likely not fit in memory." Spurious correlations "can lead to parameter compensation and predictive bias". [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.11](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md). Background: [Chen, Oliver 2017, Computational Geosciences 21:13-30](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-016-9599-7) [M]; [Luo, Bhakta 2020, Journal of Petroleum Science and Engineering 184:106559](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.petrol.2019.106559) [M]
- Prior-data conflict. "Continuing with parameter adjustments in the presence of prior-data conflict is a sure way to generate parameter bias, and ultimately, forecast bias." Detection is simple, but "resolving this issue is more problematic", and the built-in fix is a "draconian" removal of conflicting observations (ies_drop_conflicts). [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.13](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Multimodal option. Following Zhang and others (2018), each realization can be updated from a local neighbourhood of realizations (ies_multimodal_alpha), and "more realizations will be required". [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.14](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [Zhang, Lin, Li, Wu, Zeng 2018, WRR 54:1716-1733](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR020906) [V, abstract]
- Run failures. "When model run failure is encountered, PESTPP-IES drops the parameter set that precipitated this failure from the ensemble. The ensemble thus loses a member." Realizations can also be filtered by ies_bad_phi and ies_bad_phi_sigma, the latter of which "assumes a normal distribution of measured phi values, which is often violated". [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.2.7](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Structural error is assumed away. "Model predictive uncertainty can arise from many sources. These include the conceptual basis of the model itself. In the present discussion, we ignore this." [V] [PEST++ users manual, section 9.1.2](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- Categorical geology. For channelized fields, smoother theory "cannot guarantee the maintenance of long-distance hydraulic property connectedness", and preserving connected categorical fields is "an area of active research". [V] [Delottier, Doherty, Brunner 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023)
- Uncertain priors. "The so called 'hierarchical' data assimilation problem that emerges from an unknown prior can challenge the numerical performance of ensemble-based parameter adjustment", more so when hyperparameters vary in space. [V] [Kitlasten, Moore, Doherty 2025, Frontiers in Earth Science 13:1609778](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778)

#### Sequential assimilation (PESTPP-DA)

- "The solution equations implemented in PESTPP-DA are identical to those implemented in PESTPP-IES." What it adds is the assimilation "cycle" and dynamic states carried between cycles, so one tool can run a batch smoother, an EnKF, an ensemble Kalman smoother or mixtures, for state, parameter or joint estimation. [V] [PEST++ users manual, chapter 12](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- A sequential-versus-batch comparison under model error has been published using these tools. [M] [Markovich, White, Knowling 2022, Environmental Modelling & Software 156:105498](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2022.105498)

#### Optimization under uncertainty (PESTPP-OPT, PESTPP-MOU, PESTPP-SQP)

- PESTPP-OPT is sequential linear programming. Response-matrix coefficients "are calculated by PESTPP-OPT using finite differences under an assumption of local model linearity with respect to decision variables", and the LP is solved with the COIN-OR CLP simplex library, which "can handle hundreds of thousands of decision-variables". Chance constraints come from constraint weights as standard deviations, from FOSM, or from a "stack" of parameter realizations, the last being "the most rigorous in that it removes the assumed linear relation between parameters and model-based constraints". [V] [PEST++ users manual, chapter 8](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [White, Fienen, Barlow, Welter 2018, Environmental Modelling & Software 100:213-221](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2017.11.019) [M]
- PESTPP-MOU does "constrained single and multiple objective optimization under uncertainty (CMOU) with evolutionary heuristics". Generators are differential evolution (including self-adaptive), simulated binary crossover, particle swarm and polynomial mutation. Selection uses "the pareto dominance concepts and processes available in NSGA-II and SPEA-2". Chances can be evaluated at every individual or at one point and translated to other individuals by "simple inverse-distance weighting". [V] [PEST++ users manual, chapter 13](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [White, Knowling, Fienen, Siade, Rea, Martinez 2022, Environmental Modelling & Software 149:105316](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2022.105316) [M]
- PESTPP-SQP (first released in 5.2.27) "implements Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) methods with ensemble-based gradient estimation". The gradient is the pseudo-inverse of the decision-variable covariance times the cross-covariance with the objective, grad f ~ C_dd^+ C_dJ, with anomalies taken about a base realization. The Hessian is updated by BFGS or SR1 with fallback to scaled identity, globalization is by a filter, and sampling uses a "CMA-inspired adaptive covariance mechanism". Step-size adaptation "is available as an experimental option but disabled by default". [V] [PEST++ users manual, chapter 14](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)

#### Sensitivity and run utilities

- PESTPP-SEN implements the Method of Morris with the Campolongo extensions and the Method of Sobol. PESTPP-SWP runs arbitrary parameter sets from a CSV file in parallel. The manual also carries a PESTPP-PSO chapter with estimation and Pareto modes. All tools use the PANTHER-derived TCP/IP manager and agent run manager with overdue-run rescheduling and give-up controls. [V] [PEST++ users manual, chapters 5, 7, 10, 11](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)

#### Data space inversion (DSI), EVA and ENSI

- DSI origin and ensemble-smoother variant. [M] [Sun, Durlofsky 2017, Mathematical Geosciences 49:679-715](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11004-016-9672-8); [Lima, Emerick, Ortiz 2020, Computational Geosciences 24:1179-1200](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-020-09933-w)
- DSI as implemented for PEST users. The emulator is d = d_mean + C_d^(1/2) x, with x a vector of independent standard normal "latent-space parameters" that become the PEST adjustable parameters. History matching is done on the emulator, not the simulator. [V] [GMDSI notebooks, part0_intro_to_eva_and_dsi](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks)
- In the PEST suite the programs are DSI1 (simple conditioning), DSI2 (builds the statistical model and PEST or PESTPP-IES datasets), DSIMOD (the statistical model) and ADDPRED1 (predictive-analysis control file). "Often only a few hundred random realisations is fine", and "no parameters are actually adjusted". [V] [PEST help, Data space inversion](https://help.pesthomepage.org/data-space-inversion.html)
- In pyEMU the entry points are the EnDS class and an emulators module with a DSI class ("Based on DSI as described in Sun & Durlofsky (2017) and Sun et al (2017)") offering energy-threshold truncation, full or randomized SVD, and log and normal-score transforms. There is a prepare_dsivc method ("Data Space Inversion Variable Control") that sets up decision-variable optimization on the emulator with a MOU population. [V] [pyemu/emulators/dsi.py on develop](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/emulators/dsi.py)
- DSI is not in the PEST++ C++ codes. The 5.2.28 manual contains no mention of DSI. PESTPP-IES is used only as the engine that conditions the pyEMU or PEST emulator. [V, by text search] [PEST++ users manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
- An autoencoder variant exists in the pyEMU develop branch as class DSIAE ("Data Space Inversion Autoencoder (DSIAE) emulator"), requiring TensorFlow, with energy, MMD and Wasserstein loss options and a hyperparameter grid search. It is commented out of the module's public export list, so I class it as experimental. [V] [pyemu/emulators/dsiae.py](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/emulators/dsiae.py), [pyemu/emulators/__init__.py](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/emulators/__init__.py)
- Ensemble variance analysis (EVA) is the ensemble counterpart of FOSM data worth, "based on a multivariate Gaussian assumption". [V] [GMDSI notebooks, part2_10_eva_and_dsi](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks); [He, Sarma, Bhark, Tanaka, Chen, Wen et al. 2018, SPE Journal 23(2):428-448](https://doi.org/10.2118/182609-PA) [M]
- First hydrologic DSI application. With 100 prior HydroGeoSphere runs and 87 retained singular values (energy 0.999), the DSI posterior for stream infiltration closely matched the simulator-based IES posterior, while for fast travel times the DSI posterior was narrower. Linear analysis on the surrogate "requires an assumption of linearity of the DSI model", and the normal-score transform "does not guarantee that the joint probability distribution" is multi-Gaussian. [V] [Delottier, Doherty, Brunner 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023)
- Documented DSI failure modes. "DSI is vulnerable to prior-data conflict." Performance can degrade under strongly nonlinear past-to-future relationships. [V] [Kitlasten, Moore, Doherty 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778). In the GMDSI tutorial the plain emulator predicted "physically impossible values (i.e. negative times)" for travel time and produced a "saw-toothed" forecast time series, both attributed to the Gaussian assumption and eased by a normal-score transform. [V] [GMDSI notebooks, 2_freyberg_ensemble_data_space_inversion](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks)
- ENSI (ensemble space inversion). The parameter field is k0 plus realization matrices K_i times factor vectors f_i. The factors are the adjustable parameters, with preferred value zero enforced by Tikhonov regularization. A Jacobian with respect to the factors is computed and improved by Broyden updating. It runs in PEST_HP. "Unlike IES, ENSI does not sample the posterior parameter probability distribution." [V] [Kitlasten, Moore, Doherty 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778)
- ENSI cost and result on a 163,359-parameter MODFLOW 6 plus MODPATH 7 model with 5 to 30 minute runs. An acceptable fit took 6,711 runs, and running to minimum misfit took 49,245 runs over 22 iterations. DSI used 500 runs. "Deployment of both of these strategies at our case study site yields similar results." "ENSI inversion does not assess posterior parameter uncertainty." All methods in the paper except ENSI are available through PEST++ and pyEMU. [V] [Kitlasten, Moore, Doherty 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778)
- PEST_HP also has an ensemble smoother with an inversion step (RSI_HP) that "often fits well quickly", with a risk of ensemble collapse. [V, paraphrase of help page] [PEST help, PESTPP-IES](https://help.pesthomepage.org/pestpp-ies.html)

#### Other inversion and uncertainty tools

- FePEST is FEFLOW's PEST front end, with parallelization and cloud-run features added in FEFLOW 7.5 and 8.0. [V] [DHI FEFLOW version history](https://support.dhigroup.com/knowledgebase/article/KA-01203/en-us)
- iTOUGH2 is the simulation-optimization framework around TOUGH2 for parameter estimation, sensitivity and uncertainty propagation. [M] [Finsterle, Commer, Edmiston, Jung, Kowalsky, Pau et al. 2017, Computers & Geosciences 108:8-20](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2016.09.005)
- DREAM is the adaptive multi-chain MCMC family. [M] [Vrugt 2016, Environmental Modelling & Software 75:273-316](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2015.08.013)
- MADS (Mads.jl) lists sensitivity analysis, parameter estimation, model inversion and calibration, uncertainty quantification, model selection and averaging, model reduction and surrogate modeling, and decision analysis, and requires Julia 1.12 or later. [V] [Mads.jl README](https://github.com/madsjulia/Mads.jl/blob/master/README.md)
- PCGA. The quasi-linear geostatistical approach normally costs on the order of m^2 n. PCGA is "matrix-free", avoids "the direct evaluation of the Jacobian matrix through the principal components (low-rank approximation) of the prior covariance and the drift matrix with a finite difference approximation", and "requires about K runs of the forward problem in each iteration independently of m and n, where K is the number of principal components". [V, abstracts] [Kitanidis, Lee 2014, WRR 50:5428-5443](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013WR014630); [Lee, Kitanidis 2014, WRR 50:5410-5427](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014WR015483)
- pyPCGA is at "version 0.1". It has a MODFLOW example through FloPy, and its README says "MODFLOW-USG/SEAWAT/MODFLOW6 examples coming soon!" [V] [pyPCGA README](https://github.com/jonghyunharrylee/pyPCGA/blob/master/README.md)
- bgaPEST implemented the Bayesian geostatistical approach inside the PEST protocol. [M] [Fienen, D'Oria, Doherty, Hunt 2013, USGS TM 7-C9](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm7C9)

### Inferences

- The PEST family has no method that uses exact gradients. GLM assumes a finite-difference Jacobian, and every ensemble tool estimates sensitivities from sample covariances. MF6-ADJ is not wired into any of them in the documentation I read.
- The cure for IES variance collapse in the current release is reinflation to prior anomalies, a heuristic. Nothing in the manual gives a criterion for when the resulting ensemble is a valid posterior sample.
- Non-Gaussian structure is handled only by transformation (log, normal-score) or by avoiding parameters altogether (DSI). No tool in the suite adjusts categorical or connected fields while preserving their geometry.
- At 1e5 to 1e6 parameters the binding limits are the ensemble subspace rank (a few hundred), localization cost, and memory for dense parameter-by-observation matrices, not model run count.
- ENSI and SVD-assist are the same idea with different bases (random realizations versus Jacobian eigenvectors). Both give one regularized estimate and no posterior.

### Gaps

- The current PEST version number, and the PEST and PEST_HP manual text for ENSI, RSI_HP, Broyden updating and randomized Jacobians, were not retrieved. My ENSI description comes from the Kitlasten paper.
- Whether 5.2.28 is a tagged PEST++ release or only the develop-branch manual version is unverified. Release years on the GitHub page are inferred.
- I could not confirm whether FePEST exposes PEST++ tools such as PESTPP-IES. [U]
- UCODE_2014 (Poeter and others, 2014) was not checked in this session. I recall it adds SVD-based estimation and MCMC to the UCODE regression framework. [U]
- White (2018) reports PESTPP-IES test cases whose sizes I did not re-read, so I quote the manual's scale statement instead.
- I did not find a published benchmark that scores IES posterior coverage against a reference MCMC posterior on a groundwater model at realistic dimension.

## 4. What critiques, benchmark comparisons and future-directions statements has the groundwater community published?

### Takeaway

The community's own writing agrees on four weak points: non-Gaussian and geologically realistic priors, model structural error and the bias it induces through parameter compensation, the run cost of anything more rigorous than linear or ensemble approximations, and the mismatch between calibration fit and predictive value. The Doherty, White, Hunt and Fienen line argues for prediction-specific model design, ensembles and scripted workflows. The Linde, Kitanidis and Gómez-Hernández line pushes proxy models, multilevel sampling and non-Gaussian inversion.

### Cited findings

- Zhou, Gómez-Hernández and Li review the progression from direct methods through pilot points, self-calibration, MCMC, gradual deformation and ensemble Kalman filtering to multiple-point methods, and argue that inverse methods should be stochastic, should handle non-multi-Gaussian conductivity, and should integrate several data types. [M, summary from prior reading; publisher page returned 403] [Zhou, Gómez-Hernández, Li 2014, Inverse methods in hydrogeology: evolution and recent trends, Advances in Water Resources 63:22-37](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2013.10.014)
- Linde and co-authors review proxy modelling and multilevel Monte Carlo to cut forward cost, Bayesian inversion for nonlinear non-Gaussian problems including sequential Monte Carlo, treatment of forward modelling errors in the likelihood, and petrophysical uncertainty in hydrogeophysics, and conclude that uncertainty quantification in high parameter and data dimensions is only partly solved. [M, summary from prior reading; publisher page returned 403] [Linde, Ginsbourger, Irving, Nobile, Doucet 2017, On uncertainty quantification in hydrogeology and hydrogeophysics, Advances in Water Resources 110:166-181](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.10.014)
- Geological realism of inverse solutions has its own review. [M] [Linde, Renard, Mukerji, Caers 2015, Advances in Water Resources 86:86-101](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.09.019)
- The standard older review of the hydrogeological inverse problem. [M] [Carrera, Alcolea, Medina, Hidalgo, Slooten 2005, Inverse problem in hydrogeology, Hydrogeology Journal 13:206-222](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10040-004-0404-7)
- The best-known benchmark compared seven inverse methods on a synthetic well-catchment problem. As I recall, the methods performed similarly for multi-Gaussian transmissivity and all degraded when the reference field was not multi-Gaussian. [M] [Hendricks Franssen, Alcolea, Riva, Bakr, van der Wiel, Stauffer et al. 2009, Advances in Water Resources 32:851-872](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2009.02.011)
- Highly parameterized, CPU-intensive models "pose significant challenges for parameter estimation and predictive uncertainty analysis algorithms, particularly global methods which usually require very large numbers of forward runs." [V, abstract] [Keating, Doherty, Vrugt, Kang 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008584)
- Thirty-year retrospective. Rebuilding the 1988 Freyberg exercise showed that "the inverse problem was non-unique because only head data were available for calibration. When a flux observation was included, current parameter estimation approaches were able to overcome all calibration and forecast issues noted in 1988." [V, abstract] [Hunt, Fienen, White 2019/2020, Groundwater 58(2):168-182](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12907)
- Decision-support framing. Data assimilation and uncertainty quantification are central, their difficulty "depend[s] on the relationship between data that is available for assimilation and the nature of the prediction(s)", groundwater modelling "is generally aligned with the most difficult of these", and the remedy proposed is "strategic abstraction of parameters and processes in a way that is optimal for the making of one particular prediction but is not necessarily optimal for the making of another." [V, abstract] [Doherty, Moore 2019/2020, Groundwater 58(3):327-337](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12969)
- Complexity trade-off. Structural complexity helps express expert knowledge, but "such models often run slowly and are numerically" delicate, which works against history matching and uncertainty analysis. [V, abstract, truncated] [Hugman, Doherty 2022, Complex or simple, does a model have to be one or the other?, Frontiers in Earth Science 10:867379](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2022.867379)
- Arguments for many parameters with regularization, against the parsimony position. [M] [Hunt, Doherty, Tonkin 2007, Are models too simple? Arguments for increased parameterization, Groundwater 45(3):254-262](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2007.00316.x)
- Model error and parameter compensation. Structural noise has an unknown, singular covariance, and calibrating a defective model can bias predictions that depend on parameter combinations the data do not inform. [M] [Doherty, Welter 2010, A short exploration of structural noise, WRR 46:W05525](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008377); [White, Doherty, Hughes 2014, Quantifying the predictive consequences of model error with linear subspace analysis, WRR 50:1152-1173](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013WR014767); [Oliver, Alfonzo 2018, Calibration of imperfect models to biased observations, Computational Geosciences 22:145-161](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9678-4)
- Parameterization density and forecast reliability have been tested empirically. [M] [Knowling, White, Moore 2019, Role of model parameterization in risk-based decision support: an empirical exploration, Advances in Water Resources 128:59-73](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2019.04.010)
- More data types do not always help. Two regional case studies assessed tritium concentrations and tritium-derived mean residence times, with value measured as "both the improvement (or otherwise) in the reliability of forecasts through uncertainty variance reduction and bias minimization". [V, abstract] [Knowling, White, Moore, Rakowski, Hayley 2020, HESS 24:1677-1689](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-24-1677-2020)
- Workflow reproducibility. Model construction and data assimilation "are time consuming and prone to errors, which motivates a repeatable workflow". [V, abstract] [Fienen, Corson-Dosch, White, Leaf, Hunt 2021/2022, Groundwater 60(1):71-86](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13129); see also [White, Foster, Fienen, Knowling, Hemmings, Winterle 2020, Frontiers in Earth Science 8:50](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2020.00050) [M]
- USGS comparison of calibration algorithms on a production model, with the stated next step "Evaluating a combined approach is a topic for future work" (randomized Jacobian for calibration, IES for uncertainty). [V] [Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)
- Scalable inversion review from the Kitanidis and Darve groups. [M] [Ghorbanidehno, Kokkinaki, Lee, Darve 2020, Recent developments in fast and scalable inverse modeling and data assimilation methods in hydrology, Journal of Hydrology 591:125266](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2020.125266)
- Surrogate review. Long run times "hinder comprehensive calibration, sensitivity, and uncertainty analysis", and surrogates fall into data-driven, projection-based and hierarchical (multifidelity) classes. [V, abstract] [Asher, Croke, Jakeman, Peeters 2015, WRR 51:5957-5973](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR016967)
- Standard text. [M] [Anderson, Woessner, Hunt 2015, Applied Groundwater Modeling, 2nd edition, Academic Press](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-21563-7)
- GMDSI monographs currently listed include "Decision-Support Modelling Viewed through the Lens of Model Complexity" (Doherty and Moore), "Scientifically-Based, Decision-Support Groundwater Modelling" (Doherty), "Problem Decomposition in Decision-Support Groundwater Modelling", "Decision-Support Modelling Appropriateness and the Journey of Information", and the free second edition of the PEST book. [V] [GMDSI monographs](https://gmdsi.org/engagement/monographs/)
- GMDSI worked examples currently listed cover structural overlay parameters in a tunnel model, contaminant source identification, coastal saltwater intrusion, probabilistic capture zones, simultaneous interpretation of six pumping tests, exploring model defects using linear analysis, and water supply security. [V] [GMDSI worked examples](https://gmdsi.org/education/worked-examples/)

### Inferences

- Structural error is the criticism with the least tooling behind it. The literature diagnoses it, and GMDSI has a worked example on detecting model defects by linear analysis, but the production algorithms still condition parameters as if the model were correct.
- The Doherty and Moore position that models should be built for one prediction, together with DSI, moves the community toward prediction-space methods. A new method that works in data or prediction space would be building on an accepted direction, not opposing it.
- No one in these sources disputes that ensemble methods are the practical default at high dimension. The dispute is over how far their posteriors can be trusted.

## 5. Which newer methods have already been tried in groundwater practice, and with what reported results?

### Takeaway

Adjoint sensitivities, Jacobian-free geostatistical inversion (PCGA), iterative ensemble smoothers, data space inversion, deep-learning surrogates, generative geological priors, normal-score and Gaussian-anamorphosis ensemble filters, multimodal local ensemble updates, and multilevel MCMC have all been published for groundwater problems, several of them years ago. Only ensemble smoothers and DSI are in routine PEST-family practice. The rest remain research codes or papers, so a later report can claim novelty only for specific combinations or for bringing a method into the MODFLOW and PEST++ toolchain, not for the method classes themselves.

#### Adjoint sensitivities

- Adjoint-state sensitivities for MODFLOW 6 are published and distributed (details in section 2). Reported cost is one forward run plus one adjoint solve of similar order per performance measure. [V] [Hayek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025)
- Doherty treats adjoints as the exception in practice. In highly parameterized settings where adjoint sensitivities can't be computed, "ensemble methods provide the only viable option for posterior uncertainty analysis." [V] [PEST help, PESTPP-IES](https://help.pesthomepage.org/pestpp-ies.html)

#### PCGA and Jacobian-free geostatistical inversion

- PCGA has been applied to large hydraulic tomography and joint head and tracer inversion, and extended to large data sets. It "requires much smaller forward simulation runs for each iteration than the number of unknown parameters and measurements" and "can be conveniently linked to any multiphysics simulation software with independent parallel executions". [V, abstracts] [Lee, Kitanidis 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014WR015483); [Lee, Yoon, Kitanidis, Werth, Valocchi 2016, WRR 52:5213-5231](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR018483)
- The authors scope the underlying quasi-linear approach to "weakly nonlinear underdetermined inverse problems". [V, abstract] [Kitanidis, Lee 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013WR014630)
- A Bayesian geostatistical approach with head and transport data was run on a field problem through PEST-compatible tooling. [M] [Fienen, Hunt, Krabbenhoft, Clemo 2009, WRR 45:W08405](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008WR007431)

#### Ensemble smoothers and Jacobian shortcuts

- IES on a production USGS model reached the target fit in two iterations with 300 realizations, against more than 25 times as many runs for the full Jacobian. [V] [Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)
- Randomized Jacobian GLM, Broyden updating and simultaneous increments have all been tried. Randomized Jacobian matched IES for fit but "does not carry an ensemble of realizations or attempt to quantify post-history-matching parameter uncertainty", and simultaneous increments failed. [V] [Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)
- IES variants have been tested on synthetic multi-Gaussian transient problems and coupled to HydroGeoSphere. [M] [Lam, Kerrou, Renard, Benabderrahmane, Perrochet 2020, Frontiers in Earth Science 8:202](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2020.00202); [Delottier, Therrien, Young, Paradis 2022, Journal of Hydrology 606:127406](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2021.127406)
- Theory of what iterative smoothers sample. [M] [Evensen 2018, Analysis of iterative ensemble smoothers for solving inverse problems, Computational Geosciences 22:885-908](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-018-9731-y); [Evensen, Raanes, Stordal, Hove 2019, Frontiers in Applied Mathematics and Statistics 5:47](https://doi.org/10.3389/fams.2019.00047)

#### Non-Gaussian ensemble methods

- Normal-score ensemble Kalman filtering for non-Gaussian parameters and states. [M] [Zhou, Gómez-Hernández, Hendricks Franssen, Li 2011, Advances in Water Resources 34:844-864](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.04.014)
- Gaussian anamorphosis of observed states. "EnKFs are optimal in the sense of Bayesian updating only if all involved variables are multivariate Gaussian. Subsurface flow and transport state variables, however, generally do not show Gaussian dependence on hydraulic log conductivity and among each other, even if log conductivity is multi-Gaussian." [V, abstract] [Schöniger, Nowak, Hendricks Franssen 2012, WRR 48:W04502](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010462)
- Multimodal posteriors by local ensemble updates (ILUES), now in PESTPP-IES as the multimodal option. [V, abstract] [Zhang, Lin, Li, Wu, Zeng 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR020906)
- Prior-preserving perturbation for MCMC and optimization with geostatistical fields (iterative spatial resampling). [V, abstract] [Mariethoz, Renard, Caers 2010, WRR 46:W11530](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010WR009274)

#### Data space and direct forecasting methods

- DSI has been applied to an integrated surface and subsurface model and to a regional groundwater age problem (sections 2 and 3). [V] [Delottier, Doherty, Brunner 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023); [Kitlasten, Moore, Doherty 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778)
- The closely related direct-forecasting or Bayesian evidential learning line predates DSI in hydrogeology. [M] [Satija, Caers 2015, Advances in Water Resources 77:69-81](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.01.002); [Hermans, Nguyen, Klepikova, Dassargues, Caers 2018, WRR 54:2931-2948](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR022135)

#### Deep-learning surrogates and physics-informed networks

- A deep autoregressive dense convolutional encoder-decoder surrogate, trained on limited forward runs and combined with ILUES, solved joint contaminant source and conductivity identification. [V, abstract] [Mo, Zabaras, Shi, Wu 2019, WRR 55:3856-3881](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018WR024638)
- An R-U-Net style recurrent surrogate was used for data assimilation in dynamic subsurface flow. [M] [Tang, Liu, Durlofsky 2020, Journal of Computational Physics 413:109456](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2020.109456)
- An emulator of a transient three-dimensional ParFlow model (ParFlow-ML, based on PredRNN) reproduced simulator output "at a fraction of the computational expense". [V, abstract] [Tran, Leonarduzzi, De la Fuente, Hull, Bansal, Chennault et al. 2021, Water 13(23):3393](https://doi.org/10.3390/w13233393)
- Physics-informed neural networks have been used to estimate conductivity in saturated and unsaturated flow by penalizing the Darcy or Richards residual. [V, abstract] [Tartakovsky, Marrero, Perdikaris, Tartakovsky, Barajas-Solano 2020, WRR 56:e2019WR026731](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019WR026731)
- Surrogate modelling for groundwater was already reviewed in 2015, and for water resources generally in 2012. [V, abstract] [Asher et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR016967); [Razavi, Tolson, Burn 2012, WRR 48:W07401](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR011527) [M]
- A surrogate was paired with NSMC and DREAM for a CPU-intensive groundwater model as early as 2010. [V, abstract] [Keating et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008584)

#### Generative geological priors

- A spatial GAN trained on a training image "defines a (very) low-dimensional parameterization, thereby allowing for efficient probabilistic inversion using state-of-the-art Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods". "Training our deep network can take several hours." [V, abstract] [Laloy, Hérault, Jacques, Linde 2018, WRR 54:381-406](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR022148)
- Variational autoencoder parameterizations for binary and facies media, used with MCMC and with ensemble smoothers. [M] [Laloy, Hérault, Lee, Jacques, Linde 2017, Advances in Water Resources 110:387-405](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.09.029); [Canchumuni, Emerick, Pacheco 2019, Computers & Geosciences 128:87-102](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2019.04.006)
- A convolutional adversarial autoencoder for non-Gaussian conductivity with intra-facies heterogeneity, combined with a deep residual surrogate. The authors open with "Inverse modeling for the estimation of non-Gaussian hydraulic conductivity fields in subsurface flow and solute transport models remains a challenging problem." [V, abstract] [Mo, Zabaras, Shi, Wu 2020, WRR 56:e2019WR026082](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019WR026082)
- Connectivity, the property these priors try to preserve, has standard metrics. [M] [Renard, Allard 2013, Advances in Water Resources 51:168-196](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.12.001)

#### Multilevel, multifidelity and paired-model methods

- Multilevel Monte Carlo for elliptic PDEs with random coefficients, motivated by groundwater flow. [M] [Cliffe, Giles, Scheichl, Teckentrup 2011, Computing and Visualization in Science 14:3-15](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00791-011-0160-x)
- Multilevel MCMC for subsurface flow showed "consistent reductions of more than an order of magnitude in the cost of the multilevel estimator over the standard Metropolis-Hastings algorithm for tolerances epsilon < 10^-2". [V, abstract] [Dodwell, Ketelsen, Scheichl, Teckentrup 2015, SIAM/ASA JUQ 3:1075-1108](https://doi.org/10.1137/130915005)
- Multilevel delayed-acceptance MCMC extends this to model hierarchies "of arbitrary depth" with "an adaptive multilevel correction to coarse-level biases". [V, abstract] [Lykkegaard, Dodwell, Fox, Mingas, Scheichl 2023, SIAM/ASA JUQ 11(1):1-30](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1476770)
- A deep-network proxy was used as the coarse level for groundwater flow uncertainty quantification. [M] [Lykkegaard, Dodwell, Moxey 2021, CMAME 383:113895](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2021.113895)
- In the PEST tradition the multifidelity idea appears as paired simple and complex models. [V, abstract] [Doherty, Christensen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010763)

#### Hardware acceleration

- GPU Krylov solves for MODFLOW were demonstrated in 2013 and never entered a USGS release. [V, abstract; release notes by absence] [Hughes, White 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12004)

### Inferences

- "Adjoint for MODFLOW", "ensemble smoother at a million parameters", "prediction-space inversion", "autoencoder latent space for data assimilation", "GAN or VAE geological prior", "deep surrogate inside a smoother" and "multilevel MCMC for groundwater flow" are all prior art. A later report should cite them and state its increment against them.
- Things I did not find done in the MODFLOW and PEST++ toolchain, and which may therefore be open: adjoint gradients feeding a production history-matching algorithm; ensemble updates with a validity test for the posterior; categorical-field updates that preserve connectivity inside PESTPP-IES; multilevel or multifidelity estimators inside PEST++; structural-error terms estimated jointly with parameters; GPU or matrix-free solvers in MODFLOW 6. Each is an absence in the sources I read, not a proof of absence.
- The pyEMU DSIAE class shows the PEST++ developers are already moving DSI toward nonlinear latent spaces, so that direction will not be new by the time a report is read.

### Documented gaps and pain points a new method could address

Each item is tied to a source read in this session unless tagged otherwise.

1. Finite-difference Jacobians cost one run per parameter per iteration, more than 25 times the runs of ensemble or randomized alternatives on a 1,777-parameter, 6-hour model. [Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106); [PEST++ manual, section 9.1.4](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
2. Finite-difference derivatives are corrupted by loose solver convergence and adaptive time stepping, so calibration constrains how the simulator may be run. [PEST++ manual, section 1.4.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
3. Exact gradients exist for MODFLOW 6 only through MF6-ADJ, which covers GWF with NPF, STO, WEL, RCH and GHB, excludes MAW, MVR, CSUB, UZF and all transport, has zero adjoint at CHD cells, and scales linearly with the number of performance measures. No PEST-family algorithm consumes it. [Hayek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025)
4. No automatic-differentiation or differentiable MODFLOW was found. [O'Malley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.04560) (a different code, cited for contrast)
5. The IES update assumes linearity and Gaussianity, and "nobody knows precisely what distribution the method samples in the nonlinear case". [PEST help, PESTPP-IES](https://help.pesthomepage.org/pestpp-ies.html); [PEST++ manual, section 9.1.3](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
6. Ensemble variance shrinks at every iteration and can collapse. The shipped remedy, reinflation to prior anomalies, is a heuristic without a posterior-validity test. [PEST++ manual, section 9.1.15](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)
7. Spurious correlations from small ensembles cause "parameter compensation and predictive bias". Automatic adaptive localization is expensive and its matrix will not fit in memory beyond about 20,000 weighted observations. [PEST++ manual, section 9.1.11](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
8. Ensemble solutions live in a subspace of at most ensemble size, cannot always fit data as well as a finite-difference GLM, and entrain null-space components. [PEST++ manual, section 9.1.4](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
9. Categorical and connected geology is not preserved by smoother updates, and non-Gaussian conductivity inversion "remains a challenging problem". [Delottier et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023); [Mo et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019WR026082)
10. Uncertain, spatially varying geostatistical hyperparameters (the hierarchical problem) challenge ensemble methods. [Kitlasten et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778)
11. DSI rests on a Gaussian link between past and future outputs, is "vulnerable to prior-data conflict", can emit physically impossible or saw-toothed predictions, gave a narrower travel-time posterior than the simulator-based smoother in its first hydrologic test, and returns no parameter fields. [Kitlasten et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778); [Delottier et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023); [GMDSI notebooks](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks)
12. ENSI fits well but "does not assess posterior parameter uncertainty", needed 6,711 to 49,245 runs in its one published case, and is not in PEST++ or pyEMU. [Kitlasten et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778)
13. Prior-data conflict is detected but resolved only by dropping observations. [PEST++ manual, section 9.1.13](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
14. Structural error is excluded by assumption in the IES formulation, and FOSM in PESTPP-GLM patches misfit with a residual-proportional noise heuristic. [PEST++ manual, sections 9.1.2 and 6.2.8](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [Doherty, Christensen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010763)
15. Random parameter fields make simulators fail or run long. Failed members are dropped from the ensemble, and 30 of 100 HydroGeoSphere realizations were abandoned in one study. [PEST++ manual, sections 9.1.4 and 9.2.7](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [Delottier et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023)
16. Forward run times of 5 to 30 minutes (regional MODFLOW 6 with particle tracking), about 10 minutes (204,000-cell HGS) and more than 6 hours (regional MODFLOW-NWT) set the budget for any method. [Kitlasten et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778); [Delottier et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023); [Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)
17. SVD-assist needs a full Jacobian up front and again every few iterations for nonlinear models, and PESTPP-GLM dropped its automated form in 5.2.25. [PEST++ manual, section 6.2.6](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
18. Parallel MODFLOW 6 is "not practical unless the number of cells is at least 100,000 to 1,000,000", cannot run several model instances on one spatial domain in parallel, and does not support GNC. [MODFLOW users group, April 2026](https://groups.google.com/g/modflow/c/kRmPZP05iNw); [Extended MODFLOW User Guide](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/wiki/Extended-MODFLOW-User-Guide)
19. MODFLOW 6 has no GPU path. ParFlow's is beta, NVIDIA-only, with one effective preconditioner. [MF6 release notes](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/tree/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous); [ParFlow README-GPU.md](https://github.com/parflow/parflow/blob/master/README-GPU.md)
20. Steady-state Newton and dry-cell robustness in MODFLOW 6 is still being patched (PTC can worsen convergence, the PTC factor bug fixed in 6.8.2, HIGHEST_CELL_SATURATION in 6.7.0, a convergence-check bug from 6.4.2 to 6.6.3). [sln-ims.dfn](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/mf6io/mf6ivar/dfn/sln-ims.dfn); [v6.8.2](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.2.tex); [v6.7.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex); [v6.6.3](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.6.3.tex)
21. Sequentially coupled advanced packages can fail to converge, which is why an implicit Lake formulation was added in 6.8.0. [MF6 release notes v6.8.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex)
22. Anisotropy and irregular grids need XT3D, which widens the stencil and breaks symmetry, or GNC, which is excluded with XT3D and in parallel. Advection on unstructured or misaligned grids needed a new UTVD scheme in 2026. [Provost et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A56) [M]; [v6.5.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.5.0.tex); [v6.7.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex)
23. MODFLOW 6 has no three-dimensional Richards solver, and Richards-based codes lose robustness and efficiency under unfavourable conditions, which blocks many-run uncertainty work. [TM 6-A55](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A55) [M]; [Zha et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/wat2.1364); [Farthing, Ogden 2017](https://doi.org/10.2136/sssaj2017.02.0058)
24. Multigrid preconditioners slow down under high aspect-ratio anisotropy and surface coupling (ParFlow), and shared-memory efficiency falls with core count (HydroGeoSphere). [Kuffour et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-13-1373-2020); [Aquanty 2016](https://www.aquanty.com/blog/2016/2/18/hgs-parallelization-best-practices)
25. Optimization under uncertainty in PESTPP-OPT depends on finite-difference response matrices and local linearity, and PESTPP-MOU must trade rigour against cost when deciding where to evaluate chance constraints. [PEST++ manual, chapters 8 and 13](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
26. Formal Bayesian sampling is too expensive at realistic dimension without surrogates or multilevel tricks, and none of those is in the PEST or PEST++ toolchain. [Keating et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008584); [Dodwell et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1137/130915005); [PEST++ manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)
