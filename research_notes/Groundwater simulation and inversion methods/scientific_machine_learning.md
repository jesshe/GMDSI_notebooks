## 1. Physics-informed neural networks and variants (PINN, variational / deep Ritz, XPINN and domain-decomposed, hard-constraint, inverse PINNs): what is demonstrated for Darcy flow, Richards equation and heterogeneous-K inversion, and what do critical evaluations say?

### Takeaway
A PINN solves one PDE instance by non-convex optimisation, and every independent head-to-head evaluation found here has it slower than finite-element or finite-volume solvers at equal accuracy for forward problems, often by orders of magnitude, with documented failure on sharp fronts and stiff or multiscale problems. Groundwater demonstrations (Darcy, Richards, hydraulic tomography) are almost all 2D, synthetic and based on smooth Gaussian or KL-parameterised fields; no result was found for a realistic 3D heterogeneous aquifer with wells and rivers, so the defensible niche is small inverse or constitutive-relation problems, not replacement of MODFLOW-class solvers.

**Training pathologies (well documented, not folklore)**
- Loss-term imbalance: gradients of the PDE-residual, boundary and data terms differ by orders of magnitude, giving a stiff gradient flow; adaptive loss weighting ("learning-rate annealing") was proposed as mitigation. Wang, Teng & Perdikaris (2021), SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 43:A3055 (NV) — [arXiv:2001.04536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04536)
- Spectral bias: neural-tangent-kernel analysis shows low-frequency solution components converge first and loss components converge at very different rates, so high-frequency or multiscale content is learned slowly or not at all. Wang, Yu & Perdikaris (2022), "When and why PINNs fail to train: a neural tangent kernel perspective", J. Comput. Phys. 449:110768 (NV) — [arXiv:2007.14527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14527)
- Failure on simple transport: PINNs fail on 1D convection, reaction and reaction-diffusion problems once coefficients move beyond a mild regime, because the soft PDE regulariser makes the loss landscape ill-conditioned, not because the network lacks capacity; curriculum and sequence-to-sequence training reduced errors by one to two orders of magnitude. Krishnapriyan, Gholami, Zhe, Kirby & Mahoney (2021), "Characterizing possible failure modes in physics-informed neural networks", NeurIPS 34 (NV) — [arXiv:2109.01050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01050)
- Ill-conditioning traced to the differential operator: the PINN loss inherits the conditioning of the differential operator in the residual; Adam followed by L-BFGS beats either alone, and a new second-order optimiser (NysNewton-CG) improves further. Rathore, Lei, Frangella, Lu & Udell (2024), "Challenges in training PINNs: a loss landscape perspective", ICML 2024 (oral) — [arXiv:2402.01868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01868)
- Benchmark evidence: PINNacle tests about 10 PINN variants on over 20 PDEs covering complex geometry, multiscale, nonlinear and high-dimensional cases; the authors single out domain decomposition and loss reweighting as the promising directions for multiscale problems and complex geometries, i.e. the vanilla method is not adequate there. Hao et al. (2023) — [arXiv:2306.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08827)
- Getting PINNs to work on standard benchmarks needs a stack of tricks (non-dimensionalisation, Fourier features, loss balancing, causal training). Wang, Sankaran, Wang & Perdikaris (2023), "An expert's guide to training physics-informed neural networks" [preprint] (NV) — [arXiv:2308.08468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08468)
- Domain decomposition is not a free win: splitting reduces the complexity each sub-network must learn but also reduces the data per subdomain, so XPINN can generalise worse than a single PINN. Hu, Jagtap, Karniadakis & Kawaguchi (2022), "When do extended physics-informed neural networks (XPINNs) improve generalization?", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 44:A3158 (NV) — [arXiv:2109.09444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09444)

### Inferences
- No demonstration was found of a PINN forward solution for a 3D heterogeneous aquifer at field scale (10^5 to 10^7 cells, wells, rivers, drains, unconfined conditions) that is competitive with a finite-volume solve. The documented pathologies explain why: strong K contrasts produce head fields with kinks and high-wavenumber content (spectral bias), the strong-form residual needs derivatives of K (forcing mixed or weak formulations), and wells are near-singular sources that pointwise collocation resolves badly.
- The usual selling points of PINN inversion (no forward solver, no mesh, no adjoint to derive) carry little weight in groundwater, where robust solvers exist and adjoint gradients are now available non-intrusively (see section 4). The fair comparator for PINN inversion is adjoint-based MAP or randomized maximum likelihood, or an ensemble smoother, at equal wall-clock time. None of the PINN groundwater papers found makes that comparison against a tuned implementation; the MAP baseline in the PICKLE Hanford study scales as N^3.28, which suggests a dense, non-matrix-free baseline.
- The million-unknown HT-PINN claim is less impressive than it sounds: the transmissivity network has far fewer effective degrees of freedom than grid nodes, and the tests use smooth Gaussian fields where spectral bias works in the method's favour.
- Credible uses for a groundwater practitioner: learning unknown constitutive relations (Richards retention and conductivity curves) from dense sensor data, and small 1D/2D data-assimilation problems where boundary conditions are unknown. For the constitutive-relation use, the differentiable-solver route in section 4 is the stronger competitor.

### Gaps
- No like-for-like comparison of PINN inversion against adjoint-based or iterative-ensemble-smoother calibration on the same groundwater problem was found.
- No PINN results were found for 3D regional models with wells, rivers and unconfined nonlinearity, or for strongly non-Gaussian (channelised, faulted) K at scale.
- The HT-PINN full text was not opened, so its baselines and wall-clock figures could not be checked; the million-unknown figure comes from a conference abstract.
- Whether newer optimisers (NNCG, natural-gradient methods) change the cost comparison with FEM on heterogeneous elliptic problems has not been independently tested as far as this search found.

## 2. Neural operators (DeepONet, FNO and U-FNO, geometry-aware and graph operators, transformer operators, latent / convolutional operators, physics-informed neural operators): results on the Darcy benchmark, CO2 storage and reservoir problems and groundwater; generalisation; training-set size relative to a calibration

### Takeaway
Neural operators reach roughly 0.5 to 1% relative L2 error on the standard Darcy benchmark, but that benchmark is a unit square with fixed boundary conditions, constant forcing and a two-valued coefficient, so it says little about aquifer models. The strongest subsurface evidence is CO2 storage (U-FNO, nested FNO: 3D with local grid refinement, inference 10^4 to 10^5.8 times faster than ECLIPSE), bought with roughly 5,500 to 8,500 full simulations per fixed model set-up; groundwater applications are synthetic 2D or single-site, and generalisation to unseen boundary conditions, geometries and parameter distributions is weak and rarely tested.

**CO2 storage and reservoir problems**
- U-FNO (Wen, Li, Azizzadenesheli, Anandkumar & Benson 2022, Adv. Water Resour. 163:104180, doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2022.104180; technical details below read from the full text of the arXiv version). Problem: axisymmetric (r-z) CO2-brine injection, 96 x 200 cells, 24 output times over 30 years. Data: 5,500 ECLIPSE (e300) runs (4,500 train, 500 validation, 500 test), about 10 minutes per run on a Xeon CPU. Inputs varied: heterogeneous permeability and anisotropy, porosity, thickness, perforation, rate, initial pressure, temperature, capillary parameters. Training: 1,872 s per epoch on an A100 GPU for 100 epochs (saturation) and 140 epochs (pressure). Test errors: gas-saturation plume error 0.0161 (R^2 0.981) and relative pressure-buildup error 0.0068 (R^2 0.992), versus 0.0276 and 0.0082 for plain FNO and 0.0299 and 0.0089 for a CNN. Inference about 0.01 s, about 6x10^4 times faster than ECLIPSE. Stated limitations: the U-Net path gives up grid invariance; no molecular diffusion or dispersion (the simulator's numerical diffusion is baked into the training data); permeability maps are not claimed to be realistic. — [ar5iv 2109.03697](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.03697); [arXiv:2109.03697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03697)
- Nested FNO (Wen, Li, Long, Azizzadenesheli, Anandkumar & Benson 2023, "Real-time high-resolution CO2 geological storage prediction using nested Fourier neural operators", Energy Environ. Sci. 16:1732). A hierarchy of FNOs follows a semi-adaptive local-grid-refinement simulation of 4D (3D + time) basin-scale injection. Inference is reported as 700,000 times faster than the numerical solver; a multi-well probabilistic assessment that would take nearly two years of simulation took 2.8 s. Training needs fewer than 2,500 samples at the coarsest level and about 6,000 at the finer levels. REALISTIC in dimension and resolution, synthetic in geology. — [arXiv:2210.17051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17051v2); [RSC landing page](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/Content/ArticleLanding/2023/EE/D2EE04204E)
- Fourier-MIONet (Jiang, Zhu & Lu 2023/2024): on the U-FNO problem, similar accuracy with 90% fewer parameters, about 3.5x faster training, under 15% of the CPU memory and under 35% of the GPU memory [listed on arXiv as a preprint]. — [arXiv:2303.04778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04778)
- Scale-out cost: a model-parallel FNO predicting time-varying two-phase CO2 flow with over 2.6 billion output variables was trained on up to 512 A100 GPUs on Perlmutter (Grady et al. 2023, Computers & Geosciences, doi:10.1016/j.cageo.2023.105402). The hardware bill is the point to note. — [arXiv:2204.01205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01205)
- Multi-fidelity FNO (Tang, Kong & Morris 2024, J. Hydrol. 629:130641): accuracy comparable to a high-fidelity-only model with 81% less data-generation cost, tested on a reservoir model at 110,000 cells and at 1 million cells. — [arXiv:2308.09113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09113)
- Fourier operators on "realistic geologies" for CO2 storage were presented at an ICML 2024 workshop (Chandra et al.), details not retrieved. — [mlanthology entry](https://mlanthology.org/icmlw/2024/chandra2024icmlw-fourier)

Model. Simul. Eng. Sci. (doi:10.1186/s40323-023-00252-0), use FNO surrogates of multiphase CO2 flow in permeability inversion and add a normalising-flow constraint that "forces the model iterates to remain in-distribution, thereby safeguarding the accuracy of trained Fourier neural operators". The abstract gives no figures for the out-of-distribution error that the constraint is designed to prevent. — [arXiv:2307.11099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11099)

**Groundwater applications**
- Taccari, Wang, Goswami, Nuttall, Chen & Jimack (2023/2024), "Developing a cost-effective emulator for groundwater flow modeling using deep neural operators": an extended DeepONet predicts head in a confined aquifer with one pumping well for unseen K fields and well locations, tested on two forward problems, an inverse analysis and a nonlinear case. Sci. 15(24):13005](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/24/13005)
- In the small-data regime (at most about 100 training trajectories per PDE), a plain multi-channel U-Net matched or beat ViT, AFNO, PDE-Transformer and KAN-UNet on five 2D periodic PDE families with less training time [preprint]. — [arXiv:2601.08404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08404)

**Training-set sizes actually used**
- FNO on the Darcy benchmark: about 1,000 solves (NV) — [arXiv:2010.08895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895). U-FNO: 4,500 training plus 1,000 validation/test ECLIPSE runs — [ar5iv 2109.03697](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.03697). Nested FNO: fewer than 2,500 plus about 6,000 — [arXiv:2210.17051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17051v2). Recurrent R-U-Net: 1,500 (2D) and 2,500 to 3,000 (3D) — [arXiv:1908.05823](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05823), [arXiv:2007.13326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13326). GEOS-based CO2 surrogate: 2,000 — [arXiv:2308.06341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06341). MGN-LSTM graph surrogate: 450 — [arXiv:2306.09648](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09648)

### Inferences
- Break-even arithmetic for U-FNO from the paper's own numbers: 5,500 runs x 10 min is about 917 CPU-hours of data generation, plus about 52 GPU-hours (saturation) and 73 GPU-hours (pressure) of training. Counting a GPU-hour as a CPU-hour, the surrogate has consumed the equivalent of about 6,250 simulator runs before it answers its first query. It pays only if more than about 6,000 further evaluations are needed for the same model configuration.
- A typical iterative-ensemble-smoother calibration uses of the order of 10^2 to low 10^3 forward runs (ensemble size times iterations; see White 2018, Environ. Model. Softw. 109:191-201, [doi:10.1016/j.envsoft.2018.06.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2018.06.009), numbers not re-verified here). Training sets of 10^3 to 10^4 runs therefore cost as much as, or several times more than, the calibration they are meant to accelerate. A neural operator is justified for calibration only if (a) the same emulator is reused across many later analyses, (b) multi-fidelity training cuts the high-fidelity run count by the 80 to 90% reported, or (c) the forward model is so slow that full-model calibration is infeasible anyway.
- The headline property of neural operators, discretisation invariance, addresses a problem groundwater modellers rarely have. The problems they do have (irregular domains, internal boundary conditions such as wells, rivers and drains, unconfined nonlinearity, pinch-outs, changes of structure between scenarios) are exactly where the evidence is thin or negative (Lu et al. 2022; Taccari et al. 2023).
- Surrogates are trained on prior samples, but calibration moves into the posterior. If the data are informative, the posterior lies in a low-density corner of the training distribution, where surrogate error is largest (consistent with Yin et al. 2023). This is a structural reason for the error-handling measures in section 6.
- The Darcy benchmark error (about 0.5 to 1%) is not transferable to head errors in a regional model: two coefficient values, zero Dirichlet boundaries and constant forcing make the solution operator far smoother than one with wells and multi-decade log-K variance.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed demonstration was found of a neural operator emulating a regional multi-layer MODFLOW or MF6 model with an unstructured grid, rivers, drains and many wells, validated inside a calibration against the full model.
- No controlled study was found of out-of-distribution behaviour for subsurface operators under changes of boundary-condition type or location, as opposed to changes of the coefficient field.
- PDEBench's Darcy-flow error table, and the full text of de Hoop et al., were not retrieved, so no numbers are quoted from them.
- The published (journal) versions of Taccari et al. and Fourier-MIONet were not opened; journal details should be confirmed.

## 3. Graph-neural-network and mesh-based simulators (MeshGraphNets and successors) and "foundation models" for PDEs: are there credible results for unstructured-grid subsurface flow, and what are the limits?

### Takeaway
Graph simulators can run on unstructured meshes, but the subsurface evidence amounts to a 2D faulted-reservoir CO2 study (450 training simulations) and a hybrid graph/U-Net model on the 1.1-million-cell SPE-10 case whose speed-up over a conventional simulator is only up to 18x. PDE foundation models are pretrained almost entirely on regular-grid fluid-dynamics data; 2026 independent benchmarks conclude they are "conditional rather than universal generalists", and no credible groundwater result was found. The success of weather models (GraphCast, Aurora) rests on decades of reanalysis on one fixed global grid, a condition groundwater does not meet.

### Cited Findings

**Graph and mesh-based simulators**
- MeshGraphNets: encode-process-decode message passing on simulation meshes with autoregressive roll-out (Pfaff, Fortunato, Sanchez-Gonzalez & Battaglia 2021, ICLR) (NV) — [arXiv:2010.03409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03409); particle-based predecessor GNS (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020, ICML) (NV) — [arXiv:2002.09405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09405). A MeshGraphNet implementation ships in NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo (NV) — [github.com/NVIDIA/physicsnemo](https://github.com/NVIDIA/physicsnemo)
- Hybrid Graph Network Simulator (Wu et al. 2022, KDD): a subsurface graph network for fluid evolution plus a 3D U-Net for pressure; scales to "millions of cells per time step", tested on SPE-10 with 1.1 million cells; reduces inference time "up to 18 times compared to standard subsurface simulators" and long-term prediction error by up to 21% relative to other learned models; predicts tens of time steps. REALISTIC grid size, modest speed-up. — [arXiv:2206.07680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07680)
- MGN-LSTM (Ju, Hamon, Wen, Kanfar, Araya-Polo and co-authors 2024, "Learning CO2 plume migration in faulted reservoirs with graph neural networks", Computers & Geosciences 193:105711): MeshGraphNet plus a graph LSTM to limit roll-out error, trained on 450 and tested on 50 unstructured simulations with impermeable faults; generalises to unseen meshes, well locations and permeability fields. The authors state that training "is currently limited to 2D cases", that scaling to larger domains is open, and that full-graph LSTM embeddings are memory-heavy. TOY (2D). — [arXiv:2306.09648](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09648)
- A 2025 conference paper extends MeshGraphNet with an LSTM and a CO2 mass-conservation loss on PEBI grids with one injector; only partial text was retrieved. — [CILAMCE 2025](https://publicacoes.softaliza.com.br/cilamce2025/article/download/13630/8845/7838)

**PDE foundation models**
- Multiple Physics Pretraining (McCabe et al. 2023) (NV) — [arXiv:2310.02994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02994); DPOT (Hao et al. 2024, ICML) (NV) — [arXiv:2403.03542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03542); PDEformer, whose title limits it to one-dimensional PDEs (Ye et al. 2024, "PDEformer: towards a foundation model for one-dimensional partial differential equations") — [arXiv:2402.12652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12652)
- Poseidon (Herde, Raonić, Rohner, Käppeli, Molinaro, de Bézenac & Mishra 2024, NeurIPS): a multiscale operator transformer pretrained only on fluid-dynamics equations (compressible Euler, incompressible Navier-Stokes) and evaluated on 15 downstream tasks that the authors describe as out-of-distribution; they claim significant gains in sample efficiency and accuracy over baselines. — [arXiv:2405.19101](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19101v2)
- Walrus (McCabe et al., Polymathic AI, November 2025) [preprint]: a 1.3-billion-parameter transformer trained on 2D and 3D data from The Well, FlowBench, PDEArena, PDEGym and PDEBench across nineteen scenarios; the authors report best results on 56 of 65 metrics over 26 tasks (the figure reached this search via a secondary report). Weights and code are MIT-licensed. — [arXiv:2511.15684](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15684); [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/polymathic-ai/walrus/blob/main/README.md)
- Independent benchmark: Chu, Liu, Biswas & Shen (2026), "Do physics foundation models learn generalizable physics? A bias-aware benchmark across physical regimes and distribution shifts" [preprint]. Five architectures, each from scratch and at three pretrained sizes, over 8 dynamics, 3 training mixtures and 25 test regimes (about 60,000 measurements). Conclusion: current models are "conditional rather than universal generalists"; better training-data mixes only partly help; "pretraining and scaling do not reliably remove" the biases. — [arXiv:2605.29283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29283)
- Los Alamos tested Poseidon and MORPH on shock-driven interface and dynamic-fracture problems, comparing fine-tuning with training from scratch, and note that The Well and PDEBench are "largely fluid-centric" [preprint; results not retrieved, abstract only]. — [arXiv:2603.04354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04354)
- A subsurface-specific pretraining proposal (PI-JEPA, April 2026) pretrains on unlabelled permeability and porosity fields and fine-tunes on few labelled simulations [preprint; a proposal, not an established result]. — [arXiv:2604.01349](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01349)

**Earth-system models as the positive example, and why it does not transfer**
- GraphCast (Lam et al. 2023, Science 382:1416-1421): trained on 39 years of ERA5 reanalysis, about four weeks on 32 TPU devices; 10-day global forecasts at 0.25 degrees in under a minute; beats ECMWF HRES on 90% of 1,380 verification targets (NV). — [doi:10.1126/science.adi2336](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi2336)
- Aurora (Bodnar et al. 2025, "A foundation model for the Earth system", Nature 641): trained on over a million hours of geophysical data and fine-tuned to air quality, ocean waves, tropical-cyclone tracks and high-resolution weather, where it outperformed operational forecasts at much lower computational cost; predictions are deterministic, which the authors list as a limitation. — [Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09005-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09005-y); [Microsoft Research podcast](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/podcast/abstracts-aurora-with-megan-stanley-and-wessel-bruinsma)
- Documented limits even in weather: ML forecasts are smoother than physical models and lose small-scale energy and physical balances with lead time (Bonavita 2024, Geophys. Res. Lett.) (NV) — [doi:10.1029/2023GL107377](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023GL107377); ECMWF's operational-style assessment (Ben Bouallègue et al. 2024, Bull. Am. Meteorol. Soc.) (NV) — [doi:10.1175/BAMS-D-23-0162.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-23-0162.1)

### Inferences
- Pretraining corpora (The Well, PDEBench, PDEArena, PDEGym) are dominated by time-dependent fluid dynamics on uniform, mostly periodic grids. A groundwater model is a parameter-to-state map for an elliptic or parabolic operator with strongly heterogeneous coefficients, internal sinks and irregular boundaries. The 2026 benchmark evidence says transfer across regimes is unreliable even within fluid dynamics, so there is no basis for expecting zero-shot or few-shot skill on aquifer models.
- Weather AI works because one system (the atmosphere) is observed densely for 40+ years on one grid and queried millions of times. A groundwater model is a different system at every site, with sparse observations and a geometry that changes from project to project. The analogue of "ERA5 for aquifers" does not exist; the only training data are a site model's own simulations, which is the surrogate setting of sections 2 and 6.
- An 18x speed-up (HGNS) is in the range where a coarser grid, a looser solver tolerance or a better preconditioner on the physical simulator would compete, with error control that the learned model lacks.
- Graph simulators are autoregressive in time and accumulate roll-out error. For groundwater, where steady-state or long stress-period solutions are standard, a direct parameter-to-state emulator is the more natural design.

### Gaps
- No credible result was found for a GNN or foundation model on a 3D unstructured-grid (Voronoi, quadtree, DISU-style) groundwater or reservoir model at production scale.
- No foundation model pretrained on Darcy-type subsurface data with published, independently checked transfer results was found; PI-JEPA is a proposal.
- The Rautela et al. (2026) results on pretrained versus from-scratch performance were not retrieved (abstract only).
- Whether the Poseidon downstream suite includes any heterogeneous-coefficient elliptic task comparable to Darcy flow with wells was not confirmed from the abstract.

## 4. Differentiable programming and differentiable simulators (JAX, PyTorch, Julia; JutulDarcy, DPFEHM, ADCME, Enzyme, Tapenade; solver-in-the-loop and hybrid physics-ML): what exists for subsurface flow, and what does AD give compared with hand-coded adjoints?

### Takeaway
This is the most mature and least hype-prone family, because the physics solver stays exact and automatic differentiation or adjoints supply gradients at the cost of a few forward runs regardless of parameter count. Subsurface-specific tools exist now: JutulDarcy.jl (differentiable multiphase reservoir simulator, MPI and GPU, 2025 journal paper), DPFEHM.jl (differentiable groundwater, Richards and transport, 2023) and MF6-ADJ (non-intrusive adjoint for MODFLOW 6, 2025). AD mainly saves developer time and guarantees consistency with the discrete forward model; it does not beat a well-written adjoint on speed, and naive AD through iterative solvers and long time loops fails on memory.

### Cited Findings

**The general idea and cross-field exemplars**
- Shen et al. (2023), "Differentiable modelling to unify machine learning and physical models for geosciences", Nat. Rev. Earth Environ. 4:552-567: process-based models written in differentiable form can be trained end to end with embedded neural networks, combining physical structure with learning from data (NV). — [doi:10.1038/s43017-023-00450-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43017-023-00450-9)
- Solver-in-the-loop: training a neural correction through a differentiable PDE solver, so the network sees its own effect on later time steps, outperforms training on precomputed data (Um, Brand, Fei, Holl & Thuerey 2020, NeurIPS) (NV). — [arXiv:2007.00016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00016)
- Learned discretisation and closure in a differentiable CFD code (JAX-CFD): Kochkov, Smith, Alieva, Wang, Brenner & Hoyer (2021), PNAS 118:e2101784118 (NV) — [doi:10.1073/pnas.2101784118](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2101784118); [github.com/google/jax-cfd](https://github.com/google/jax-cfd). Caution: McGreivy & Hakim's re-evaluation turned the 80x speed-up claim into "slightly slower" than a stronger baseline, and Bar-Sinai et al.'s "4-8x fewer degrees of freedom" into 2-4x. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- Hybrid model at operational scale: NeuralGCM couples a differentiable atmospheric dynamical core with learned physics and is competitive with leading physical and ML models for weather and with climate models for multi-decade statistics (Kochkov et al. 2024, Nature 632:1060-1066) (NV). — [doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07744-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07744-y)
- General differentiable PDE frameworks: JAX-FEM (Xue et al. 2023, Comput. Phys. **Subsurface-specific differentiable simulators**
- JutulDarcy.jl: Møyner (2025), "JutulDarcy.jl - a fully differentiable high-performance reservoir simulator based on automatic differentiation", Computational Geosciences 29:30 (open access; earlier ECMOR 2024 version). **MODFLOW, ParFlow and legacy Fortran**
- MF6-ADJ: Hayek, White, Markovich, Hughes & Lavenue (2025), "MF6-ADJ: a non-intrusive adjoint sensitivity capability for MODFLOW 6", Groundwater 63(6):874-888. - Source-transformation AD on a large legacy Fortran geoscience code is feasible but is a sustained project: MITgcm-AD v2 replaces the proprietary TAF tool with open-source Tapenade for tangent-linear and adjoint ocean and atmosphere models, integrated in the main branch (Gaikwad et al. 2024). - Hydrology precedent: SMASH v1.0 is a differentiable distributed hydrological model whose adjoint is generated by automatic code differentiation — [EGUsphere 2025-690](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-690/). A February 2026 preprint (Vrugt et al.) argues instead for analytic sensitivities in conceptual hydrological models to avoid the cost and truncation error of finite differences and AD libraries [preprint] — [arXiv:2602.06429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06429)

**AD compared with hand-coded adjoints**
- AD removes the need to derive and maintain Jacobian and adjoint code by hand, the main cost for non-differentiable simulators. — [DPFEHM JOSS paper](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.04560)
- AD-assembled Jacobians need not be slower than hand-coded ones in a compiled simulator. — [Møyner 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10596-025-10366-6)
- Naive reverse-mode AD through an implicit time-stepping scheme suffers "gradient vanishing and high memory demand"; the remedy is a discretise-then-optimise adjoint that differentiates the converged implicit equations instead of the solver iterations. In large-scale differentiable hydrology this cost about 5 to 10 times the explicit sequential model and gave median KGE 0.75 versus 0.73 (explicit) and 0.77 (LSTM). Song, Knoben, Clark, Feng, Lawson, Sawadekar & Shen (2024), Hydrol. Earth Syst. Sci. 28:3051-3077. — [HESS 28:3051](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/3051/2024/)
- AD can return derivatives that look wrong for chaotic dynamics, averaged oscillations, discretisation-dependent code, iterative fixed-point loops, lookup tables and linear solvers; users need to know these cases. Hückelheim, Menon, Moses, Christianson, Hovland & Hascoët (2023), "Understanding automatic differentiation pitfalls" [preprint]. — [arXiv:2305.07546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07546)
- Gradients through long unrolled simulations can explode or carry no useful signal (Metz, Freeman, Schoenholz & Kachman 2021, "Gradients are not all you need") [preprint] (NV). — [arXiv:2111.05803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05803)
- A hand-built (non-AD) adjoint remains practical when the simulator exposes its matrices: MF6-ADJ reads them through the MODFLOW API. — [PMC12652305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)

### Inferences
- From MF6-ADJ's numbers, the gradient of one scalar performance measure costs about 2 to 4 forward-run equivalents (22 s and 40 s adjoint versus 10 s forward) whatever the number of parameters. One adjoint solve of a weighted sum-of-squares objective therefore gives the full objective gradient for 10^4 to 10^5 parameters. A full Jacobian needs one adjoint per observation, so the benefit over perturbation holds when parameters outnumber observations, the normal case for highly parameterised models.
- For a groundwater modeller this family has the best ratio of evidence to risk: no training set, no out-of-distribution failure, no surrogate bias. The gradient can feed existing workflows: gradient-based MAP, randomized maximum likelihood, Hessian or low-rank posterior approximations, Jacobian filling for Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt, and data-worth analysis.
- JutulDarcy targets reservoir physics; single-phase aquifer flow is a subset, but MODFLOW-style packages (rivers, drains, ET, unconfined cell wetting and drying) are not its focus, so use for regional groundwater models would need those boundary types implemented. DPFEHM covers groundwater equations directly but is a research code with a small release history.
- Hybrid physics-ML closures have operational proof in weather (NeuralGCM) and catchment hydrology (differentiable parameter learning), but for groundwater the candidates (recharge and evapotranspiration parameterisations, stream-aquifer exchange, upscaled K tensors, constitutive curves) are so far demonstrated only for 1D Richards columns and synthetic reservoirs.
- Applying Tapenade or Enzyme directly to the MODFLOW 6 Fortran source is technically conceivable (the MITgcm precedent) but would need a maintained AD-compatible code path. MF6-ADJ's API route achieves most of the benefit for flow without touching the source.

### Gaps
- No AD-enabled or adjoint-enabled ParFlow was found; no Tapenade- or Enzyme-differentiated MODFLOW was found.
- No adjoint for MODFLOW 6 transport (GWT) or for the MAW, UZF, CSUB and MVR packages exists in MF6-ADJ as published.
- JutulDarcy's licence, unstructured-grid support and benchmark timings against OPM Flow or commercial simulators were not retrievable from the pages opened (the Springer full text redirected to a login); the performance statement comes from the abstract.
- No published comparison was found of Enzyme versus Tapenade on a groundwater code, nor of AD-based versus hand-coded adjoint run time on the same subsurface simulator beyond the qualitative statements above.
- A JAX- or PyTorch-native, maintained, general-purpose finite-volume groundwater simulator was not found; the items located are paper-specific research codes.

## 5. ML-accelerated classical solvers: learned preconditioners and multigrid components, neural initial guesses for Newton, learned nonlinear-solver control and time-step selection, ML coarse-grid corrections and super-resolution of coarse simulations

### Takeaway
Keeping the numerical solver and letting ML supply an initial guess or a preconditioner component preserves the solver's convergence test and accuracy, and reported gains are modest but real: 14% to 53% fewer nonlinear or linear solves in reservoir and CO2-storage cases. Learned linear solvers and preconditioners have not been shown to beat tuned algebraic multigrid or sparse direct solvers under fair comparison, and production-scale subsurface evidence is scarce.

### Cited Findings

**Learned multigrid and preconditioners**
- Learned prolongation operators for geometric multigrid on diffusion problems (Greenfeld, Galun, Kimmel, Yavneh & Basri 2019, ICML) (NV) — [arXiv:1902.10248](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10248); graph networks that learn algebraic-multigrid prolongation for sparse matrices (Luz, Galun, Maron, Basri & Yavneh 2020, ICML) (NV) — [arXiv:2003.05744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05744)
- Learned iterative solver with convergence guarantees (Hsieh, Zhao, Eismann, Mirabella & Ermon 2019, ICLR) (NV) — [arXiv:1906.01200](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01200). Re-evaluation: its "faster" claim against multigrid became about 10^3 times slower once the baseline was a sparse LU solve, which is much faster than multigrid for problems of that size. — [McGreivy & Hakim, arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- Graph networks that output approximate factorisations as preconditioners for conjugate gradients: Li, Chen, Du & Matusik (2023), tested on linear second-order PDEs in 2D and 3D, also yielding an initial guess at no extra cost — [arXiv:2305.16432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16432); NeuralIF, a self-supervised learned incomplete factorisation (Häusner et al.) — [arXiv:2305.16368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16368v2.pdf); learned incomplete LU/Cholesky factors for GMRES with a guaranteed-invertible output (2024) [preprint] — [arXiv:2409.08262](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08262v2)
- Operator-network hybrids: HINTS alternates DeepONet corrections (good at low frequencies) with classical relaxation (good at high frequencies) to obtain uniform convergence across modes (Zhang, Kahana, Kopaničáková, Turkel, Ranade, Pathak & Karniadakis 2024, Nat. Mach. Intell. 6:1303-1313) — [arXiv:2208.13273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13273); DeepONet-based preconditioners, where a subspace built from the trunk basis improved Krylov convergence over standard preconditioners in the authors' tests, with no numbers in the abstract (Kopaničáková & Karniadakis 2024) — [arXiv:2401.02016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02016)
- McGreivy & Hakim judged 8 articles to have used an inefficient baseline for elliptic PDEs, noting that direct LU solves beat multigrid for small problems. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)

**Neural initial guesses and nonlinear-solver assistance**
- Aghili, Franck, Hild, Michel-Dansac & Vigon (2025), "Accelerating the convergence of Newton's method for nonlinear elliptic PDEs using Fourier neural operators", Commun. Nonlinear Sci. Numer. Simul.: an FNO trained with data and residual losses supplies the Newton starting point; in 1D and 2D tests it speeds convergence "by a large margin" over a naive guess, most for highly nonlinear, anisotropic problems on coarse grids. TOY. — [arXiv:2403.03021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03021)
- Lechevallier, Desroziers, Faney, Flauraud & Nataf (2025), "Hybrid Newton method for the acceleration of well event handling in the simulation of CO2 storage using supervised learning", Computers & Geosciences: the fully implicit solver is kept; pressure is initialised from a linear implicit approximation and saturation from an FNO prediction, so Newton starts near its quadratic-convergence region after a well opening. The number of linear systems solved fell by 53% on the first test case and 38% on the second, both from a realistic saline-aquifer CO2 benchmark. — [publication page](https://thibaultfaney.github.io/publication/lechevallier-hybrid-2025); [OPM summit slides](https://opm-project.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Global_Hybrid_Newton_OPM_summit.pdf)
- A 2025 hybrid reservoir solver uses a network to initialise saturation in Newton-Raphson iterations and reports up to 14% fewer nonlinear iterations. — [Springer, doi:10.1007/s44288-025-00132-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44288-025-00132-7)
- In CFD, an in-situ graph network predicting warm starts for implicit solves lowered the initial Newton residual by about one order of magnitude on its test cases. — [ICCFD13 paper 184](https://easychair.org/smart-program/ICCFD13/Paper_184.pdf)
- A machine-learned near-well model has been integrated into the open-source OPM Flow simulator (von Schultzendorff, Sandve, Kane, Landa-Marbán, Both and co-authors; ECMOR 2024 proceedings, posted to arXiv January 2026; details not examined). — [arXiv:2601.11193](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11193v1)

**Coarse-grid corrections, super-resolution and multi-fidelity**
- Super-resolution of coarse flow fields with CNNs originated in turbulence (Fukami, Fukagata & Taira 2019, J. Fluid Mech. 870:106-120) (NV). — [doi:10.1017/jfm.2019.238](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2019.238)
- Learned corrections that let a solver run on a coarser grid (Bar-Sinai et al. 2019; Kochkov et al. 2021) shrink or vanish against stronger baselines: 4-8x fewer degrees of freedom became 2-4x, and 80x faster became slightly slower. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- In subsurface flow the coarse-fine idea has been used mainly to cheapen surrogate training. Jiang & Durlofsky (2023), J. Comput. Phys. 474:111800: a recurrent residual U-Net is trained in three steps, mostly on flow-based upscaled (coarse) models, with about 2,500 low-fidelity and 200 high-fidelity runs, and is "nearly as accurate" as a surrogate trained only on high-fidelity runs. — [arXiv:2204.11138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11138v1); [Stanford record](https://suetri-b.stanford.edu/publications/journal-articles/use-multifidelity-training-data-and-transfer-learning-efficient). The multi-fidelity FNO of Tang, Kong & Morris reports 81% lower data-generation cost. — [arXiv:2308.09113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09113)

### Inferences
- A learned initial guess cannot make the answer wrong, only slower or faster to reach, because the solver's own residual test still decides convergence. That makes it the lowest-risk way to use ML in a regulated or decision-support setting.
- The groundwater cases most likely to benefit are those where Newton or Picard iterations struggle after abrupt stress changes: unconfined cells drying and rewetting, Richards-equation infiltration fronts, density-dependent flow, and well switching. These resemble the well-event case where the 38 to 53% reduction was shown. For linear confined flow solved with a good AMG-preconditioned Krylov method there is little to gain.
- Reductions of 14 to 53% in solver work are worthwhile across thousands of calibration runs but do not change what is computationally feasible; they should be weighed against the cost of generating training data and maintaining a model tied to one grid and one physics configuration.
- Learned preconditioners are trained per problem class and usually per discretisation; classical AMG needs no training and adapts to each matrix. Without a comparison against tuned AMG on large, strongly heterogeneous, anisotropic Darcy systems, claims of superiority should be discounted.

### Gaps
- No credible, peer-reviewed study was found of learned time-step selection or learned nonlinear-solver control for groundwater or reservoir simulators with quantified savings against a tuned heuristic controller.
- No study was found of learned preconditioners or learned AMG components tested inside MODFLOW, ParFlow, PFLOTRAN or a comparable groundwater code, nor at 10^6+ unknowns against production AMG (hypre, AMGX) on heterogeneous Darcy systems.
- No study was found of ML super-resolution or downscaling applied to coarse groundwater-model heads or concentrations with validation against fine-grid runs; the subsurface literature located uses multi-fidelity training instead.
- The published HINTS and Lechevallier journal versions were not opened; numbers come from abstracts and the author's publication page.

## 6. Surrogates used inside inversion and uncertainty quantification: deep-learning emulators for groundwater and reservoir history matching, Gaussian-process and polynomial-chaos emulators, multi-fidelity and active-learning construction, and handling of surrogate error

### Takeaway
Image-to-image deep surrogates trained on roughly 1,500 to 3,000 full simulations reproduce pressure, saturation or head fields to within a few percent for a fixed model configuration and have been used inside ensemble smoothers and MCMC, almost always against synthetic "truth" models. Surrogate error biases the posterior unless it is handled; the remedies with evidence behind them (two-stage or delayed-acceptance MCMC, adaptive refinement near the posterior, explicit error models, multi-fidelity training) all keep the full model in the loop, and the measured net saving is far smaller than raw inference speed-ups suggest (for example, up to 50% in one careful groundwater study).

### Cited Findings

**How surrogate error is handled**
- Running MCMC on the surrogate alone gives a posterior biased by the surrogate-simulator discrepancy; Zeng et al. (2012) and Laloy et al. (2013) used two-stage MCMC, exploring with the surrogate and correcting with the full model. — [Zhang et al., arXiv:1807.05187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05187); Laloy, Rogiers, Vrugt, Mallants & Jacques (2013), "Efficient posterior exploration of a high-dimensional groundwater model from two-stage MCMC simulation and polynomial chaos expansion", Water Resour. Res. 49:2664-2682 (NV) — [doi:10.1002/wrcr.20226](https://doi.org/10.1002/wrcr.20226)
- Adaptive refinement: build the surrogate, locate the approximate posterior, add full-model runs there, and account for the remaining approximation error (Zhang, Zheng, Chen, Wu & Zeng, "Surrogate-based Bayesian inverse modeling of the hydrological system: an adaptive approach considering surrogate approximation error"). — [arXiv:1807.05187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05187)
- Delayed acceptance with a neural proxy: Lykkegaard, Dodwell & Moxey (2021), "Accelerating uncertainty quantification of groundwater flow modelling using a deep neural network proxy", CMAME 383:113895. Short sub-chains on a DNN generate proposals that the full model accepts or rejects, with an adaptive error model correcting DNN bias on the fly; UQ cost "can be reduced by up to 50% compared to single-level MCMC", depending on precomputation cost and DNN accuracy; tested on a 2D isotropic and a 3D anisotropic problem. The exact posterior is preserved. — [arXiv:2007.00400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00400)
- Multilevel delayed acceptance with an embedded DNN two-stage step, designed so the coarse chain does not diverge because of DNN error (Reddy & Fairbanks 2024, "Accelerating multilevel Markov chain Monte Carlo using machine learning models") [preprint]. — [arXiv:2405.11179](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11179)
- Adaptive delayed acceptance with a coarse-model error model was demonstrated on a large geothermal reservoir model (Cui, Fox & O'Sullivan 2011, Water Resour. Res. 47:W10521) (NV). — [doi:10.1029/2010WR010352](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010WR010352)
- Explicit model-error correction: project out the model-error component of the residual using a dictionary of nearest-neighbour error realisations (Köpke, Irving & Elsheikh); learn the error as a Gaussian process during inversion (Xu & Valocchi). — [review in this article](https://serval.unil.ch/resource/serval:BIB_C920392FB030.P001/REF)
- Adaptive multi-fidelity Gaussian-process construction with its error tracked during MCMC (Zhang, Man, Lin and co-authors, "Inverse modeling of hydrologic systems with adaptive multi-fidelity Markov chain Monte Carlo simulations"). — [arXiv:1712.02070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.02070v1)
- Paired simple/complex models in the PEST tradition: the surrogate populates the Jacobian while the complex model tests upgrades (Burrows & Doherty 2015, "Efficient calibration/uncertainty analysis using paired complex/surrogate models", Groundwater 53:531-541) (NV). — [doi:10.1111/gwat.12257](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12257)
- McGreivy & Hakim propose reporting the number of surrogate evaluations needed to break even after data-generation and training costs are counted. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)

### Inferences
- Deep surrogates rely on a low-dimensional or image-like parameterisation of the geology (KLE, CNN-PCA, GAN latent space) fixed before training. The posterior can only be as good as that prior parameterisation; if the conceptual model, layering or boundary configuration changes during a project, which is routine in groundwater work, the surrogate must be retrained from new simulations.
- A reported median error of 1 to 5% in pressure or saturation is not obviously negligible relative to head-measurement noise in a well-observed aquifer. When surrogate error is comparable to observation error and is not included in the likelihood, posterior variances will be too small and means biased. Most deep-surrogate history-matching papers found use synthetic truths generated by the same simulator and do not test this.
- The honest gain from surrogates in exact-posterior schemes is modest (up to 50% in Lykkegaard et al.), because the full model still evaluates every accepted proposal. Large gains appear only when the full model is dropped from the loop, which is where bias enters.
- For ensemble-smoother workflows a deep surrogate offers least: the smoother already needs only hundreds of runs. Surrogates fit better where far more evaluations are unavoidable (MCMC, rare-event probabilities, global sensitivity analysis, optimisation under uncertainty, real-time use) or where one emulator is reused for years.
- GP and PCE emulators remain the sound choice for under roughly 10 to 20 effective parameters and scalar or low-dimensional outputs, with error estimates built in (GP) and cheap sensitivity indices (PCE). Their weakness is input dimension, not accuracy.

### Gaps
- No study was found that calibrates a real regional groundwater model with a deep surrogate and then verifies the surrogate-based posterior against a full-model posterior (or a full-model ensemble smoother) on the same case.
- How Han et al. (2024) and Tang et al. treat surrogate error in the likelihood was not determined from abstracts.
- Training-set sizes for the Zabaras-group papers were not confirmed (the abstracts give none), so none are quoted.
- No recent (2023-2026) review of GP/PCE emulation specific to groundwater calibration was surfaced; the material found dates from 2011 to 2020.
- A search snippet described a benchmark of GP, PCE and a third emulator on 8- to 13-parameter reactive-transport models (I believe Laloy & Jacques 2019, Computational Geosciences), which would bound the input dimensions at which GP/PCE comparisons have been made, but the source link could not be confirmed, so it is not cited above.
- Active-learning acquisition strategies for deep (as opposed to GP) surrogates in groundwater inversion were not found beyond the adaptive-refinement papers cited.

## 7. Critical assessments: weak baselines, reproducibility and negative results, and the conditions under which ML approaches pay off

### Takeaway
The best systematic evidence (McGreivy & Hakim 2024) is that 79% of fluid-PDE papers claiming ML beats a numerical method used a weak baseline, that re-run comparisons erased or reversed most speed-ups, and that negative results almost never reach abstracts. ML pays off under a narrow set of conditions: very many queries of one fixed configuration, a need for answers in seconds, abundant training data from the same distribution, and tolerance for unquantified error, as in weather forecasting and basin-scale CO2 screening.

### Cited Findings

**Systematic evidence of over-optimism**
- McGreivy & Hakim (2024), "Weak baselines and reporting biases lead to overoptimism in machine learning for fluid-related partial differential equations", Nat. Mach. Intell. (published 25 September 2024). Of 82 articles comparing ML solvers with standard methods, 76 claimed to outperform; 60 of the 76 (79%) compared against a weak baseline, 2 (2.6%) possibly weak, and 14 (18.4%) used a strong baseline. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1); [doi:10.1038/s42256-024-00897-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00897-5)
- Their two rules for a fair comparison: (1) compare at equal accuracy or equal run time, coarsening the standard solver until accuracies match; (2) compare against an efficient, state-of-the-art numerical method for that PDE. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- Reproduction of 10 articles: in 9 of 10 the stronger baseline was at least two orders of magnitude faster than the published one, and in 7 of 10 it outperformed the ML method. Examples: FNO from "up to 10^3x faster" to 7x; PINO from 10^3x to 7x; DeepONet from 24x faster to 10x slower; Kochkov et al. from 80x faster to slightly slower; Hsieh et al. from faster to 10^3x slower; Brandstetter et al. from "much faster" to 10^3x slower. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- Larger claimed gains were more likely to come from weak baselines; articles with strong baselines showed smaller improvements. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- Reporting bias: of 232 abstracts mentioning results, 94.8% (220) mention only positive results, 5.2% (12) both, and none only negative results. The authors infer outcome-reporting and publication bias and recommend that funders and venues adopt policies against weak baselines. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1); [news release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1058911)
- The review covers forward, fluid-related problems only; the authors state it does not establish whether the same holds elsewhere and note that ML could help downstream tasks such as optimisation, inverse problems and UQ, and where standard methods are "extremely tedious" to apply. — [arXiv:2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- Data leakage and irreproducibility are widespread across ML-based science generally (Kapoor & Narayanan 2023, "Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science", Patterns 4:100804) (NV). — [doi:10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804)

**Method-specific negative results**
- PINN versus FEM: no win on time or accuracy for Poisson, Allen-Cahn or Schrödinger problems (Grossmann et al. 2024) — [arXiv:2302.04107](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04107). PINN versus finite differences: 32 hours against under 20 seconds, and failure to capture vortex shedding (Chuang & Barba 2022) [preprint] — [arXiv:2205.14249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14249). PINN failure modes on convection and reaction problems (Krishnapriyan et al. 2021) (NV) — [arXiv:2109.01050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01050). PINN failure on Buckley-Leverett shocks (Fuks & Tchelepi 2020) (NV) — [doi:10.1615/JMachLearnModelComput.2020033905](https://doi.org/10.1615/JMachLearnModelComput.2020033905)
- FNO: sensitivity to noise and complex geometry (Lu et al. 2022) — [arXiv:2111.05512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05512); outperformed by U-Net on MODFLOW-generated groundwater data with wells (Taccari et al. 2023) [preprint] — [arXiv:2307.04010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04010); matched or beaten by a simple U-Net in the small-data regime (Nguyen & Sandfeld 2026) [preprint] — [arXiv:2601.08404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08404)
- Foundation models: "not universally generalizable across physical regimes, scales, and distribution shifts" (Chu et al. 2026) [preprint] — [arXiv:2605.29283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29283)
- Balanced reviews that list open problems alongside promise: Brunton & Kutz (2024), "Promising directions of machine learning for partial differential equations", Nat. Comput. Sci. 4:483-494 (journal title and pages NV; the arXiv version is titled "Machine learning for partial differential equations") — [arXiv:2303.17078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17078); Karniadakis et al. (2021) (NV) — [doi:10.1038/s42254-021-00314-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42254-021-00314-5)

**Where ML has demonstrably paid off**
- Global weather: one system, about 40 years of reanalysis, one grid, queried continuously; GraphCast beats the leading physical model on 90% of targets at a tiny fraction of forecast cost after a four-week training run (NV) — [doi:10.1126/science.adi2336](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi2336); Aurora extends this to several earth-system tasks — [Nature 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09005-y)
- Many-query screening with a fixed configuration: nested FNO reduces a multi-well probabilistic CO2 assessment from about two simulator-years to 2.8 s after about 8,500 training runs — [arXiv:2210.17051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17051v2); continental ParFlow emulation at over 1,000x — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/fe2dfe9eb7814bddbfd75e9f2b6606e1)
- Exact gradients from differentiable or adjoint-enabled physics solvers: 2 to 4 forward-run equivalents per performance measure versus 10^4 to 10^5 perturbation runs — [MF6-ADJ, PMC12652305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
- Hybrid schemes that keep the solver: 38 to 53% fewer linear solves after well events — [Lechevallier et al. 2025](https://thibaultfaney.github.io/publication/lechevallier-hybrid-2025); up to 50% lower MCMC cost with the exact posterior preserved — [arXiv:2007.00400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00400)

### Inferences
- Conditions under which a learned surrogate pays for itself: (1) the number of queries exceeds the training-set size by a wide margin (roughly more than 10^4 for the published subsurface surrogates); (2) geometry, boundary-condition types and stress configuration are fixed, or varied only within the sampled range; (3) latency matters (real-time operation, interactive decision support); (4) the inputs at use time come from the training distribution; (5) error of a few percent without a certificate is acceptable, or a full-model check is run on the decisions that matter.
- Typical groundwater calibration fails conditions (1), (2) and (4): hundreds to a few thousand runs, a conceptual model that evolves, and a posterior that departs from the prior. That is why sections 4 to 6 point to gradients and solver-retaining hybrids as better bets than stand-alone emulators.
- Elliptic and parabolic problems such as confined groundwater flow are where classical solvers are strongest (optimal-complexity multigrid, direct solvers at moderate size), so the headroom for ML speed-ups on a single forward run is smallest there. McGreivy & Hakim's finding that eight papers used inefficient elliptic baselines fits this.
- Any speed-up claim should be discounted unless it states: baseline solver and tolerance, accuracy at which the comparison is made, cost of training-data generation and training, and number of evaluations to break even.

### Gaps
- No systematic weak-baseline review equivalent to McGreivy & Hakim exists for inverse problems, UQ or subsurface applications; their conclusions are for forward fluid problems only.
- No groundwater-specific audit of ML surrogate claims (baseline solver settings, training cost accounting, reproducibility) was found.
- The second half of the McGreivy & Hakim article (beyond 100,000 characters of the arXiv HTML) was not read.

## 8. Synthesis requested by the assignment: ranked shortlist of the most credible opportunities for groundwater simulation and calibration, and approaches judged over-hyped for this use

### Takeaway
The most credible opportunities keep the full-physics groundwater model in the loop and use differentiation or learning to make it cheaper to interrogate: adjoint and AD gradients first, then error-controlled surrogate acceleration, then site-specific emulators for many-query tasks. The approaches most over-sold for groundwater are PINNs as solvers, general-purpose neural operators and PDE foundation models, and headline speed-up factors that ignore training cost.

### Cited Findings

**Ranked shortlist (most credible first), with the evidence each rests on**

1. **Adjoint or AD gradients of the full-physics model.** Evidence: MF6-ADJ gives sensitivities for 17,625 parameters (Freyberg) and for a 115,531-cell model at an adjoint cost of about 40 s and 22 s against 10 s forward runs, with unstructured-grid and Newton support, no source changes, and open code. — [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/). For new model builds or research codes, JutulDarcy.jl (adjoints, MPI, GPU) — [Møyner 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10596-025-10366-6) and DPFEHM.jl (groundwater equations, AD, ML coupling) — [JOSS 2023](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.04560). Limits: no transport adjoint and several unsupported MODFLOW packages; cost linear in the number of performance measures. — [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
2. **Surrogate-accelerated inference with full-model correction** (delayed acceptance, multilevel schemes, adaptive refinement, paired simple/complex models). Evidence: up to 50% lower MCMC cost with the exact posterior on 2D and 3D groundwater problems — [Lykkegaard et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00400); adaptive posterior-focused refinement — [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05187); multilevel delayed acceptance with DNN [preprint] — [arXiv:2405.11179](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11179). Gains are modest but unbiased.
3. **Site-specific emulators for many-query, fixed-configuration tasks**, built with multi-fidelity training. Evidence: F-Area contaminant-plume U-FNO under climate scenarios — [Meray et al. 2024](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/physics-informed-surrogate-modeling-for-supporting-climate-resili/); ParFlow-CLM emulator at over 1,000x — [Bennett et al. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/fe2dfe9eb7814bddbfd75e9f2b6606e1); nested FNO — [Wen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17051v2); multi-fidelity training cutting high-fidelity data cost by 81% and to about 200 fine runs — [Tang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09113), [Jiang & Durlofsky 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11138v1). Limits: thousands of training runs, no independent validation at the groundwater site, retraining on any structural change.
4. **ML assistance inside the nonlinear solver** (learned initial guesses after stress changes). Evidence: 38 to 53% fewer linear solves for well openings in a realistic CO2 benchmark — [Lechevallier et al. 2025](https://thibaultfaney.github.io/publication/lechevallier-hybrid-2025); up to 14% fewer nonlinear iterations — [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44288-025-00132-7). Accuracy is untouched because the solver's convergence test remains.
5. **Hybrid differentiable models that learn unknown closures or constitutive relations.** Evidence: Richards constitutive relations learned through a JAX solver — [Bandai et al.](https://escholarship.org/content/qt29b1d8cz/qt29b1d8cz.pdf?t=snk60p); permeability inference with a differentiable simulator in the training loop halving error relative to a data-only network [preprint] — [arXiv:2604.13291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13291); adjoint-based implicit differentiable hydrology at 5 to 10x the explicit cost — [Song et al. 2024](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/3051/2024/). Mostly 1D or synthetic so far.
6. **Local emulators for repeated sub-problems** (well drawdown, near-well behaviour). Evidence: probabilistic drawdown networks trained on MODFLOW and MODFLOW 6 — [Dahl et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frwa.2023.1028922/pdf), [GEUS Bulletin](https://geusbulletin.org/index.php/geusb/article/view/8357/14385); ML near-well model in OPM Flow [preprint] — [arXiv:2601.11193](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11193v1)
7. **Physics-informed parameterisations on the discretised equations** (watch list). Evidence: PICKLE on a 2D Hanford model, comparable to MAP with near-linear scaling, synthetic data only — [Yeung et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00037)

**Approaches judged over-hyped for groundwater use, with the evidence**

- **PINNs as forward solvers for aquifer models.** No time or accuracy win over FEM even on Poisson problems — [Grossmann et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04107); orders of magnitude slower, and efficiency rarely reported — [McGreivy & Hakim 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1); slower than traditional methods and initialisation-sensitive for layered Richards problems by the authors' own account — [Bandai & Ghezzehei 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11274)
- **PINN inversion as a replacement for adjoint-based or ensemble calibration at scale.** Demonstrations are 2D, synthetic and smooth-field — [HT-PINN](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2022.128828), [Tartakovsky et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019WR026731) — while adjoint gradients for MODFLOW 6 now cost a few forward runs — [Hayek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12652305/)
- **General-purpose neural operators judged by the Darcy benchmark.** The benchmark is a fixed-boundary, constant-forcing, two-valued-coefficient unit square — [Li et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895); FNO lost to U-Net on MODFLOW data with wells [preprint] — [Taccari et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04010); FNO degrades with noise and complex geometry — [Lu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05512); boundary conditions are commonly neglected in operator designs — [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/125983)
- **PDE foundation models for groundwater.** Pretrained on fluid dynamics — [Poseidon](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19101v2), [Walrus](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15684); independent benchmark finds them "conditional rather than universal generalists" [preprint] — [Chu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29283)
- **Headline speed-ups of 10^4 to 10^6.** These are inference-only figures; U-FNO needed 5,500 ten-minute ECLIPSE runs plus 50 to 70 GPU-hours per output variable before its first prediction — [ar5iv 2109.03697](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.03697); matched-accuracy re-evaluation cut FNO and PINO claims from 10^3x to 7x — [McGreivy & Hakim 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- **Learned linear solvers and preconditioners as replacements for AMG or direct solvers on elliptic problems.** One prominent learned solver was 10^3x slower than sparse LU on re-evaluation, and eight papers used inefficient elliptic baselines — [McGreivy & Hakim 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)
- **Graph-network simulators for large unstructured subsurface grids.** Published evidence is 2D with 450 training runs and the authors' own statement that 3D scaling is open — [Ju et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09648) — or an 18x speed-up on SPE-10 — [Wu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07680)
- **Transfer of weather-AI success to aquifers.** GraphCast and Aurora depend on decades of reanalysis on one fixed global grid — [Lam et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi2336), [Bodnar et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09005-y)

### Inferences
- Ranking logic: items 1, 2 and 4 cannot make the answer wrong, since the physical model still decides; they are ranked above items 3, 5 and 6, which can, and which therefore need validation budgets. Within each group the order follows the strength and realism of the published evidence.
- Item 1 is ranked first also because it needs no training data and its cost does not grow with parameter count, which matches how highly parameterised groundwater models are calibrated. Its practical value depends on software integration with existing calibration tools, which this research did not assess.
- Item 3 becomes the top choice in one situation: an operational or decision-support setting where one stable site model must answer tens of thousands of scenario or risk queries, or respond in seconds. In that setting the 10^3 to 10^4 training runs are amortised and the emulator's error can be audited against held-out full-model runs.
- A reasonable test before investing in any learned surrogate for a groundwater project: count the evaluations needed after training, divide by training runs plus training time expressed in run-equivalents, and proceed only if the ratio is well above one and the model structure is expected to stay fixed.
- The over-hyped list is about fitness for groundwater use as of late 2026, not about scientific merit. Several items (physics-informed operators, subsurface-specific pretraining, learned AMG) could move to the credible list if tested against strong baselines on realistic aquifer models.

### Gaps
- The integration status of MF6-ADJ with PEST/PEST++ or pyEMU workflows was not examined in this research.
- No cost-benefit study comparing these options on a single realistic groundwater model was found; the ranking is assembled from separate studies with different baselines.
- Developments after about July 2026 were not surfaced; the web-search budget for this session was exhausted before follow-up searches (for example on learned time-stepping, groundwater super-resolution, and 2026 neural-operator groundwater applications) could be run.
- Items marked (NV) rest on recalled bibliographic details and should be confirmed before quotation in a final report.
