# Twelve borrowed methods can sharpen groundwater modelling

The fields that solve Darcy-type equations and large inverse problems outside hydrogeology point a MODFLOW and PEST++ user in one direction. Keep the physics-based simulator and replace the algebra and statistics around it. Seismology, weather prediction, oceanography, glaciology and reservoir engineering all stopped forming Jacobians and now invert 10^5 to 10^9 unknowns with adjoint gradients, matrix-free Gauss-Newton steps and low-rank Hessians, at a cost that does not grow with parameter count. MODFLOW 6 gained the missing ingredient in 2025, an adjoint that costs 2.2 to 4 forward runs, yet no PEST-family algorithm consumes it. On the forward side the best-supported transfers are old ones, multilevel linear solvers and safeguarded nonlinear iteration, while physics-informed networks, neural operators and PDE foundation models have no credible result on a realistic aquifer model. For uncertainty analysis, weather prediction and statistics have cheap tests and corrections for the known failure modes of ensemble smoothers, and groundwater practice uses almost none of them. This report reviews that literature and proposes twelve methods: four for simulation, seven for calibration and uncertainty analysis, and one for management optimization. Each states what it builds on, what is new against published groundwater work, how to implement it around MODFLOW 6, PEST++ and pyEMU, the evidence, and the risks. Three limits apply throughout. The researchers' web-search quota ran out part-way through, most Elsevier, Springer and Wiley pages were blocked, and coverage of 2025 and 2026 is thin. Every statement that something has not been done in groundwater therefore means "not found", and the ordering of proposals is a judgement that no common benchmark yet supports.

## MODFLOW 6 and PEST++ document their own weak points

A note on evidence comes first. Seven sets of research notes sit behind this report, and their authors tagged every citation as either checked during the research session or recorded from memory or a bibliographic lookup. I carry that through with one mark. A dagger (‡) after a link means nobody re-opened the paper during the research. I take only qualitative statements from daggered sources, and where I repeat a number from one I say in the sentence that it is unverified. Unmarked sources were opened as an abstract, full text, documentation page or search-engine extract. Preprints are labelled.

Mainstream groundwater codes share a numerical design. They use low-order, fully implicit schemes with Picard or Newton outer loops and Krylov inner solvers preconditioned by incomplete factorization or multigrid. MODFLOW 6, at version 6.8.2 of 30 October 2026, solves a control-volume finite-difference system whose two-point flux is consistent only on grids aligned with the conductivity tensor, and it offers XT3D as a multi-point alternative that widens the stencil and makes the matrix asymmetric ([MODFLOW 6 releases](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/releases); [Provost et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A56)). Its serial linear solver offers conjugate gradients or BiCGSTAB with ILU-family preconditioners. Its nonlinear controls are under-relaxation, backtracking and pseudo-transient continuation, and the input guide warns that continuation "can worsen the convergence behavior" in some cases ([IMS input definition](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/mf6io/mf6ivar/dfn/sln-ims.dfn)). The release notes of 2025 and 2026 read as a list of nonlinear repairs. A convergence check wrongly passed failed linear solves from version 6.4.2 until 6.6.3. Version 6.7.0 added an option "to prevent flow from leaving a dry cell". Version 6.8.0 added an implicit lake formulation that "can solve problems for which the default formulation fails to converge". Until 6.8.2 the continuation factor depended on the order of models in the solution ([v6.6.3](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.6.3.tex); [v6.7.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.7.0.tex); [v6.8.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex); [v6.8.2](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.2.tex)). Parallel runs arrived in version 6.5.0 through MPI and PETSc. A USGS developer's rule of thumb on the user forum is that they are "not practical unless the number of cells is at least 100,000 to 1,000,000", and no release note from 6.5.0 to 6.8.2 mentions GPU support ([v6.5.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.5.0.tex); [MODFLOW users group, April 2026](https://groups.google.com/g/modflow/c/kRmPZP05iNw)). The usual calibration workload of hundreds of independent ten-minute runs gains little from this kind of parallelism, because PEST++ already distributes whole runs.

These numerics set the terms for calibration. The PEST++ manual asks for tight solver convergence and restraint with adaptive time stepping so that finite-difference derivatives stay clean, and it warns that random parameter fields "trigger occasional model run failures" ([PEST++ users manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)). In one ensemble-smoother study with a 204,000-cell HydroGeoSphere model, **30 of 100 realizations were abandoned** for slow convergence ([Delottier et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023)). A regional MODFLOW-NWT model with 1,777 parameters took more than six hours per run, and its full finite-difference Jacobian needed over 25 times the runs of the ensemble and randomized-Jacobian alternatives ([Hunt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13106)).

The PEST family covers three algorithm classes. Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt (GLM) estimation with truncated SVD and Tikhonov regularization, plus linear FOSM uncertainty analysis, needs one model run per parameter to fill each Jacobian. The iterative ensemble smoother PESTPP-IES and its sequential sibling PESTPP-DA replace the Jacobian with ensemble covariances. PESTPP-OPT, PESTPP-MOU and the ensemble-gradient PESTPP-SQP handle optimization under uncertainty. PESTPP-SQP first appeared in release 5.2.27, which the researchers date to mid-2026 by inference ([PEST++ users manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [PEST++ releases](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/releases)). The documentation is frank about limits. The smoother's update equations "assume model linearity", and Doherty's help pages quote Evensen and co-authors that "nobody knows precisely what distribution the method samples in the nonlinear case" ([PEST help, PESTPP-IES](https://help.pesthomepage.org/pestpp-ies.html)). Parameter variance usually ends up "decreasing substantially each iteration", and the shipped remedy resets it toward the prior with no test of whether the result is a valid posterior sample. The automatic localization matrix "will likely not fit in memory" beyond about 20,000 weighted observations. Prior-data conflict is detected and then resolved by a "draconian" removal of the conflicting observations. Structural error is set aside in one sentence, "we ignore this" ([PEST++ users manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md)). For channelized fields, smoother theory "cannot guarantee the maintenance of long-distance hydraulic property connectedness" ([Delottier et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023)).

Two newer directions already sit inside the toolchain. Data space inversion (DSI) builds a linear emulator of observations and predictions from a prior ensemble and conditions it without adjusting any parameter. In its first hydrologic test, with 100 prior runs, it matched the simulator-based smoother for stream infiltration and gave a narrower posterior for fast travel times, and a later paper calls it "vulnerable to prior-data conflict" ([Delottier et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-4213-2023); [Kitlasten et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2025.1609778)). pyEMU's release notes also list a DSI autoencoder, which source inspection found still commented out of the public exports. Nonlinear latent spaces for DSI are therefore in progress, and I do not count them as new ([pyEMU releases](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/releases); [pyEMU DSIAE source](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/emulators/dsiae.py)). The second direction is INTERA's MF6-ADJ, published in 2025. It reads the assembled matrices from one forward run through the MODFLOW API, stores them, and solves the transposed system backward in time with no change to the Fortran source. For a Freyberg model with 17,625 parameters the forward run took about 10 s and the adjoint about 40 s, where perturbation needs 17,626 runs. For the 115,531-cell San Pedro model the adjoint took about 22 s. It covers flow only, excludes the MAW, MVR, CSUB and UZF packages and all transport, returns zero sensitivity at constant-head cells, and costs one adjoint solve per performance measure ([Hayek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025)). The research notes disagree on exactly which head-dependent boundary packages it differentiates, so check the current documentation. Neither the paper nor the repository mentions PEST++ or pyEMU ([mf6adj repository](https://github.com/INTERA-Inc/mf6adj)).

A caution on novelty follows from this baseline. Adjoint sensitivities, Jacobian-free geostatistical inversion, ensemble smoothers at a million parameters, prediction-space inversion, deep-learning surrogates inside smoothers, GAN and VAE geological priors, normal-score filters, multimodal local updates, multilevel MCMC and reduced-order models of MODFLOW have all been published for groundwater. The proposals below claim novelty only for specific combinations, or for bringing a method into the MODFLOW and PEST++ chain. Each names the nearest published groundwater work.

## Forward runs get faster through multigrid and safeguards, not neural solvers

The forward run sets the budget for every calibration method, so I start there.

### The oldest solver evidence is the strongest

Algebraic multigrid (AMG) is the standard solver for Darcy-type pressure systems outside groundwater practice. The groundwater evidence dates from 2002, when Detwiler and co-authors linked AMG into MODFLOW-2000 and measured run times **up to 140 times faster than the PCG2 solver**, at up to nine times the memory in three dimensions ([Detwiler et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2002.tb02654.x)). A 2014 rebuild of MODFLOW on a parallel AMG framework ran nine times faster on 40 cores than MODFLOW with its geometric multigrid package on a strongly heterogeneous aquifer ([Cheng, Mo and Shao 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12047)). Yet the largest published MODFLOW 6 application, the 278-million-cell GLOBGM model built on a parallel prototype, used conjugate gradients with a one-level additive Schwarz preconditioner. Partitioning raised its iteration counts by 34 to 58%, and its largest submodel reached 62% parallel efficiency on 224 cores ([Verkaik et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-17-275-2024)). Iteration growth under partitioning is the textbook symptom that a multilevel method removes.

Regional groundwater grids are thin, layered and extruded, which is the mesh class that ice-sheet and weather modellers built dedicated multigrid for. Tuminaro and co-authors coarsen the thin direction first until one layer remains and then apply standard AMG. Müller and Scheichl solved an atmospheric pressure equation with more than 10^10 unknowns using a tensor-product multigrid that "exploits the strong vertical anisotropy" ([Tuminaro et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1040839); [Müller and Scheichl 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2327)). ParFlow's developers report the mirror image, that high cell aspect ratios make their standard multigrid converge slowly ([Kuffour et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-13-1373-2020)). Reservoir simulation adds the constrained-pressure-residual idea for coupled or asymmetric systems, in which AMG solves the near-elliptic part and a cheap smoother sweeps the rest ([Gries et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.2118/163608-PA)).

GPUs are a narrower win than the headlines suggest. Published gains are 2 to 8 times for a 2013 MODFLOW conjugate-gradient solver, 2 to 5 times for NVIDIA's AmgX on one GPU, and 1.6 to 3.2 times for hypre's AMG against 640 CPU cores. ParFlow reports 10 to 30 times on its structured grid and still labels GPU support beta ([Hughes and White 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.12004); [Naumov et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1137/140980260); [Exascale Computing Project 2021](https://www.exascaleproject.org/highlight/a-new-approach-in-the-hypre-library-brings-performant-gpu-based-algebraic-multigrid-to-exascale-supercomputers-and-the-general-hpc-community/); [Piotrowski et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2023-1079), a preprint; [ParFlow GPU readme](https://github.com/parflow/parflow/blob/master/README-GPU.md)). In OPM Flow a consumer GPU with a simple preconditioner overtook a 16-core CPU running the stronger preconditioner only above about two million cells, and a PFLOTRAN test ran slower overall on a GPU because matrix transfers dominated ([ACROSS project 2024](https://www.acrossproject.eu/progress-of-gpu-acceleration-in-opm-flow/); [Dyngeland and Elster 2023](https://www.ntnu.no/ojs/index.php/nikt/article/view/5668)). For ensemble workloads on shared nodes, where memory bandwidth limits throughput, reduced precision is the cheaper experiment. ECMWF ran its forecast model in single precision for about 40% more throughput with "no noticeable reduction in accuracy" ([Váňa et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-16-0228.1)). Nobody has measured mixed precision on a groundwater solver that the researchers could find.

### Other fields treat the nonlinear solver as a research subject

MODFLOW's package-by-package fixes suggest room for a more systematic approach, and four results from elsewhere transfer. For Richards' equation, an adaptive switch between the slow but always-convergent L-scheme and Newton "is as fast as Newton's method when it converges, and is significantly more robust". The plain L-scheme needed about 373% of the adaptive scheme's computing time in one benchmark ([Stokke et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2023.10.020)). Anderson acceleration extrapolates from the last few fixed-point iterates. According to a search summary of a paper the researchers could not open, it improved the speed and reliability of modified Picard iteration for variably saturated flow ([Lott et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2011.12.013)). Reservoir simulators stop each Newton update from crossing a known kink in the flux function, which makes Newton converge for any time step in that setting ([Jenny et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2009.06.032)‡). Nonlinear domain decomposition, which solves hard subdomains locally before the global Newton step, is now a user option in the open-source OPM Flow. Its developers give an honest balance. The method "is outperformed by Newton when this method works well" and "avoids time-step cuts" where Newton struggles, and their own timings on two field models show run-time reductions of 12 to 27% ([Møyner et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-023-10215-4); [Rasmussen and Møyner 2024](https://opm-project.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/rasmussen-nldd-opm-oslo2024.pdf)).

Two further ideas are cheap. A posteriori error estimates can stop linear and nonlinear iterations once their error no longer affects the answer, with "important computational savings" in the original paper ([Ern and Vohralík 2013](https://doi.org/10.1137/120896918)). Glaciologists pose the moving ice margin, a degenerate diffusion problem close to unconfined flow with drying cells, as a variational inequality solved by semi-smooth Newton methods, and seepage faces have been posed the same way in groundwater ([Jouvet and Bueler 2012](https://doi.org/10.1137/110856654)‡; [Ashby et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/qjmam/hbab001)). None of this pays for confined linear models.

### Discretization, time stepping and model reduction have groundwater precedents

On discretization the main transfer has already happened. XT3D is a multipoint flux scheme of the kind reservoir simulators have used for twenty years. Bardot and co-authors showed why it matters. Before MODFLOW 6, a 30-degree dipping layer with a 100:1 conductivity ratio conducted at only 2% of its input value ([Bardot et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13273)). XT3D alone does not fix steep dip, which also needs unstructured connectivity and at least two model layers per aquifer ([Provost et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.13459)). What reservoir practice has and MODFLOW lacks is a monotone nonlinear two-point flux option and a pre-run indicator of where the two-point flux is inconsistent, which Raynaud and co-authors built from static grid properties ([Raynaud et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.2118/203920-PA)). The 2025 SPE11 benchmark tempers enthusiasm for new schemes. Across 18 participating groups, simulator choice, grid type and a twofold refinement "generally matter less (or at least not more) than the variability among participating groups", and 80% of submitted data were corrected after the deadline ([Nordbotten et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijggc.2025.104519)). Workflow quality control moved those results more than the flux scheme did.

Time integration in groundwater codes is mostly backward Euler with heuristic step control. MODFLOW 6's adaptive stepping reacts to solver failure and to transport and stream-routing criteria, and none of the controls described in its release notes estimates truncation error in head ([v6.6.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.6.0.tex); [v6.8.0](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/blob/develop/doc/ReleaseNotes/previous/v6.8.0.tex)). Library integrators with variable order and error control have been applied to Richards problems since 1997 ([Tocci, Kelley and Miller 1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708%2896%2900008-5)‡). A careful comparison found heuristic control better under strong nonlinearity and error-based control better at strict tolerances, and recommended mixing them ([D'Haese et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.1369)). For linear confined flow the alternatives are older and more radical. The Laplace-transform finite-difference method needs "no more than eight matrix solutions" per output time where standard stepping "may require several hundred time steps" ([Moridis and Reddell 1991](https://doi.org/10.1029/91WR01190)). Rational Krylov integration in transient electromagnetics, which obeys the same parabolic operator, converges in a number of shifted solves "independent of mesh size and conductivity structure" ([Börner, Ernst and Güttel 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggv224)). Groundwater papers demonstrated Lanczos and Laplace-domain methods between 1989 and 1991, and the field then dropped them ([Dunbar and Woodbury 1989](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR025i003p00551); [Sudicky 1989](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR025i008p01833)). Parallel-in-time integration is a poor fit. The reference study broke even at about 256 processors and reached speedups of 6 to 10 at 4,096, and calibration already spends spare cores on more runs ([Falgout et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1137/130944230)).

Projection-based model reduction has a groundwater record too. A reduced model of a confined basin-scale aquifer "runs approximately 1,000 times faster than the full model" ([Siade, Putti and Yeh 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008792)). An independent test found the limit. With unconfined conditions or head-dependent boundaries, plain proper orthogonal decomposition "either greatly looses accuracy or does not significantly reduce model run time", and the hyper-reduced variant struggled with river boundaries ([Gosses, Nowak and Wöhling 2017](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2017/EGU2017-7925.pdf), a conference abstract). Multiscale finite-volume solvers reached a commercial reservoir simulator with about an order of magnitude speedup, well short of the thousandfold first claimed, and as linear solvers they are "comparable to state-of-the-art algebraic multigrid methods" ([Lie et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9627-2); [Zhou and Tchelepi 2012](https://doi.org/10.2118/141473-PA)). Their groundwater case rests on reusing basis functions across an ensemble, which nobody has measured. The embedded discrete fracture model generalizes what MODFLOW-USG's connected linear network already does for wells and conduits ([Panday et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A45)). Stochastic Galerkin, lattice Boltzmann, particle and meshfree solvers and high-order hybridizable or isogeometric schemes are poor bets for regional flow in the numerical-methods researcher's judgement, because finite volumes on layered grids with multigrid are already cheap and conservative, and heterogeneous aquifers lack the smoothness that high-order methods exploit.

### Most machine-learning solvers fail a fair comparison

The machine-learning literature needs the most filtering. A 2024 audit of 82 papers comparing ML solvers with standard methods for fluid PDEs found that **79% of those claiming a win used a weak baseline**. Re-running ten of them cut the Fourier neural operator's claimed speedup from "up to three orders of magnitude" to about 7 and reversed several others ([McGreivy and Hakim 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00897-5)). Physics-informed neural networks did not beat finite elements on time or accuracy even for Poisson problems, and the authors of a layered-soil Richards study call their own networks "significantly slower than traditional numerical methods" ([Grossmann et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/imamat/hxae011); [Bandai and Ghezzehei 2022](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/4469/2022/)). Neural operators have their strongest subsurface results in CO2 storage, where the U-FNO surrogate needed 5,500 ten-minute simulator runs for one fixed model configuration ([Wen et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03697)). An ensemble-smoother calibration uses hundreds to a few thousand runs, so such a surrogate costs more than the calibration it would accelerate. On MODFLOW-generated data with pumping wells, a plain U-Net beat the Fourier operator in a preprint, and an independent 2026 preprint benchmark calls PDE foundation models "conditional rather than universal generalists" ([Taccari et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04010); [Chu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29283)). The researchers found no neural-operator, graph-network or foundation-model result on a regional multi-layer MODFLOW model with rivers, drains and wells.

Two ML-adjacent families hold up. Differentiable simulators keep the solver exact and supply gradients. JutulDarcy.jl and DPFEHM.jl are open Julia codes built that way, and MF6-ADJ reaches the same end for MODFLOW 6 through the API ([Møyner 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10596-025-10366-6); [O'Malley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.04560)). Learned initial guesses keep the solver's convergence test, so they can change only the time to the answer. One CO2-storage study reports 38 to 53% fewer linear solves after well events, a figure taken from the author's publication page ([Lechevallier et al. 2025](https://thibaultfaney.github.io/publication/lechevallier-hybrid-2025)).

### Proposal 1. Layer-aware multilevel preconditioning with hierarchy reuse across an ensemble

This proposal builds on AMG-preconditioned Krylov solvers, the extruded-mesh multigrid of ice-sheet and weather codes, and the reservoir two-stage preconditioner. It has four parts. Make AMG-preconditioned conjugate gradients or BiCGSTAB the normal choice for MODFLOW 6 models above roughly 10^5 to 10^6 cells. Add a variant that relaxes or coarsens through the layers first and then applies AMG to the collapsed two-dimensional problem, for grids with thin layers and strong vertical anisotropy. For the asymmetric Newton and XT3D matrices, run the multilevel solve on the symmetric conductance part and a cheap incomplete-factorization sweep on the full matrix. Then build the multigrid hierarchy once per grid and reuse it across ensemble realizations whose conductivity fields differ modestly, since calibration runs the same grid hundreds of times.

AMG in groundwater codes is not new. MODFLOW-2000 had the LMG package from 2001, FEFLOW offers the SAMG solver, and ParFlow is built on multigrid preconditioning ([Mehl and Hill 2001](https://doi.org/10.3133/ofr01177)‡; [Diersch 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-38739-5)‡). The new parts are the layer-aware coarsening, the two-stage treatment of XT3D and Newton matrices, and hierarchy reuse, none of which the researchers found tested on a groundwater code. Implementation can start outside MODFLOW. The API exposes the assembled matrix and right-hand side at run time, so PyAMG or hypre can be benchmarked on real MODFLOW 6 systems from Python before any Fortran changes ([Hughes et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2021.105257)‡). Production use goes through the PETSc link in the extended executable. The 6.5.0 release notes mention a modified ILU preconditioner for parallel runs, and the researchers could not retrieve the full list of PETSc preconditioners that build exposes, so establishing that is the first task. The evidence is the 2002 and 2014 MODFLOW results and GLOBGM's iteration growth. The benefit is largest for big models and near zero below 10^5 cells. The risks are memory, up to nine times in the 2002 study, which hurts when many ensemble members share a node, and stalling of black-box AMG on models with dry cells and strong heterogeneity. No peer-reviewed scaling study of the PETSc-based MODFLOW 6 exists to measure against.

### Proposal 2. A safeguarded nonlinear solver stack for MODFLOW 6, with ensemble warm starts

MODFLOW's Newton option relies on upstream weighting, under-relaxation and backtracking. This proposal layers four safeguards from other fields on top, in order of effort. The first is Anderson acceleration of the outer Picard loop, including the loop that couples lakes, wells, streams and subsidence to the flow solution. It needs only stored iterates and a small least-squares solve. The second is update limiting at known kinks. Reservoir solvers stop a Newton step at the inflection point of the flux function. The groundwater kinks are the cell top and bottom, drain and river switching elevations and the pumping-reduction threshold, so a step that would cross one stops there and relinearizes. The third is adaptive stopping of the linear solve early in the nonlinear iteration. The fourth is nonlinear domain decomposition on MODFLOW 6 multi-model simulations, whose exchanges already define subdomains, for cases where a mine pit or a perched zone controls the convergence of a large model. For ensembles I add an element that is my own synthesis. Start each realization's solve from its own converged heads in the previous smoother iteration, or from a principal-component emulator of heads built from the prior ensemble. Train a classifier on the run history to flag parameter sets likely to fail before they consume hours of compute. The simulation-based-inference literature uses such classifiers to keep proposals away from invalid runs ([Lueckmann et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01861)‡).

Published groundwater work covers Anderson acceleration for Richards problems, pseudo-transient continuation, and seepage faces as variational inequalities. The researchers found no test of Anderson acceleration, kink-aware limiting, a posteriori stopping or nonlinear domain decomposition inside MODFLOW, FEFLOW or HydroGeoSphere. The kink-aware rule is an inference from the reservoir principle, since S-shaped flux functions do not occur in single-phase flow. Anderson acceleration and warm starts can be prototyped through the API's extended model interface, which lets an external program control the simulation inside a time step. The other elements need changes in the IMS solver. The benefit is fewer failed and overlong runs, which matters more to calibration than raw speed, because the smoother drops failed members and a failed run can leave an empty Jacobian column. The gain is zero for confined linear models, and nonlinear preconditioning slows runs in which Newton already converges in a few iterations. Two questions are open. Do gains measured for multiphase reservoirs carry over to unconfined single-phase flow? And how do warm starts interact with pseudo-transient continuation, which the input guide says can hurt when the initial guess is already close?

### Proposal 3. A linear many-query engine built through the MODFLOW API

Many management and screening problems run a confined or linearized model with fixed boundary types thousands of times under different pumping or recharge. For that class, extract the system matrices once through the API and replace time stepping with a handful of shifted solves, either Laplace-domain solves with numerical inversion or rational Krylov integration with a fixed set of poles. The same shifted solves span a reduced model of order 10 to 100 for repeated forcing scenarios, because rational Krylov integration and projection-based reduction are the same mathematics. The reduced model then serves three users: response-matrix generation for PESTPP-OPT, the cheap level in the multifidelity estimators of Proposal 9, and scenario screening.

Nothing in the mathematics is new to groundwater. Laplace-transform Galerkin and finite-difference methods, Lanczos reduction, reduced-order MODFLOW models used for inversion and a Laplace-Krylov solver for hydraulic tomography are all published ([Vermeulen, Heemink and Valstar 2005](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004WR003698); [Bakhos, Saibaba and Kitanidis 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2015.07.007)‡). The delivery is new. By the researchers' account the earlier MODFLOW reduction work had to modify MODFLOW itself. The API and pyMOR, which wraps external solvers "without adding any model reduction specific code to these solvers", make a non-intrusive tool possible ([Milk, Rave and Schindler 2016](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1026614)). The researchers found no test of rational Krylov or modern contour inversion at MODFLOW scale, and no operator-inference or dynamic-mode-decomposition application to groundwater flow. The expected benefit is the factor of about 1,000 that Siade and co-authors measured for a confined regional model. The risk is scope. Rivers, drains and evapotranspiration are switches that break the linear subspace, as the Gosses comparison showed, so the tool must treat those cells explicitly or refuse the model. Plain Galerkin projection also does not conserve mass cell by cell, which matters if reduced heads feed particle tracking.

### Proposal 4. Pre-run flux-consistency indicators, then an optional monotone flux

Add to FloPy a static indicator, computed from grid geometry and the conductivity tensor before any run, that maps where the two-point flux is inconsistent, plus a dynamic check based on an idealized tracer run. The user then knows where XT3D or extra connectivity is needed and where it is wasted. This builds on the error estimators Raynaud and co-authors designed for a commercial reservoir simulator and on the MODFLOW dipping-layer studies. A second step is a nonlinear two-point flux option for models whose heads drive transport or density, where small overshoots feed nonlinear terms. That scheme keeps a two-point stencil, restores consistency and guarantees non-negative solutions, at the price of a nonlinear system even for a linear PDE ([Terekhov, Mallison and Tchelepi 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2016.11.010)‡). Reservoir researchers have applied it to corner-point grids with degenerate faces, according to a search summary of the paper ([Schneider et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9710-8)).

XT3D and its accuracy studies are published groundwater work. The indicator and the monotone option are not, and the researchers found no head-to-head test of XT3D against other consistent schemes. The indicator is a small Python utility with almost no risk, and its benefit is diagnostic. The SPE11 result argues that it should come before any new flux scheme, because resolution and analyst choices moved those results more than the scheme did. The monotone flux is a research item whose cost in extra iterations on MODFLOW grids is unknown.

## Calibration elsewhere runs on adjoints, low-rank Hessians and audited ensembles

### Every large-scale field dropped the explicit Jacobian

Seismic full-waveform inversion, 4D-Var in weather prediction, ocean state estimation, ice-sheet inversion and reservoir history matching share one recipe. They compute the gradient of a scalar misfit with one forward and one adjoint solve, get curvature from Hessian-vector products that cost two more linearized solves each, and stop after tens to thousands of solves whatever the parameter count. The best-documented case is the Antarctic ice sheet, where Isaac and co-authors inferred a basal sliding field of 409,545 parameters. On a smaller glacier problem in the same paper, **the number of linearized solves fell from 7,031 to 5,376 as the parameter dimension grew 140-fold**, from 10,371 to 1,456,225 ([Isaac et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221)). ECMWF's operational 4D-Var adjusts a control vector of order 10^9 with three outer nonlinear loops of about 30 inner iterations each, and a single linear update "causes large degradation" ([Bonavita, Lean and Holm 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/npg-25-713-2018)). The stated design principle is that work should scale with the number of parameter directions the data inform, not with the number of parameters or observations.

The cost of an adjoint is modest. Because the backward solve is always linear, a discrete adjoint of an implicit simulator costs about as much as the forward run. Measured ratios are 2.2 to 4 for MF6-ADJ, 2 to 5.6 for the mature MITgcm ocean-model adjoint, and about a fifth of a forward solve for a nonlinear Stokes ice model whose forward run needs five Newton iterations ([Hayek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025); [Gaikwad et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11952); [Isaac et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221)). Against a finite-difference Jacobian the adjoint wins by roughly the ratio of parameters to observations. That advantage flips for history matching against 10^4 to 10^5 transient observations with 10^3 to 10^4 pilot points, where an explicit adjoint-filled Jacobian costs more than a finite-difference one. Every large-scale field escaped that trap by changing the algorithm to matrix-free products. Filling the same Jacobian a different way was never the answer.

Groundwater is the outlier for software reasons, not mathematical ones. Adjoint sensitivities have been in the hydrogeology literature since 1985, a 2023 review counted over 60 published applications in saturated flow, and a production adjoint for MODFLOW-2005 existed from 2007 and lapsed because nobody maintained it ([Sykes, Wilson and Andrews 1985](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR021i003p00359); [Turnadge et al. 2023](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2023/files/turnadge.pdf); [Hayek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gwat.70025)). The record of automatic differentiation on legacy Fortran explains the caution. The MITgcm adjoint depends on about 350 storage directives in its dynamical core and 600 more in packages, hand-written wrappers for MPI, I/O and solvers, and regression tests that catch code changes breaking the differentiation. The open-source differentiation tool still ran 2.2 to 6.4 times slower than the commercial one ([Gaikwad et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11952)). The researchers found no attempt to apply such tools to MODFLOW. The home-grown competitor is the principal component geostatistical approach (PCGA), which needs 2 + p + K forward runs per Gauss-Newton iteration for K prior principal components and p drift terms, whatever the number of unknowns, and no adjoint ([pyPCGA documentation](https://pypcga.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html); [Kitanidis and Lee 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013WR014630)). In one laboratory tracer case it reconstructed 99,072 log-conductivity values with 500 forward runs per iteration ([Lee et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015WR018483)).

The applied-mathematics vocabulary maps term by term onto what a PEST user already does. The table is the researchers' synthesis from the hIPPYlib paper and the PEST++ manual.

| Applied-mathematics term | PEST and pyEMU counterpart |
|---|---|
| Gauss-Newton data-misfit Hessian | The normal matrix JᵀQJ |
| Eigenpairs of the prior-preconditioned misfit Hessian | Squared singular values and right singular vectors of the weighted, prior-scaled Jacobian |
| Retain eigenvalues above about 1 | Solution space, the number of super-parameters |
| Laplace approximation, prior covariance minus a low-rank update | FOSM Schur-complement posterior covariance |
| Likelihood-informed subspace | A solution space averaged over an ensemble instead of one linearization point |
| Randomized eigensolver, about 2(r+l) linearized solves | Fill the Jacobian with n runs, then take its SVD |

The saving sits in the last row. hIPPYlib's steady Darcy-type example retains about 30 eigenvectors out of 4,225 parameters with 50 observations, at a cost of about 100 linearized solves, and in the Antarctic problem the data inform "only about 5,000 (out of 1,190,403) modes" ([Villa, Petra and Ghattas 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948); [Isaac et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1221)). Data and noise set the rank, so dense transient datasets can push it into the thousands. In a global seismic problem it rose from about 40 to at least 700 when two sources were added ([Bui-Thanh et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1137/12089586X)).

### Other fields know how ensemble methods fail and have tools to measure it

PESTPP-IES already contains the main reservoir-engineering ideas: the Levenberg-Marquardt ensemble update, multiple data assimilation, correlation-based adaptive localization and rejection of lagging realizations. What it lacks, relative to weather and applied-mathematics practice, is principled variance maintenance and a way to measure its own reliability. Theory is clear on the limits. A 2025 Acta Numerica synthesis states that analysis of the accuracy of ensemble Kalman methods, "especially for uncertainty quantification", has lagged their empirical success ([Calvello, Reich and Stuart 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492924000060)). For a nonlinear forward map the analysis ensemble does not converge to the Bayesian posterior even with infinitely many members, and small ensembles underestimate variance on average even for a perfect linear model ([Ernst, Sprungk and Starkloff 2015](https://doi.org/10.1137/140981319)‡; [Sacher and Bartello 2008](https://doi.org/10.1175/2007MWR2323.1)‡). The first open groundwater benchmark with reference posteriors confirms the second effect. On a 10,000-parameter log-conductivity problem with references from 800,000-step MCMC runs, a standard ensemble Kalman filter with 1,000 members scored 0.3 to 0.4 on normalized error metrics, and with 100 members the paper reports filter collapse ([Xu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-5375-2024)). The error does not always run toward overconfidence. In a 2026 preprint with a strongly nonlinear latent-diffusion parameterization, the smoother kept more uncertainty than surrogate-based MCMC and sequential Monte Carlo, and the authors conclude that ensemble Kalman methods "may provide overestimated posterior uncertainty" there ([Di Federico, Teng and Durlofsky 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140)). The researchers found no published comparison of PESTPP-IES itself against a reference posterior.

Operational weather prediction handles the variance problem with tools that have long records. These are gain corrections that shrink each regression coefficient according to ensemble size and sample correlation, relaxation of the posterior spread back toward the prior, and hybrid covariances that blend a static covariance with the ensemble one so the update can leave the ensemble's span ([Anderson 2012](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-11-00013.1)‡; [Whitaker and Hamill 2012](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-11-00276.1)‡; [Bannister 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2982)‡). Reservoir engineers added tempering schedules chosen by a discrepancy principle in place of a fixed iteration count ([Le, Emerick and Reynolds 2016](https://doi.org/10.2118/173214-PA)‡; [Iglesias and Yang 2021](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6420/abd29b)‡). Multilevel ensembles are a mixed story. The telescoping multilevel filter "does not perform well" on reservoir history matching according to a search summary of the assessment paper, and a hybrid form that pools levels into one covariance was proposed in its place ([Fossum, Mannseth and Stordal 2020](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10596-019-09911-x)).

Exact samplers remain expensive. Derivative-free MCMC is realistic for tens of parameters at 10^4 to 10^6 runs. The exceptions are delayed-acceptance and multilevel schemes that screen proposals with a cheap model and correct with the full one. In the multilevel delayed-acceptance paper's Darcy example, an adaptive error model for the coarse level tripled the effective sample size, from 326 to 1,012 of 40,000 samples, and the authors note that the fine-level chain "must be sequential" ([Lykkegaard et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03876)). A neural-network first stage cut the cost of exact MCMC for groundwater flow by up to 50% ([Lykkegaard, Dodwell and Moxey 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00400)). Sequential Monte Carlo fits parallel hardware better, because each tempering stage is an independent batch of runs, and the multiple-data-assimilation smoother can be read as its Gaussian approximation ([Del Moral, Doucet and Jasra 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2006.00553.x)‡; [Stordal and Elsheikh 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.09.030)‡). Climate modellers reach a Bayesian posterior for convection parameters of an idealized climate model in "O(10^2) model runs" by calibrating with an ensemble Kalman method, fitting a Gaussian-process emulator to those runs, and sampling the emulator ([Dunbar et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13262)).

Two statistical traditions have no counterpart in routine groundwater practice. Simulation-based inference supplies diagnostics. Simulation-based calibration draws many synthetic truths from the prior and checks that each truth's rank within its own posterior sample is uniform ([Talts et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788)‡). The same literature found that every benchmarked neural estimator can produce overconfident posteriors and that ensembles of estimators are more reliable ([Hermans et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581)). In a ParFlow watershed study built on 183 simulator runs, posteriors were accurate when the surrogate generated the data and biased and overconfident when ParFlow did ([Hull et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-4685-2024)). DSI is the linear-Gaussian member of this family, so the diagnostics apply to it directly. The second tradition is Bayes linear history matching, which does not seek a posterior at all. It uses emulators and an implausibility measure whose denominator adds observation, emulator and model-discrepancy variances, and it rules out regions of input space in waves. A 22-input epidemic model was matched to 18 outputs over nine waves this way ([Andrianakis et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003968)). The method can return the empty set, an explicit signal that no parameter values reconcile the model with the data. The researchers found no groundwater application.

### Priors and model error decide more than the update equation does

Three findings on priors bear on PEST practice. First, a hand-picked stationary variogram is a strong assumption. An MCMC study that freed the mean, sill, integral scales, anisotropy and smoothness of a 10,000-cell field found posterior realizations spanning a wide range of geostatistical models ([Laloy et al. 2015](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3011n0x1)). But a plain iterative smoother handles free hyperparameters badly. On a small two-phase test with uncertain anisotropy and range, a standard smoother ended with a mean squared data mismatch of about 13,000 against an expected 240, and a hybrid that computed the hyperparameter-to-field derivative analytically reached about 1,151 ([Oliver 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01116)). Kitlasten and co-authors report the same hierarchical difficulty from the PEST side. Second, the spatial-statistics and PDE-constrained-inversion communities represent a Gaussian prior by a sparse precision operator, the inverse of an elliptic differential operator, in place of a dense covariance matrix. Sampling becomes a sparse solve on white noise, anisotropy and non-stationarity enter through the operator's coefficients, and the construction works on unstructured meshes ([Lindgren, Bolin and Rue 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01084); [Villa, Petra and Ghattas 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03948)). It has been used with adjoints on a poroelastic aquifer model in Nevada ([Alghamdi et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04577)). pyEMU's geostatistics module, by source inspection, offers exponential, Gaussian and spherical variograms and a spectral simulator for regular grids only ([pyEMU geostats](https://github.com/pypest/pyemu/blob/develop/pyemu/utils/geostats.py)).

Third, for categorical geology the prior decides the answer. A benchmark on a channelized aquifer with 720 transient heads compared three inversion methods under three training images. All did well with the training image that generated the truth and degraded with the others. The ensemble smoother converged fast and then placed confident but wrong channel patterns ([Juda, Straubhaar and Renard 2023](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.160/)). Latent-space generative priors now reach 524,288 cells compressed to 1,024 latent variables in a preprint, but the demonstrations are synthetic, need thousands of training realizations and interact unreliably with Kalman updates ([Di Federico, Teng and Durlofsky 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11140)). An early GAN study needed 387,200 forward runs for a two-dimensional case with 49 heads ([Laloy et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017WR022148)). Edge-preserving regularizers such as total variation have been tried in hydrogeology and can be emulated in a GLM scheme by reweighting preferred-difference equations between iterations. Sparse heads cannot locate edges, though, and these penalties have no ensemble-smoother equivalent ([Lee and Kitanidis 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/2012WR013431)).

On model error every community reaches the same two conclusions. Ignoring it gives biased parameters and overconfident predictions, and an additive discrepancy term is not identifiable from calibration data alone, a confounding that "will only be resolved with meaningful priors" ([Brynjarsdóttir and O'Hagan 2014](https://tonyohagan.co.uk/academic/abs/simmach.html)). Weather centres find that weak-constraint 4D-Var separates model error from initial-state error only when the two have different spatial scales ([Laloyaux et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3891)). The practical recipes come from reservoir engineering. One estimates a correlated total-error covariance from the residuals of a first smoother pass and assimilates again, after which parameter updates shrink and forecasts improve ([Lu and Chen 2020](https://doi.org/10.2118/199358-PA); [Oliver and Alfonzo 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-017-9678-4)). The other estimates a low-dimensional error model jointly with the parameters. Without it, calibration of a coarsened model gave "extreme values of the calibrated model parameters and a biased posterior distribution" ([Rammay, Elsheikh and Chen 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2019.02.056)). Groundwater has its own diagnosis of structural noise and its own warning case. Assimilating tritium into one- and two-layer models biased a ten-year nitrate-load forecast, by about 40% in the mean for the one-layer model, while a seven-layer model was largely unaffected ([Doherty and Welter 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008377); [Knowling et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-24-1677-2020)). Gaussian-process error models have been fitted jointly with groundwater parameters, including on a real regional model ([Xu et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR019831)).

### Proposal 5. Hybrid-operator, Jacobian-free GLM and randomized low-rank FOSM

Replace the explicit Jacobian in a GLM solver with two operators. The transpose product Jᵀw comes from one MF6-ADJ solve with w as weights on the simulated observations. The forward product Jv comes from one finite-difference directional run of the whole PEST model chain, as PCGA does. With these, a Krylov least-squares solver such as LSQR, damped in the Levenberg-Marquardt manner and preconditioned by the prior covariance, computes each parameter upgrade in a number of solves that tracks the data-informed dimension ([Paige and Saunders 1982](https://doi.org/10.1145/355984.355989)‡). A randomized two-pass eigensolver applied to the same operators returns the leading eigenpairs of the prior-preconditioned misfit Hessian in about 2(r+l) solves for rank r and oversampling l. Those eigenpairs are the super-parameters and the FOSM posterior covariance, without the n-run Jacobian. Repeating the solve from perturbed prior fields and perturbed observations gives randomized maximum likelihood with exact gradients, which is the structure of ECMWF's ensemble of data assimilations ([Bonavita, Isaksen and Hólm 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.1899)‡).

The proposal builds on hIPPYlib-style inexact Gauss-Newton and low-rank Laplace approximation, on PCGA, on MF6-ADJ and on the randomized SVD that PESTPP-GLM already applies to an explicit matrix. Much of it is published for groundwater in pieces: PCGA, a Krylov-recycling Levenberg-Marquardt method ([Lin, O'Malley and Vesselinov 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016WR019028)‡), randomized uncertainty measures for the geostatistical approach ([Saibaba and Kitanidis 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2015.04.012)), and Hessian-based inference on the Nevada poroelastic model. The MF6-ADJ developers themselves name quasi-Newton optimization and randomized maximum likelihood as next steps ([White 2025](https://cwemf.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/28.4_AdjointStateMethodSensitivityAnalysis_White.pdf)). The new element is the pairing of an adjoint transpose with a model-independent directional derivative inside the PEST++ protocol. That pairing keeps scripted pre- and post-processors intact for half the products and needs no tangent-linear code. The researchers found no application of randomized low-rank Hessian uncertainty analysis to a MODFLOW-class regional model.

Implementation has a near-term and a longer-term form. Today, adjoint-computed rows can enter PEST++ through the base_jacobian() option, which serves the first PESTPP-GLM iteration, FOSM analysis and PESTPP-OPT, and PEST reads external derivatives through its JACFILE variable ([PEST++ users manual](https://github.com/usgs/pestpp/blob/develop/documentation/pestpp_users_manual.md); [PEST help, sensitivities](https://help.pesthomepage.org/sensitivities.html)). That already delivers grid-scale forecast sensitivity vectors and capture maps at one solve each. The matrix-free solver itself is a new PEST++ program or a pyEMU driver, plus a chain rule through pilot-point kriging factors and multiplier arrays, which is linear. The evidence is the dimension-independent solve counts quoted above and the measured adjoint cost. By the researchers' estimate the saving per Jacobian-equivalent is n divided by 2(r+l), one to two orders of magnitude for 10^5 parameters and 10^2 to 10^3 informed directions.

The risks are specific. The adjoint covers heads and boundary fluxes in flow models only, so concentrations, lake stages and derived observations still need finite differences or ensembles. Whether MF6-ADJ accepts an arbitrary weighted combination of outputs as one performance measure needs confirming against its documentation. Exact gradients at grid scale make the starting point and continuation strategy matter more, which is the lesson of cycle skipping in seismic inversion. Drains, rivers and drying cells switch, and the adjoint is exact only locally. A dot-product test and a directional finite-difference check on every new model should be routine. Where no adjoint exists, PCGA delivers much of the benefit and should be benchmarked first.

### Proposal 6. A variance-preserving, subspace-aware iterative ensemble smoother

Change the PESTPP-IES update in four ways and leave the model interface alone. Correct each ensemble-estimated gain element for sampling error as a function of ensemble size and sample correlation. Replace covariance reinflation with relaxation of posterior spread toward the prior, the operational weather-prediction remedy. Choose the tempering schedule adaptively and stop by a discrepancy principle instead of a fixed iteration count. Blend the ensemble covariance with the static prior covariance, so the update can reach directions outside the span of 100 to 300 realizations. A fifth, more experimental change comes from the dimension-reduction literature. Estimate the likelihood-informed subspace from the cross-covariances the smoother already forms, update only the coordinates in that subspace, and leave the complement at its prior draws. That would act as localization for parameters with no spatial position, such as hyperparameters and latent variables.

PESTPP-IES already has adaptive localization, reinflation, multiple data assimilation and a multimodal option, and variance underestimation in groundwater filters was documented in 2008 ([Hendricks Franssen and Kinzelbach 2008](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007WR006505)‡). The researchers found no PEST++ implementation or groundwater test of sampling-error-corrected gains, spread relaxation or hybrid covariances. The subspace-restricted update is one researcher's own proposal, with no published test that the notes record, and I pass it on as that. All five changes are C++ work inside PESTPP-IES. The hybrid covariance needs the prior covariance as an operator, which Proposal 10 supplies. The evidence for the first four is long operational use in weather prediction, cited here from the bibliographic record, and the collapse at 100 members in the 2024 groundwater benchmark. The expected benefit is a posterior spread that survives iteration without the reinflation heuristic. None of these changes makes the smoother exact for a nonlinear model. They treat sampling error, not the linear-update approximation, and each should be judged by the audit in Proposal 7.

### Proposal 7. A routine audit of ensemble posteriors

Before trusting any other proposal, measure how well calibrated the current tools are. The audit has three tiers. On DSI and other emulator-based posteriors, run simulation-based calibration. Hold out prior realizations as synthetic truths, condition on each one's simulated observations, and test the rank of each truth in its own posterior sample for uniformity. This is nearly free because it needs no new model runs. On real-data studies, hold out later time periods or wells and score the posterior ensemble with the continuous ranked probability score and rank histograms. As a regression suite for PESTPP-IES options, adopt the open 2024 benchmark with its reference posteriors and its four accuracy metrics. Add a multivariate prior-data conflict test, a Mahalanobis or one-class check in reduced data space, to catch conflicts in gradients and trends that the current one-observation-at-a-time test misses.

The diagnostics are standard in weather forecasting and simulation-based inference ([Gneiting and Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)‡; [Hamill 2001](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493%282001%29129%3C0550:IORHFV%3E2.0.CO;2)‡). The benchmark is published groundwater work, and its published comparison covers only a standard ensemble Kalman filter. The researchers found no simulation-based calibration or coverage study of PESTPP-IES, null-space Monte Carlo or PEST's DSI. Everything here is pyEMU post-processing plus emulator runs. The benefit is a quantitative answer to whether 200 realizations suffice for a given problem, and evidence for or against each option in Proposal 6. Two cautions apply. A single synthetic truth inside a posterior ensemble says almost nothing, so the test needs many truths, which for the full smoother costs one smoother run each. And calibration against truths drawn from the model's own prior cannot detect structural error. That is the job of Proposal 11.

### Proposal 8. An exact-posterior check in a reduced space, with the emulator as coarse level

For a reduced parameter set of 10 to 100 dimensions, run an asymptotically exact sampler seeded by the smoother. The set can be global multipliers, boundary conductances, recharge factors and geostatistical hyperparameters, or the leading likelihood-informed coordinates of a field. Two designs fit PEST++ resources. Tempered sequential Monte Carlo starts from the IES posterior ensemble as its population and moves it through a sequence of tempered likelihoods, and every stage is an independent batch of runs that the PEST++ run manager can distribute. Multilevel delayed acceptance uses the DSI or Gaussian-process emulator as the coarse level and the full model as the fine level, with an adaptive error model between them, run as many short chains started from IES realizations. A cheaper third form is calibrate-emulate-sample, which fits a Gaussian process to the runs IES has already made and samples the emulated posterior ([Cleary et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03689)).

Two-stage and delayed-acceptance MCMC with surrogates are published for groundwater, with polynomial-chaos and neural-network first stages and a geothermal reservoir application ([Laloy et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/wrcr.20226)‡; [Cui, Fox and O'Sullivan 2011](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010WR010352)‡). DREAM was compared with null-space Monte Carlo on a groundwater surrogate in 2010 ([Keating et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008584)). What is new is using PEST++'s own emulators as the coarse level, its ensemble as the proposal source and starting population, and its run manager for the tempering stages. The researchers found no field-scale MODFLOW application of multilevel delayed acceptance and no groundwater use of calibrate-emulate-sample. Open samplers such as [tinyDA](https://github.com/mikkelbue/tinyDA) can drive the PEST model interface through a pyEMU worker. The expected benefit is a reference posterior on a real model against which IES can be judged, at 10^3 to 10^4 fine-model runs by the researchers' estimate. The risks are the dimension limit, the sequential fine-level chain, and surrogate error. Delayed acceptance stays exact however poor the emulator. Calibrate-emulate-sample is exact only for the emulated model.

### Proposal 9. Multifidelity estimators for prediction statistics and exceedance probabilities

Turn an IES posterior ensemble into prediction statistics with a multifidelity control-variate estimator. Run a cheap partner model for every realization. The partner can be a coarse grid, a steady-state version, the reduced model of Proposal 3 or a DSI emulator. Run the expensive predictive model, with its long scenarios, transport or particle tracking, for a subset chosen by the optimal allocation formulas. Combine the two so the estimate stays unbiased for the expensive model whatever the quality of the cheap one ([Peherstorfer, Willcox and Gunzburger 2016](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1046472)‡). For decisions framed as the probability that drawdown or concentration exceeds a threshold at the 1% level or below, use subset simulation on the emulator with a multifidelity importance-sampling correction on the full model ([Au and Beck 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0266-8920%2801%2900019-4)‡).

Multilevel Monte Carlo was demonstrated on Darcy flow with random coefficients in 2011 and on Richards' equation in 2020, and the PEST literature has paired simple and complex models since 2011 ([Cliffe et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00791-011-0160-x)‡; [Kumar et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-019-09922-8); [Doherty and Christensen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011WR010763)). The researchers found no field-scale groundwater use of multifidelity Monte Carlo, approximate control variates or multilevel best linear unbiased estimators, and no verified use of subset simulation with a real groundwater model. The method is a wrapper around PESTPP-SWP run management and open allocation codes such as [MXMCPy](https://github.com/nasa/MXMCPy), with no change to PEST++. The benefit is tighter percentile estimates from the same number of fine runs, and tail probabilities that a few hundred realizations cannot resolve and that FOSM takes as Gaussian by assumption. The speedups in the foundational papers were not re-read, so I quote none. The estimator corrects sampling error only. If the IES ensemble is a poor posterior sample the estimate inherits that, and the rare-event step needs an emulator that can be trusted in the tails.

### Proposal 10. A non-centred sparse-precision prior for unstructured grids, with staged hyperparameter estimation

Generate grid-scale log-conductivity in the PstFrom pre-processor as the solution of a sparse linear system, field = A(θ)⁻¹z. The vector z holds one standard-normal number per cell or node. The matrix A is a discretized elliptic operator whose coefficients θ set correlation range, anisotropy tensor and variance, and θ can vary in space through a few hyper-pilot-points. Expose both z and θ as PEST parameters. The prior ensemble is then trivial to draw, the covariance matrix is never formed, and the generator works on DISV and DISU grids, where FFT-based simulators do not. The same sparse solve supplies the prior-covariance products that Proposals 5 and 6 need.

Gaussian fields from stochastic PDEs are standard in spatial statistics and in adjoint-based inversion, where a groundwater application exists. Hierarchical parameterizations of this kind have been used in ensemble Kalman inversion with a groundwater-flow example ([Chada et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01781)), and uncertain variogram parameters have been sampled by MCMC in hydrogeology. Pilot points remain the PEST standard, and their developers describe multi-scale schemes as current practice ([White and Lavenue 2023](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.5802/crgeos.161/)). One researcher recalled, without being able to verify it, that PLPROC and pypestutils already support spatially varying anisotropy and range as adjustable hyperparameters for pilot points. Check those tools before treating non-stationarity as new. What two targeted searches did not find is a sparse-precision prior inside a non-intrusive ensemble smoother for groundwater or reservoir history matching. A dense covariance for 10^5 cells needs about 80 GB, which is why current practice confines it to pilot points.

The risk is the one Oliver measured. A plain smoother fails on the coupled, strongly nonlinear problem in z and θ. Three mitigations are candidates. Estimate hyperparameters first in a staged scheme. Supply the analytic derivative of the field with respect to θ and z, which is cheap because the generator is linear in z. Or use local or multimodal updates. No groundwater ensemble demonstration exists for any of them. For facies-controlled systems the same pre-processor can threshold a second such field into a level-set categorical model. That idea is published for reservoirs and for groundwater, alongside the PEST-compatible travelling pilot point method ([Khambhammettu, Renard and Doherty 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2020.103556)). The Juda benchmark's warning stands. Data will not correct a wrong geological concept.

### Proposal 11. A total-error two-pass smoother with implausibility screening

Make model error an explicit quantity at two points in the workflow. Before the smoother, history-match the low-dimensional inputs by Bayes linear emulation in waves. These inputs are the global multipliers, boundary and structural parameters and hyperparameters. Treat spatial heterogeneity as stochastic simulator noise, and put a stated model-discrepancy variance for each output in the implausibility denominator. If the non-implausible region is empty, the conceptual model is rejected before any expensive assimilation. Then run IES inside the surviving region in two passes. The first pass uses weights that represent total error. Its residuals, grouped by observation type, yield an estimated correlated total-error covariance, which the second pass uses for its noise ensemble and weights. Where a defect is understood, add a small error model such as a bias per observation group, boundary multipliers or coarse-grid correction terms, and estimate it jointly, with identifiability enforced by informative priors or physical constraints.

Structural noise, its prediction-specific consequences and Gaussian-process error models are published groundwater topics, and GMDSI teaching already says observation weights should represent "both measurement error and model error" ([White, Doherty and Hughes 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013WR014767); [GMDSI notebooks](https://github.com/gmdsi/GMDSI_notebooks)). The two-pass residual scheme and the joint error model come from reservoir history matching. The researchers found no groundwater application of implausibility-based history matching, and no controlled comparison of the reservoir total-error workflow with PEST-style weighting. All of it is non-intrusive. The emulation step can use open software such as the hmer package ([Iskauskas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05265)), and the two-pass scheme changes only weights, noise ensembles and the choice of observations. The benefit is a workflow that can say "no acceptable model" and that stops parameters chasing misfit the model cannot explain. The Rammay study and the tritium case show the size of the bias at stake.

The risks start with the zero-mean assumption in the residual scheme. Inflating noise guards against overconfidence but not against bias. An unconstrained discrepancy term will absorb signal that should inform parameters, which is easy to do in a groundwater model with a large null space. A common design rule, which I repeat without its source having been re-checked, is about ten runs per active input for a Gaussian-process emulator ([Loeppky, Sacks and Welch 2009](https://doi.org/10.1198/TECH.2009.08040)‡). That limits the screening step to tens of inputs.

## Management optimization can reuse what PEST++ already computes

Classical groundwater management is structured programming. A response matrix from superposition feeds a linear program, with sequential linearization for mild nonlinearity. GWM-2005 and PESTPP-OPT both work this way, and GWM has solved mixed-binary formulations by branch and bound since 2005 ([GWM-2005 summary](https://water.usgs.gov/nrp/gwsoftware/mf2005_gwm/Summary_gwm2005.txt)). PEST++'s chance constraints have groundwater roots too. The FOSM form is the Wagner and Gorelick design of 1987, and the stack option is their multiple-realization method of 1989 ([Wagner and Gorelick 1987](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR023i007p01162); [Wagner and Gorelick 1989](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR025i010p02211)). Both have known weaknesses. On an island aquifer, **a stack-based evolutionary analysis allowed 98 m3/d of pumping at a 6% probability of salinization, where the earlier linear FOSM analysis had allowed 260 m3/d** ([Coulon et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-28-303-2024)). The stack quantile treats constraints one at a time and ignores how badly the tail violates them. Groundwater's own literature gives a sample-size rule. Reliability with N stacked realizations follows roughly N/(N+1), so confident claims at the 95 to 99% level need hundreds of realizations or an independent validation stack ([Chan 1993](https://doi.org/10.1029/93WR01410)).

Operations research offers replacements that stay linear. Minimizing conditional value-at-risk over scenarios reduces to a linear program ([Rockafellar and Uryasev 2000](https://doi.org/10.21314/JOR.2000.038)). Optimizing against the worst distribution in a Wasserstein ball around the empirical sample reduces to a finite convex program, in many cases a linear one, with finite-sample guarantees ([Mohajerin Esfahani and Kuhn 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-017-1172-1)). The second formulation answers a worry specific to smoothers, that the posterior ensemble feeding the stack is too narrow.

For many decision variables the reservoir community's standard is the ensemble gradient. EnOpt estimates the gradient of expected value from the cross-covariance between random control perturbations and the objective, at a cost "nearly independent of the number of control variables", and it has handled 3,360 controls with thousands of constraints ([Chen, Oliver and Zhang 2009](https://doi.org/10.2118/112873-PA); [Dehdari and Oliver 2012](https://doi.org/10.2118/141589-PA)). PESTPP-SQP imports the idea. The reservoir literature also records its main failure. Pairing one control perturbation with one geological realization "does not always yield satisfactory results", the StoSAG variant fixes it, and on a CO2 pressure-management problem plain EnOpt failed because the objective varied so much between geological models ([Fonseca et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.5342); [Jeong et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2020.00108)). The PEST++ manual does not say how PESTPP-SQP pairs control perturbations with parameter realizations, and the only groundwater evidence is a 2025 conference abstract without numbers ([Macasieb, Knowling and White 2025](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2025/files/A04.macasieb.pdf)).

Other strands deserve a sentence each. Surrogate-assisted multi-objective search is already in PESTPP-MOU, where it reached 125 feasible nondominated designs in 500 runs of a three-hour coastal model on which direct particle swarm found none ([Macasieb et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024WR038554)). Feedback policies beat robust open-loop optimization by 15 to 33% in net present value in one reservoir study, and groundwater had real-time and dual-control formulations around 1990 before the topic went quiet ([Nasir and Durlofsky 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.111945); [Lee and Kitanidis 1991](https://doi.org/10.1029/91WR01307)). For monitoring design, pyEMU's greedy data-worth loop is the algorithm that submodularity theory certifies to within 63% of the optimum for suitable objectives, though forecast-variance reduction is not guaranteed to qualify. Adjoint-based sparse design optimizes all sensor weights at once at a PDE-solve cost reported as independent of parameter and sensor dimension ([Krause, Singh and Guestrin 2008](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v9/krause08a.html); [Alexanderian et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5899)). For calibration at moderate dimension, structured derivative-free least-squares solvers make progress with fewer runs than one finite-difference Jacobian needs, and a radial-basis-function global search wrapped around PEST "outperforms PEST by a large margin" on two 28-parameter problems according to a thesis abstract ([Cartis et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3338517); [Wan 2015](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/775d6507-aa5c-462e-9b2a-b5bfa1087837)). Neither displaces the smoother at high dimension. Optimal-transport misfits, which address cycle skipping in seismic inversion and have been adapted to hydrographs, are untested for breakthrough curves and seasonal heads as far as the researchers found ([Magyar and Sambridge 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-27-991-2023)).

### Proposal 12. Risk-aware management programs on the existing response matrix and stack

Once PESTPP-OPT has run, the user holds a Jacobian of constraints with respect to decision variables and can generate a stack of constraint anomalies from a posterior ensemble. Export both to an algebraic modelling layer such as Pyomo or JuMP and solve richer programs at no further model-run cost. The candidates are conditional value-at-risk objectives or constraints, joint reliability across all constraints estimated by counting the realizations that satisfy every one, Wasserstein-robust constraints, fixed-charge well selection as a mixed-integer linear program, and an epsilon-constraint sweep that traces a two-objective front from one matrix. Keep an outer sequential-linearization loop to restore accuracy for mild nonlinearity, and report reliability from a fresh validation stack. Where MF6-ADJ supports the model, fill the response matrix with one adjoint solve per constraint in place of one run per decision variable, which changes how many decision variables are affordable. As a companion test, reproduce the EnOpt-versus-StoSAG pairing difference with PESTPP-SQP on the Freyberg tutorial model. Then extend the decision variables to the trigger levels and cutback fractions of an operating rule, so the optimizer returns a policy instead of a schedule.

Response matrices, chance constraints, multiple-realization stacks and mixed-binary well selection are established groundwater methods. The researchers found no groundwater application of conditional value-at-risk or distributionally robust formulations, no published coupling of MF6-ADJ to PESTPP-OPT or PESTPP-MOU, and no journal paper applying ensemble-gradient optimization to groundwater. Exporting a MODFLOW response matrix to an algebraic modelling language is itself an inference from the structure of GWM and PESTPP-OPT, not a verified practice. Implementation is a pyEMU script plus an open solver, and PESTPP-OPT's base_jacobian() option already accepts a supplied matrix and can re-solve with no model runs. The evidence is the Coulon comparison, which shows how far the Gaussian linear shortcut can sit from a sampled answer, and a reservoir study in which robust optimization over 100 realizations raised expected value 9.5% above reactive control with much smaller variance ([van Essen et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.2118/102913-PA)). Three risks stand out. Linear response is a poor approximation for unconfined, density-dependent or threshold-controlled systems. Nonlinear constraints on streams, multi-aquifer wells and lakes fall partly outside the adjoint's coverage. And the scenario-count guarantees from control theory assume convexity ([Calafiore and Campi 2006](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2006.875041)).

If I had to order the work, I would start with the three proposals that need no new code inside any simulator or PEST++ program: the audit, the multifidelity prediction statistics and the risk-aware programs. Next come the two that change inversion itself, the hybrid-operator GLM and the smoother refinements, with the sparse-precision prior as their shared prerequisite. The solver proposals matter most for models that are large or that fail to converge, and they compound with everything else because every method here pays per forward run. This ordering is mine. No source ranks these methods against each other.

The most useful missing evidence is one benchmark. It would run, on one realistic MODFLOW 6 model, PESTPP-GLM with finite-difference Jacobians, PESTPP-GLM with adjoint-supplied Jacobians, a gradient-based matrix-free solver, PCGA and PESTPP-IES, and score all of them for cost and against a reference posterior. The 2024 open benchmark supplies such a reference for a small two-dimensional problem. On the forward side the equivalent gap is a side-by-side test of candidate solvers inside MODFLOW 6. Apart from the 2002 AMG study on MODFLOW-2000, none exists.

The coverage limits are material. The researchers ran out of shared web-search quota part-way through and fell back on direct fetches of known URLs, DOI lookups and the local GMDSI repository. Publisher pages at Elsevier, Springer and Wiley, and several USGS and repository hosts, returned errors or login redirects, so many papers from those publishers are cited from the bibliographic record and carry the dagger. Coverage of 2025 and 2026 is thin and uneven. It includes MODFLOW 6 releases through 6.8.2, the PEST++ manual at version 5.2.28, the SPE11 results, MF6-ADJ, and roughly a dozen preprints, with nothing later than July 2026 on the machine-learning side. Planned searches that never ran include 2026 updates to MF6-ADJ, groundwater simulators written in JAX or PyTorch, groundwater uses of implausibility-based history matching, multifidelity Monte Carlo and subset simulation, optimal-transport misfits, GRACE and InSAR assimilation with PEST++, and changes in operational weather-prediction data assimilation during 2025 and 2026. The FEFLOW numerics described in the notes come from a 2014 book, not from FEFLOW 10 or 11 documentation. Current HydroGeoSphere and PFLOTRAN releases were not checked, and the PEST and PEST_HP manuals for ensemble space inversion were not retrieved. Every "not found" in this report is an absence in a truncated search.

## Conclusion

The review changes the question a groundwater modeller should ask of a new method. The useful test is whether the full-physics model still decides the answer. Adjoints, multilevel solvers, safeguarded Newton iterations, delayed acceptance and control variates all pass it, because they change the cost of interrogating MODFLOW and leave its solution alone. Stand-alone emulators and learned solvers fail it unless a validation budget is set aside, and a typical calibration makes too few runs of too changeable a model to pay for their training.

The less obvious finding is that model independence, PEST's founding design choice, is what kept groundwater on finite-difference Jacobians while other fields moved on, and it no longer forces that outcome. The MODFLOW API exposes matrices and states at run time, so an adjoint, a reduced model, a multigrid prototype or an Anderson accelerator can live outside the Fortran source. Hybrid schemes can then use exact derivatives where they exist and model-independent runs where they do not. Most of the twelve proposals take that middle path. Its price is upkeep. The one groundwater adjoint that reached production before 2025 was lost to lack of maintenance, and MF6-ADJ depends on an interface staying stable. A funded benchmark and a funded maintainer would do more for this agenda than any single algorithm.
